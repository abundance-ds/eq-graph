# Validity, reliability, and responsiveness of the EQ-5D in diseases of the upper respiratory tract: a systematic review of measurement properties

## Abstract

### Purpose

This study aimed to conduct a systematic review of the published evidence regarding the measurement properties of the EQ-5D (EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L) in patients with upper respiratory tract diseases, encompassing conditions associated with the nose, pharynx, and larynx.

### Methods

A comprehensive literature search was carried out utilizing the MEDLINE, EMBASE, and EuroQol website (up to November 21, 2024). Full-text English articles focusing on original research concerning the measurement properties of the EQ-5D, including construct validity, test-retest reliability, or responsiveness, were included. Studies involving experimental versions of the EQ-5D were excluded. The quality assessment utilized the COSMIN Risk of Bias checklist, and data synthesis followed the COSMIN methodology.

### Results

A total of 22 studies, published across 23 publications (20 to 17,780 patients), were included, with the majority focusing on chronic rhinosinusitis and allergic rhinitis. There was high certainty of the evidence of sufficient construct validity for EQ VAS and the EQ-5D Index (3L and 5L). In case of chronic rhinosinusitis and allergic rhinitis, test in line with the hypotheses constituted 100% and 92%, respectively. Evidence regarding responsiveness had moderate certainty of sufficient quality. There was no available data for test-retest reliability.

### Conclusion

Both EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L are suitable for assessing health-related quality of life in upper respiratory tract diseases, though their responsiveness needs further confirmation. The available evidence suggests that both versions have sufficient construct validity, and may be used to assess HRQoL especially in chronic rhinosinusitis and allergic rhinitis.

## Introduction

To establish a straightforward yet comprehensive measure of health for clinical and economic assessment, the EuroQol Group developed the EQ-5D questionnaire, a standardized tool for evaluating health status. Over the past 25 years, the EQ-5D has been extensively employed, not only as a tool for quality-adjusted life years (QALY)-type calculations, but also in clinical trials, demographic research, and real-world clinical settings, demonstrating its validity, reliability, and responsiveness across various diseases and populations \[1, 2\].

The recent “Methods of health technology evaluation” consultation by the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) utilizes psychometric evidence from EQ-5D to assess ongoing support for its use as a reference case \[3\]. This evidence, sourced from systematic reviews and overviews of systematic reviews, highlights the construct validity and responsiveness of EQ-5D across numerous disease areas and conditions. However, NICE notes a scarcity or absence of evidence regarding EQ-5D construct validity and responsiveness in rare diseases, autoimmune conditions, haematological problems, gynaecological issues, musculoskeletal conditions, and conditions related to the nose \[4\].

In the field of upper respiratory tract diseases, condition that significantly affect global health and quality of life, EQ-5D has been recognized as a useful generic tool \[5, 6\]. These diseases, affecting structures like nose, sinuses, pharynx, larynx, and large airways, contribute to substantial morbidity and mortality worldwide \[7\]. Besides the social cost of morbidity and mortality, upper respiratory diseases significantly diminish patients’ quality of life \[8\]. Thus, addressing chronic illnesses, including various upper respiratory conditions, should prioritize improving HRQoL.

Among existing reviews in the field of upper respiratory tract diseases, in March 2002, Linder et al. \[9\] reviewed tools for assessing symptoms and HRQoL in adults with sinusitis, identifying three validated instruments for chronic sinusitis: the Chronic Sinusitis Survey (Duration-based form), the Rhinosinusitis Outcome Measure-31, and the Sinonasal Outcomes Test-16. The EuroQoL instrument showed limited responsiveness. No validated tools were found for acute sinusitis or primary care settings. In June 2015, Rudmik et al. \[10\] evaluated patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) for chronic rhinosinusitis, highlighting the Questionnaire of Olfactory Disorders, the Sinusitis Control Test, and the 22-item Sinonasal Outcome Test as the most robust chronic rhinosinusitis-specific PROMs. The EQ-5D was noted as the highest quality generic HRQoL instrument. Published reviews are outdated and do not include the latest published measurement properties.

Despite EQ-5D’s widespread use, there remains a gap in systematically summarizing its measurement properties in diseases of the upper respiratory tract. Therefore, the purpose of this review is to consolidate the existing evidence on the measurement properties - validity, test-retest reliability, and responsiveness of the EQ-5D in patients with upper respiratory tract diseases, including conditions related to the nose, pharynx and larynx.

## Methods

### Followed guidelines

This systematic review used the COSMIN guidelines regarding both terminology and measurement properties for health-related PROs \[11\], methodological quality \[12\] and risk of bias (RoB) \[13\] of studies on measurement properties. To ensure transparent and consistent reporting of outcome measurement instruments, the systematic review followed an extension of the Preferred Reporting of Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guideline: PRISMA-COSMIN for Outcome Measurement Instruments (OMIs) 2024 (Supplementary Table 1) \[14\]. Protocol for this systematic review was registered in the PROSPERO database (CRD42023481369) \[15\].

### EQ-5D questionnaire

The EQ-5D instrument consists of two main components: a descriptive system and a visual analogue scale (EQ VAS). The EQ VAS is a 20-cm, thermometer-like scale ranging from 0, denoting "the worst health you can imagine," to 100, indicating "the best health you can imagine". The descriptive system evaluates health across five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. Patients assess their health using either a three-level scale (EQ-5D-3L) or a five-level scale (EQ-5D-5L) \[16, 17\]. In the EQ-5D-3L instrument, each dimension is categorized into three levels: no problems, some problems, and extreme problems. In contrast, the EQ-5D-5L instrument expands the response options to five levels: no problems, slight problems, moderate problems, severe problems, and unable to perform/extreme problems \[17\]. The optimal health state is represented by the profile 11111 \[18\].

### Eligibility criteria

Publications were considered for inclusion in the systematic review if they presented original research, without restrictions on study design, pertaining to validation and non-validation studies (the primary objective of such studies was not validation; however, they provided results that reflected aspects of measurement properties). These publications had to be published in peer-reviewed journals and report on the measurement properties of EQ-5D (EQ-5D-3L or EQ-5D-5L), specifically addressing construct validity, test-retest reliability, or responsiveness. Additionally, the publications needed to focus on adult patients (as defined by authors of studies) with diseases of the upper respiratory tract. These diseases encompassed a variety of specific conditions related to the upper respiratory tract, including nasopharyngitis, rhinitis, sore throat, pharyngitis, sinusitis, pansinusitis, laryngopharyngitis, laryngitis, laryngotracheitis, tonsillitis, epiglottitis, nasal polyp, epistaxis, stridor or other issues related to anatomic areas like sinus, nose, nasopharynx, tonsils, adenoids, pharynx, vocal cords, larynx. Articles were required to be published in English to be eligible for inclusion in the current systematic review.

Publications were excluded from the study if they involved assessments not related to EQ-5D-3L or EQ-5D-5L, experimental or not approved versions of EQ-5D, non-empirical studies, studies published in languages other than English, or studies not available in full text. Additionally, the following types of publications were excluded: reviews, letters, books, editorials, notes, conference posters, presentations, or papers.

### Information sources and search strategy

To conduct a systematic review, three databases were searched: MEDLINE (via PubMed), EMBASE (via Elsevier access), and the EuroQol Group publication database. The search utilized a pre-established strategy (Supplementary Table 2) incorporating a combination of free-text phrases and Medical Subject Headings relevant to the EQ-5D questionnaire and diseases of the upper respiratory tract. No time constraints were applied, and the search encompassed articles from inception up to November 21, 2024. Additionally, the search process involved examining references within the identified publications.

### Selection process

Publication selection was conducted using EndNote version 20.1. The search and initial selection of abstracts were independently performed by two individuals (A.D. and D.G.). Selected abstracts were then complemented with the full texts of the articles, and the second stage of paper qualification commenced. The full texts were evaluated by two authors (A.D. and D.G.) to determine eligibility for inclusion in this systematic review. In cases of disagreement, discussions were undertaken until consensus was reached, if necessary. The list of articles meeting the study’s criteria was presented in a reference list. Additionally, the appendix includes a list of articles excluded from the study, along with reasons for their rejection (Supplementary Table 3).

### Data extraction

Data on the measurement properties of EQ-5D, extracted from publications included in this study, were compiled and analysed in a prepared Excel file (Electronic Supplementary Material 1). The extracted data encompassed information on various measurement properties, including validity (construct and content), test-retest reliability, responsiveness, feasibility, and distributional properties. Additionally, data entries related to study design, study population characteristics (such as age, sex, and country), EQ-5D properties (including outcome used, level, and value set if an Index was utilized), type of reporting (self-reported or proxy-reported, with details on the type of proxy if applicable), measurement properties, statistical analyses, and RoB assessments were collected. Data extraction was independently conducted by one of the authors (A.D. or D.G.), followed by a cross-validation process by the second author, to ensure accuracy and consistency of the findings. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion until a consensus was reached.

### EQ-5D measurement properties

Construct validity refers to the extent to which a questionnaire or test actually measures the theoretical concept (construct) it is intended to measure. It can be assessed in several ways, including convergent validity – demonstrated when the measure shows strong correlations with other instruments that assess the same or closely related constructs and known-groups validity – demonstrated when the measure can distinguish between groups that are theoretically expected to differ in the construct (e.g., patients with severe illness vs. healthy controls). Reliability refers to the degree to which a measurement tool produces consistent and stable results over repeated applications under similar conditions. One way to assess reliability is test–retest reliability, which evaluates the stability of scores over time. This involves administering the same questionnaire to the same respondents on two different occasions and then calculating the correlation between the two sets of scores.

All individual tests or studies assessing the construct validity, responsiveness, and test-retest reliability of EQ-5D, as reported in eligible publications, were independently reviewed by two authors (A.D. and D.G.). In cases of disagreement, discussions were held until a consensus was reached. Two authors (A.D. and D.G.) independently evaluated the quality of individual tests using the COSMIN RoB checklist (Supplementary Table 4, Supplementary Fig. 1) \[13\]. Tests assessing different measurement properties were rated as very good (1), adequate (2), doubtful (3), or inadequate (4). It’s important to note that a single publication might include multiple tests of an individual measurement property. Final scores were determined based on the "worst score counts" principle.

For the assessment of construct validity, hypotheses were formulated for each individual test (Supplementary Fig. 1), following the latest COSMIN guidelines \[19\]. The hypotheses guaranteed that the evidence given in all papers, including nonvalidation articles, was evaluated in a standardised manner. The developed list of hypotheses is summarized in the appendix (Supplementary Table 5). Correlation coefficients above 0.60 were considered strong, those between 0.40 and 0.59 as moderate, between 0.21 and 0.39 as weak, and 0.20 or below as indicating no correlation.

Responsiveness was assessed based on the reported Standardized Effect Size (SES) or Area Under the Curve (AUC) values. For both SES and AUC, a "positive" rating was assigned if the values were ≥ 0.7; otherwise, a "negative" rating was given.

Test-retest reliability was planned to be assessed using Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC), with a "positive" rating for ICC values ≥ 0.7.

The overall quality of EQ-5D was assessed as sufficient (+), inconsistent (±), or insufficient (-), based on the percentage of relevant tests with a "positive" rating. To rate qualitatively summarized results as sufficient at least 75% of tests must receive a "positive" rating. To rate summarized results as insufficient less than 25% of tests must receive a "positive" rating.

### Certainty assessment

The overall certainty of the evidence for each measurement property was evaluated using the modified GRADE criteria (Supplementary Fig. 1). Certainty of evidence was rated as high (H), moderate (M), low (L), or very low (VL). This method evaluates evidence based on five factors: risk of bias (study quality), inconsistency (variability in study results), indirectness (applicability of evidence to the population, interventions, or outcomes of interest), imprecision (wide confidence intervals or small sample sizes), and publication bias (underrepresentation of negative results). If there were concerns about the trustworthiness of a result, the certainty of evidence for the summarized findings was downgraded. According to the RoB criterion, the certainty of evidence could be downgraded by 1 level (serious) if 25% to 74% of studies had at least adequate methodological quality, or by 2 levels (very serious) if fewer than 25% of studies met this standard. Based on the imprecision criterion, the certainty of evidence could be downgraded by 1 level if the sample size ranged from 50 to 100, or by 2 levels if the sample size was below 50. According to the inconsistency criterion, the certainty of evidence could be downgraded by 1 level when overall rating is based on the majority of consistent results. Indirectness does not occur. Definitions for construct and/or target population have been stated in the inclusion criteria. Only studies meeting the eligibility criteria regarding population characteristics, involving patients with diseases of the upper respiratory tract, were included \[19, 20\].

The measurement properties and certainty of evidence were assessed for both the EQ VAS and the EQ-5D Index. Given that the EQ-5D questionnaire is available in three- and five-level versions, the measurement properties and certainty of evidence for the EQ-5D Index were evaluated for both versions jointly, and afterwards - separately.

## Results

### Selection of studies

The publication selection process adhered to the PRISMA-COSMIN for OMIs 2024 guideline for reporting systematic reviews of outcome measurement instruments (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" data-ref-type="fig">1</a>) \[14\]. During the initial stage of selection process, a review of 382 non-duplicated abstracts was performed, resulting in the exclusion of 322 records. In the subsequent stage, based on full-text review, 57 publications were included for further consideration, out of which 23 met the eligibility criteria for the study \[21–43\] (Fig. <a href="#Fig1" data-ref-type="fig">1</a>). ### EQ-5D characteristics

The majority of studies (70%; 16 out of 23) utilized the newer 5-level version of the EQ-5D questionnaire \[22–25, 27–29, 31, 32, 34–38, 40, 41\]. In 26% of the studies (6 out of 23 publications), a 3-level version was utilized \[21, 26, 30, 39, 42, 43\]. In one study, the specific version of EQ-5D used was not specified \[33\]. In all studies, the EQ-5D questionnaire was completed independently by patients, with no proxy reporting.

The most commonly used language version of the questionnaire was English, found in 74% of the studies (17 out of 23) – 11 studies for EQ-5D-5L and 5 studies for EQ-5D-3L \[21–24, 26, 27, 30–35, 39–42\]. Among the other language versions identified, German, Japanese, Spanish, and Thai versions were each used in a single article, each \[25, 29, 36, 43\]. Additionally, two multi-country publications reported using 19 different language versions of the EQ-5D questionnaire \[37, 38\].

Reported EQ-5D outcomes included: only EQ VAS in 57% (13 out of 23) publications \[22, 23, 27, 28, 30, 34, 35, 37–40, 42\], only EQ-5D Index in 9% (2 out of 23) publications \[21, 29\], and both EQ VAS and EQ-5D Index simultaneously in 35% (8 out of 23) publications \[24–26, 31, 32, 36, 41, 43\].

### Study characteristics

All studies included adult patients aged 18 years or older, with the exception of one study that enrolled patients aged 16 years or older \[37\]. More than 65% (15 out of 23) of the publications originated from the United States \[21–24, 26–28, 30, 31, 33–35, 39, 40, 42\]. One international study, conducted in 25 countries and published in two articles, was identified \[37, 38\]. Another international study, conducted in 10 countries, was identified and published in one article \[41\]. Other studies were single-country and were conducted in Canada \[32\], Germany \[25\], Thailand \[36\], Spain \[43\] and Japan \[29\]. A total of 11 validation \[21–23, 28, 30, 32–34, 37, 38, 42\] and 12 non-validation articles \[24–27, 29, 31, 35, 36, 39–41, 43\], with cross-sectional (n=11) \[21, 24–29, 31, 32, 41, 43\] and longitudinal (n=12) \[22, 23, 30, 33–40, 42\] study design were included in this review.

The studies included in the review varied in population size, ranging from 20 to 17,780 patients, with a median of 242 patients. Most of the eligible studies focused on patients diagnosed with chronic rhinosinusitis (10 articles) \[23, 24, 26, 31, 33–36, 39, 40\] and allergic rhinitis (5 articles) \[27–29, 37, 38\]. Other studies involved patient populations with asthma and rhinitis/allergic rhinitis (4 articles) \[21, 25, 41, 42\], chronic rhinosinusitis or rhinitis (1 article) \[31\], functional septorhinoplasty (1 article) \[22\], head and neck cancer (1 article) \[32\] , hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia (1 article) \[43\], idiopathic subglottic stenosis (1 article) \[43\], and rhinitis (1 article) \[30\]. The average age of patients in the eligible studies was 47.7 years. One study enrolled patients ranging in age from 16 to 90 years \[37\].

### Results of synthesis

Overall risk of bias per measurement property is presented in Table <a href="#Tab4" data-ref-type="table">4</a>. Construct validity was analysed in all publications included in the study, with 78% (n=18) examining convergent validity (n=12 for EQ-5D-5L and n=5 for EQ-5D-3L) and 35% (n=8) examining known-groups validity (n=6 for EQ-5D-5L and n=2 for EQ-5D-3L). Convergent validity was the most frequently reported for the EQ VAS (70%; n=16), followed by the EQ-5D Index (23%; n=7 papers).

Known-groups validity was reported in 26% (n=6) and 26% (n=6) studies, for the EQ VAS and EQ-5D Index, respectively. Overall, results were met for 99% (77 out of 78) of tests for convergent validity (98%; 52 out of 53 for EQ-5D-5L and 100%; 23 out of 23 for EQ-5D-3L) and 97% (61 out of 63) of tests for known-groups validity (96%; 54 out of 56 for EQ-5D-5L and 100%; 7 out of 7 for EQ-5D-3L).

For construct validity, the overall quality of EQ-5D was determined to be sufficient, both for the EQ VAS (99%; Table <a href="#Tab2" data-ref-type="table">2</a>), and the EQ-5D Index (97%; Table <a href="#Tab3" data-ref-type="table">3</a>). Subgroup analysis showed that the quality of EQ-5D was sufficient for all disease entities analysed. Specifically, for the EQ VAS, 92% of the results in allergic rhinitis were in accordance with the hypothesis. For the EQ-5D Index, 88% of the results in head and neck cancer and 92% of the results in allergic rhinitis were in accordance with the hypothesis (Table 4).

Depending on the EQ-5D version, for construct validity, the overall quality of EQ-5D Index was determined to be sufficient, both for the EQ-5D-3L (100%), and the EQ-5D-5L (96%). Subgroup analysis showed that the quality of both EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L was sufficient for all disease entities analysed. For the EQ-5D-5L Index, 88% of the results in head and neck cancer and 92% of the results in allergic rhinitis were in accordance with the hypothesis.

Responsiveness was studied in 2 out of the 23 publications selected for the study (10%). A total of 4 tests were conducted to assess the responsiveness, all of them for EQ VAS. In the majority of the tested hypotheses, a correct directional change was observed (3 out of 4 tests). The overall quality of EQ-5D was determined to be sufficient, as 75% or more of the relevant studies received a ‘positive’ rating.

No publication that met the criteria for inclusion in the study examined test-retest reliability.

### Certainty of evidence - construct validity

There was high certainty of evidence of sufficient construct validity for EQ VAS (Table <a href="#Tab2" data-ref-type="table">2</a>) and EQ-5D Index (for both 3L and 5L versions) (Table <a href="#Tab3" data-ref-type="table">3</a>). Subgroup analysis revealed that the certainty of evidence for the EQ VAS was downgraded by 1 level due to imprecision and was rated as moderate for idiopathic subglottic stenosis and head and neck cancer. The certainty of evidence for the EQ-5D Index was high for all disease entities studied, except for head and neck cancer (moderate) and rhinitis (low), where the certainty was downgraded by 1 level and by 2 levels due to imprecision, respectively.

The overall certainty of evidence was rated as high, both for the EQ-5D-3L, and the EQ-5D-5L (Supplementary Table 6). Subgroup analysis revealed that the certainty of evidence for the EQ-5D-3L Index was high for all disease entities studied, except for rhinitis, where the certainty was downgraded by 2 levels due to imprecision. The certainty of evidence for the EQ-5D-5L Index was high for all disease entities studied, except for chronic rhinosinusitis and head and neck cancer, where the certainty was downgraded by 1 level due to imprecision.

### Certainty of evidence - Responsiveness

There was moderate certainty of evidence of sufficient responsiveness for EQ VAS. The certainty of evidence was downgraded by 1 level due to the RoB (Table <a href="#Tab2" data-ref-type="table">2</a>).

### Inconsistency and indirectness

Certainty of evidence was not downgraded due to inconsistency and indirectness. With regard to inconsistency, the results of the included studies were either consistent or were summarized and assessed within subsets of studies before grading. In terms of indirectness, the review included only studies that met the strictly defined eligibility criteria for the target population, namely patients with diseases of the upper respiratory tract.

## Discussion

This systematic review synthesizes the evidence on measurement properties of EQ-5D (the EQ VAS and EQ-5D Index) in diseases of the upper respiratory tract, focusing on construct validity, reliability, and responsiveness. According to the study findings, both the 3-level and 5-level versions of the EQ-5D questionnaire appear to demonstrate sufficient construct validity. The evidence suggests that among the measurement properties most frequently examined in diseases of the upper respiratory tract are convergent validity and known-groups validity. Responsiveness evidence is limited to the EQ VAS outcomes. Notably, no test-retest reliability data were found. This may be due to the fact that the design of test-retest reliability studies may require stable health conditions between testing intervals to yield meaningful results. However, diseases of the upper respiratory tract, such as rhinitis, often involve symptoms that fluctuate over short periods, making such studies more challenging to conduct.

Our systematic review provides a comprehensive synthesis of EQ-5D measurement properties focusing on diseases of the upper respiratory tract. To explore this topic area thoroughly, broad criteria were used to qualify studies for inclusion. This allowed for the analysis of studies not explicitly aimed at examining the measurement properties of EQ-5D in diseases of the upper respiratory tracts, including both validation and non-validation studies. Additionally, studies in which the analysis of EQ-5D measurement properties in diseases of the upper respiratory tract was indirectly indicated by the methodology used and results obtained were eligible for inclusion. The analysis indicates that the overall quality of EQ-5D was deemed sufficient for both construct validity and responsiveness, with high certainty of evidence for construct validity and moderate certainty for responsiveness (downgraded by one level due to RoB).

The findings of this review align with previous research assessing the use of EQ-5D in diseases of the upper respiratory tract. Linder et al. \[9\] identified symptom scores and quality of life instruments for chronic sinusitis, and while the EuroQoL instrument was shown to have limited responsiveness in sinusitis, it still held considerable value as a generic HRQoL measure. Similarly, Rudmik et al. \[10\] recognized the EQ-5D as a high-quality generic PROM for chronic rhinosinusitis, supporting its utility in broader applications. These findings reinforce the validity of the EQ-5D in assessing health outcomes in diseases of the upper respiratory tract, although responsiveness and reliability remain areas needing more research.

Several limitations of the current review must be acknowledged. The generalizability of the results may be limited as the studies included in the review were mainly focused on chronic rhinosinusitis and allergic rhinitis. Reporting results, even for a limited number of disease entities, remains essential as more evidence emerges. Limited evidence is available for EQ-5D construct validity and responsiveness in other conditions related to the nose including rhinitis, and asthma. Furthermore, among the measurement properties of EQ-5D, mainly convergent validity has been studied. Known-group validity and responsiveness were analysed less frequently. Test-retest reliability has not been investigated in diseases of the upper respiratory tract at all. Additionally, the data primarily represent a patient population aged over 40 years, raising uncertainties about the transferability of the findings to a younger population with diseases of the upper respiratory tract. Furthermore, the generalizability of our findings is inherently restricted by the English language in the inclusion criteria of the study, which might result in the exclusion of research conducted in non-English-speaking populations. Moreover, the majority of included studies consisted of patient populations from the United States, indicating the need for further studies in other geographic regions, including Europe and Asia.

There is a clear need for studies examining the test-retest reliability of EQ-5D in diseases of the upper respiratory tract. Additionally, further exploration into the responsiveness of EQ-5D, particularly in longitudinal studies assessing treatment effects, would provide valuable insights into its sensitivity to change. Studies targeting a broader range of diseases of the upper respiratory tract and individuals from different regions, would also enhance the generalizability of EQ-5D in clinical and economic assessments. Moreover, further work should focus on expanding the evidence base regarding EQ-5D’s measurement properties in conditions that have not been well represented in the current literature, such as asthma or nasal polyps.

## Conclusions

The data supporting the measurement properties of the EQ-5D in diseases of the upper respiratory tract are compiled in this review. Currently available evidence indicate that both the EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L questionnaires appear to demonstrate sufficient construct validity, particularly in terms of convergent validity, and may be used to assess HRQoL in diseases of the upper respiratory tract. However, caution should be exercised in using the EQ-5D questionnaire to detect clinically significant changes in quality of life, as responsiveness has not been widely studied in the disease area under review. Further studies are needed to confirm the test-retest reliability of EQ-5D in diseases of the upper respiratory tract.
