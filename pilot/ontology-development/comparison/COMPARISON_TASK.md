# Anonymous ontology comparison

Compare the three completed ontology lineages in `inputs/`.
They used the same 30 papers and the same three-round process.

Do not propose a harmonized ontology in this step.
Do not rank the candidates or select a winner.
Treat different names and file structures as irrelevant when their meanings are equivalent.

Identify:

- equivalent, overlapping, conflicting and unique elements;
- different levels of granularity or different boundaries between elements;
- stable common ideas and important unresolved alternatives;
- application consistency, change across rounds, coverage, redundancy and clarity;
- cases where the paper applications support or contradict an ontology choice.

Use the lineage records as the main evidence.
Use the source papers in `papers/` only when you must check a disputed or unclear case.
Cite the candidate files and paper identifiers that support each important observation.

Do not use majority rule, element counts or a combined numerical score to decide content.
Keep material alternatives explicit for later human review.

Write a clear semantic comparison under `output/` in the Markdown structure that best communicates the result.
