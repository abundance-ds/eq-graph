## 1. The Danish EQ-5D-5L Value Set: A Hybrid Model Using cTTO and DCE Data

DOI: `10.1007/s40258-021-00639-3`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed primary valuation study. It develops the first Danish EQ-5D-5L value set and selects a final model.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Preference evidence and scoring artifact. The target artifact is a national value set for the EQ-5D-5L descriptive system.
- **Evidence source:** 1,014 included adult general-public respondents in Denmark. Recruitment aimed to match age, gender, education, and region. It began with a Statistics Denmark sample and later added a market-research panel. Data came from computer-assisted personal interviews.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D-5L is the data generator and the instrument to score. The new Danish 5L value set is the output. The Danish 3L and crosswalk value sets are comparators.
- **Methods and comparison:** Each respondent valued ten states with cTTO and seven pairs with DCE under EQ-VT 2.1. The study compared cTTO-only, DCE-only, and hybrid models. It examined censoring, heteroscedasticity, logical consistency, and sensitivity to the reintroduction of feedback-flagged responses.
- **Output and interpretation:** The study selected a heteroscedastic censored hybrid model. It reports logically ordered coefficients and utilities from 1 to -0.757. The value set is presented for Danish QALY and priority-setting use.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** The recruitment source changed during collection, and people with low education and people aged 18 to 24 were slightly under-represented. The paper states that statistical and theoretical grounds for hybrid modelling do not fully coincide. DCE has no independent dead-to-full-health anchor.

## 2. Psychometric properties of the EQ-5D-5L: a systematic review of the literature

DOI: `10.1007/s11136-020-02688-y`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed systematic review with limited quantitative pooling.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Instrument, component, observation, and scored index. The evaluation target is the official EQ-5D-5L descriptive system and index, not its valuation methods.
- **Evidence source:** 99 publications from 32 countries, found in searches through January 2019. Eligible evidence concerned adults and official EQ-5D-5L versions.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D-5L is the evaluation target. Other HRQoL, clinical, and global-health measures are validity references. EQ-5D-3L is a version comparator in some included studies.
- **Methods and comparison:** The review extracted distribution, missingness, test-retest reliability, validity, and responsiveness. It pooled full-health proportions, mean index values, and correlations with random-effects models when data permitted.
- **Evaluation:** Feasibility, floor and ceiling effects, index and item reliability, content and construct validity, convergent and known-groups validity, and responsiveness.
- **Output and interpretation:** The review reports broad support for feasibility, validity, and index-level reliability. It also reports item-level instability, persistent ceilings, and weak coverage of cognition or communication. Responsiveness evidence was heterogeneous and less mature.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Experimental 5L versions and application studies without an explicit psychometric purpose were excluded. The review did not assess valuation methods. Its positive global conclusion is therefore narrower than the full instrument-to-utility chain.

## 3. EuroQol data for assessment of population health needs and instrument evaluation (EQ-DAPHNIE): a study for enhancing population health assessment

DOI: `10.1007/s11136-025-03983-2`

- **Contribution and stage:** Study protocol and resource description with mixed status. A UK pilot and two multi-country rounds had been collected when the paper reported the infrastructure. Later rounds and some extensions remained planned.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Use context and data resource across several instruments. The direct output is standardized population-health data infrastructure, not a new instrument or value set.
- **Evidence source:** Cross-sectional online general-adult samples. The pilot included the UK. Rounds 1 and 2 included 15 countries, usually with about 4,500 completed responses per country. Recruitment used a commercial panel and quotas for demographic and geographic characteristics.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D-5L, bolt-ons, EQ-HWB, PROMIS-10, ASCOT or ICECAP-A, WHO-5, OPQOL-brief, PHQ-2, and GAD-2 are data generators. An EQ-5D-5L anchoring vignette is a response-heterogeneity tool. Instruments vary by country and survey burden.
- **Methods and comparison:** Standardized online collection, translations and local adaptation, fixed and randomized instrument order, conditional questions, a soft launch, repeated age and marital-status questions, quota monitoring, and planned post-stratification. Intended comparisons include population norms, countries, instruments, social determinants, and reporting heterogeneity.
- **Output and interpretation:** The infrastructure supports comparable population-health assessment and instrument evaluation. The paper documents collection methods and governance. It does not report substantive instrument-performance results from the main rounds.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Online panels can exclude people without internet access and can retain selection bias after quotas. Cultural response differences, cross-sectional timing, and country-specific survey variations can affect comparison. The term *representative* is an aim and claim; the design evidence must remain visible.

## 4. What do HTA agencies need for generating health-related quality of life evidence? Findings from a global survey

DOI: `10.1017/s0266462326103602`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed cross-sectional practice and needs survey with quantitative description and content analysis.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Decision-use context. The paper studies how HTA personnel use instruments, elicitation methods, preference sources, and utility data.
- **Evidence source:** 238 individual respondents from 45 countries and six regions. Respondents worked in or for HTA agencies. Recruitment was purposive and network-based. Respondents gave personal views and did not act as verified official agency representatives.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D, SF-6D, EQ-5D-Y, and other utility instruments are reported options. TTO, VAS, SG, DCE, BWS, and PTO are reported elicitation methods. Local or foreign general-public and patient preferences are reported data sources.
- **Methods and comparison:** An anonymous online survey used four-point frequency scales, regional summaries, importance scores for research topics, and content analysis of open responses. Comparisons were across instruments, methods, preference sources, quality concerns, and regions.
- **Output and interpretation:** EQ-5D was the most-used utility instrument. TTO, VAS, and SG were the most-used elicitation methods. Respondents often met poor representativeness, small samples, mismatched data, and mixed methods within one model. Recent tariffs, child instruments, and instruments that cover health and social care were leading global priorities, with regional variation.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Some countries had fewer than three responses. Network recruitment can over-represent people familiar with EuroQol. Agency identity and respondent eligibility were not independently verified. Country-level aggregation does not establish official national or agency policy.

## 5. EQ-5D-Y-5L: developing a revised EQ-5D-Y with increased response categories

DOI: `10.1007/s11136-019-02115-x`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed multi-phase instrument-development study. It produces a self-report EQ-5D-Y-5L version for more psychometric and valuation research.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Instrument version, severity labels, and response-level structure.
- **Evidence source:** Children and adolescents aged 8 to 15 in Germany, Spain, Sweden, and the UK. Sources included focus groups, 255 sorting or response-scaling interviews, and later cognitive interviews. Phase 2 included healthy participants and, in three countries, participants receiving treatment for a health condition.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D-Y-3L is the starting version. Draft 4L and 5L versions are development targets and direct comparators. The selected EQ-5D-Y-5L is the output.
- **Methods and comparison:** Instrument review and focus groups generated labels. Younger children used sorting with faces, and older children used response scaling. Selection used position on a severity continuum, agreement, spread, comprehension, and ordinary language. Cognitive interviews tested understanding and feasibility. National teams then harmonized language versions.
- **Evaluation:** Label severity ordering, comprehension, feasibility, and preference for four or five levels.
- **Output and interpretation:** Most participants preferred the 5L form. The study selected five severity levels in each of five dimensions and retained some language-specific differences after harmonization.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Samples were convenient and not nationally representative. UK procedures and recruitment differed from the other countries. Recruitment of children with health conditions was difficult. The study did not yet establish reliability, validity, responsiveness, proxy forms, or value sets for the new version.

## 6. Estimating an EQ-5D-Y-3L Value Set for China

DOI: `10.1007/s40273-022-01216-9`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed primary valuation study. It establishes a Chinese EQ-5D-Y-3L value set and tests an expanded cTTO design.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Preference evidence, valuation model, and scoring artifact for child health profiles.
- **Evidence source:** Two independent adult general-public samples. In total, 1,058 respondents completed DCE tasks and 418 completed cTTO tasks. Recruitment covered 14 Chinese provinces or cities and used quotas plus non-probability snowball and purposive methods. Adults valued health for a hypothetical 10-year-old child.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D-Y-3L is the profile system to score. DCE gives relative preference evidence. cTTO gives anchored evidence. A hybrid value set is the output.
- **Methods and comparison:** The study compared a DCE mixed-logit model with mapping against hybrid DCE-cTTO models. The cTTO design expanded from ten to 28 states. Evaluation used coefficient significance, monotonicity, prediction error, and the treatment of the unusually low `33333` state.
- **Output and interpretation:** The selected hybrid model adds an `A3` term for severe problems on all dimensions. It had no inconsistent or insignificant coefficients and predicted the observed negative value for `33333`. The paper recommends the resulting value set for Chinese pediatric economic evaluation.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** cTTO and DCE covered different geographic samples. The imagined child's relationship to the respondent was not fixed. The cTTO task can create ethical and temporal problems when adults trade a child's life years. High youth-state values and the gap around `33333` remain unresolved. The paper's key-points box says four regions, but the abstract and methods say 14 regions or provinces/cities. The method description is more specific and is used here.

## 7. Frequency and severity response scales for pain and discomfort: psychometric insights from EQ-HWB

DOI: `10.1007/s11136-025-04003-z`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed secondary psychometric analysis of a cross-sectional dyadic survey.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Item and response-scale behavior.
- **Evidence source:** Existing data from 504 caregiver-care-recipient dyads, for 1,008 adults. Health conditions were self-reported. The analysis treated respondents as individuals rather than as linked dyads.
- **Artifact roles:** Four EQ-HWB pain and discomfort items are evaluation targets. Frequency and severity scales are direct comparators. The EQ-5D-5L pain/discomfort item is a convergent reference.
- **Methods and comparison:** Spearman correlations, Shannon indices, graded-response IRT, DIF analysis, and ordinal logistic regression. The paper compared frequency with severity, pain with discomfort, trait ranges, and health-condition subgroups.
- **Evaluation:** Association, response-category informativity, latent-trait discrimination and thresholds, DIF, and condition associations.
- **Output and interpretation:** Frequency and severity were strongly related but had complementary behavior. Frequency responses used categories more evenly and were more sensitive at lower trait levels. Severity items discriminated better at higher levels. The paper favors both scales in long instruments and frequency in a short instrument when broad informativity is the priority.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** The analysis was cross-sectional and used self-reported diagnoses. The EQ-HWB used a seven-day recall period, while EQ-5D used *today*. Recall period, item wording, and construct differences can therefore affect some scale comparisons. Cultural and language transfer needs more testing.

## 8. Developing a quality of life framework from the perspective of laypeople: a qualitative comparison with the EQ-HWB framework

DOI: `10.1007/s11136-025-04038-2`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed qualitative framework study and partial content-validity evaluation.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Intended construct and conceptual framework.
- **Evidence source:** Thirty Chinese lay participants from two regions: ten healthy people, ten patients, and ten informal caregivers. Quotas covered age, gender, education, health experience, and rural or urban registration. Semi-structured face-to-face interviews continued until the authors judged that saturation had occurred.
- **Artifact roles:** The inductively and deductively developed Chinese QoL framework is the study output. The EQ-HWB framework and its 96 candidate items supply an initial codebook and the comparison target.
- **Methods and comparison:** Two coders used thematic framework analysis. The team filtered codes with explicit rules, organized the retained codes into subthemes and themes, and compared the result with EQ-HWB at both levels.
- **Evaluation:** Content-validity comprehensiveness and cultural concept coverage. Relevance and comprehensibility were explicitly deferred to other work.
- **Output and interpretation:** The Chinese framework has eight themes: feeling and emotion, cognition, self-identity, coping, physical sensation, relationship, activity, and mindset. Seven align broadly with EQ-HWB. Mindset is additional, and several subthemes or placements differ. The paper interprets the overlap as support for EQ-HWB comprehensiveness in China.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** The sample had regional and severity gaps. Saturation had no preset criterion. Translation of *mindset* and *positive/negative energy* was difficult. External and material factors were removed by the analytic rules, so the resulting framework reflects the study's definition of personal QoL outcomes. The reported statement "68% (18/57)" for subtheme alignment is arithmetically inconsistent and needs correction or clarification.

## 9. Statistical Methods for Analyzing EQ-5D in Randomized Clinical Trials: A Systematic Literature Review

DOI: `10.1016/j.jval.2025.02.001`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed systematic review of analytic practice in randomized trials.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Analyzed outcomes. The paper examines how trial reports analyze EQ-5D utility, EQ VAS, and dimension responses after baseline.
- **Evidence source:** 2,125 unique RCTs. MEDLINE and EMBASE were searched from inception through November 2021. ClinicalTrials.gov was searched in August 2023, with publications or posted results used when available.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D is the trial data generator. Utility, EQ VAS, and dimension responses are separate analytic targets. Statistical model families are the reported options under review.
- **Methods and comparison:** The review categorized descriptive, bivariate, multivariable, repeated-measure, categorical, and survival methods. It compared single with multiple postbaseline collections and recorded assumption checks, baseline adjustment, and missing-data methods. The review itself used descriptive synthesis and did not appraise trial risk of bias.
- **Output and interpretation:** Utility was analyzed most often, then EQ VAS; dimension responses were much less common. Linear fixed-effect models were common for one postbaseline measure, and linear mixed-effect models were common for repeated measures. Few trials reported model-assumption checks or baseline adjustment. Missing data were explicitly examined in less than one third of trials, and last observation carried forward remained common among imputed analyses.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Sparse reporting can make adequacy checks appear less common than they were. The long search period combines changing practices. The review did not compare method performance or give a single preferred model. The supplied structured record omits authors, journal, and publication date, although the converted article text contains them. This is a structured-metadata gap and was not reconstructed here.

## 10. Comparing the measurement properties of the EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L and CHU9D in children and adolescents: a measurement property study

DOI: `10.1007/s10198-025-01770-x`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed comparative measurement-property study with a seven-day retest.
- **Measurement-chain layer:** Instruments, dimension responses, health profiles, and EQ VAS. Utilities were not the evaluation target.
- **Evidence source:** 356 Brazilian children and adolescents aged 8 to 18 from public and private schools in São Paulo state. Of these, 181 met a self-report definition of musculoskeletal pain that also affected activity or school. Instruments were self-completed at baseline and after seven days.
- **Artifact roles:** EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, and CHU9D are co-targets and comparators. PedsQL is a construct-validity reference. PIP-Kids and a pain rating scale define groups and severity.
- **Methods and comparison:** Missing-response and ceiling or floor analysis, Kappa for dimensions, ICC and error measures for EQ VAS, prespecified correlation hypotheses, and known-groups tests. Comparisons included instrument, pain group, pain severity, and 3L versus 5L ceiling reduction.
- **Evaluation:** Feasibility, distribution, test-retest reliability, measurement error, hypothesis-based construct validity, and known-groups validity.
- **Output and interpretation:** All three instruments had poor-to-moderate dimension reliability but could distinguish children with and without musculoskeletal pain. Construct-validity results depended on instrument pairing and pain group. Measurement properties were generally stronger in the pain group. The authors consider the instruments usable, especially for children with musculoskeletal pain.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Musculoskeletal pain was self-reported, heterogeneous, and used a one-month recall, while the target instruments used *today*. Consecutive instruments can cause recall or response carryover. There was no gold standard, and criterion validity was not assessed. Failure to confirm 75% of prespecified hypotheses does not by itself prove invalidity, as the paper notes. Ceiling effects and weak retest reliability qualify the broad conclusion of good validity and feasibility.
