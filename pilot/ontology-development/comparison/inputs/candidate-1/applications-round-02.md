## 1. A Value Set for EQ-5D-5L in the United Arab Emirates

DOI: `10.1016/j.jval.2025.01.003`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed primary valuation study. It produces the first reported UAE EQ-5D-5L value set and selects a final model.
- **Purpose and layer:** Elicit health preferences, compare valuation models, and create a scoring artifact for UAE cost-utility and population-health use.
- **Evidence source and perspective:** The analysis includes 1,005 adults from all seven emirates. The sample contains UAE nationals and expatriates who had lived in the UAE for at least five years. Two-stage quotas covered emirate, age, and sex. Recruitment also used networks, snowball methods, public places, posters, and social media. Interviews took place from January through August 2023, in Arabic or English, face-to-face or online.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** EQ-5D-5L profiles are the objects valued and the objects that the output scores. cTTO and DCE responses supply preference evidence. The selected hybrid model is the transformation from that evidence to the UAE value set.
- **Methods and comparison:** Respondents completed ten cTTO and seven DCE tasks under the EQ-VT protocol. The study compared cTTO-only, DCE-only, and hybrid models, with Tobit censoring, heteroskedasticity corrections, sensitivity exclusions, and cross-validation. The same study sample supplied both elicitation methods.
- **Evaluation:** Logical consistency, coefficient significance, model fit, prediction error, out-of-sample prediction, censoring treatment, and sensitivity to flagged responses. The hybrid heteroskedastic Tobit model censored at -1 was selected.
- **Output and interpretation:** The value set ranges from 1 to -0.654, with 15.3% of profiles below zero. Mobility has the largest modeled effect. The paper presents the value set for local economic evaluation and other health-system uses.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Quotas do not remove selection risk from network and snowball recruitment. The population is unusually dynamic, and expatriate employment and residency can affect valuation. The English tasks used the UK English instrument because no UAE English version was available. Language, citizenship, residence duration, and lived experience therefore remain linked. The abstract gives a mean age of 39 years, but the main results give 32.1 years. The correct value is unclear from the supplied paper.

## 2. A Direct Comparison Between Discrete Choice With Duration and Composite Time Trade-Off Methods: Do They Produce Similar Results?

DOI: `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.016`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed comparative valuation-method study.
- **Purpose and layer:** Test whether a DCE with a duration attribute (DCEd) can produce EQ-5D-5L health-state values similar to cTTO and other EQ-VT models.
- **Evidence source and perspective:** An EQ-VT sample included 1,079 adults interviewed from June through September 2022. A separate DCEd sample included 970 adults after quality exclusions, with collection from November 2022 through June 2023. Both samples represent general-public preferences in Trinidad and Tobago, but they differ in respondent composition, collection period, mode, and recruitment process.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** EQ-5D-5L profiles are the common valuation target. cTTO, DCE without duration, and split-triplet DCEd are elicitation methods. Linear and nonlinear duration models transform DCEd choices into values on the dead-to-full-health scale. cTTO, rescaled DCE, and hybrid EQ-VT models are comparison outputs.
- **Methods and comparison:** Each DCEd respondent completed 18 split-triplet tasks. Each EQ-VT respondent completed ten cTTO and 12 DCE tasks. Mixed-logit DCEd models used linear or nonlinear time preferences. Scatterplots, correlations, mean absolute differences, and Bland-Altman plots compared all 3,125 profiles and the 86 states observed in cTTO.
- **Evaluation:** Agreement, dimension ordering, scale range, observed-state prediction, sensitivity to response-time exclusion, and treatment of temporal preference. Agreement was strong only after the DCEd model allowed nonlinear time preference.
- **Output and interpretation:** The nonlinear DCEd model gave values close to the EQ-VT models and a worst-state value of about -0.54. The paper proposes DCEd as a less costly candidate for further valuation work, not as an established interchangeable method in all settings.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** The comparison is direct at the method-output level but not within respondent. The two samples differ in education, ethnicity, and some age and sex distributions. The study removed 611 of 1,581 DCEd completers; it stopped public-place collection because of speeding and flatlining. A single-country comparison cannot establish transfer across cultures or collection systems. Flatlining can indicate either strong preferences or low engagement, and the model cannot separate them.

## 3. Psychometric properties of EQ-5D-5L for use in patients with Graves' disease

DOI: `10.1186/s12955-023-02177-z`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed prospective measurement-property study with baseline, one-month, and six-month collection.
- **Purpose and layer:** Evaluate EQ-5D-5L dimension responses, the Hong Kong value-set index, and EQ VAS in adults with relapsed Graves' disease.
- **Evidence source:** A convenience cohort of 125 patients came from endocrinology and surgical clinics at three Hong Kong public hospitals. Of these, 101 completed the one-month follow-up and 100 completed the six-month follow-up. Sixty-four patients formed the stable subgroup for test-retest analysis.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** EQ-5D-5L is the evaluation target and data generator. Its index is scored with the Hong Kong value set. EQ VAS is a separate target. ThyPRO-39 is a disease-specific convergent reference. A patient global change response is an anchor for six-month responsiveness groups.
- **Methods and comparison:** The study assessed ceiling and floor distributions, Spearman correlations, Gwet's AC2 and agreement for dimensions, ICC for the index and EQ VAS, and effect sizes for improved, unchanged, and worsened groups.
- **Evaluation:** Convergent validity was supported. Dimension agreement was substantial to very high, while index and EQ VAS ICCs were moderate. Responsiveness was supported for worsened health, especially for EQ VAS, but not for self-reported improvement. The index had a baseline ceiling.
- **Output and interpretation:** The paper supports EQ-5D-5L use in this clinical population, with an important directional qualifier: the evidence for deterioration is stronger than the evidence for improvement.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** The worsened group included only 21 people, and confidence intervals were wide. Attrition, convenience recruitment, public-clinic sampling, and baseline ceiling limit transfer. The methods say that the global change question was asked at six months, but the paper does not clearly state how the 64-person stable group for the one-month retest was identified. This limits interpretation of the retest reference condition.

## 4. Developing the EuroQol toddler and infant populations (EQ-TIPS) instrument: qualitative analysis of expert views on content validity and conceptual challenges

DOI: `10.1007/s11136-025-04150-3`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed qualitative consultation within an active instrument-development program. EQ-TIPS version 2.0 remained experimental, and the five-level version was outside this study.
- **Purpose and layer:** Review the construct, content, wording, proxy completion, age range, response levels, recall period, and possible uses of an infant and toddler HRQoL instrument.
- **Evidence source:** Thirty-three experts from 15 countries joined three online groups: EuroQol measurement experts, pediatric health and development experts, and pediatric instrument developers. Recruitment was purposive and came from author networks, colleagues, and literature.
- **Perspective and artifact roles:** EQ-TIPS version 2.0 is the development and evaluation target. It asks a proxy to describe a child aged zero to three years. The evidence comes from experts, not from the target children or a caregiver sample. EQ-5D-Y and EQ-5D are lifespan-transition references.
- **Methods and comparison:** Three semi-structured online consultations used group-specific guides, breakout discussions, transcripts, and deductive thematic analysis with scope for new themes. The comparison covers six dimensions, candidate missing dimensions, age-specific meaning, proxy type, and consistency with later-life EuroQol measures.
- **Evaluation:** Expert-assessed relevance, comprehensibility, comprehensiveness, cultural transfer, proxy interpretability, and intended-construct clarity. Experts generally accepted the six dimensions but asked for tests of sleep and emotion. They preferred observable examples over the phrase *age-appropriate behavior*.
- **Output and interpretation:** The paper supplies revision priorities, not a finalized version. It calls for clearer construct definition, age-specific examples, tests of additional content, and later caregiver work across literacy, socioeconomic, cultural, and health-condition groups.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Known-network recruitment can align expert views. Group knowledge and size differed, and breakout rooms limited whole-group consensus. Proxy answers can mix the child's health with caregiver knowledge, expectations, and spillover. The best proxy can depend on context. The report says that 44 experts were invited, but the three reported invitation counts sum to 43. The missing allocation is not explained.

## 5. Translation and cultural adaptation of the EQ-5D-Y-5L into Modern Standard Arabic for use in Egypt

DOI: `10.1186/s41687-025-00985-z`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed translation and initial cultural adaptation. It produces a final approved Modern Standard Arabic EQ-5D-Y-5L version for Egypt. Psychometric evaluation is outside this report.
- **Purpose and layer:** Create and test a language version, with focus on instructions, dimension wording, physical-pain meaning, and the order of five severity labels.
- **Evidence source:** Professional translators completed two forward and two back translations. Eleven Egyptian children aged 8 to 15 years took part in cognitive interviews; eight completed the full card-ranking exercise and three tested revisions. The convenience sample covered two governorates, both sexes, and children with and without chronic conditions.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** The UK English EQ-5D-Y-5L is the source version. The Egyptian Arabic EQ-5D-Y-3L is a terminology reference. Draft Arabic paper and digital versions are development targets. The approved Modern Standard Arabic version is the output.
- **Methods and comparison:** Forward translation, reconciliation, version-management review, back translation, card ranking, self-completion, cognitive debriefing, targeted retesting, and proofreading. Comparisons tracked semantic equivalence, consistency with Y-3L wording, severity order, and paper versus digital instructions.
- **Evaluation:** Comprehensibility, semantic and conceptual equivalence, cultural fit, severity ordering, completion burden, and instruction clarity. Ten of 160 card placements were inverted. Interview evidence led to a clearer physical-pain term and a child-friendly term for *item*.
- **Output and interpretation:** Children could complete and explain the final version. Younger children often needed to reread instructions or ask for confirmation, so the authors recommend interviewer support for ages 8 to 10.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Eleven children from two Egyptian governorates cannot establish use across Egypt or other Arabic-speaking countries. Modern Standard Arabic does not remove regional dialect and cultural differences. The conclusion permits possible wider use, but the discussion also says that other countries need local validation. Reliability, validity, responsiveness, and scoring were not reported here.

## 6. Testing the Psychometric Properties of 9 Bolt-Ons for the EQ-5D-5L in a General Population Sample

DOI: `10.1016/j.jval.2024.03.2195`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed secondary psychometric analysis of an online population survey. The nine bolt-ons remained in development.
- **Purpose and layer:** Test whether added EQ-5D-5L dimensions improve content coverage, distribution, validity, discrimination, and explanation in a general population and selected condition groups.
- **Evidence source:** The final analysis included 1,587 Hungarian adults after 113 data-quality exclusions. Soft quotas covered age, gender, residence, and region. Health conditions were physician-diagnosed but respondent-reported. Collection was cross-sectional in November 2020.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** EQ-5D-5L is the core evaluation target. Breathing, cognition, hearing, self-confidence, skin irritation, sleep, social relationships, tiredness, and vision are candidate extension components. PROMIS measures, SF-6D, PROMIS Global Health, SWLS, EQ VAS, and known groups are references. Transformed level-sum scores are unweighted analytic summaries, not utility scores.
- **Methods and comparison:** Response distribution, ceiling reduction, correlations, PCA-informed confirmatory factor analysis, known-group relative efficiency, and regression explanatory power. The study compared the core instrument with single and selected multiple bolt-ons.
- **Evaluation:** Distribution, divergent and convergent validity, structural validity, known-group validity, and incremental explanatory power. Sleep, tiredness, and vision reduced the core ceiling most. Condition-relevant bolt-ons often improved known-group discrimination. Added explanatory power after the five core items was modest.
- **Output and interpretation:** The paper supports selective use and further development of relevant bolt-ons. It does not support attaching all nine or treating them as an established preference-weighted system. One or two bolt-ons often supplied most of the measured gain.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Severe responses were sparse. Item and bolt-on order was fixed. Level-sum scores give equal weight to dimensions and can hide profile differences. The factor model lacked enough reference items for several sensory or symptom concepts. Psychometric gain does not establish that the extended profiles can be valued, compared with national EQ-5D value sets, or used as a reference-case utility measure.

## 7. Impact of mapped EQ-5D utilities on cost-effectiveness analysis: in the case of dialysis treatments

DOI: `10.1007/s10198-018-0987-x`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed secondary methodological and decision-model analysis.
- **Purpose and layer:** Test how the provenance of utilities changes incremental QALYs and ICERs in two dialysis cost-utility models.
- **Evidence source:** Reused cross-sectional data came from 75 hemodialysis and 75 peritoneal-dialysis patients in Singapore. The two Markov models represented diabetic and non-diabetic patients over ten years. Mapping algorithms came from separate US development samples, and all value sets were from the UK or England.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** Observed EQ-5D-5L profiles are scored with the England 5L value set and serve as the reference. SF-12 responses are the source for five mapped EQ-5D-3L estimates and for SF-6D scores. Mapping functions and value sets are transformation artifacts. Utilities feed the same decision models, which produce QALYs and ICERs.
- **Methods and comparison:** Three direct-mapping and two response-mapping functions, SF-6D scoring, regression-adjusted treatment-state utilities, two Markov models, and 1,000 bootstrap replications. The downstream comparison holds the decision-model structure constant while the utility source changes.
- **Evaluation:** Agreement in treatment-state utility differences and resulting incremental QALYs and ICERs. All mapped EQ-5D and SF-6D variants gave smaller QALY gains and higher ICERs than directly collected EQ-5D-5L in these models.
- **Output and interpretation:** The paper recommends direct EQ-5D collection when possible and one jurisdictional reference measure for consistent decisions. Its main contribution is not a new mapping model; it is evidence that a measurement transformation can change a downstream decision result.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** The comparison mixes observed 5L with mapped 3L, different descriptive systems, different recall periods, US mapping samples, UK preference weights, and Singapore patient data. The cross-sectional clinical sample was small, and utility was held constant in each model state. The result is a case study and does not prove that mapping always lowers incremental QALYs.

## 8. Health Inequality Analysis in Europe: Exploring the Potential of the EQ-5D as Outcome

DOI: `10.3389/fpubh.2021.744405`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed cross-sectional population association study.
- **Purpose and layer:** Compare an EQ-5D-5L profile summary with EQ VAS as outcomes in education-related health-inequality analysis.
- **Evidence source:** An online panel supplied 10,172 complete responses from adults aged 18 to 75 years in Italy, the Netherlands, and the UK during June and July 2017. Samples were selected to match age, sex, and education. Participants reported education, income, work status, and chronic conditions.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS are data generators and co-targets. Education is a proxy indicator for socioeconomic status. The EQ-5D-5L level-sum score gives equal weight to the five responses and was linearly transformed to a 0-to-100 scale. It is an unweighted analytic representation, not a value-set utility.
- **Methods and comparison:** Country and education strata, condition subgroups, ANOVA, rank tests, and stepwise multivariable regression. The study compared discrimination and predictor strength for the transformed level-sum score and EQ VAS.
- **Evaluation:** Known-group discrimination and analytic usefulness for inequality analysis. The level-sum score usually separated education groups slightly more than EQ VAS. Chronic conditions and inability to work had stronger adjusted associations than education in most models.
- **Output and interpretation:** The paper supports a multidimensional EQ-5D summary as a population-health outcome when utility comparisons are not suitable. It reports associations, not causal pathways. It does not show that chronic disease or work status removes the underlying effect of education.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** Online access and literacy can exclude people with lower socioeconomic status. Education alone is an incomplete socioeconomic indicator. Self-reported conditions and cross-sectional data limit causal interpretation. The equal-weight score can treat distinct profiles as equivalent. The authors avoided utilities because Italy had no 5L value set, so this comparison does not test national preference-weighted outcomes.

## 9. Patient Experiences Regarding Feasibility of Implementing Real-World EQ-5D Collection at an Oncology Centre in Ontario, Canada

DOI: `10.3390/curroncol32060308`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed single-site pilot implementation study with longitudinal follow-up and patient interviews.
- **Purpose and layer:** Assess patient acceptability and operational feasibility of routine EQ-5D-3L collection during systemic cancer therapy, before a proposed province-wide program.
- **Evidence source and perspective:** A convenience sample of 170 consenting adults at one Ontario oncology center completed an initial EQ-5D-3L. Of these, 103 completed at least one follow-up, 160 answered the optional feasibility survey, and nine of 57 willing patients were interviewed. Collection ran from May 2024 through February 2025. A caregiver proxy form was available, but the number of proxy completions is not reported.
- **Artifact and workflow roles:** EQ-5D-3L and EQ VAS are collection targets and planned data generators for clinical, economic, and population uses. Paper forms and REDCap are administration components. The chemotherapy workflow, coordinators, timing, and proposed electronic delivery are implementation components. EQ-5D-5L is a proposed future version, not an evaluated target in this study.
- **Methods and comparison:** Enrollment and follow-up proportions, willingness and acceptability questions, and content analysis of semi-structured interviews. Patients compared paper with electronic delivery, three with five levels, and several collection times and frequencies.
- **Evaluation:** Reach, repeat completion, willingness, comprehension, burden, content acceptability, timing, mode, workflow fit, and staff burden. Initial questionnaire acceptability was high, but only 60.6% completed a follow-up. Patients described both brevity and limited content.
- **Output and interpretation:** The pilot supports further implementation work and gives practical changes for timing, format choice, and integration with other symptom measures. It does not establish feasibility across Ontario or the measurement performance of EQ-5D-5L.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** One site, convenience recruitment, high education, coordinator burden, English-language barriers, and nine interviews limit transfer. Treatment-day answers can change with immediate treatment effects. The abstract appears to use 170 as the denominator for willingness, while the results use 160 survey respondents. The abstract's `115 (67.3%)` is also not arithmetically consistent with 170; the results report `115 (71.9%)`. The intended denominator should be clarified.

## 10. Psychometric Performance of the EQ Health and Wellbeing Short in a United Kingdom Population Sample

DOI: `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.007`

- **Contribution and stage:** Completed secondary comparative psychometric analysis. EQ-HWB-S version 1.0 and its UK feasibility value set remained experimental.
- **Purpose and layer:** Compare the items and preference-weighted scores of EQ-HWB-S with EQ-5D-5L in a UK general-population sample.
- **Evidence source:** The analysis combines 429 respondents from the 2021 EQ-HWB-S valuation stage and 248 respondents from a 2022-to-2023 EQ-5D-5L valuation pilot. The total sample is 677. Recruitment, collection mode, instrument order, background questions, and time period differ between the two sources.
- **Artifact roles and derivation:** EQ-HWB-S is the main evaluation target. EQ-5D-5L is a comparator. The EQ-HWB-S score comes from a directly estimated experimental UK value set. The EQ-5D-5L score comes from mapping 5L profiles to the UK 3L value set. EQ VAS, long-term condition status, life and health satisfaction, caring, age, and employment define reference groups.
- **Methods and comparison:** Ceiling and floor analysis, Pearson and Spearman correlations, Bland-Altman plots, concordance, and known-group effect sizes. The study compares overlapping and non-overlapping items, instrument-level ceilings, and utility discrimination.
- **Evaluation:** Distribution, convergent validity, known-group validity, and score agreement. Overlapping items correlated strongly. EQ-HWB-S had a lower instrument ceiling and captured exhaustion, loneliness, cognition, and control. Known-group performance was similar. Score concordance was high overall, but individual differences reached from -0.61 to 0.31.
- **Output and interpretation:** The paper supports further use and testing of EQ-HWB-S. It does not establish equivalence with EQ-5D-5L or automatic interchangeability in QALY analysis.
- **Limits and uncertainty:** The combined datasets used different definitions of long-term condition, different instrument order, and different data-collection periods; one source was collected during the COVID-19 pandemic. Utility agreement is partly a comparison of scoring provenance because one score uses a direct experimental value set and the other uses a 5L-to-3L mapping. The general sample had limited severe health, and disease-specific responsiveness was not assessed.
