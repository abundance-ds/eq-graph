# Applications to batch 01

## 1. `10.1007/s40258-021-00639-3`

**Contribution and status.** Completed value-set development with new primary preference data.

**Design and perspective.** The study used a face-to-face valuation experiment with an adult Danish general-population sample. Respondents reported their own health and valued hypothetical EQ-5D-5L states. The analysis included 1,014 interviews. Recruitment began with a national register sample and later added a market-research panel.

**Focal entities and procedures.** The paper develops a Danish EQ-5D-5L value set. It uses EQ-VT 2.1, cTTO, and DCE. It compares cTTO-only, DCE-related, and hybrid models. Logical consistency, censoring, and heteroscedasticity are important evaluation criteria.

**Output and decision.** The paper selects a heteroscedastic censored hybrid model. The model provides values for 3,125 health states and supports Danish QALY estimation and priority setting. The paper also compares the new value set with the Danish EQ-5D-3L and crosswalk value sets.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The final sample had small age and education imbalances. The recruitment-source change can affect sample comparability. The paper reports that data and code are available on request.

## 2. `10.1007/s11136-020-02688-y`

**Contribution and status.** Completed evidence synthesis and measurement-property evaluation.

**Design and evidence units.** The systematic review searched publications from 2011 through January 2019. It included 99 publications from 32 countries. The evidence covers adult general populations and patient groups.

**Focal entities and evaluation basis.** The review evaluates the EQ-5D-5L descriptive system and index evidence. It covers missing responses, floor and ceiling distributions, test-retest reliability, several forms of validity, and responsiveness. It pools selected proportions, index means, and correlations with random-effects models.

**Output.** The paper provides a broad evidence map. It reports good overall reliability and validity, but it identifies item-level instability, persistent ceiling effects in some populations, and a weak and heterogeneous evidence base for responsiveness.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The review excludes experimental EQ-5D-5L versions and many application studies. It does not review valuation methods. These choices restrict conclusions about content development, value-set effects, and some responsiveness evidence.

## 3. `10.1007/s11136-025-03983-2`

**Contribution and status.** Data-resource and study-infrastructure development. This is a design and resource report. It describes data collection that had occurred in the first 15 countries, but it does not report substantive population-health or instrument-comparison analyses.

**Design and population.** EQ-DAPHNIE uses a cross-sectional online survey of adults. The first two rounds cover 15 countries, with a target of approximately 4,500 complete responses per country. An online panel supplies respondents. Quotas cover age, sex, income, region, and, where applicable, language. The plan also includes post-stratification weighting.

**Focal resource and procedures.** The paper defines a comparable multi-country data resource for population-health assessment and instrument evaluation. The core survey includes social determinants, health and well-being measures, behaviors, health-service use, and insurance. It uses EQ-5D-5L, country-specific bolt-ons, an anchoring vignette, and selected measures such as EQ-HWB, PROMIS-10, ASCOT, ICECAP-A, WHO-5, OPQOL-brief, PHQ-2, and GAD-2.

**Output and intended use.** The output is a governed survey infrastructure and a large cross-country dataset for later population-health and instrument-comparison research.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Online panel coverage, internet access, cross-cultural interpretation, and the cross-sectional design limit inference. Data access is restricted. The paper mixes past-tense descriptions of completed rounds with future-oriented protocol language. Therefore, “design/resource report” fits better than either “planned protocol” or “completed outcome study.”

## 4. `10.1017/s0266462326103602`

**Contribution and status.** Completed practice and needs assessment with new survey data.

**Design and contributors.** The cross-sectional online survey includes 238 HTA practitioners from 45 countries and 65 agencies. Recruitment used purposive professional networks. Respondents gave their own professional views; they did not give formal agency positions.

**Focal practices and procedures.** The survey maps use and perceived importance of utility instruments, preference-elicitation methods, preference-data sources, data-quality problems, and research topics. Closed questions support descriptive and regional summaries. Open responses support structured content analysis.

**Output.** The paper reports the strong use of EQ-5D and TTO, frequent use of foreign preference data in several regions, recurring data-quality concerns, and regional and global research priorities. Priorities include recent tariffs, measures for children, and measures that cover health and social care.

## 5. `10.1007/s11136-019-02115-x`

**Contribution and status.** Completed instrument and descriptive-system development.

**Design and contributors.** The study has two phases in Germany, Spain, Sweden, and the UK. Children and adolescents aged 8 to 15 years contributed. Phase 1 used an instrument review, focus groups, sorting, and response scaling to generate and order severity labels. Phase 2 used cognitive interviews to test preliminary four-level and five-level versions. Phase 1 included 255 sorting and scaling interviews. Phase 2 included 120 participants.

**Focal entities and comparison.** The paper develops EQ-5D-Y-5L from EQ-5D-Y-3L. It compares four-level and five-level descriptive systems for comprehensibility, feasibility, response precision, and participant preference. It also harmonizes four language versions without requiring literal word equivalence.

**Output and decision.** The paper selects the five-level version and gives final labels in the four languages. Children generally preferred the five-level form because it offered more precise response choices.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Samples were convenient rather than nationally representative. The UK tested only the five-level draft in phase 2 and did not recruit children in treatment. This study does not establish psychometric performance, valuation feasibility, proxy forms, or value sets.

## 6. `10.1007/s40273-022-01216-9`

**Contribution and status.** Completed value-set development with new primary preference data.

**Design and perspective.** Two independent adult Chinese general-population samples completed DCE or cTTO tasks. The full sample included 1,476 respondents. Adults valued the health of a hypothetical 10-year-old child. The relationship between respondent and child was not fixed. Quota targets covered sex, age, education, and rural or urban registration. Recruitment was non-probability based.

**Focal entities and comparison.** The paper develops a Chinese EQ-5D-Y-3L value set. It expands the standard cTTO design and compares a mapped DCE mixed-logit approach with hybrid models. Main evaluation criteria are coefficient significance and order, and prediction error against observed cTTO values. An additional `A3` term represents the value gap for state 33333.

**Output and decision.** The selected hybrid model with the `A3` term provides the Chinese value set. It uses both DCE and cTTO data and accounts for the sharp difference between state 33333 and other observed states.

**Scope and uncertainty.** cTTO data came from fewer regions than DCE data. The imagined-child relation can cause response variation. The paper also questions the fit of lead-time cTTO for child-health states. The Key Points state that the sample came from “4 different regions,” but the Methods name 14 provinces or cities across five geographical parts. This is an internal reporting conflict and needs source clarification.

## 7. `10.1007/s11136-025-04003-z`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary measurement-property analysis of an existing cross-sectional dataset.

**Design and contributors.** The dataset contains 504 caregiver and care-recipient dyads, for 1,008 adult respondents. The analysis treats respondents as individual evidence units, not as paired outcomes. Health conditions are self-reported.

**Focal entities and comparison.** The paper compares frequency and severity response scales for pain and discomfort in the EQ-HWB. It also uses the combined EQ-5D-5L pain/discomfort item as a related measure. It evaluates correlations, response informativity, item discrimination and threshold coverage, differential item functioning, and associations with reported conditions.

**Output and decision.** Frequency and severity responses supply complementary information. Frequency items cover milder trait levels and use response categories more evenly. Severity items discriminate better at higher trait levels. The paper favors both forms in a long instrument and suggests frequency when a short instrument must use one form.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The cross-sectional design does not show change or causation. Conditions are self-reported. The study does not establish transfer across languages or cultures. The dyadic recruitment design is a sample feature, but the focal analysis is at the individual level.

## 8. `10.1007/s11136-025-04038-2`

**Contribution and status.** Completed conceptual and content-validity evaluation with new qualitative data.

**Design and contributors.** The study uses semi-structured interviews with 30 Chinese laypeople from two regions. Quotas create three equal groups: healthy people, patients, and informal caregivers. Two coders use inductive and deductive thematic framework analysis. The final interviews showed repeated concepts, but the study did not use a formal saturation rule.

**Focal entities and comparison.** The paper develops a Chinese quality-of-life framework and compares it with the EQ-HWB conceptual framework. The stated content-validity question is comprehensiveness. It does not test relevance or comprehensibility in this report.

**Output.** The new framework contains eight themes. Seven correspond to EQ-HWB themes, and “mindset” is additional. The paper uses this broad overlap as support for EQ-HWB comprehensiveness in the Chinese context.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The sample has regional and health-status imbalances. Most patients had chronic conditions in community settings. Translation of “mindset” and “positive/negative energy” is difficult. The analysis excluded external conditions, economic factors, and future outcomes by rule. Thus, its quality-of-life framework is also shaped by the authors' outcome-measurement boundary.

## 9. `10.1016/j.jval.2025.02.001`

**Contribution and status.** Completed evidence synthesis and analysis-practice assessment.

**Design and evidence units.** The systematic review includes 2,125 randomized clinical trials that analyzed postbaseline EQ-5D data by treatment group. It searched MEDLINE and EMBASE through November 2021 and also searched a clinical-trial registry in August 2023. Trials, not publications, are the main evidence unit because the authors linked multiple reports to one trial.

**Focal entities and evaluation basis.** The paper reviews how trials analyze EQ-5D utilities, EQ VAS, and dimension responses. It distinguishes numerical and categorical formats, and single from multiple postbaseline assessments. It records model families, assumption checks, baseline adjustment, missing-data methods, and use of minimal important differences.

**Output.** The review gives a large map of analysis practice. Utilities are the most common data product. Linear fixed-effect models are common for one postbaseline assessment, and linear mixed-effect models are common for repeated assessments. The review identifies low reporting or use of assumption checks, baseline adjustment, and explicit missing-data methods.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The review does not appraise trial risk of bias because its target is method use, not treatment effects. Sparse reports can cause undercounting of analytic details. The supplied structured record has no authors, journal, or publication-date fields. These are metadata gaps; this application does not reconstruct them from the article text.

## 10. `10.1007/s10198-025-01770-x`

**Contribution and status.** Completed measurement-property evaluation with new primary data.

**Design and contributors.** The study includes 356 Brazilian school children and adolescents aged 8 to 18 years. It compares groups with and without self-reported musculoskeletal pain. A 7-day retest supplies reliability data for participants classified as stable.

**Focal entities and comparison.** The paper compares EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, and CHU9D. It evaluates descriptive-system feasibility, missing responses, floor and ceiling effects, test-retest reliability, hypothesis-based construct validity, and known-group validity. PedsQL and pain measures supply external comparison data. The focal EQ-5D-Y analysis uses profiles and EQ VAS, not preference-weighted utility.

**Output.** All three instruments distinguish participants with and without reported musculoskeletal pain. Descriptive-system reliability is poor to moderate. Construct-validity results are stronger in participants with pain. The paper supports use of the instruments in this population but does not identify one instrument as best overall.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Pain status is self-reported and has a one-month frame, while the preference-based instruments refer to “today.” Instrument recall periods and response formats differ. Consecutive administration can cause recall effects. A failed prespecified correlation hypothesis does not by itself prove inadequate validity, as the authors state.

# Applications to batch 02

## 1. `10.1016/j.jval.2025.01.003`

**Contribution and status.** Completed value-set development with new primary preference data.

**Design, contributors, and perspective.** A valuation experiment included 1,005 adults who lived in the United Arab Emirates (UAE). Quotas covered age, sex, emirate, and national or expatriate status. Recruitment used personal and professional networks, snowball methods, public locations, and social media. Interviews were face-to-face or online and in Arabic or English. Respondents reported their own health and valued hypothetical EQ-5D-5L states. Expatriates had to have lived in the UAE for at least five years.

**Focal entities and evaluation.** The paper derives a UAE EQ-5D-5L value set from cTTO and DCE data. It compares ten cTTO-only, DCE-only, and hybrid models. Evaluation includes logical consistency, significance, fit, censoring, heteroscedasticity, sensitivity analyses, and cross-validation.

**Output and decision.** The selected model is a hybrid heteroscedastic tobit model censored at -1. The output is a tariff for 3,125 states for use in UAE QALY estimation, economic evaluation, and population-health work.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The authors describe demographic coverage as representative, but recruitment was non-probability based. The large and transient expatriate population makes the intended national perspective unusual. The five-year residence rule has no established validation. The study used the UK English version because no UAE English version existed. The article abstract gives a mean age of 39 years, while the Results give 32.1 years. This internal reporting conflict is unresolved.

## 2. `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.016`

**Contribution and status.** Completed preference-elicitation method evaluation with new primary data. It does not produce the official value set reported elsewhere.

**Components and contributors.** Two adult general-population samples from Trinidad and Tobago completed different studies. In the EQ-VT study, 1,079 respondents completed cTTO and DCE tasks in interviews. In the separate DCE-with-duration study, 1,581 respondents completed an online or recruiter-assisted survey; 611 were excluded and 970 remained. The studies occurred at different times and used different recruitment and administration modes.

**Focal methods and evaluation.** The paper compares cTTO-only, DCE-only, and hybrid EQ-VT estimates with linear and nonlinear DCE-with-duration estimates for EQ-5D-5L. Criteria include health-dimension order, utility scale, correlation, Bland-Altman agreement, mean absolute difference, agreement with observed cTTO means, and response-time quality rules.

**Output and decision.** The nonlinear DCE-with-duration model, which corrects for time preference, gives close agreement with the EQ-VT estimates. The paper identifies this method as a possible lower-cost substitute and rejects the linear time specification for this use.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The two methods were not tested in the same respondents. Sample demographics differed. Public-place DCE-with-duration collection had extensive speeding and flatlining, so the researchers stopped that mode and removed its data. The result comes from one country and depends on exclusion and nonlinear-time assumptions. It does not establish general interchangeability.

## 3. `10.1186/s12955-023-02177-z`

**Contribution and status.** Completed measurement-property evaluation with new longitudinal patient data.

**Design and contributors.** A convenience cohort included 125 adults with relapsed Graves' disease at three Hong Kong public hospitals. Patients completed EQ-5D-5L and ThyPRO-39 at baseline and at one and six months. Follow-up included 101 patients at one month and 100 at six months. The contributor and analysis unit are the patient, and the target of judgment is the patient's own health.

**Focal entities and evaluation.** The paper evaluates EQ-5D-5L profiles, the Hong Kong value-set index, and EQ VAS. It tests floor and ceiling distributions, convergent validity against ThyPRO-39, one-month item and score reliability among patients who report unchanged health, and six-month responsiveness against patient-reported global change.

**Output.** The study supports convergent validity and test-retest reliability. Responsiveness evidence is stronger for reported worsening than for improvement. It supplies a population-specific performance result, not a new instrument or value set.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The convenience clinical sample is small, and the worsened subgroup includes 21 patients. Baseline ceiling limits the detection of improvement. The global change classification is self-reported, and incomplete follow-up can affect responsiveness results. The results apply to relapsed Graves' disease in the participating Hong Kong services.

## 4. `10.1007/s11136-025-04150-3`

**Contribution and status.** Completed instrument-development stage and conceptual or content-validity evaluation with new qualitative expert data.

**Design, contributors, and perspective.** Three online consultation groups included 33 purposively selected experts from 15 countries. They represented EuroQol instrument expertise, pediatric health and development, and pediatric instrument development. Experts judged the experimental EQ-TIPS version 2.0. They did not report children's health, and caregivers and children did not contribute data in this study.

**Focal entity and evaluation.** The study evaluates and informs EQ-TIPS, a proxy-completed HRQoL instrument intended for children aged 0 to 3 years. Thematic analysis covers the construct boundary, age range, proxy perspective, recall period, response levels, dimensions, overlap, missing content, cultural transfer, relevance, comprehensibility, and comprehensiveness.

**Output and decision point.** The output is a set of recommendations for later versions, not a final revised instrument. Experts generally supported the six dimensions. They recommended tests of sleep and emotions, observable age-specific examples in place of “age-appropriate” wording, and continued caregiver testing. They did not reach consensus on some dimension overlap or age-range questions.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Recruitment used researcher networks. The instrument-developer group was small and concentrated in Europe and Canada. Expert opinion cannot replace evidence from parents, caregivers, children, low-literacy respondents, or varied care settings. The study does not establish measurement properties or valuation feasibility. The article reports 44 invited experts, but its three invitation-group counts are 21, 13, and 9, which total 43.

## 6. `10.1016/j.jval.2024.03.2195`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary measurement-property evaluation of an existing general-population survey dataset. It also informs instrument-extension choices.

**Design and contributors.** The cross-sectional online dataset includes 1,587 Hungarian adults after 113 exclusions. Soft quotas covered age, gender, place of residence, and region. Respondents reported their own health, well-being, and diagnosed conditions.

## 7. `10.1007/s10198-018-0987-x`

**Contribution and status.** Completed method evaluation and economic-outcome analysis through secondary use of patient data and two existing decision models.

**Components and evidence units.** Cross-sectional data came from 75 hemodialysis and 75 peritoneal-dialysis patients in Singapore. The study re-runs separate 10-year Markov models for patients with and without diabetes. Patients are the source evidence units. The downstream analysis units are model scenarios and hypothetical cohorts of 10,000 patients.

**Focal methods and comparison.** The paper compares directly collected EQ-5D-5L utilities with EQ-5D-3L utilities from three direct-mapping and two response-mapping algorithms, and with SF-6D utilities derived from SF-12. It compares utility differences, incremental QALYs, incremental cost-effectiveness ratios, and bootstrap intervals for hemodialysis versus peritoneal dialysis.

**Output and decision.** In these models, mapped EQ-5D and SF-6D reduce incremental QALYs and increase cost-effectiveness ratios relative to observed EQ-5D. The paper recommends direct EQ-5D collection when possible and one jurisdictional reference measure for consistent decisions.

**Scope and uncertainty.** This is one Singapore dialysis case, not a general validation of mapping. Available mapping algorithms came from different US populations, used EQ-5D-3L, and applied UK value weights. The observed measure was EQ-5D-5L. EQ-5D and SF-12 recall periods differed. Cross-sectional utilities were held constant over ten years. These differences prevent attribution of the modeled effect to mapping alone.

## 8. `10.3389/fpubh.2021.744405`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary population-health outcome analysis and measurement-performance evaluation of an existing cross-sectional survey dataset.

**Design and contributors.** An online-panel survey included 10,172 adults aged 18 to 75 years from Italy, the Netherlands, and the UK. Samples were selected to match age, sex, and education distributions. Respondents reported their own health, education, work status, income, and chronic conditions.

## 10. `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.007`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary measurement-property evaluation by combining two existing valuation-study datasets.

**Components and contributors.** The analysis combines 429 UK general-population respondents from the EQ-HWB-S valuation study and 248 from an EQ-5D-5L valuation pilot. Both datasets contain EQ-HWB-S and EQ-5D-5L responses, but their collection periods, modes, instrument order, and some background questions differ.

# Applications to batch 03

## 1. `10.3390/curroncol32110645`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary health and equity outcome analysis of existing cross-sectional patient data.

**Evidence lineage, design, and contributors.** The paper reuses the initial responses of the 170 patients in the Ontario oncology implementation pilot reported in `10.3390/curroncol32060308`. The convenience sample came from one urban cancer center. Adults starting publicly funded systemic therapy reported their own health with EQ-5D-3L, EQ VAS, and a demographic questionnaire. One regression uses all 170 patients without birth sex. A second uses 111 patients after it excludes sex-specific cancers and includes birth sex.

**Focal outcome and procedures.** The paper estimates associations between Canadian-tariff EQ-5D-3L utilities and age, sex, education, marital status, employment, family income, ethnicity, and cancer site. It also compares income groups on dimension-level problem severity. The target output is an equity-oriented utility profile that can inform health technology assessment.

**Output and decision.** The lowest reported family-income group and the undisclosed-income group had lower adjusted utility than the highest-income reference group. The paper recommends that analysts examine whether trial utilities transfer to lower-income real-world patients. It does not estimate the effect on incremental QALYs or a funding decision.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The study is small, single-center, cross-sectional, and non-probability based. Income was not reported by 79 patients. Cancer stage, comorbidities, and other possible confounders were not measured. The EQ-5D-3L ceiling and wide model intervals also restrict interpretation. The associations do not establish that income caused the utility differences.

## 2. `10.1016/j.jval.2018.05.002`

**Contribution and status.** Completed preference-elicitation method evaluation with new primary data.

**Design, contributors, and perspective.** A randomized two-by-two experiment assigned 805 adults in Germany, the Netherlands, Spain, and England to EQ-5D-3L or EQ-5D-Y wording and to an adult-self or hypothetical 10-year-old-child perspective. Convenience recruitment was monitored for age and sex. Computer-assisted interviews included ranking, cTTO, DCE, and comparisons with immediate death.

**Focal methods and evaluation.** The paper compares instrument wording and valuation perspective on health-state values. The respondent is an adult general-public member. The target of judgment changes from the respondent's own adult health to another person's child health. MANOVA, post hoc tests, and DCE choice comparisons assess interactions and value differences.

**Output and decision.** Wording and perspective interact. The paper concludes that adult EQ-5D-3L value sets must not score EQ-5D-Y states and that EQ-5D-Y needs separate value sets. It also cautions that adult and child values can interpret the dead anchor differently.

**Scope and uncertainty.** A technical error retained only one of two planned DCE blocks. Country recruitment differed, and the study had too little power for full country-specific analysis. The adult arm values the respondent's own health, while the child arm values another person's health. Thus, age perspective and self-versus-other perspective are not separated. The study informs method choice but does not produce national value sets.

## 3. `10.1007/s40273-018-0642-5`

**Contribution and status.** Completed evidence synthesis and comparative measurement-property evaluation.

**Design and evidence units.** The systematic review searched PubMed, EMBASE, PsycINFO, and the EuroQol website for English- or German-language studies from 2007 through January 2018. It included 24 adult empirical studies from general and patient populations. Publications are the review units. Most included studies administered both instruments to the same respondents.

**Focal entities and evaluation.** The review compares the EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L descriptive systems. It evaluates missing responses, floor and ceiling distributions, Shannon informativity, inconsistent paired responses, responsiveness, and test-retest reliability. It pools profile ceilings and appraises included-study quality.

**Output.** The evidence map supports similar or better distributional performance for EQ-5D-5L and a clear gain in informativity. Evidence for responsiveness and reliability is mixed and sparse. The review therefore supports both versions but favors EQ-5D-5L when discrimination among milder states is important.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Populations, languages, value sets, administration order, intervening questionnaires, and designs differ. Crosswalked EQ-5D-5L scores complicate some responsiveness results. The review covers a defined set of comparative properties and does not establish that one version is best for every use.

## 4. `10.1016/j.jval.2023.03.003`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary valuation-method evaluation and candidate value-set analysis.

**Evidence lineage, design, and perspective.** The paper reuses the 418-adult cTTO component of the Chinese EQ-5D-Y-3L valuation study reported in `10.1007/s40273-022-01216-9`. Transfer beyond this sample and country is uncertain.

## 5. `10.1038/s41433-023-02860-x`

**Contribution and status.** Completed economic and policy outcome analysis with mixed primary and secondary evidence. It is a prevalent cost-of-illness study, not an instrument study.

**Components, people, and perspective.** The study combines individual cost and service-use responses from the 2014 National Eye Survey of Trinidad and Tobago, national prevalence estimates, a contemporaneous eye-care-system survey, national unit costs, and published disability weights. The survey used multistage random cluster sampling of people aged 40 years or older. Individuals supply cost and use data. The modeled unit is the 2014 national population with vision impairment. The economic perspective is societal.

**Focal outcomes and procedures.** The paper estimates direct medical and nonmedical costs, productivity loss, informal care, and intangible well-being loss. It converts disability weights and prevalent cases to DALYs and then assigns a monetary value to those DALYs. Sensitivity analyses vary costs, prevalence, and disability-weight assumptions. EQ-5D is not administered in this analysis, and previously reported QALYs are background only.

**Output.** The output is a national economic-burden profile and a policy case for wider prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, and access. It separates who bears each cost and shows the result with and without monetized well-being loss.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Cost and use response rates were 59% to 66%. Institutionalized people were excluded, and blind people can be underrepresented. Twelve-month recall, omitted health and carer opportunity costs, labor-market assumptions, and unit-cost assumptions limit the estimates. Alternative disability weights change the well-being-loss estimate about fourfold. Monetizing DALYs adds a disputed ethical and analytic assumption.

## 6. `10.1177/0272989x251325828`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary preference-elicitation and anchoring method evaluation.

**Evidence lineage and design.** The paper reuses the 970 respondents retained in the Trinidad and Tobago DCE-with-duration study reported in `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.016`. It also uses the cTTO tariff from `10.1186/s12955-024-02266-7` as a population-level benchmark. Each respondent completed split-triplet DCE tasks with full health or immediate death. Mixed-logit models compare linear and nonlinear time preferences and two anchor choices.

**Focal method and evaluation.** The paper tests duration-zero and immediate-death anchors for DCE-derived EQ-5D-5L values. Criteria include the estimated discount parameter, face validity of the utility range, and agreement with the separate cTTO tariff.

**Output and decision.** Nonlinear time preferences with a duration anchor give the closest match to the cTTO benchmark. The paper recommends designs that can estimate discounting and suggests that DCE valuation omit immediate-death tasks and anchor on duration.

**Scope and uncertainty.** The result comes from one country and one instrument. The DCE survey had no interview debrief, so respondent understanding and engagement are not directly known beyond timing exclusions and result patterns. Different people completed DCE and cTTO tasks. The comparison therefore shows population-level correspondence, not individual agreement or general interchangeability.

## 7. `10.1186/s12955-024-02266-7`

**Contribution and status.** Completed national value-set development with new primary preference data.

**Design, contributors, and perspective.** Face-to-face EQ-VT 2.1 interviews included 1,079 Trinidad and Tobago adults. Quotas covered age, sex, and geography. Streets and households were sampled, and one adult per household was invited. Respondents valued hypothetical EQ-5D-5L states with cTTO and DCE and reported their own health.

**Focal entities and evaluation.** The paper derives a directly elicited national EQ-5D-5L value set and compares it with the prior crosswalk tariff. It evaluates protocol compliance, censoring, heteroscedasticity, logical order, coefficient significance, model fit, and state- and block-level out-of-sample prediction. It compares cTTO-only, DCE-only, and hybrid models.

**Output, decision, and evidence lineage.** The selected hybrid heteroscedastic Tobit model becomes the national tariff and replaces the crosswalk for Trinidad and Tobago applications. The same participant sample forms one component of the population-norm analysis in `10.1186/s12955-024-02323-1`. The tariff is also the comparator in `10.1177/0272989x251325828` and scores both current and 2012 profiles in the population-norm paper.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Some female age groups and people with more education were overrepresented. Quality control flagged 164 interviews, and interviewer effects remained possible. The abstract reports 236 negative states, or 7.6%, while the Results report 275 negative states, or 8.8%. The correct count is unresolved.

## 8. `10.1186/s12955-024-02323-1`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary population-health and inequality analysis with a national population-norm output.

**Evidence lineage and components.** The analysis combines three samples that were intended not to overlap: the 1,079-person value-set study in `10.1186/s12955-024-02266-7`, the 970 retained DCE-with-duration respondents also analyzed in `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.016` and `10.1177/0272989x251325828`, and a new 940-person online panel survey. The final sample has 2,989 adults. Collection modes, incentives, recruitment, and source-study purposes differ.

**Focal outcomes and procedures.** The paper produces EQ-5D-5L profiles, EQ VAS results, current-tariff utilities, ceilings, age-sex norms, subgroup results, Kakwani inequality indices, and dimension-level ordered-logit comparisons. It compares 2022-2023 data with 2012 profiles after it scores both periods with the current tariff.

**Output and decision.** The paper provides updated national norms and reports lower population-health measures and greater inequality than in 2012. It recommends periodic norm updates and use of the new norms in Trinidad and Tobago applications.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Two components use online panels, and one uses mixed online and public-place recruitment. Response rates are unavailable for two surveys. Incentives, mode, source-study exclusions, and a possible participant overlap between the household and panel sources can affect the pooled results. The study cannot separate health change from changes in reporting, sampling, or mode over ten years. The abstract gives a July 2022 to May 2023 collection period, while the Methods say the third survey ran from March through August 2023.

## 9. `10.1007/s11136-025-04074-y`

**Contribution and status.** Completed data-resource development and survey data-quality method evaluation with new multi-country data.

**Evidence lineage and components.** The paper extends the EQ-DAPHNIE design/resource report in `10.1007/s11136-025-03983-2`. A UK pilot with 3,012 completed surveys tested six survey versions and informed changes. The main implementation includes 68,411 completed responses across 15 countries. This dataset later supplies the eight-country source sample for `10.1007/s11136-026-04294-w`.

**Focal process and evaluation.** The study evaluates online-panel survey integrity through consent and completion, reCAPTCHA bot flags, speeding, duplicate records, missing data, outliers, repeated-item agreement, completion time, and quota achievement. It also records pilot-driven changes to question format, order, sensitivity information, mental-health item length, vignettes, and response requirements.

**Output and decision.** The output is an implemented quality-control process, country-level quality profile, and guidance for users of the EQ-DAPHNIE data. The paper recommends that later norm and cross-country analyses account for country-specific missingness, quota shortfalls, coverage, and weighting limits.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Dynata supplies a non-probability panel. Quota achievement ranges from 68.7% to 98.6%, and the representativeness of the underlying panels cannot be tested directly. Weighting cannot remove bias from unobserved digital access, health literacy, vulnerability, or response propensity. The abstract calls the 80.1% to 100% link-click proportions “response rates,” while the Results distinguish link clicks, consent, and completion. This denominator language can mislead comparisons.

## 10. `10.1007/s11136-026-04294-w`

**Contribution and status.** Completed secondary population-inequality analysis and conceptual or measurement evaluation.

**Evidence lineage, design, and contributors.** The paper reuses EQ-DAPHNIE data described in `10.1007/s11136-025-03983-2` and evaluated for quality in `10.1007/s11136-025-04074-y`. It includes 32,327 online-panel respondents aged 25 to 79 years from eight high-income countries. The analysis also replicates and extends an earlier published within-profile method by adding subjective income, distribution checks, and country-specific models.

**Focal entities and evaluation.** Within ten selected EQ-5D-5L profiles, the paper estimates adjusted associations between EQ VAS and education or perceived income adequacy. It uses survey weights and full-information maximum likelihood. It excludes very low BMI values and EQ VAS values below profile-specific thresholds. The comparison tests whether the descriptive profile contains the same socioeconomic signal as overall self-rated health.

**Output and decision.** Education and, more consistently, perceived income show residual gradients in EQ VAS among people with the same profile. The paper recommends complementary EQ VAS, bolt-on, or socioeconomic information when equity is central. It does not decide whether the residual signal is omitted health content, reporting heterogeneity, or both.

**Scope and uncertainty.** Online-panel selection, cross-sectional associations, subjective-income and EQ VAS response styles, and restriction to high-income countries limit transfer and causal interpretation. Some country-profile estimates are imprecise. The profile-specific EQ VAS exclusion thresholds preserve replication but are analyst-defined assumptions. The Data Availability statement says that no datasets were generated or analyzed, which conflicts with the Methods and Results description of an EQ-DAPHNIE analysis.
