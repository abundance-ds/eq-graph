## Cross-paper consistency

- **Study-family meaning:** Health-state valuation means elicitation or modeling of health-state values in papers 1 and 2. Preference-method study is used only when the elicitation or administration method is a research target in papers 2 and 9. Mapping or scoring in paper 2 refers to a DCE-to-cTTO score path, not a source-measure-to-target-instrument map. Measurement-property study has an exact property or assessment target in papers 3, 4, 5, 7, and 8. Translation and Cultural adaptation apply only to the material language work in paper 4. Implementation study applies to workflow introduction in paper 6, but not to routine data reuse in paper 8. Evidence synthesis applies to publications and studies in paper 10.
- **Qualitative work:** Paper 5 uses qualitative methods to assess response behavior. It does not elicit a new concept framework. The candidate boundary therefore prevents a false Qualitative concept study family. Paper 6 uses thematic evidence inside an implementation purpose and also does not receive that family.
- **People and perspectives:** A respondent is never assumed to be the referent. Adults value a child's hypothetical health in paper 2. Children and parents report on the same child in paper 3. Carers report on people with dementia in paper 5. Staff and families report on aged-care residents in paper 6. Parents report on children with intellectual disability in paper 7. Missing requested perspectives remain not reported.
- **Instrument roles:** EQ-5D can be a target of valuation, an administered self-health measure, a scoring input, an object of review, or a comparator. PedsQL can be a translation product and a property target. QI-Disability can be a source instrument and QID-12 a product. The same `instrument involvement` idea does not replace these exact roles.
- **Administration:** Web or digital technology does not imply unsupervised completion. Paper 9 is a remote, full-interviewer cTTO. Paper 6 contains digital, paper, telephone, self, and proxy paths. Paper 3 includes assisted self-report and independent proxy completion. The same administration axes retain the same meaning across domains.
- **Methods and outcomes:** A task is separate from its model and output in valuation papers. Cross-informant agreement is not reliability in paper 3. Internal responsiveness is not external responsiveness in paper 8. A directly scored utility is not a mapped utility, QALY, or decision result. Pooled means in paper 10 are modeled estimates and not a new value set.
- **Products and status:** Papers 1 and 2 produce completed value sets. Paper 4 produces an approved language version with limited psychometric evidence. Paper 6 produces a pilot workflow. Paper 7 produces a completed short form without independent validation. Paper 9 produces paper-local method guidance. Experimental use of EQ-HWB in papers 5 and 6 does not make a completed product.
- **Reuse and evidence relation:** Same respondents, independent samples, pooled reused data, protocol reuse, scoring-source reuse, and partially overlapping outcome subsets remain distinct. This supports cross-paper independence and lineage retrieval without one paper-level reuse flag.
- **Main consistency risks:** Two wording points need care. First, family-only mapping retrieval can mix valuation-scale anchoring with instrument mapping. Second, the exact target for recall and perspective response behavior lacks a named stable property term. Exact paths and narrative targets prevent term misuse in these applications, but wording clarification would improve extraction consistency.

## Frozen probe checks

1. **Not testable in this holdout — DCE with duration.** Papers 1 and 2 use standard DCE, and papers 1, 2, and 9 use cTTO. No paper uses DCE with duration or PTO. A broad DCE query would falsely match papers 1 and 2, but the exact task form prevents that false match.
2. **Not testable in this holdout — unsupervised online cTTO.** Paper 9 compares remote Tele-TTO with face-to-face cTTO, but both conditions use full interviewer administration. Tele-TTO uses Zoom, screen sharing, and spoken responses. It is a false match for unsupervised web cTTO, and the administration axes exclude it.
3. **Supported — translation and language adaptation.** Paper 4 has US English source forms, Chichewa target forms, Malawi as study country and locale, and a separate publication language layer. Papers 1 and 3 use Arabic, French, or Amharic instruments but do not become translation papers without a translation purpose.
4. **Supported — another person's health.** Paper 2 has adults valuing a 10-year-old child's hypothetical health. Papers 3, 5, 6, and 7 have proxies or caregivers reporting on another person. Respondent, referent, requested perspective, exact form, and target population remain separate. Where the source omits a perspective, the value stays not reported.
5. **Supported — same instrument family in different roles.** EQ-5D forms are targets of valuation, administered measures, scoring inputs, source descriptions, measurement-property targets, and review objects. Exact roles exclude background mentions and companion-paper results.
6. **Supported by equivalent property tests — stronger and weaker property evidence.** The Graves' disease paper is not in this holdout. Papers 3, 4, 7, and 8 test equivalent semantic behavior. They keep each property, subgroup, criterion, method, direction, and result separate, so one broad validity label does not replace mixed evidence.
7. **Not testable in this holdout — mapped utility through QALY and cost-effectiveness analysis.** Paper 2 maps DCE disutility to a cTTO utility scale, but it does not calculate QALYs or compare costs. It is a false match for the full probe if retrieval uses only the Mapping or scoring family. Exact source, target, outcome, QALY route, and decision result exclude it.
8. **Supported — valuation task, protocol, design, administration, and model.** Papers 1 and 2 provide cTTO and DCE task forms, blocks, framing, protocol technology, administration, models, and model purposes. Paper 9 adds an administration-method comparison for cTTO.
9. **Supported — product states.** Papers 1 and 2 have completed selected value sets. Paper 4 has an approved language version with limited supporting evidence. Paper 6 has a pilot workflow. Paper 7 has a completed short form without independent validation. Experimental EQ-HWB forms are not treated as products of papers 5 or 6. No planned protocol product occurs in this holdout.
10. **Supported — concise finding and meaning retrieval.** Each application separates principal finding, author interpretation, limitation, and future work and does not copy every estimate.
11. **Supported — implication, use, and inference.** Proposed HTA, clinical, policy, or implementation uses remain author implications. Paper 6 alone documents pilot workflow use and limited staff-reported care responses. No extractor inference is presented as effect.
12. **Supported — reuse and independent evidence.** Paper 7 reuses and deduplicates several data sets. Papers 1, 2, and 9 reuse protocols or designs. Papers 3, 6, and 8 link same-referent or same-respondent evidence. Object-specific status prevents these cases from becoming one paper-level reuse claim.
13. **Supported — extraction uncertainty.** Count conflicts, summary-versus-main-text conflicts, unclear procedures, not-reported perspectives, and transfer limits are separate from author conclusions in all applicable papers.
14. **Supported — holdout-bounded corpus gap.** Within these ten papers, no study combines an African youth language form, local child self-report evidence, and a local youth value set. The Ethiopian study uses Amharic child and proxy reports but scores with a Zimbabwe value set. The Malawi study produces Chichewa PedsQL forms but no youth value set. The Indonesian paper produces a youth value set outside Africa. This is a ten-paper holdout gap only.

## False-match summary

- Papers 1 and 2 are standard DCE papers, not DCE-with-duration papers.
- Paper 9 is remote interviewer-administered cTTO, not unsupervised online cTTO.
- Papers 1 and 3 administer more than one language, but they are not translation studies.
- Paper 2 produces utilities from preference-scale anchoring, but it does not calculate QALYs or a cost-effectiveness result.
- Paper 6 studies a pilot workflow, not scaled routine implementation or a measured resident-health effect.
- Paper 8 reuses routine PROM data, but it is not an implementation study.
- Paper 10 pools study outcomes, but it does not produce a value set or establish causal geographic effects.
