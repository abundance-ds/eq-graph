# B09: experience scaling for US value assessment

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1007/s40273-024-01444-1`.
- Purposes: valuation-method research; value-assessment methods; secondary
  preference analysis.
- Design: secondary analysis of a representative US online DCE survey.
- EuroQol support: study funding and dissertation support, project `304-PHD`.

## Population and samples

- Source survey: 631 US adults, collected 29 September to 10 October 2023.
- This analysis: 179 parents of children aged 18 years or younger, including 99
  mothers and 80 fathers.
- Valued target: health gains for a hypothetical ten-year-old child.

## Concepts and themes

- alternatives to QALYs; patient experience; equity and discrimination;
  stakeholder perspective; child health valuation; no-experience anchor;
  US Medicare policy.

## Instruments and administration

- EQ-5D-Y-3L described child health problems in the valuation tasks.
- The instrument was not administered to measure respondent health.
- Online, self-completed DCE survey.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Ten kaizen tasks asked respondents to choose successive improvements from one
  child-health profile.
- Five paired comparisons anchored selected problems against `being in a coma`,
  defined as no experience.
- Analysis separated parent, mother, and father perspectives.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Three conditional logit models with ten incremental health-gain effects and
  separate scale terms for kaizen and paired-comparison tasks.
- Cluster bootstrap with replacement and percentile inference accounted for
  within-respondent correlation.

## Products

- Experimental parent, mother, and father experience-scale estimates for
  EQ-5D-Y-3L improvements.
- These are not standard EQ-5D-Y-3L value sets and are not on the QALY scale.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Value of each level improvement; perspective difference; worst-profile value
  relative to no experience; feasibility of small-sample experience scaling.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Parents assigned positive values to most child-health improvements.
- Mothers valued improvement from `a bit` to `not worried, sad, or unhappy`
  more than fathers; the reported difference had p = 0.023.
- The 33333 profile had parent value -0.294 relative to no experience, with
  p = 0.03.
- The authors propose experience scaling as a lower-cost, multi-perspective
  alternative for HRQoL gains when QALY use is contested.
- They state that it does not include life expectancy and is not necessarily
  comparable across conditions or stakeholder groups.

## Limitations and source issues

- Experience scaling is specialized, lacks open-source software, and needs
  further method development.
- It can support selective reporting if analysts choose favorable perspectives.
- The approach cannot replace QALYs for comparisons that require both HRQoL and
  survival.
- The abstract says some differences were not significant and then gives
  `p-values < .05`; this is internally inconsistent. The specific reported
  mother-father difference has p = 0.023 in Results.

- Product type and status must prevent these estimates from appearing as an
