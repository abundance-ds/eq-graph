# B19: Swedish experience-based EQ VAS valuation

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1186/s12955-023-02115-z`.
- Purposes: experience-based health-state valuation; longitudinal outcomes
  research; patient-versus-population comparison.
- Design: register-based observational study with baseline and one-year
  follow-up data.
- The research was supported by the EuroQol Research Foundation under project
  `2016480` and by Region Stockholm.

## Population and samples

- Patients in nine Swedish National Quality Registers that cover six
  intervention groups and three diagnosis groups.
- Patient sample: 172,070 records with EQ VAS data.
- Comparator: 41,761 Swedish general-population respondents.
- Register groups covered spine, hip, knee, ankle, cruciate-ligament, and
  first-line osteoarthritis treatment, plus heart failure, respiratory
  failure, and bipolar disorder.

## Concepts and themes

- experience-based valuation; patient values; health-state severity; change
  over time; patient-versus-public values; mental health; dead-state anchoring;
  clinical-register evidence.

## Instruments and administration

- EQ-5D-3L described patient health states and supplied dimension levels.
- EQ VAS supplied patients' ratings of their own health and was analyzed as an
  experience-based valuation of the reported health states.
- The Swedish experience-based EQ-5D-3L VAS value set supplied index scores.
- Data came from register collection at baseline and one-year follow-up and
  from earlier general-population surveys. Collection mode differed between
  registers and was not standardized by this paper.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Secondary analysis of nine National Quality Registers and two
  general-population survey sources.
- Compared mean EQ VAS scores for nine selected EQ-5D-3L states.
- Tested correlations between EQ VAS and EQ-5D-3L index values and between
  their changes over time.
- Assessed dimension-level decrements and consistency at baseline and
  follow-up.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Descriptive statistics and Spearman rank correlations.
- Unadjusted and age-and-sex-adjusted ordinary least-squares regressions.
- Pooled-patient OLS models and two-level random-slope and random-intercept
  models were sensitivity analyses.
- Inconsistency meant that a more severe level had a smaller decrement than a
  milder level.

## Products

- Experience-based patient-valuation evidence for EQ-5D-3L and EQ VAS.
- The paper identifies potential inputs for patient-perspective value sets; it
  does not produce an anchored value set for direct economic evaluation.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Mean EQ VAS by health state, patient group, and time point.
- EQ VAS and EQ-5D-3L index correlation; dimension and level decrements;
  severity consistency; explained variance.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- EQ VAS values were generally ordered by health-state severity at baseline
  and one-year follow-up.
- Correlations with EQ-5D-3L index values were mostly moderate at baseline and
  increased at follow-up; spine and hip groups reached strong correlations.
- Dimension decrements were mostly ordered, but self-care level 3 caused the
  main inconsistency.
- Anxiety/depression had the largest decrement in most patient groups and in
  the general population.
- The same state could receive different EQ VAS values across groups and time.
  The authors relate this to disease experience and to the broader content of
  EQ VAS.

## Limitations and source issues

- Data-collection procedures could differ between registers.
- The study did not anchor values at dead, which limits direct use in economic
  evaluation.
- EQ VAS has no explicit choice or trade-off and can have end-aversion bias.
- Some severe self-care and mobility groups were small.

## High-value exact terms

- EQ-5D-3L; EQ VAS; experience-based valuation; Swedish National Quality

  multilevel model; anxiety/depression; dead-state anchoring.

- Keep instrument role, source value set, time point, and respondent
  perspective separate.
- Store baseline and follow-up as report time. Do not confuse these with the
  health duration that a valuation method asks a respondent to consider.
