# B20: Individualized knee-arthroplasty decision-aid usability

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1016/j.pecinn.2025.100421`.
- Purposes: implementation research; usability, usefulness, and acceptability
  evaluation; clinical decision support.
- Design: mixed quantitative and qualitative usability study before routine
  implementation.
- The EuroQol Research Foundation funded the work under project `445-RA`.

## Population and samples

- Adults with knee osteoarthritis who were possible surgery candidates at one
  urban, publicly funded hip-and-knee clinic in Alberta, Canada.
- Twenty participants completed the study; mean age was 68 years, 13 were
  female, and 19 reported a White ethnic background.
- Eligibility required English literacy and the ability to use the online aid.

## Concepts and themes

- digital health; patient decision aid; shared decision-making; personalized
  outcomes; usability; acceptability; treatment expectations; implementation
  timing; digital divide.

## Instruments and administration

- The online decision aid displayed individualized historical EQ-5D outcomes
  for before surgery and one year after surgery. EQ-5D was not administered as
  a study outcome.
- Participants self-completed the Preparation for Decision Making Scale, System
  Usability Scale, and Acceptability Scale online in Qualtrics.
- The decision aid and evaluation survey were completed at home after a
  surgical screening appointment.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Participants used the individualized decision aid and then completed
  demographic and evaluation forms.
- The aid compared treatment outcomes for similar age, sex, and body-mass-index
  groups and produced a printable or savable summary report.
- Three validated evaluation instruments and optional open-ended questions
  assessed usefulness, usability, acceptability, and the summary report.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Descriptive statistics for closed questions, with age and sex strata.
- Instrument scoring followed the PDMS and SUS developer guidance.
- Content analysis of open-ended responses by topic.
- No inferential or predictive model was fitted.

## Products

- An individualized online decision aid for total knee arthroplasty.
- Status: usability tested before broader routine implementation; the authors
  recommended no further revision before implementation.
- A personalized summary report is a component of the aid.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Preparedness for decision-making; system usability; acceptability;
  usefulness; qualitative user feedback.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Mean PDMS was 66.4, which the authors interpreted as above-average perceived
  preparedness.
- Mean SUS was 63.4, below the cited average-usability cut point and classified
  as marginal usability.
- Most participants rated presentation as good or excellent; 75% considered
  the length suitable and the information balanced.
- Users asked for simpler content, fewer variables, and earlier delivery in the
  care pathway. Some preferred direct discussion with their surgeon.
- The authors judged the aid useful and acceptable for routine care but called
  for longitudinal evaluation of outcomes and satisfaction.

## Limitations and source issues

- The aid was tested after screening, although routine use is intended before
  screening.
- The sample came from one of 12 Alberta clinics and contained only English-
  literate, computer-literate participants.
- The small usability sample cannot establish clinical effectiveness or
  long-term impact.
