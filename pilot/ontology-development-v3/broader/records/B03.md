# B03: EQ-HWB-9 proxy recall and perspective in dementia

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1007/s11136-026-04230-y`.
- Purposes: content interpretation; proxy-response research; instrument-use
  evaluation.
- Design: qualitative descriptive think-aloud study.
- EuroQol support: study funding, project `1557-RA`.

## Population and samples

- Target context: proxy reporting for community-dwelling people with dementia.
- Respondents: paid or unpaid adult carers in Australia with prior proxy-report
  experience.
- Convenience recruitment through a dementia online-course webpage.
- Thirty-nine potential participants contacted; 19 completed interviews.
- Almost all participants were women. Interviews lasted 49 to 69 minutes.

## Concepts and themes

- proxy reporting; proxy perspective; recall-period adherence; dementia;
  health fluctuation; observability; caregiver interpretation; response
  process.

## Instruments and administration

- Experimental EQ-HWB-9 proxy version, English for Australia, version 1
  (2022), with a seven-day recall period.
- Three positive items from the longer EQ-HWB were also tested.
- Carers completed the measure during an online Zoom interview. They received
  a paper copy before the interview.
- Respondent: carer. Intended referent: person with dementia. Observed
  perspectives: proxy-proxy and proxy-patient.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Semi-structured cognitive think-aloud interviews with item-level probing.
- Interviews were recorded, transcribed, and managed in NVivo 14.
- Recruitment continued until information power; no new concepts appeared
  after the tenth interview.
- Participants and a lived-experience adviser reviewed findings.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Integrated inductive and deductive thematic analysis.
- Two researchers independently coded the first five transcripts and made the
  initial framework. One researcher coded the remainder with team review.
- No statistical model.

## Products

- Evidence for revision of EQ-HWB-9 proxy recall and perspective instructions;
  no finalized new instrument version.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Recall-period interpretation and adherence; retrieval strategy; proxy
  perspective; item observability; response-process evidence.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Most carers could recall relevant events from seven days, but some used
  important events outside the period or older behavior as context.
- Carers used different response rules, including averaging, most difficult
  moments, and frequency.
- All carers used their own observations and perceptions. Nearly half also
  imagined how the person with dementia would answer. No carer used only the
  proxy-patient perspective.
- Less observable feelings required indirect behavioral signs. Some carers
  disagreed with the person's own assessment, especially for pain and control.
- The authors recommend clearer perspective instructions, study of adherence,
  and possible item-wording changes.

## Limitations and source issues

- Convenience recruitment can select people with dementia interest and higher
  digital literacy.
- The Australian English-speaking sample limits transfer to other settings.
- Think-aloud evidence depends on what participants verbalize; sensitive topics
  can be omitted.
- Proxy subgroups were too small for detailed comparison, and almost all
  carers were women.

## High-value exact terms

- EQ-HWB-9 proxy version; seven-day recall period; proxy-proxy perspective;
  proxy-patient perspective; cognitive think-aloud interview; integrated
  thematic analysis; dementia; recall consistency.

## Extraction fit

- The ontology fits if administration stores respondent, referent,
  perspective, channel, language, form, and recall period separately.
- The observed perspective can differ from the instructed perspective. Both
  need provenance.
