# B10: person trade-off protocol for child health priority

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0302886`.
- Purposes: protocol; valuation-method research; social-priority research.
- Design: planned mixed-method person trade-off experiment with qualitative
  interviews and focus groups.
- JATS funding metadata links EuroQol project `348-PHD` to the work. The main
  Markdown conversion omits this funding record.

## Population and samples

- Planned survey: 2,000 Australian residents aged 16 years or older, with age,
  sex, education, and region quotas.
- Planned arms: 1,000 forced-choice and 1,000 with an equivalence option.
- Planned qualitative sample: about 40 think-aloud interviews and four focus
  groups of no more than five participants.
- Target subgroups include young people, parents, nonparents, and parents of a
  child with a health condition.

## Concepts and themes

- child-health priority; social value of health gains; age weighting; QALY
  equity; fair innings; public deliberation; preference consistency; health
  technology assessment.

## Instruments and administration

- No EQ instrument is administered as a health measure.
- Planned online, self-completed survey; online or one-to-one qualitative
  interviews and small deliberative focus groups.
- Perspective: a public respondent acting as a social decision maker.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Person trade-off choices compare identical health gains for age groups from
  infancy to age 24 with adults aged 40 or 55.
- Separate tasks cover life extension and quality-of-life improvement in pain,
  mobility, distress or low mood, and anxiety.
- Method experiment compares forced preference with an equivalence option.
- Chaining, attitudinal questions, comprehension checks, pictograms,
  randomization, pilot testing, and soft launch form part of the protocol.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Planned PTO ratios and midpoint inference when the exact equivalence point is
  not observed.
- Planned group comparisons, sensitivity analyses, bootstrap percentile
  intervals, and framework-based thematic analysis.
- These are planned analyses. The paper reports no main study results.

## Products

- A detailed, ethically approved PTO and qualitative study protocol.
- Planned outputs are age-specific social-priority weights and explanatory
  qualitative evidence; they were not yet produced.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Planned relative social weight by recipient age and gain type; forced-choice
  effect; consistency; comprehension; reasoning; qualitative themes.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- The article's contribution is the rationale and finalized protocol, not an
  empirical preference result.
- Pilot work supported simple PTO wording, age 16 as a respondent lower bound,
  separation of life and quality-of-life gains, and sensitive-topic safeguards.
- The authors expect the study to inform Australian HTA and PTO method practice.

## Limitations and source issues

- Online, unassisted PTO tasks can have engagement and comprehension problems.
- Commercial panels can underrepresent some groups.
- A focus on age can overstate the strength of age preference relative to other
  allocation factors.
- PTO ratios are sensitive to question structure and extreme responses.
- The article Markdown omits structured JATS funding metadata. This is direct
  evidence for the metadata-first process.
