# B14: cognitive debrief of OPUF valuation

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1007/s11136-025-03989-w`.
- Purposes: valuation-method evaluation; cognitive validity; online data-quality
  research.
- Design: qualitative cognitive-debrief and think-aloud study.
- EuroQol support: study funding, project `1508-RA`.

## Population and samples

- UK adults recruited through Prolific.
- Twenty-seven one-to-one interviews, August to September 2023.
- Ages 22 to 79; 14 women; 16 with a degree; 14 with a long-term illness.
- Recruitment targeted gender balance and six age bands.

## Concepts and themes

- online valuation; respondent comprehension; cognitive burden; anchoring;
  scale reversal; respondent engagement; states worse than dead; interviewer
  support; data validity.

## Instruments and administration

- EQ-HWB-S, with nine dimensions, was first self-reported for current health and
  then valued hypothetically.
- Adapted EQ VAS was used for warm-up and familiarization.
- Online survey during a Google Meet cognitive interview with screen sharing.
  Participants self-completed without routine guidance.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Online elicitation of Personal Utility Functions (OPUF).
- Tasks: dimension ranking, swing weighting, level rating, pairwise choice
  against dead, and anchoring dead on a VAS.
- Think-aloud and probing assessed whether participants completed each task as
  expected and whether concerns were minor or major.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Framework-based qualitative coding with systematic counts by task and concern
  severity.
- No preference model was fitted in this paper.

## Products

- Cognitive-validity evidence and recommendations for further OPUF development;
  no value set.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Task comprehension; response-process validity; major and minor concern rate;
  age pattern; instruction engagement.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Every participant had at least one concern; 18 of 27 had a major concern.
- Major concerns affected ranking in 19%, swing weighting in 30%, level rating
  in 56%, and anchoring dead in 48%. Pairwise choice had no major concern.
- Participants confused scale direction, valued current rather than hypothetical
  health, assumed treatment or dimension interactions, and misread anchors.
- Older participants had more major problems on three tasks.
- Standard quantitative data checks would not find all misinterpretations.
- The authors recommend interviewer-led OPUF with quality control and more
  qualitative and test-retest work.

## Limitations and source issues

- Prolific recruitment limits representativeness.
- Interview observation and occasional help can alter behavior.
- The small sample does not support firm quantitative subgroup inference.
- Some problems relate to valuation or EQ-HWB-S wording, not only to OPUF.
