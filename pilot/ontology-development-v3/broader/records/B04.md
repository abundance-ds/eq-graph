# B04: COVID-19 EQ-5D systematic review and meta-analysis

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1186/s12955-025-02421-8`.
- Purposes: evidence synthesis; population-health burden; utility evidence for
  economic evaluation.
- Design: systematic review, random-effects meta-analysis, and qualitative
  predictor synthesis.
- EuroQol support: study funding, project `1627-RA`.

## Population and samples

- Target: adults with acute or post-acute COVID-19.
- Search period: December 2019 to 1 March 2025.
- Screening input: 3,539 references.
- Included evidence: 187 studies and 116,525 participants.
- Review evidence subsets: 127 studies for utility, 161 for EQ VAS, and 104 for
  dimension profiles.

## Concepts and themes

- COVID-19; long COVID; health-related quality of life; population burden;
  health inequality; cross-country comparability; QALY inputs; evidence
  heterogeneity.

## Instruments and administration

- EQ-5D-5L in 80.2% of included studies; EQ-5D-3L in the remainder.
- EQ VAS, index values, and dimension profiles were separate review outcomes.
- Primary studies used self-complete, interviewer, telephone, face-to-face, and
  digital modes. The review did not administer an instrument.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- PRISMA-guided review without prospective protocol registration.
- Searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, and Cochrane Library, plus
  reference lists. Excluded gray and non-English literature.
- Two reviewers independently screened, extracted data, and assessed quality.
- Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies.

## Analysis and statistical models

- DerSimonian-Laird random-effects meta-analysis.
- I-squared heterogeneity; subgroup meta-analysis; leave-one-out sensitivity;
  Egger regression for publication bias.
- Converted some medians and ranges to estimated means and standard deviations.
- Used the final observation when a study reported several time points.

## Products

- Pooled COVID-19 EQ-5D utility and EQ VAS estimates for research and economic
  modeling.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- EQ-5D utility; EQ VAS; problems in each EQ-5D dimension; predictors of poor
  HRQoL; study quality; heterogeneity; publication bias.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Pooled utility was 0.76 (95% CI 0.74 to 0.79); pooled EQ VAS was 70.76
  (68.48 to 73.04).
- Pain/discomfort affected 51% and anxiety/depression affected 46% of
  participants. These were the most affected dimensions.
- Heterogeneity was extreme: I-squared 99.9% for utility and 99.7% for EQ VAS.
- Region and country-income group showed significant subgroup differences;
  instrument version, design, follow-up time, and age group did not.
- Common predictors of worse HRQoL included older age, female sex, severe
  disease, comorbidity, and persistent symptoms.
- The authors propose the estimates for QALY and policy work, but they also
  call for standardized EQ-5D reporting.

## Limitations and source issues

- Primary-study quality, sample size, response, and completeness varied.
- Reporting did not support planned subgroups for several clinical factors.
- Final-observation selection can bias results toward recovery.
- Converted summary statistics can be inaccurate for skewed data.
- English-only, peer-reviewed inclusion can omit relevant evidence.
- Very high heterogeneity limits direct use of the pooled values.

## High-value exact terms

- systematic review; meta-analysis; EQ-5D-5L; EQ-5D-3L; EQ VAS;
  DerSimonian-Laird random-effects model; Newcastle-Ottawa Scale; I-squared;
  long COVID; health-state utility.

- One publication must link to many review evidence units. A pooled review
