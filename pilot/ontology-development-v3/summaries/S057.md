## Aim and setting

The study co-designed use of EQ-5D-Y-5L as a generic paediatric patient-reported outcome measure (P-PROM) in Routine Outpatient Care for Kids (ROCK), aiming to improve patient–clinician visits. It used a co-design framework for public service design and the Double Diamond model (discover, define, develop, deliver). The setting was Royal Children’s Hospital (RCH), Melbourne, Australia, a tertiary paediatric hospital using Epic Electronic Medical Record (EMR). Eligible stakeholders were service providers, adolescents aged 12–18, and caregivers of children aged 2–18 with outpatient-care experience (Methods).

Data collection occurred August–November 2023 for workshops and November 2023 for feedback and optimisation. Five co-design workshops, nine feedback sessions, and two optimisation sessions involved two adolescents, three caregivers, and 11 service providers across the broader workshop set; the abstract reports nine service providers in the co-design participant count. Workshops were online, in person, or hybrid, using Zoom, Zoom Whiteboard, and Mentimeter. Workshops addressed scoring/display, workflow, response actions, and patient/caregiver engagement (Abstract; Methods, “Data collection”).

## Instrument and process

EQ-5D-Y-5L has mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and sad/worried/unhappy items, each with five levels from no problems to extreme problems/cannot do, plus EQ VAS 0–100. The paper states it was officially launched September 2024. Participants completed the questionnaire during sensitisation. Workshop activities used case vignettes, creative displays, group discussion, independent work, consensus, and iterative prototype feedback. Mock patient–clinician visits tested the full prototype (Methods, “EQ-5D-Y-5L,” “Co-design workshops,” and “Feedback and optimisation sessions”).

## P-PROM ROCK Program

The final program has six linked elements (Results, “Final co-designed prototype”). (1) Clinician training: 60-minute small-group session covering PROMs, EQ-5D-Y-5L, EMR location, and response action. (2) Patient/caregiver information package: flyer and video before visits and brief information before/after completion; the questionnaire is introduced as a “general health tracking questionnaire.” (3) Displays: item-and-level wording in EMR plus longitudinal line graph; for the graph, scores are flipped so higher means better health. Patients or caregivers control which items are flagged for discussion; no external threshold or traffic-light judgement is imposed. (4) Workflow: completion attaches to an outpatient visit; portal completion up to seven days before, reminders at seven days, two days, and waiting room; paper is available. (5) Family resources: automatic child or caregiver resources after completion, including support pathways. (6) Clinician resources: Clinical Decision Support Tool to locate, identify, discuss, and act; urgent clinician support and mild/moderate community-support pathways.

## Findings and limitations

Participants considered simple collection insufficient. They preferred item-level display over a single or preference-weighted score, patient-controlled flagging, and supports that ensure review and action. They rejected displays resembling school reports or framing responses as failure. The authors state that asking for P-PROM data creates a duty of care for appropriate review and action (Abstract; Discussion; Conclusion).

Strengths include direct involvement of adolescents, caregivers, and providers and use of two complementary frameworks. Limitations include small participant numbers, scheduling constraints, limited fully mixed-group workshops, online technology barriers, possible power imbalance, likely early-adopter providers, no non-English-speaking participants, and uncertain generalisability beyond RCH, other ages, and other settings. Phase 3 was planned to evaluate the program (Discussion; Conclusion).
