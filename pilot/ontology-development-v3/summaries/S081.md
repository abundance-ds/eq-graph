# Measuring health-related quality of life in multiple sclerosis: comparing the acceptability, validity and responsiveness of the EQ-5D-3L and MSIS-8D

## Aim and population

The study compared acceptability, distributional properties, construct validity, and responsiveness of the generic EQ-5D-3L and condition-specific Multiple Sclerosis Impact Scale Eight Dimensions (MSIS-8D) in people with multiple sclerosis (MS). Data came from the UK MS Register, an ongoing prospective longitudinal observational cohort launched in 2011. Recruitment used clinical partner sites, word of mouth, and events; membership was described as broadly representative of UK people with MS. Analyses used members with complete EQ-5D-3L data at Spring 2023 and at least four earlier occasions (Abstract; Methods—UK MS Register).

The cross-sectional sample included 3,676 people with MS, mean age 58.32 years (SD 10.8); 50.67% had relapsing-remitting MS, 30.2% secondary progressive MS, 13.36% primary progressive MS, and 2.07% benign MS. Mean EQ-5D-3L value was 0.567 (SD 0.321; range −0.594 to 1); mean MSIS-8D value was 0.626 (0.185; range 0.079–0.882). Acceptability analysis included 6,141 members (Results—Sample characteristics).

## Measures and events

EQ-5D-3L asks about today across mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, with three levels and 243 health states. UK value-set utilities range from −0.594 to 1. MSIS-8D derives eight dimensions from MSIS-29-v2: physical tasks, social activities, mobility, daily activities, fatigue, emotion, cognition, and depression. It uses four levels and 65,536 states, asks about MS impact over the past two weeks, and has UK tariff range 0.079–0.882 (Methods—Data and instruments).

Construct validity covered discriminative and convergent validity against web-EDSS disability, MSWS-12 walking impairment, Fatigue Severity Scale, HADS-A, HADS-D, and MS type. ANOVA, independent *t* tests, and Spearman correlations were used. Responsiveness compared pre-event and event utility values for relapse and onset of at least two new symptoms, using mean change, standardized response mean (SRM), standardized effect size (SES), paired *t* tests, and Bonferroni correction. Relapse values were compared with a preceding point within 400 days; symptom-onset values were compared with a preceding point and a point within 30 days after onset (Methods—Statistical analyses; Table 1).

## Findings

EQ-5D-3L had better acceptability: 3.14% of 6,141 members did not complete it, versus 8.57% for MSIS-8D (*p* < 0.001). EQ-5D-3L had a greater ceiling effect, 11.13% versus 3.86% for MSIS-8D, while both had floor effects below 1%. The commonest EQ-5D-3L profile was 22222 (13.41%); nearly half the sample was represented by five EQ-5D states. MSIS-8D’s five commonest states covered only 8.19%. Health state density index was 0.215 for EQ-5D-3L and 0.577 for MSIS-8D, indicating more even MSIS-8D distribution (Results—Acceptability and Distributional properties; Figures 1–2).

Both measures discriminated significantly by MS type, disability, walking impairment, fatigue, depression, and anxiety (*p* < 0.0001). Both had strong correlations (rho >0.5) with disability, fatigue, walking, and depression, and moderate correlations (rho >0.4) with anxiety. EQ-5D-3L correlated slightly more with disability and walking; MSIS-8D correlated more with anxiety, depression, and fatigue (Results—Construct validity; Table 3).

Responsiveness was limited. Relapse analyses included 156 observations from 142 participants; symptom-onset analyses included 97 observations from 93 participants. Neither measure met responsiveness criteria for onset of new symptoms. For relapse, only MSIS-8D met all criteria, but its standardized effect size was small (−0.228). Mean relapse change was −0.047 for both instruments; symptom-onset mean change was −0.022 for EQ-5D-3L and −0.034 for MSIS-8D (Results—Responsiveness; Table 4).

## Interpretation and limits

MSIS-8D had superior distributional properties and content coverage, while EQ-5D-3L was more acceptable and both showed excellent construct validity. The authors caution that utility range differs: the narrower MSIS-8D range may limit mean changes despite greater descriptive sensitivity. EQ-5D-3L omits fatigue and cognition, and its ceiling effect may reduce sensitivity; EQ-5D-5L might improve this, but the register used 3L during the study period. Responsiveness analysis relied on self-reported event timing, fixed three- or six-month data collection, selected members with repeated complete data, and did not control for other events. Results may have survivorship or selection bias. Further research should evaluate EQ-5D-5L in MS (Discussion; Conclusions).
