# Measurement properties of the EQ-5D-3L, EQ-5D-5L, and SF-6Dv2 in patients with late-onset Pompe disease

## Aim, design, and sample

The study evaluated EQ-5D-3L, EQ-5D-5L, and SF-6Dv2 psychometric properties in Chinese patients with late-onset Pompe disease (PD; glycogen storage disease type II), and compared their performance. A web-based cross-sectional survey ran in China January–April 2023. The China PD Care Center (PDCC) recruited registered members aged ≥16 years with late-onset PD, no cognitive impairment, ability to complete independently, and informed consent. All participants were invited to repeat EQ-5D and SF-6Dv2 after one week. Of 117 patients, about half were male, 73% lived in rural areas, 55.6% had secondary education or below, and 77.8% were not active in employment. Mean completion time was about 15 minutes; all questions were compulsory, so no missing data occurred. [Abstract; Methods—Data and participants; Results—Participants’ characteristics and Table 1]

PD-related groups included wheelchair use, ventilator use, disability, difficulty standing from sitting, unstable walking/prone to falling, difficulty changing from lying to sitting, difficulty lifting objects above the head, difficulty breathing, atrophy of paraspinal muscles, and scapula alata. Test–retest included 110 patients (94%) one week later. A research assistant excluded completion under one-third of median survey length and flatlining. [Methods—Data and participants; Measures]

## Instruments and analyses

EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L contain mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. 3L levels are “no problem”, “some problems”, and “confined to bed/extreme problems/unable”; 5L levels are “no problems”, “slight problems”, “moderate problems”, “severe problems”, and “extreme problems/unable”. Chinese value sets yielded utility ranges −0.149 to 1 (3L) and −0.391 to 1 (5L); EQ VAS is 0 (worst health imaginable) to 100 (best). SF-6Dv2 has physical functioning, role limitations, social functioning, pain, mental health, and vitality; dimensions use five levels except pain (six); Chinese utility range is −0.535 to 1. WHODAS-12 covers cognition, mobility, self-care, social, household, and society; the WHO simple score ranges 12–60, higher = more disability. [Methods—Measures]

Ceiling/floor effects were proportions at full-health and worst-health profiles. Convergent validity used Spearman correlations with WHODAS-12 and between measures; weak ≤0.29, moderate 0.30–0.49, strong ≥0.50. Known-group validity used chi-square for problem proportions and ANOVA *F*-statistics for utility-score discrimination. Test–retest used Gwet’s AC for dimensions and two-way mixed-effects, single-measure, absolute-agreement ICC for utility scores; Bland–Altman plots assessed graphical agreement. Analyses used R, with *p*<0.05. [Methods—Data analysis]

## Findings

- **Distribution:** All 3L dimensions had ceiling effects: 42.7% no self-care problems, 30.8% no pain/discomfort, and 17.1% no usual-activities problems. 5L self-care had the largest 5L ceiling (29.9%); no SF-6Dv2 dimension had a ceiling effect. Full health was 6.8% (3L), 0.9% (5L), and 0% (SF-6Dv2). The 3L worst profile 33333 and 5L 55555 were each 1.7%; SF-6Dv2 555655 was 0%. Floor concerns included 21.4% confined to bed on 3L mobility and 24.8% unable on 5L mobility. [Results—Ceiling and floor effects; Table 2]
- **Test–retest:** 3L Gwet’s AC was 0.72–0.83 (four of five dimensions very good); 5L was 0.56–0.65; SF-6Dv2 was 0.51–0.74 (three good, three moderate). Utility ICCs were 0.87 (3L), 0.85 (5L), and 0.85 (SF-6Dv2); EQ VAS ICC was 0.71. PD had lower agreement than most other EQ dimensions (3L 0.80; 5L 0.56). [Results—Test–retest; Table 3]
- **Convergent validity:** 3L–5L dimension correlations were 0.72–0.83. Cross-measure dimension correlations were 0.56–0.78; utility correlations were 0.83 (3L–5L), 0.82 (3L–SF-6Dv2), and 0.89 (5L–SF-6Dv2). Utility–EQ VAS correlations were 0.42, 0.49, and 0.47. All WHODAS hypotheses were supported. 3L, 5L, and SF-6Dv2 ranges were 0.44–0.79, 0.53–0.89, and 0.45–0.72; 5L utility–WHODAS was −0.84 versus −0.71 (3L) and −0.78 (SF-6Dv2). [Results—Convergent validity; Tables 4–5]
- **Known groups:** Utility declined for every reported symptom/disability. 5L had higher *F*-statistics than 3L for all conditions (5L 5.8–54.4; 3L 1.3–28.3) and than SF-6Dv2 for most (SF-6Dv2 4.4–35.8). For wheelchair status, *F* was 54.4 (5L), 28.3 (3L), and 35.8 (SF-6Dv2); for disability, 37.8, 27.5, and 27.6. SF-6Dv2 was better for unstable walking/prone to falling and difficulty breathing. [Results—Discriminatory power; Table 6]

The authors conclude that 5L has lower ceiling/floor effects, stronger convergent validity, and greater discriminant ability, and may show larger utility gains in cost-effectiveness analysis. 3L had better test–retest agreement, but this may reflect insensitivity if health changed during the one-week interval. Limitations include volunteer recruitment through a patient organisation, self-report, web administration, possible selection bias and engagement problems, and clinical information not verified from medical records. WHODAS-12 lacks pain, so pain/discomfort validity remains insufficiently assessed. [Discussion; Conclusion]
