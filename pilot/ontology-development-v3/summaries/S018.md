## Aim, design, and sample

The study replaced the existing Trinidad and Tobago (T&T) EQ-5D-5L crosswalk with a directly elicited set using EQ-VT version 2.1 (Abstract; Introduction). It used face-to-face, computer-assisted interviews in a national adult sample targeted to age, sex, and geography. Quota sampling covered 14 Trinidad regions and seven Tobago parishes, using 2011 census distributions; streets were randomly selected and one household member selected by most recent birthday. Data collection ran July–September 2022. Fourteen interviewers received one week of training and at least two sets of five pilot interviews; four failed QC and were removed, leaving ten interviewers. Of 1,079 completed interviews, the response rate was 34% (Methods; Results).

## Instruments and elicitation

The EQ-5D-5L has mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each at five levels and forming 3,125 states. Respondents completed self-report EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS, five practice tasks, ten cTTO states, the feedback module, 12 DCE pairs, and demographics (Methods, “Interview procedures”). cTTO used conventional TTO for better-than-dead states and lead-time TTO for worse-than-dead states: 10 years in a state versus variable full health, or 10 full-health years followed by 10 years in the impaired state versus variable full health. DCE asked which of two states was preferred without duration.

The cTTO design contained 86 states in ten blocks of ten, including one mild state, 55555, and eight efficient-design states. DCE used 20 blocks of 12 pairs from a Bayesian efficient design based on priors from 19 valuation studies (Methods, “Selection of health states”). QC flags were missing worse-than-dead explanation, under 3 minutes on wheelchair examples, under 5 minutes for ten cTTO tasks, or a 55555 inconsistency. A batch failed when over 40% of an interviewer’s interviews were flagged; retraining or removal followed.

## Models and selection

The paper estimated random-intercept cTTO, random-intercept Tobit, heteroskedastic and heteroskedastic Tobit models, conditional and mixed logit DCE models, and hybrid models. The cTTO scale is censored at −1 and heteroskedastic; DCE models address preference heterogeneity. Model choice used coefficient logic/significance, MAE, in-sample and out-of-sample predictivity for states and blocks, and whether heteroskedasticity and censoring were handled (Methods, “Analyses”). Stata 18 commands included `xtreg`, `xttobit`, `intreg`, `clogit`, `mixlogit`, and `hyreg`.

## Results and tariff

The 1,079 respondents produced 10,790 cTTO and 12,948 DCE observations. Mean interview time was 43 minutes 7 seconds. QC flagged 164 interviews (15%): 63 missing worse-than-dead explanation, 18 55555 inconsistencies, 45 short wheelchair examples, and 50 short cTTO sessions. Nineteen-point-two-seven percent of cTTO responses were negative, 6.33% were −1, and two respondents were non-traders. Heteroskedasticity was confirmed by a Breusch–Pagan test (Results, “Valuation results”).

The preferred hybrid heteroskedastic Tobit model had all internally consistent, statistically significant coefficients and best single-state out-of-sample predictivity. The tariff is `U = 1 − 0.027MO2 − 0.085MO3 − 0.187MO4 − 0.368MO5 − 0.024SC2 − 0.072SC3 − 0.150SC4 − 0.232SC5 − 0.011UA2 − 0.065UA3 − 0.146UA4 − 0.219UA5 − 0.044PD2 − 0.128PD3 − 0.311PD4 − 0.480PD5 − 0.020AD2 − 0.074AD3 − 0.161AD4 − 0.264AD5`. For example, state 21354 = 0.267. Pain/discomfort had the largest weight, followed by mobility, anxiety/depression, self-care, and usual activities. 55555 = −0.563 and the range is −0.563 to 1.

Compared with the crosswalk, the EQ-VT mean was 0.386 versus 0.524, negative states 275 (8.8%) versus 21 (0.7%), MAD 0.157, correlation 0.879, and range −0.563 to 1 versus −0.163 to 1 (Results, Fig. 2). The authors attribute differences to protocol, non-trading, scale, time, culture, and the “confined to bed” versus “unable to walk about” mobility wording.

## Implications and limitations

The tariff is recommended for T&T QALYs, clinical studies, and resource allocation, and as a reference for Caribbean settings. Limitations include female and education over-representation, persistent protocol issues, minor interviewer effects, 2011 census comparison, and differences between interviewers’ respondent compositions.
