## Aim and data

The study tested whether the EQ-5D-5L level summary score (LSS) is justified and how it can be interpreted when utility weights are unavailable or unsuitable. It applied Mokken scaling, a non-parametric item response theory (NP-IRT) approach, to test the monotone homogeneity model (MHM; ordering persons) and double monotonicity model/manifest invariant item ordering (DMM/MIIO; ordering items). Data came from the 2012 Multi Instrument Comparison (MIC) project in Australia, Canada, Germany, Norway, UK, and USA. The analytic sample was 7,933 adults from healthy and chronic-illness groups; COPD and stroke groups sampled only in Australia were excluded (Background; Methods, “Dataset”).

The EQ-5D-5L contains mobility (MO), self-care (SC), usual activities (UA), pain/discomfort (PD), and anxiety/depression (AD), each with five ordered levels from “no problems” to “extreme problems”/“unable to.” LSS assigns levels 1–5 and sums them, ranging 5 (11111) to 25 (55555), with higher score indicating worse health. EQ VAS was analysed separately. The paper notes that LSS is also called “equally weighted,” “unweighted,” or “misery” score/index (Methods, “EQ-5D-5L”).

## Methods

Analyses used the R package `mokken`. MHM assumptions were unidimensionality, local independence, and monotonicity. Loevinger H coefficients were evaluated at pair, item, and scale levels; H_i >0.3 was sufficient and H_S ≥0.5 conventionally strong. Reliability used Molenaar–Sijtsma rho and Guttman lambda-2. Automated item selection procedure (AISP) varied H_i lower bounds 0–0.5. Manifest monotonicity examined item-step and item-response functions; violations > minvi=0.03 were reported. MIIO ordered items by conditional means, checked pairwise violations, and used H^T: <0.3 too low, 0.3–0.4 low, 0.4–0.5 moderate, >0.5 highly accurate (Methods, “Mokken scale analysis”).

## Results

Respondents reported 566 of 3,125 possible profiles; 11111 was most common (19.3%), followed by 11121 (14.3%). The scale reliability was rho=0.822 and lambda-2=0.819. AISP placed all five items on one latent variable at H_i=0.3. AD had weak scalability (H_i=0.377) and became unscalable at H_i ≥0.378; PD was rejected at ≥0.685 and SC at ≥0.721. All five items showed monotonicity with no manifest violations (Results, “AISP and scalability” and “Fit of the MHM model”).

MIIO found two violations: AD–MO and AD–UA. Full-scale H_S was 0.559 (strong) and H^T=0.463 (moderate accuracy). Removing AD raised H_S to 0.714 and H^T to 0.743, with no MIIO violations. Stratified results were broadly consistent across disease and country groups, although the healthy subgroup had weak H_S=0.363 and lower variation (Results, “Fit of MIIO” and “Stratified analysis”).

The proposed interpretation is that low LSS values mainly represent PD and AD problems; lower-to-mid values add MO and UA problems; middle-to-higher values reveal SC limitations. AD is easier to endorse than MO/UA at lower scores but may be harder at moderate/higher scores, and its role differs by subgroup, especially depression. The authors regard the five-item LSS as a simple, international, value-set-independent composite for group applications, but advise caution for healthy samples and note that the adult Western online-panel sample does not establish performance in children or other populations (Discussion; Conclusion).
