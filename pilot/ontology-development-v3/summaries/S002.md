## Purpose and scope

This paper introduces and critiques three EuroQol TTO data-collection protocols: Measurement and Valuation of Health (MVH), Paris and EuroQol Valuation Technology (EQ-VT). MVH and Paris were developed for EQ-5D-3L; EQ-VT was developed for EQ-5D-5L. The paper focuses on trade-off framework, iteration, administration, training and data quality. Sampling frameworks, health-state experimental design and statistical modelling are not protocol requirements and can vary by country (Introduction). The decision-maker summary recommends Paris for EQ-5D-3L and EQ-VT for EQ-5D-5L.

TTO asks a respondent to choose between longer life in an impaired state and shorter life in better health until preference indifference. Conventional TTO uses different procedures for better-than-death (BTD) and worse-than-death (WTD) states. For BTD, U(h)=x/t; for WTD, conventional TTO uses U(h)=−x/(t−x). The WTD construction has a lower bound of negative infinity and has been criticised because BTD and WTD are elicited by fundamentally different tasks (The TTO Tasks; Conventional TTO).

Composite TTO is a compromise using conventional TTO for BTD states and lead-time TTO for WTD states. With lead time l, its WTD value is U(h)=(x−l)/t, and its possible scale is −1 to 1. Lead-time TTO was developed to avoid the scale problem but created framing and comprehension difficulties; composite TTO makes the BTD/WTD distinction explicit (Composite TTO).

## Protocol components and comparison

Iteration requires a starting point, algorithm and termination rule. Algorithms are titration, bisection or “ping-pong”, or combinations. A 10-year time horizon is common but was not strongly supported by empirical evidence. Traditional one-to-one personal interviews are preferred because group and online administration produced more respondents declaring indifference after only one or two steps, indicating reduced comprehension or engagement. Time boards and computerised visual aids help respondents understand the length-of-life trade-off. Warm-up tasks explain health states, quality of life and trade-offs. Interviewer training should include a multi-day workshop, script, demonstrations, practice and feedback. Quality control should examine protocol proficiency, task times and interviewer behaviour (Iteration Procedures and Mode and Method of Administration; Warm-Up; Training; Quality Control).

The MVH protocol (1993) values EQ-5D-3L with conventional TTO, a fixed 10-year horizon, full health 11111 and death. It starts with bisection for three steps, then one-year titration and a six-month correction at preference reversal. It is face-to-face, paper-and-pencil, uses a time board, and has no formal QC or interviewer-training guideline. Reported problems include logical inconsistency, respondent variance, interviewer effects and non-continuous values (The MVH Protocol; Appendix Table).

The Paris protocol updates MVH for EQ-5D-3L. It simplifies iteration and uses one-year increments, terminating at indifference or a one-year indifference range. This reduces the number of x values and interview complexity, but comparative empirical research against MVH was not available (The Paris Protocol).

EQ-VT uses composite TTO for EQ-5D-5L. It inherits initial comparison with 10 years in full health, BTD/WTD separation, bisection and one-year increments followed by six-month correction. Unlike MVH and Paris, EQ-VT continues until the respondent states indifference. It uses computer-assisted personal interviews, digital visual aids, a wheelchair example and three practice states. It includes standardised interviewer materials, training, monitoring and a QC tool that can identify non-compliance for retraining or removal (The EQ-VT Protocol).

EQ-VT also includes DCE. DCE is easier to understand but gives an arbitrary latent scale without death and full-health anchors. It cannot alone provide QALY utilities until anchoring is resolved. A hybrid model combines TTO and DCE likelihoods and links their coefficients with a scale link function (The EQ-VT Protocol).

## Implications and research gaps

The authors describe EQ-VT as feasible but still under continuous investigation. Future work should test non-iterative TTO-like tasks, alternative fixed or variable horizons, and protocols outside EuroQol. Time horizon may change values, especially for severe states and older respondents. The paper identifies the absence of a gold standard, possible framing and response-strategy bias, and the need for direct protocol comparisons as limitations. It does not report a new population sample or value set; publication type is methodological review. Key protocol labels and formulas are retained above as reported.
