# Selecting Bolt-on Dimensions for the EQ-5D: Testing the Impact of Hearing, Sleep, Cognition, Energy, and Relationships on Preferences Using Pairwise Choices

## Aim and sample

The study tested whether pairwise choices can identify EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons that change preferences, and compared the impact of five candidate dimensions: hearing, sleep, cognition, energy, and relationships. A UK general-population online panel survey ran in May 2017. Quotas covered sex, age, education, children, religion, and marital status. Of 1,581 entrants, exclusions for consent, noncompletion, speeding, and full quotas left 1,040 respondents: 520 female and 520 male; 11% were aged 65+. Mean completion time was 9.19 minutes and median 7.05 (Abstract; Methods—Sample; Results).

Candidate bolt-ons came from prior psychometric work. Hearing, energy, and relationships were selected based on large, moderate, and small coefficients against Health VAS; cognition was selected because a prior study showed a large preference impact; sleep because a prior study found no significant preference impact. Labels and descriptors were reviewed by researchers and two focus groups: five general-public members and six patients with chronic conditions (COPD, type 1 diabetes, chronic fatigue syndrome, and endometriosis) (Methods—Bolt-on selection and development).

## Choice design and instruments

Three EQ-5D-5L health-state pairs were selected from an England valuation study because prior choices were approximately 50:50: pair 1, 11122 versus 23111; pair 2, 52211 versus 11325; pair 3, 33142 versus 34333. Bolt-ons were tested at levels 1, 3, and 5 only. Level 1 tested presence; levels 3 and 5 tested severity. For bolt-on additions, the bolt-on was added to state A (11122, 52211, or 33142) while state B had bolt-on level 1. The survey included 48 pairwise questions, including three without bolt-ons (Methods—Health state pairs; Table 2).

Respondents completed background questions, EQ-5D-5L plus bolt-ons, familiarisation, and eight pairwise choices. They chose A or B without an indifference option. Six randomized blocks each contained all three health-state pairs at least once. Participants were randomized to blocks; option side A/B was randomized, but question order within blocks was not randomized. Blocks 1–3 included one no-bolt-on task and seven bolt-on tasks; blocks 4–6 included eight bolt-on tasks (Methods—Survey administration and design).

## Analysis

Logistic regressions with clustered sandwich estimators modelled respondents’ choice. A main-effect model used bolt-on presence and health-state-pair dummies, adjusted for sociodemographics. Wald tests assessed whether level-specific dummies improved fit. Level models estimated marginal effects (log odds coefficients) for each bolt-on at levels 1, 3, and 5, overall and separately by health-state pair. Analyses used STATA/MP 14.1 (Methods—Analyses).

## Findings

In the overall bolt-on model, hearing had the largest negative marginal effect (−0.16), followed by cognition (−0.15), relationships (−0.12), sleep (−0.09), and energy (−0.08). Sociodemographic variables and health-state pairs did not significantly confound results. A Wald test showed that level-3 and level-5 dummies improved model fit; level-1 additions did not (Results).

At level 3, cognition had the largest overall effect (−0.16); hearing and sleep were both −0.12, energy −0.05, and relationships −0.04. At level 5, hearing was largest (−0.43), followed by cognition (−0.31), relationships (−0.29), energy (−0.27), and sleep (−0.19). Thus ranking changed with severity. Level-3 effects were generally negative and significant in pairs 1 and 3; cognition was largest in all three pairs. Pair 2 had smaller effects, with hearing and energy not significant. Level-5 effects were always negative and significant; hearing was largest in all three pairs, while sleep was often smallest (Results—Table 4).

The tested wording retained EQ-5D-5L style: hearing “I have no problems hearing” through “I am unable to hear”; sleep “I have no problems sleeping” through “I have extreme problems sleeping”; similar five-level formats were used for cognition, energy, and relationships (Methods—Table 1). The sample’s level-1 response proportions were 82% hearing, 47% sleep, 63% cognition, 43% energy, and 70% relationships (Results—Table 3).

## Interpretation and limits

Pairwise choices were a low-cost, flexible way to compare bolt-on preference impact. Hearing and cognition appeared most important overall, but severity-dependent rankings mean selection depends on whether level 3, level 5, or overall impact is prioritised. Adding a bolt-on requires revaluation of the extended tariff. Level-1 additions may have little impact on core dimensions, but the study did not establish interactions at levels 3 or 5.

Only three health-state pairs were used, and levels 2 and 4 were not tested. The study was not powered for advanced interaction models. Pairwise question order was not randomized, and preferences may depend on the base health-state pair. Results should be confirmed with more pairs, all levels, and other valuation methods such as ranking or TTO (Discussion; Limitations).
