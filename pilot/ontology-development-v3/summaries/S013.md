## Aim, setting, and sample

The aim was to develop a French EQ-5D-5L value set for academic and clinical research and for French regulatory price-setting of drugs and medical devices (Abstract; Introduction). The study used EuroQol’s standardized valuation protocol and EQ-VT version 2.0, with French translations validated by the study team. A market research company conducted computer-assisted personal interviews (CAPI) in respondents’ homes under research-team supervision. The target was 1,000 adults with quotas for age, sex, and socioeconomic status based on national statistics; geographical representativeness was not a quota. Interviews ran March–November 2018. Of 1,143 interviewed people, 95 were excluded for protocol or data-quality problems, leaving 1,048 respondents (Results, “Sample Characteristics”). Mean age was 49.4 years, 55.44% were women, and the socioeconomic distribution met the planned strata. The sample had excess women, an age imbalance, and geographical deviations, so age/sex adjustment was evaluated.

## Instruments and preference tasks

Respondents first completed the French EQ-5D-5L questionnaire and EQ VAS for their own health, plus age, sex, disease experience, education, and professional activity questions (Methods, “Interview Process”). The instrument has five dimensions—mobility (MO), self-care (SC), usual activities (UA), pain/discomfort (PD), and anxiety/depression (AD)—with five levels and 3,125 possible health states.

Each respondent used interactive EQ-VT for two tasks: (1) ten composite time trade-off (cTTO) valuations from one of ten blocks of ten states, drawn from an 86-state design; and (2) seven DCE pairs from one of 28 blocks, covering 196 pairs (Methods, “Eliciting Preferences”). In cTTO, respondents compared 10 years in a state with *x* years in full health. For a state judged “worse than being dead,” the protocol switched to lead-time TTO: 20 years comprising 10 years full health plus 10 years in the state versus *x* years full health. The interview used a wheelchair example and three practice states. The feedback module ranked respondents’ states and allowed correction of illogical ordering. DCE required choosing A or B; indifference was not allowed.

## Quality control and analysis

Predefined flags were: less than 3 minutes explaining the wheelchair TTO; no “worse than dead” explanation; 55555 not the lowest value and at least 0.5 above the lowest state; or less than 5 minutes for ten TTO tasks. A batch of ten interviews was rejected when at least 40% were flagged (Methods, “Interviewers Monitoring”).

The cTTO dependent variable was transformed to positive disutility (1 minus cTTO), with a Tobit model for the lower bound at −1, excluding feedback-flagged cTTO observations. DCE used a conditional logit model. A hybrid model combined cTTO and DCE using a scaling parameter (theta), and incremental level dummies (20 parameters) represented changes from level 1. Heteroskedasticity was modelled. Seven specifications were compared: cTTO Tobit, DCE logit, unadjusted hybrid, age/sex-adjusted hybrid, age-only, sex-only, and adjusted main-effect hybrid. Respondent weights were applied when sample imbalance affected values (Methods, “Modeling Methods”).

## Findings and product

The final sample produced 9,803 cTTO observations and 7,336 DCE observations; 20.2% of cTTO values were negative, 2.3% were −1, and 13.7% were 1 (Results, “Data Characteristics”). The preferred adjusted hybrid model corrected age/sex imbalance and had logically ordered coefficients; its theta was 5.226. The model provides values for all 3,125 states, with full health (11111) = 1 and 55555 = −0.5255 (Table 2; Table 3). In the adjusted model, cumulative level-5 decrements were approximately MO 0.325, SC 0.258, UA 0.240, PD 0.444, and AD 0.258. The ranking was PD, MO, SC, AD, UA. The model’s utility for state 54321 was illustrated as 0.441 (Results, “Value Set”).

Compared with the French EQ-5D-3L tariff, the 5L distribution shifted toward higher values: 12.8% of 5L states versus 32% of 3L states were negative. The worst 5L state was about −0.52 versus −0.53 for 3L/crosswalk. Dimension ranking changed from 3L mobility, self-care, pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression, usual activities to 5L pain/discomfort, mobility, self-care, anxiety/depression, usual activities. The authors caution that labels differ (“confined to bed” versus “unable to walk about”) (Results, “Comparing Value Sets”; Discussion).

## Implications, limitations, and funding

The tariff was intended to support French QALY and cost-effectiveness analyses, disease-specific studies, clinical trials, and HTA submissions. Limitations include incomplete age/sex/geographical representativeness, interviewer effects despite QC, and possible historical inconsistency when replacing 3L tariffs. Data were available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. Funding was EuroQol plus unrestricted grants from listed pharmaceutical companies; the authors report consultancy and EuroQol-related interests (Compliance with Ethical Standards).
