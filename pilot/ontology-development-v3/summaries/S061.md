## Aim and setting

The study reviewed EQ-TIPS V2.0 wording and content and examined possible uses and development challenges. EQ-TIPS is an experimental generic Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQoL) measure for infants and toddlers. It was initially developed in South Africa and is intended to support research, clinical decision-making, health technology assessment, and economic evaluation. Preference-weighted scores and value sets are planned but were not produced by this study (Introduction; Abstract).

Three online, semi-structured focus-group consultations took place on Zoom between December 2022 and February 2023. The groups were: EuroQol members with expertise in younger populations, descriptive systems, and version management; paediatric health and development experts; and paediatric HRQoL instrument developers. Forty-four experts were invited, 11 did not attend, and 33 took part: Group 1 *n*=17, Group 2 *n*=11, and Group 3 *n*=5. Fifteen countries were represented. Experts were purposively selected for expertise, experience, and geographical spread (Methods > Experts; Results > Sample characteristics).

## Instrument and procedure

EQ-TIPS V2.0 was the experimental EQ-TIPS-3L. Its six dimensions were **Movement**, **Play**, **Social Interaction**, **Communication**, **Eating**, and **Pain**. Each dimension had three response levels: no, some, and a lot of problems. All dimensions except Pain referred to “age-appropriate behaviour”. Pain used observable examples: “Painful behaviour includes inconsolable crying, restless movement, grimacing”. The EQ-TIPS Visual Analogue Scale (EQ VAS) asked the proxy to rate the child’s overall health from 0 to 100. The five-level version was not available for testing in this study (Introduction; Fig. 1).

Experts received the instrument, a short video, and preparatory reading. Breakout-room discussions used iterative topic guides. Topics included overall impression; understanding, relevance, and comprehensiveness; dimension overlap; “age-appropriate” wording; response levels; recall period; proxy completion; transition to EQ-5D-Y; and future research. Recordings were transcribed verbatim and de-identified. Two researchers used NVivo Version 14 and Braun and Clarke’s six-phase thematic analysis. Coding was mainly deductive, with inductive themes and sub-themes allowed. The team checked the extraction table and interpretations (Methods > Procedure; Data management and analysis).

## Main findings

- **Overall performance and use:** Most experts found the measure short, easy to complete, and suitable for clinical trials and research. They endorsed the layout, response options, and EQ VAS, but requested evidence across diverse health conditions. Experts considered EQ-TIPS useful because few preference-weighted measures exist for this age group (Results > Overall impressions; Discussion).
- **Construct:** Some experts were unclear whether EQ-TIPS measured health, health status, HRQoL, or development. They asked for a clear definition and for wording to focus on the child’s health, not the effect on the family or caregiver. Spillover should be measured by a separate outcome. Experts discussed consistency with EQ-5D-Y and EQ-5D for lifespan measurement, but most prioritised age-relevant content over direct dimension mapping (Results > Conceptual framework).
- **Age and context:** The proposed 0–3-year range was supported, but experts noted different developmental trajectories, prematurity, acute versus chronic conditions, and cultural differences in developmental norms. There was no consensus on multiple instruments for the age range. Examples for each dimension were viewed as a practical way to improve relevance across ages (Results > Age range; Child health circumstances; Cultural and translation considerations).
- **Proxy completion:** Proxy subjectivity may reflect age, experience, emotional development, number of children, social support, and access to resources. The best proxy depends on the study context. Familiar parents may be suitable, while hospital clinicians may be better in some hospital settings. Proxy characteristics, caregiver HRQoL, and potential spillover should be recorded. “Age-appropriate behaviour” could add unnecessary subjectivity and cross-cultural variation (Results > Proxy completion).
- **Content:** “Today” was generally preferred for acute conditions and repeated measures; a longer recall period could help for chronic or fluctuating conditions. The three levels in V2.0 raised few concerns, but extended levels should be tested. Examples were preferred to the phrase “age-appropriate behaviour”. Movement, Play, Social interaction, Communication, Eating, and Pain each raised wording or coverage questions. Communication and Social interaction may overlap. Sleep was widely proposed as an additional dimension. Emotions were also proposed; bowel habits had little support (Results > Content validity; Discussion).

## Implications and limitations

The consultation supports further testing of EQ-TIPS V2.0 wording and additional content. It supports examples of observable behaviour, careful proxy instructions, and caregiver consultation from diverse geographical, socio-economic, and cultural settings. Experts also considered EQ-TIPS relevant to routine outcome measurement and clinical trials. The study did not test caregivers or children directly, did not test EQ-TIPS V5.0, and did not produce psychometric or preference evidence.

The authors note recruitment through the research team’s networks, unequal expertise across groups, and limited general debate caused by breakout rooms. Quotations could not include country or work setting. Parent and caregiver engagement was identified as future work (Discussion; Conclusion).
