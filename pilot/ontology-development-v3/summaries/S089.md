## Project, design, and sampling

EQ-DAPHNIE (EuroQol Data for Assessment of Population Health Needs and Instrument Evaluation) aims to generate population norms, support psychometric evaluations, and enable cross-country comparisons from self-reported health measures. The UK pilot ran 6–20 April 2023; 3012 completed responses informed the full survey. Full implementation used 68,411 completers across 15 countries, with a target of 4500 adults per country. Dynata recruited online-panel participants by quotas for age, sex, income, community setting (rural, urban, suburban), and first language where relevant. Quotas used census or equivalent data; one to three independent health-measurement experts reviewed local translations. A 200-person soft launch preceded each country’s main fieldwork, scheduled for six weeks with a possible two-week extension. [Abstract; Methods—Pilot phase design and Main data collection design]

Surveys ran on LimeSurvey with conditional branching, randomization, skip patterns, and Google reCAPTCHA v3. A reCAPTCHA score ranges 0–1; the default 0.5 threshold was used across four interactions, and any score below 0.5 flagged a bot. Dynata controls included incentives, IP monitoring, participant controls, government-ID validation, “Virtual Resume”, and AI-driven QualityScore. [Methods—Brief overview]

## Quality-control protocol

Pilot checks examined missing-data patterns, dropout, completion times, open-text numerical outliers, bots, duplicates, link-click/consent/completion flow, and repeated-question agreement. Pilot respondents completing in under five minutes were “speeders” and excluded. Main-phase checks used real-time dashboards, automatic bot exclusion at score ≤0.5, automatic exclusion at completion time ≤5 minutes, duplicate screening with multiple identifiers, country-level completion-time summaries, repeated-item percentage agreement, variable/country missingness, numerical outliers, and fieldwork duration. [Methods—Data analysis]

Six pilot survey versions were randomly assigned. Versions varied EQ-HWB short/long forms, three EQ-5D-5L response-heterogeneity vignettes, and mandatory versus non-mandatory questions. Education and self-rated health were initially duplicated. Pilot findings changed the final survey: retain one moderate-health vignette with full EQ-5D-5L descriptive system and EQ VAS, make it gender-neutral, retain questions as non-mandatory without a “prefer not to answer” option, move socio-demographics to the beginning, replace numerical open-text fields with drop-downs/limits, reduce PHQ-9 and GAD-7 to 2-item versions, add hover-over context for sensitive questions, and remove political affiliation. [Methods—Pilot phase; Results—Pilot phase]

## Findings from pilot and 15 countries

- **Pilot:** Of 4538 invitations, 96.6% clicked the link, 75.1% consented, and 66.4% (3012) completed. Eleven records (0.36%) showed possible bot activity and were excluded. Repeated education agreement was 92.7%; repeated 1-item self-rated health was 78.5%, leading to replacement with date of birth and marital status. Average missingness was 0.4% (range 0.0–3.3%), while “prefer not to answer” was selected by 0.2–20.2% across variables. [Results—Pilot phase]
- **Engagement and completion:** Across countries, link-click response ranged 80.1–100%; completion ranged 22.9% (Brazil) to 60.8% (Japan), average 42.4%. The main panel frame contained 175,392 people. [Abstract; Results—Summary of quality control indicators]
- **Time and quotas:** Mean completion time ranged 18.3 minutes (France) to 31.4 (New Zealand), overall average 23 minutes. Average proportional quota achievement ranged 68.7% (New Zealand) to 98.6% (China), with an overall average of 81.8%; absolute quota achievement exceeded 90% in all countries (93.1% Argentina to 99.6% China). Data collection lasted 32 days (Japan) to 58 (Chile), average 43 days. [Results—Summary; Table 1]
- **Bots, speeding, duplicates, consistency:** Bot flags averaged 3.0%, lowest in the UK pilot (0.2%) and highest in China (11.7%). Speeding averaged 0.3%, highest in France (0.9%). Marital-status agreement ranged 92.8% (France) to 98.9% (New Zealand), average 96.3%; age agreement ranged 92.3% (Germany) to 98.7% (US), average 96.9%. No duplicates occurred in 10 countries; three had one each, one had three, and one had 10. [Abstract; Results—Summary; Table 2]
- **Missingness and outliers:** Japan had 0.0–3.8% missingness and Spain 0.0–4.9%; Australia reached 48.7% for height and 48.6% for weight, the Netherlands 13.8% for education and 7.7% for EQ VAS, China 10.1% for household size, and Brazil 10.6% for EQ VAS. EQ VAS was among the three most-missing variables in all countries; the vignette EQ VAS was among the top three in 14/15 and household size in 13/15. Height outliers ranged 0.0–2.2% and weight outliers 0.0–0.8%. [Results—Missing data and outliers; Table 3]

## Interpretation and limitations

The authors report that bot, speeding, duplicate, and missing-data controls produced generally high-quality datasets, but country variability must be considered in norm-setting and comparisons. The Dynata nonprobability (convenience) panel can create selection bias; quotas and poststratification weights reduce but do not remove bias from digital access, health literacy, socioeconomic vulnerability, or response propensity. Researchers should examine missingness, consider complete-case analysis or multiple imputation, test weighting sensitivity, and triangulate with probability-based data. The dataset’s representativeness could not be directly evaluated, and regional internet access and cultural factors may affect engagement. [Discussion; Conclusion]
