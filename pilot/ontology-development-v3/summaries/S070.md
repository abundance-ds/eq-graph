# EQ-5D-5L population norms and health inequality for Trinidad and Tobago in 2022–2023 and comparison with 2012

## Aim and data sources

The study produced updated EQ-5D-5L population norms for Trinidad and Tobago, measured health inequality, and compared 2022–2023 with 2012. Data came from three mutually exclusive adult samples collected from July 2022 through May 2023. The combined sample had 2,989 respondents. Survey 1 was a face-to-face computer-assisted valuation sample (target 1,000; actual *n* = 1,079) selected from randomly selected streets and every fourth house. Survey 2 was an online DCE with Duration sample (actual *n* = 970 after 611 respondents were removed for DCE completion-time quality issues). Survey 3 was an online panel sample of 940 respondents. Survey 2 and 3 participants received a small gift (Abstract; Methods > Data collection).

The target was representative by age, sex, and region using 2011 Central Statistical Office census data. Demographic fields included age group, sex, marital status, ethnicity, region, income, education, employment, private health insurance, and self-employment. The English EQ-5D-5L questionnaire had been linguistically adapted and validated for Trinidad and Tobago.

## Instrument and analysis

EQ-5D-5L has five dimensions in order: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. Each has five levels from no problems to mild, moderate, severe, and extreme problems/unable to. The state 12543 means no mobility problem, mild self-care problems, unable to do usual activities, severe pain/discomfort, and moderate anxiety/depression. There are 3,125 possible states. EQ VAS ranges from 0 (worst imaginable health) to 100 (best imaginable health). A 2024 Trinidad and Tobago 5L value set was used. For 2012 comparisons, 2012 states were revalued with the 2024 value set; the original 2012 analysis had used a crosswalk from 3L values (Methods > Instrument).

Means, EQ VAS scores, ceilings (state 11111), age-sex groups, demographic subgroups, and dimension-level distributions were calculated. Welch’s t-tests and ANOVA tested means. Ordered logit models estimated odds ratios for reporting level 3 or higher. The Kakwani index measured inequality in EQ VAS and index values from 0 (equality) to 1 (extreme inequality). 2012 and 2022–2023 values were compared with t-tests (Methods > Analysis).

## Main population norms

The 2022–2023 mean index was **0.921**, mean EQ VAS was **79.6**, and ceiling was **31.5%**. Pain/discomfort had the highest rate of any reported problem (levels 2–5, 43%), followed by anxiety/depression (39%). The ten most common states made up 75% of the sample. State 11111 occurred in 943 respondents (31.6%), with index value 1.000 and EQ VAS 88.7. States 11121 and 11112 were the next most frequent and represented mild-to-moderate pain/discomfort or anxiety/depression (Results > Dimensions and states; Tables 4–5).

Health was generally better among people with higher income and education, private insurance, employment, and self-employment. Single respondents reported the highest health and separated/divorced respondents the lowest. Afro-ethnicity respondents had higher index values than indo- and mixed/other respondents. Self-employed respondents had index 0.948, EQ VAS 78.7, and ceiling 37.0%, compared with 0.928, 76.1, and 27.0% for people working for others (Results > EQ-5D measures and demographics).

## Inequality and time comparison

The Kakwani index was 0.113 for EQ VAS and 0.058 for index values. For EQ VAS inequality, sex contributed 50.7%, income 27.9%, and age 21.4%. For index inequality, sex contributed 41.9%, age 30.9%, income 26.0%, and education less than 1%. Ordered-logit odds ratios generally showed higher problem reporting among female, lower-education, lower-income, and older groups, with exceptions for anxiety/depression and some age or education comparisons (Results > Inequality; Table 6).

Using the 2022–2023 value set for both periods, 2012 mean index was 0.965 versus 0.921 in 2022–2023. EQ VAS fell from 83.6 to 79.6 and ceiling from 71.6% to 31.5%. Problem reporting increased most for anxiety/depression (+35%), pain/discomfort (+28%), usual activities (+14%), mobility (+8%), and self-care (+2%). The Kakwani EQ VAS index rose from 0.103 in 2012 to 0.113 in 2022–2023. COVID-19 experience, mental-health awareness, and changes in willingness to report problems may have contributed, but these are interpretations, not measured causes (Results > Comparison; Discussion).

## Uses and limitations

The paper recommends periodic national norms updates and use of these values in Trinidad and Tobago clinical and economic applications until a later study is available. Limitations include mixed online and face-to-face modes, panel recruitment and incentives, possible duplication, dichotomised predictors for comparability with 2012, and an aggregated 65+ age group. Inequality measures were limited to EQ-5D-5L outcomes (Discussion; Conclusion).
