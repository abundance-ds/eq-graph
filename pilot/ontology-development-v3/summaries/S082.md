## Aim and data

The study assessed psychometric performance and construct validity of the experimental EQ Health and Wellbeing Short (EQ-HWB-S), using EQ-5D-5L as comparator. Data came from two UK general-population valuation datasets: the Extending the Quality-Adjusted Life-Year (E-QALY) valuation stage (429 respondents with both measures; collected May–November 2021) and the EQ-5D-5L UK valuation pilot (248 respondents; October 2022–February 2023). The combined sample was 677, with 59% female, mean age 47.02 (SD 15.85), 83% White, 59% employed, and 36.69% reporting a long-term condition (Abstract; Methods—Data).

## Instruments and analyses

EQ-HWB-S experimental version 1.0 has nine items: mobility, activities, exhaustion, loneliness, cognition, anxiety, sadness/depression, control, and physical pain. It asks about the past seven days and its UK feasibility-study utility range was −0.384 to 1. EQ-5D-5L asks about today using mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, plus EQ VAS 0–100. EQ-5D-5L utilities used NICE-recommended mapping to the UK EQ-5D-3L value set, producing a mapped range −0.255 to 0.960. The study also reported Devlin scores and an alternative value-set sensitivity analysis (Methods—Measures).

Ceiling was defined as >70% at the “no problems” level; floor used <5% at the worst level. Convergent validity used Spearman correlations for dimensions and Pearson correlations for utilities; correlations ≥0.5 were strong, 0.3–<0.5 moderate, and <0.3 weak. Known-group validity used t tests or ANOVA and Cohen’s *d* for healthy versus unhealthy (EQ VAS ≥80), long-term condition, above/below average life or health satisfaction, carer status, age, and employment. Agreement used Bland–Altman plots and Lin’s concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) (Methods—Statistical analysis).

## Findings

At instrument level, 9.45% reported EQ-HWB-S 111111111, compared with 36.04% reporting EQ-5D-5L 11111. No one reported 555555555 or 55555. EQ-HWB-S mobility had a ceiling of 83.01%; activities was 68.24%, exhaustion 31.46%, loneliness 61.30%, cognition 42.54%, anxiety 42.54%, sadness/depression 55.10%, control 63.66%, and pain 46.97%. EQ-5D-5L ceiling was 78.58% mobility, 90.99% self-care, 69.28% usual activity, 54.80% pain/discomfort, and 58.20% anxiety/depression. Few respondents (<5%) selected the worst level; no worst-level responses occurred for EQ-HWB-S mobility or EQ-5D-5L self-care (Results—Responses; Table 2).

Utility convergence was strong: the EQ-HWB-S and EQ-5D-5L utility correlation was 0.852. Overlapping dimensions showed strong correlations, including EQ-HWB-S mobility with EQ-5D mobility (0.764), activities with usual activities (0.760), pain with pain/discomfort (0.775), anxious with EQ-5D anxiety/depression (0.685), and sadness/depression with EQ-5D anxiety/depression (0.660). Non-overlapping wellbeing dimensions were usually weakly related to EQ-5D dimensions, especially in healthy and above-average health-satisfaction groups. Bland–Altman differences ranged −0.61 to 0.31, mean absolute difference 0.17; Lin CCC was 0.848, interpreted as excellent concordance (Results—Convergent validity; Table 3; Figure 2).

Both instruments discriminated known groups comparably. EQ-HWB-S effect sizes were large for unhealthy versus healthy (Cohen’s *d* −1.047), long-term condition (1.169), below versus above average life satisfaction (−1.017), and below versus above average health satisfaction (−0.883). EQ-5D-5L values had similar effects: −1.069, 1.330, −0.763, and −0.942. Employment differences were large for both; carer differences were small (0.428 EQ-HWB-S; 0.426 EQ-5D-5L). Age effects were small or absent for EQ-5D-5L and small for EQ-HWB-S (Results—Known-group validity; Table 4).

## Interpretation and limits

EQ-HWB-S showed favourable known-group validity, strong convergence in overlapping concepts, and additional coverage of loneliness, cognition, and control. People reporting EQ-5D-5L 11111 could still report problems in these wellbeing domains. The study used an experimental version and a feasibility value set; a definitive EQ-HWB-S value set was not yet available. Datasets differed in instrument order, mode, and long-term-condition wording, and data were collected partly during COVID-19. The UK EQ-5D-5L mapped value set may be replaced, so HTA implications need reassessment (Discussion; Conclusions).
