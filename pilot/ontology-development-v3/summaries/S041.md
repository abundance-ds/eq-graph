## Aim, design, and population

The study assessed patient-perceived feasibility of routinely collecting real-world EQ-5D-3L during chemotherapy, as a first step toward province-wide collection in Ontario (Abstract; Introduction). Adults (18+) starting publicly reimbursed systemic therapy for histologically confirmed solid or hematological malignancy were recruited prospectively at Sunnybrook Odette Cancer Centre, Toronto. Accrual ran May–November 2024 and follow-up to February 2025. The convenience sample included 170 eligible, consenting patients (Methods, Section 2.1).

## Instrument, administration, and outcomes

EQ-5D-3L was the primary measure: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each with no problems, some problems, or extreme problems/unable to perform activities. EQ-VAS recorded self-rated health from 0 (worst imaginable) to 100 (best imaginable). English EQ-5D-3L and EQ-VAS were administered on REDCap or paper. EQ-5D-3L proxy version 1 in English was available for a caregiver proxy who rated the patient according to the proxy’s opinion (Methods, Sections 2.2–2.3). The academic license came from the EuroQol Foundation.

Feasibility outcomes were completion of at least one questionnaire and willingness to complete future questionnaires. Nine consenting patients completed two-on-one Zoom interviews. Interview topics were overall experience, presentation/layout, frequency, and intended analysis. Transcripts were verbatim and analyzed by content analysis using an emerging codebook (Methods, Section 2.4).

## Results

Of 170 participants, 103 (60.6%) completed at least one follow-up; 115 (71.9%) definitely and 35 (21.9%) very likely agreed to future completion. The abstract reports 115 (67.3%) definitely and 35 (20.5%) very likely when denominated among eligible/consenting respondents; both figures are source-reported. Most initial respondents found instructions/questions easy to understand (80.0% strongly agree), completion easy (83.1% strongly agree), questions acceptable (82.5% strongly agree), and length acceptable (82.5% strongly agree) (Results, Section 3.2, Fig. 3). Nine patients (7 female, 2 male) were interviewed.

Patients described the questionnaire as “straightforward” and quick, but some considered the five questions too limited to capture treatment-related pain, discomfort, stress, anxiety, and the broader spectrum of cancer HRQoL. Suggested additions included optional free text. Paper and electronic formats each had supporters: paper aided reading and comprehension, while electronic completion offered flexibility and use of a personal phone. All patients were generally satisfied with the layout; some liked three response options, while one suggested five levels (Results, Section 3.3–3.4, Table 2).

Chemotherapy-unit administration was convenient for some patients with time available, but timing could be affected by concurrent treatment, antihistamines, allergic reactions, and moment-to-moment changes. Suggested timing included before chemotherapy, two weeks after, or at treatment end. Frequency suggestions ranged from every visit or every 2–3 weeks to beginning/middle/end of systemic therapy. Patients also asked that analysis consider age, institution, mental health, weather/traffic, and treatment context (Results, Sections 3.4.1–3.4.3).

## Interpretation and limitations

The authors conclude routine EQ-5D-3L collection was feasible at this pilot centre and could support micro-level clinical decisions, meso-level economic analysis, and macro-level system planning. Existing infrastructure such as the “Your Symptoms Matter platform” may support scale-up. Limitations include one urban oncology centre, coordinator burden, missed patients with short appointments or limited English, timing-related response variation, and EQ-5D-3L’s restricted content and ceiling effects. The paper notes EQ-5D-5L may offer better performance and sensitivity for broader implementation, while EQ-5D-3L preserves comparability with historic trials and HTA evidence (Discussion; Conclusions).
