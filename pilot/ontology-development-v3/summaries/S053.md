# Validity of EQ-5D-5L breathing and cognition bolt-ons in non-hospitalized patients after COVID-19

## Aim and cohort

The study evaluated EQ-5D-5L breathing problems and cognition bolt-ons about 30 months after acute COVID-19, compared them with disease-specific instruments and earlier objective tests, and assessed their contribution to EQ VAS variability. It used a geographical Norwegian cohort from Akershus University Hospital and Østfold Hospital catchment areas. Adults with positive SARS-CoV-2 PCR before 1 June 2020 were eligible if not admitted within 22 days; exclusions included residence outside the catchment area, no valid national identity number, nursing-home residence, or dementia. The source cohort comprised 450 non-hospitalized patients; 938 eligible subjects were invited to the survey and 458 responded. The 4th-round November/December 2022 sample contained 220 respondents (Methods; Results).

The 220 respondents had mean age 53.2 years (SD 14.3), 60% female, 92% Norwegian origin, median one comorbidity, and median nine acute-COVID symptoms. Fifty-seven percent reported acute dyspnea and 15% confusion. Pulmonary function testing (n=280) and tablet cognitive testing (n=233) were available from the 3rd round, 8–13 months after infection; the current survey was 959 days after PCR, median (25th–75th percentile) 949–968.5 days (Results, Table 1).

## Measures and analysis

The questionnaire used EQ-5D-5L mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression plus a breathing bolt-on (beta version adapted into Norwegian) and cognition bolt-on (EQ-5D-5L + Cognition, adapted from English and harmonized with Swedish). Comparators were modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea scale, Dyspnoea-12 (D-12; physical, emotional, total), DePaul Symptom Questionnaire short form (DSQ-SF) memory/attention frequency and severity items, Chalder Fatigue Questionnaire (CFQ-11) cognitive items and mental-fatigue subscale, acute-COVID symptoms, spirometry/TLCO, and Cambridge Neuropsychological Test Automated Battery (CANTAB) tests: DMS, OTS, RVP, and SWM (Methods, “Other self-rated instruments/items” and “Objective tests”).

Descriptive statistics, missingness, ceiling effects, and Spearman rank correlations tested construct validity. Hypotheses expected ≥0.60 with same-construct self-report measures, 0.20–0.50 with clinical/objective measures, and higher correlations than core EQ-5D-5L dimensions. Multiple linear regression used EQ VAS as dependent variable to test added explanatory power (Methods, “Statistical analysis”).

## Results and interpretation

Breathing responses were 54% no problem, 40% slight, 6% moderate, <1% severe, and none extreme. mMRC had 66% class 0 and 26% class 1; 6% reported moderate-to-extreme bolt-on problems versus 8% mMRC ≥2 (Results, “Questionnaire and objective test results”). Breathing correlated 0.539 with mMRC and 0.611–0.736 with D-12 scales, higher than core-dimension correlations (0.166–0.479). Cognition correlated 0.681–0.743 with DSQ-SF neurocognitive items, 0.375–0.719 with CFQ-11 items, and 0.746 with mental fatigue, higher than core-dimension correlations (0.189–0.489) (Results, “Assessment of construct validity”).

Five core dimensions explained 54.2% of EQ VAS variance (adjusted R² 0.542). Adding breathing raised adjusted R² to 0.565; cognition alone raised it to 0.543 and did not add to the five-item-plus-breathing model. The five-item ceiling was 21.4%; adding breathing reduced it to 15.9%, cognition to 14.1%, and both to 10.9% (Table 7). The authors support validity and further use of both bolt-ons, while noting the cognition contribution to EQ VAS was small. Limitations include few severe symptoms, community rather than hospital sample, objective tests at an earlier time, and EQ VAS as a limited criterion (Discussion; Conclusion).
