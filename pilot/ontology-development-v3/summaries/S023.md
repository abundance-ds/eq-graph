## Aim, setting, and sample

The study developed the first Danish EQ-5D-5L value set for hospital-medicine priority setting and QALYs. It used EQ-VT 2.1 computer-assisted personal interviews with a nationally representative adult sample selected using Statistics Denmark data on age, gender, education, and region (Abstract; Introduction). Target sample size was 1,200 to obtain at least 1,000 high-quality interviews. Between October 2018 and November 2019, 1,052 interviews were conducted; after software, withdrawal, availability, cognitive/emotional, and incomplete cTTO/DCE exclusions, 1,014 remained. The sample was broadly representative but underrepresented ages 18–24 and the lowest education level; 64% reported very good/excellent health and mean EQ VAS was 82.4 (Results, “The Sample”).

## Instrument and tasks

Each respondent completed self-reported EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS, background questions, cTTO instructions and three practice states, ten cTTO states, cTTO feedback/debriefing, seven DCE pairs, DCE debriefing, and additional prioritisation questions. Conventional TTO compared 10 years in a state with variable full health; lead-time TTO addressed worse-than-dead states and values −1 to 0. DCE asked respondents to choose between two non-dominant EQ-5D-5L states without duration (Methods, “The Valuation Interview”).

Each cTTO block contained one of five mild states, eight moderate states, and 55555; the design had 86 states in total. DCE used 196 pairs in 28 blocks of seven, balanced by level-sum score, with randomized order and left/right position. Interviewers had master’s degrees, 2.5 days’ intensive training, and fortnightly QC reports. An interview was flagged for missing worse-than-dead explanation, wheelchair example under 3 minutes, ten cTTO tasks under 5 minutes, or 55555 inconsistency. Four or more flags in an interviewer’s first ten interviews triggered retraining and dropping those interviews (Methods, “Data Quality”).

## Analysis and model selection

Values for all 3,125 states were modelled from cTTO alone, DCE alone, and combined hybrid data. cTTO models were GLS random-intercept and random-effects Tobit; Tobit accounted for censoring at −1. DCE used conditional logit, including a heteroscedastic robustness model. Hybrid models combined GLS or random-effects Tobit with conditional logit and a multiplicative scale parameter; heterogeneity was represented through scale. No interactions were investigated. Model performance focused on logical consistency and goodness of fit; AIC/BIC and MAE were not considered suitable for the hybrid (Methods, “Statistical Analyses”).

## Findings and final value set

All 1,014 respondents completed ten cTTO tasks (10,140 observations); cTTO means fell and SDs rose with severity. Observed values ranged 1 to −1, and 22% of states were worse than dead. Removing 712 feedback-identified misranked observations left 9,428 cTTO observations. Standalone Tobit had inconsistencies in mobility level 3 and insignificant self-care/usual-activities increments; DCE had inconsistencies in self-care level 3 and usual activities level 3. Combining data in a heteroscedastic censored hybrid removed logical inconsistencies, although some adjacent increments were not statistically different (Results, “cTTO and DCE Hybrid Models”).

The final heteroscedastic censored hybrid model gives all 3,125 values; 55555 = −0.757 and 11111 = 1. An example state 13224 has `U = 1 − 0 − 0.050 − 0.033 − 0.048 − 0.430 = 0.439`. Dimension ranking was anxiety/depression, pain/discomfort, mobility, self-care, usual activities. The level-5 decrements in the preferred model were MO 0.220, SC 0.209, UA 0.174, PD 0.537, AD 0.618 (Table 2). Reintroducing feedback-flagged data did not materially change results.

Compared with Danish 3L and crosswalk sets, the 5L had a lower 55555 value and about 22% of states worse than death versus 20% for 3L and 11% for the crosswalk. The 5L dimension ranking differs from the 3L ranking, with anxiety/depression replacing mobility as largest decrement. The paper discusses changes in mental-health salience, mobility wording, rehabilitation, and historical preferences. The paper does not report the EQ-5D-5L interview language; it reports a Danish translation of training material.

## Implications and limitations

The value set is recommended for Danish QALYs and health-care decision-making. Strengths include Statistics Denmark recruitment and rigorous EQ-VT/QC. Limitations include switching partly to a market-research panel, underrepresentation of low education/young adults, long interviews, no direct payment, and unresolved theoretical questions about combining TTO and DCE.
