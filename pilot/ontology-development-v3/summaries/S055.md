# The added value of the cognition, dining, gastrointestinal problems, sleep and tiredness bolt-on dimensions to the EQ-5D-5L in patients with coeliac disease

## Aim, setting, and measures

The study developed and tested five EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons for adults with coeliac disease (CD): two new condition-specific items, dining (DI) and gastrointestinal problems (GI), and existing cognition (CO), sleep (SL), and tiredness (TI). The survey included 312 Hungarian adult CD patients recruited through patient organisations and social-media groups in December 2020–January 2021. Participation was voluntary and anonymous; all participants followed a gluten-free diet (GFD), and no responses were missing (Methods, “Cross-sectional survey”).

The EQ-5D-5L dimensions were mobility (MO), self-care (SC), usual activities (UA), pain/discomfort (PD), and anxiety/depression (AD), with five severity levels. EQ VAS ranged 0–100. The study used unweighted LSS rather than value-set utilities. Bolt-ons were completed after EQ VAS in order DI, GI, CO, TI, SL. GI examples were diarrhoea, constipation, nausea, vomiting, heartburn, bloating, and gases; DI referred to following a diet and eating out (Methods, “Development and selection” and “Outcome measures”).

The development panel included a CD patient, gastroenterologist professor, and two health economists. Selection drew on literature, condition-specific measures, expert input, and a conceptual model with gastrointestinal and extra-intestinal symptoms and six HRQOL areas. Social relationships was excluded because it could overlap with DI and UA.

## Analysis

The study assessed response distributions, ceiling, Shannon absolute and relative informativity, convergent/divergent validity, known-group validity, explanatory power, and dimensionality. Spearman correlations tested hypotheses against the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS), EQ-5D dimensions, EQ VAS, and Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS). Known groups used health-status categories, GSRS tertiles, and current symptoms; relative efficiency was the ANOVA F-statistic ratio with EQ-5D-5L as reference and 3,000 bootstrap replications. Linear regressions used EQ VAS or SWLS as outcomes, with incremental bolt-on selection. Principal component analysis and confirmatory factor analysis used promax rotation and DWLS for ordinal data (Methods, “Analyses of psychometric properties”).

## Findings

The EQ-5D-5L ceiling was 38.8%. Adding TI, SL, GI, DI, or CO reduced it to 17.0%, 23.1%, 24.0%, 26.3%, and 36.5%, respectively; all five reduced it to 7.4%. Individual bolt-on ceilings were TI 22.8%, GI 39.1%, SL 44.6%, DI 52.9%, and CO 74.0% (Results, “Distributional characteristics”). GI correlated strongly with GSRS total (r_s=0.712) and moderately with GSRS domains (0.419–0.584); GI–PD was r_s=0.508. TI correlated with PD (0.465) and AD (0.425). CO, DI, and SL had weak or very weak correlations with core dimensions (Results, “Convergent and divergent validity”).

GI significantly improved known-group discrimination for self-rated health, GSRS tertiles, and symptoms, with relative efficiencies 1.30–1.84; no further bolt-on improved known-group validity after GI. EQ VAS adjusted R² increased from 0.411 to 0.440 with GI and CO; SWLS adjusted R² increased from 0.193 to 0.206 with DI, GI, and SL. CFA showed GI as the only bolt-on loading on a different factor from the five core dimensions; DI, CO, SL, and TI loaded with AD on “psychosocial health” (Results, “Known-group validity,” “Exploratory power,” and “Dimensionality”).

The authors conclude that DI, GI, SL, and TI improve measurement performance, especially GI. Limitations include self-reported diagnosis, convenience online sampling, all participants on GFD, cross-sectional design, COVID-19 timing, no test–retest or responsiveness, and unfinalized new-item wording. Utility and QALY effects remain to be tested (Discussion; Conclusion).
