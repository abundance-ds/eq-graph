# EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L proxy report: psychometric performance and agreement with self-report

## Aim, design, and sample

The study asked whether extending the EQ-5D-Y proxy classification from 3 to 5 response levels improves feasibility, distribution, convergent validity, test–retest reliability, responsiveness, and agreement with child self-report. It used an Indonesian, cross-sectional repeated-measures design with baseline, test–retest, and follow-up administrations. Data were collected 6 March–30 June 2019 in five hospitals in Jakarta and Bandung. Participants were 286 children aged 8–16 years and caregivers or other people who knew the child well. Conditions were beta thalassemia major (68; 23.7%), severe or intermediate hemophilia (54; 18.8%), acute lymphoblastic leukemia (40; 14.0%), and acute illness (124; 43.5%). Proxies were mainly mothers (228; 79.4%), followed by fathers (41; 14.3%), siblings (5; 1.7%), and other relatives (12; 4.2%). [Abstract; Methods—Participants and setting; Results—Table 1]

Children and proxies completed questionnaires independently, with two interviewers present to reduce interference. Inpatients completed them at bedside and outpatients while waiting for an appointment. Baseline was at the first hospital visit; test–retest was timed when health was expected to be stable, and follow-up followed treatment at a disease-appropriate interval. The same questionnaire was used at all occasions. At test–retest and follow-up, a seven-option proxy change question (much worse through much better) classified deterioration, stability, or improvement. [Methods—Procedure and Data collected]

## Instruments and analysis

The two instruments were the Indonesian translations of EQ-5D-Y-3L and an experimental EQ-5D-Y-5L proxy version 1 (in-progress UK English source versions; no official EQ-5D-Y-5L existed). Both had five dimensions: mobility (walking about; MOB), looking after myself (LAM), doing usual activities (UA), having pain or discomfort (P/D), and feeling worried, sad, or unhappy (WSU), followed by EQ-VAS (0–100). The 3L response labels were “no problems”, “some problems”, and “a lot of problems”. The 5L labels were “no problems”, “a little bit of a problem”, “some problems”, “a lot of problems”, and “cannot/extreme problems”. Proxy version 1 asks how the proxy would rate the child’s health; version 2, not used here, asks how the proxy thinks the child would rate their own health. The 5L was administered first to reduce avoidance of intermediate levels. In Indonesian, semantic correspondences were 1(L3)-1(L5), 2(L3)-2(L5), and 3(L3)-4(L5); valid redistribution pathways also included 1(L3)-2(L5), 2(L3)-3(L5), 3(L3)-3(L5), and 3(L3)-5(L5). [Methods—Health questionnaire instruments; Statistical analysis—Re-distribution patterns]

Reference instruments were proxy PedsQL Generic Core Scale (23 items; physical, emotional, social, school; physical, psychosocial, and total scores), PedsQL Cancer Module (27 items; eight domains), TranQol (thalassemia; four domains), and short Haemo-Qol (35 items; eight dimensions). The instruments used Bahasa Indonesia; TranQol used a local translation with cognitive debriefing. [Methods—Health questionnaire instruments]

Feasibility used missing responses. Ceiling effect was the proportion of 11111 (“no problems” in all dimensions), compared by McNemar test. Redistribution assessed whether 3L answers followed the semantically logical 5L pathways; responses differing by two or more levels were inconsistent, with an “average inconsistency weight” of 1–3. Convergent validity used Spearman rank correlations and expected domain relationships; correlation interpretation was absent (<0.20), weak (0.20–<0.35), moderate (0.35–<0.50), and strong (>0.50). Test–retest used Gwet’s agreement coefficient (AC) among proxies reporting no change; Landis & Koch categories were applied. Responsiveness compared lower, higher, or equal dimension levels against the change question. Proxy/self-report agreement also used Gwet’s AC. Analyses used IBM SPSS Statistics 28 and STATA 16; no EQ-5D-Y-5L utility comparison was possible because a value set was not available. [Methods—Statistical analysis]

## Findings and interpretation

- **Feasibility and ceiling:** Missingness was 0–4 (up to 1.4%) for 5L dimensions and 0–3 (about 1%) for 3L dimensions. At baseline, full-health proxy profile 11111 was 21.3% for 3L and 16.7% for 5L (*p*=0.03); at follow-up it was 63.2% and 61.8% (*p*=0.68). The authors stress that a large ceiling can be consistent with the health-care purpose of EQ-5D and is not automatically “bluntness” or insensitivity. [Results—Feasibility and Ceiling effect; Table 2; Discussion]
- **Redistribution:** Semantically consistent responses dominated. Across dimensions, 73.6–95.0% of 3L level-1 responses were also 5L level 1. Pain/discomfort showed a notable 1(3L) to 2(5L) shift (26.4%), and 2(3L) to 3(5L) shift (66%). Larger redistribution occurred for LAM and UA, with at least 64% of 3L level-3 responses distributed to 5L level 5. Inconsistent redistribution ranged 5.9% (MOB) to 15.7% (UA); average inconsistency weight was lowest for P/D (0.79) and highest for WSU (1.19). [Results—Redistribution; Table 3]
- **Convergent validity:** Correlations with PedsQL Generic Core ranged weak to moderate, with no consistent superiority of 5L; MOB did not relate to any PedsQL Generic Core dimension. Disease-specific measures produced higher, expected correlations, from weak to strong, but again no clear 5L superiority. [Results—Convergent validity; Tables 4–5]
- **Test–retest and responsiveness:** Only 59 of 247 possible pairs (23.9%) met the “no change” criterion. Percentage agreement was 83.0–95.5%. Mean Gwet’s AC was 0.83 for 3L and 0.84 for 5L, showing comparable reliability. At follow-up, 91.4% of 222 proxies reported improvement; 4.1% reported no change and 4.5% deterioration. Except for self-care, 5L detected positive changes more often than 3L; self-care was 33.3% versus 33.8%. [Results—Test–retest and Responsiveness; Table 6; Fig. 2]
- **Proxy/self-report agreement:** Except in acutely ill children, agreement was mainly “substantial” or “almost perfect”. At baseline, 5L proxy agreement was higher than 3L; after treatment, both versions were generally high and comparable. Acute illness and unstable health may reduce a proxy’s ability to assess current health precisely. [Results—Agreement; Table 7; Discussion]

The authors conclude that 5L proxy reporting improves classification accuracy, sensitivity to change, and baseline proxy/self-report agreement, with little disadvantage. The main limitation is the small stable test–retest subset, which limits generalisability. Additional limitations include Indonesian translation-specific level labels, experimental 5L status, and the absence of a 5L value set. [Discussion; Conclusions]
