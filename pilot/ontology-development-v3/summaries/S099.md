## Aim and design

The study measures HRQoL from conception through postpartum using monthly EQ-5D-5L profiles, US utility values and EQ VAS. It tests whether reported problems and values change by pregnancy month and postpartum month, and examines differences for pre-existing conditions and severe anxiety/depression. The authors conducted repeated cross-sectional national online surveys, not longitudinal follow-up of the same individuals (Abstract; Methods—Study design).

Wave 1 ran May–July 2021 and wave 2 December 2021–April 2022. Social-media intercept recruitment used Facebook, Instagram and Twitter. Eligibility was age 18–49, residence in the US or a US territory, a pregnancy ending after March 2020, and survey completion during pregnancy or within nine months after delivery. The English/Spanish survey was a 30-minute online survey; it could be completed over multiple sessions but submitted once. Of 12,733 invitations, 6,661 completed; 9 did not complete EQ-5D-5L items and 1,402 lacked pregnancy-timing information or were >9 months postpartum. The final analytic sample was 5,250 (3,618 pregnant and 1,632 postpartum). Representation covered all US states and two territories, but Black respondents were underrepresented (Methods; Results).

## Instruments and timing variables

EQ-5D-5L has mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, with five responses: no, slight, moderate, severe and extreme problems. A US adult value set produced utility values. EQ VAS asked overall health “today” from 0 (worst health imagined) to 100 (best health imagined). The survey also included PHQ-4, GAD-7, sociodemographic and diagnosed-condition items, including asthma, diabetes, coronary heart disease, hypertension and depression.

For pregnant respondents, month of pregnancy was estimated from survey date and self-reported last menstrual period (LMP), or expected delivery date if LMP was missing. For postpartum respondents, postpartum month used survey date minus pregnancy-end/delivery date. Ceiling was the 11111 state or EQ VAS 100; floor was 55555 or EQ VAS 0 (Methods—Data collection and Statistical analysis).

## Analyses

The authors estimated monthly proportions reporting any problem in each dimension and distributions of utility and EQ VAS. Quantile regression modelled utility and EQ VAS by pregnancy/postpartum month using cubic splines, adjusted for calendar month of LMP. Analyses were repeated by survey wave and by quarter/year of conception to assess COVID-19 calendar-time effects. Subgroups included at least one self-reported pre-existing medical condition and severe anxiety/depression by PHQ-4 or GAD-7 (Methods—Statistical Analysis).

## Findings

Anxiety/depression was the most commonly reported problem overall (60.6%), followed by pain/discomfort (59.9%); self-care was least common (11.6%). Anxiety/depression remained above 50% and fairly stable through pregnancy and postpartum. Mobility, self-care, usual activities and pain/discomfort increased through the ninth month, then declined in the first postpartum month and returned to a first-trimester-like level by month nine postpartum. Median utility was lowest in month nine (0.78, IQR 0.30) and highest in month nine postpartum (0.94, IQR 0.07). Utility declined during pregnancy (β=−0.21, SE 0.02, p<0.001), increased 0.10 in the first postpartum month (SE 0.02, p<0.001), and stabilised thereafter (β=0.02, SE 0.02, p=0.214).

EQ VAS showed little variability by pregnancy or postpartum month: pregnancy β=−1.1 (SE 2.1, p=0.59), first postpartum month β=0.02 (SE 0.02, p=0.54), and later postpartum β=1.1 (SE 1.4, p=0.41). EQ-5D-5L ceiling was 17.0% in the first pregnancy month and 22.1% in the first postpartum month; no EQ-5D-5L floor effects were observed. There was no consistent EQ VAS ceiling/floor pattern. Respondents with pre-existing conditions generally reported more item problems, but utility and EQ VAS were similar. Increasing PHQ-4/GAD-7 anxiety/depression severity showed a dose-response increase in item problems and decrease in utility and EQ VAS (Results—Figures 1–2; Tables 2–3).

## Interpretation and limitations

The progressive decline and rapid first-month postpartum recovery were mainly driven by pain/discomfort and usual activities, with additional mobility and self-care changes; anxiety/depression did not drive the monthly trajectory. The authors recommend accounting for stage of pregnancy in HRQoL estimates. The cross-sectional design cannot estimate within-person trajectories, responsiveness or test-retest reliability. Self-report and web recruitment can cause recall and selection bias; minority groups were underrepresented. Calendar time and COVID-19 effects are difficult to separate from gestational timing, although the pattern was broadly consistent across waves and prior pre-pandemic evidence. Data are not publicly available because of privacy and ethical restrictions. Funding included EuroQol Research Foundation grants 260-2020-RA and 384-2021-RA; authors reported no conflict of interest (Discussion; Strengths and Limitations; Declarations).
