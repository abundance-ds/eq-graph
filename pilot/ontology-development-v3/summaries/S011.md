## Research question and design

The study tested whether DCE-derived health-state values depend on how well selected states cover the severity range. It compared current Bayesian efficient (“unstratified”) designs with “severity-stratified” designs in three formats: standard DCE, DCE-death and DCE-duration. About 3000 Dutch respondents were targeted, 500 per arm, and randomly assigned to six arms and one of eight sub-designs. Fieldwork was an online SSI survey in December 2015. Of 3815 respondents, 693 who did not complete the choice tasks were excluded, leaving 3122; characteristics were broadly comparable with the Dutch population (Methods; Results).

EQ-5D-5L states used mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, each levels 1–5. Standard DCE compared 10 years in state A versus 10 years in B on a latent scale. DCE-death compared A versus B and then B versus immediate death. DCE-duration compared A versus B and then B versus full health 11111 at a shorter duration (2, 4 or 6 months and 1–9 years). A–B pairs had at least two overlapping dimensions, implausible combinations were avoided and colour coding reduced task burden (DCE Choice Tasks).

Each arm had 168 choice tasks over eight sub-designs, with 21 tasks per sub-design. Bayesian D-error was optimised with a Latin hypercube and greedy algorithm. Unstratified designs used all 3125 states minus 225 implausible states. Severity-stratified designs divided states into 21 strata, removed the 225 implausible states and forced one choice task from each stratum in each sub-design. Prior values came from 350 Dutch respondents per DCE format. The design was implemented in Julia (Experimental Designs).

## Analysis

Mixed-logit models used 20 EQ-5D-5L main-effect dummies, with level 1 as reference. DCE-death values were rescaled by the death coefficient; DCE-duration values were rescaled by the duration coefficient. Mean errors (MEs) compared predicted and observed choice probabilities, including cross-validation between unstratified and stratified arms. State selection and value distributions were compared across designs; DCE-death and DCE-duration values used the QALY scale, while standard DCE remained latent (Statistical Analysis).

## Results and interpretation

Severity stratification changed state selection strongly for DCE-duration. The unstratified DCE-duration design over-sampled mild states: included-state mean and SD were 0.31 and 0.44 versus 0.09 and 0.35 for the stratified design. For all 3125 states, 56.0% were worse than death under the unstratified design versus 42.8% under the stratified design. Standard DCE and DCE-death showed little value difference between designs (Results; Fig. 2–3).

For DCE-duration, the unstratified design underestimated choice probability for bad states, overestimated willingness to trade years to avoid them and produced too-low values. Bad-state out-of-sample mean error on B–C tasks was −0.0559 for unstratified versus 0.0028 for stratified. The authors attribute the bias to extrapolation from a pro-mild state sample and model misspecification, amplified because full health is fixed and death is not included. The stratified design improved predictive performance. Severity stratification had limited effect for standard DCE and DCE-death (Results; Discussion).

The paper concludes that severity stratification is a validity requirement for DCE-duration designs. Limitations include focus on matched pairwise formats, fixed full-health comparator, a 10-year impaired-state duration, lack of comparison with non-efficient designs, uncertain generalisation beyond EQ-5D-5L, unbounded normal parameter distributions and possible differences in how respondents treat months versus equivalent years. The authors caution against using DCE-derived values when the design does not span severity adequately (Discussion; Conclusion).
