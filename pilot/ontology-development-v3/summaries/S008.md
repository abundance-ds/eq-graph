## Aim, sample and protocol

The study aimed to develop a Moroccan EQ-5D-5L value set for QALYs and HTA. A stratified quota sample targeted sex, age category and place of residence using 2014 census data, with geographical coverage. Eligible adults were at least 18 years old and able to understand Moroccan dialect or French. Computer-assisted face-to-face interviews ran November 2022–December 2023. Of 1048 interviewed, 976 were included after 42 incomplete and 30 poor-quality interviews were removed. The final sample was representative for age, sex and residence but under-represented illiterate people; mean age was 41.6 years. Mean EQ VAS was 77.25 (SD 17.05) and 42.54% reported 11111 (Study Design; Participants’ Characteristics).

The study used EQ-VT version 2.6.1 in Moroccan Arabic or French. EQ-5D-5L dimensions were mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, each with levels no, slight, moderate, severe and extreme/unable. cTTO included 86 states in ten blocks, with five mild states repeated, 55555 in all blocks and 80 unique states from Monte Carlo simulation. Each interview included wheelchair examples, three practice states (21121, 15411 and 35554), ten real cTTO tasks, feedback and seven DCE pairs. DCE used 196 states in 28 blocks (Valuation Techniques; Interview Procedure).

## QC and analysis

Twelve interviewers received two-day training; nine completed data collection. The EQ-VT Support Team reviewed QC after each interviewer’s first ten interviews and regularly thereafter. A study was flagged for less than three minutes explaining wheelchair examples, no WTD explanation, a 55555 inconsistency, or less than five minutes for ten cTTO tasks. The exclusions were 3 for short explanation, 2 for no WTD explanation, 5 for 55555 inconsistency, 17 for short cTTO time and 3 for both criteria. Feedback reduced incorrectly ranked responses from 28.3% to 20.9% (Quality Control; Results).

Model 1 was heteroskedastic censored Tobit for cTTO; Model 2 was conditional logit for DCE; Model 3 was a hybrid combining both likelihoods. cTTO was left-censored at −1 because respondents could not trade more than the lead time. Each model used 20 main-effect dummy variables. Heteroskedasticity allowed error variance to vary with health-state attributes. Selection considered logical consistency, statistical significance, parsimony, AIC and BIC. STATA 18 was used (Statistical Analysis).

Observed cTTO values were 62% positive and 38% negative; 8.6% were −1 and 8.4% +1. Means ranged from 0.970 for 11211 to −0.940 for 55555. Arabic and French cTTO distributions did not differ (p=0.921). All models were logically consistent. Pain/discomfort and usual activities were the most and least important dimensions, respectively, in the tested models (Data Characteristics; Modelling Results).

## Preferred value set and interpretation

The preferred product was the hybrid heteroskedastic Tobit model. Predicted utilities range from −1.492 for 55555 to 1 for 11111; 11211 and 21111 are each 0.979. Dimension order is pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression, mobility, self-care and usual activities. The authors compare the result with the Moroccan EQ-5D-3L value set, whose 33333 is −0.5736; the EQ-5D-5L 55555 is lower at −1.492. They attribute differences in dimension order partly to the additional levels and the different most-severe mobility wording.

The value set is intended for Moroccan health economic evaluations, HTA, routine PROM use and international comparison. Strengths are national sampling, EQ-VT standardisation, hybrid use of cTTO and DCE and frequent QC. Limitations are low representation of rural, illiterate and low-literacy participants, difficulties applying an EQ-VT designed for literate populations, and possible language/cultural effects. The authors state Morocco is the third MENA country with an EQ-5D-5L value set (Discussion; Conclusions).
