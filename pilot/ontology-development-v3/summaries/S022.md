## Aim, design, and sample

The study developed the first Egyptian EQ-5D-5L value set using EQ-VT 2.1, after a previous Egyptian value set was retracted for model inconsistency (Abstract; Introduction). It was a cross-sectional, interviewer-administered, face-to-face survey with multi-stratified quotas for age, sex, and geographical distribution across Egypt’s seven regional units and 27 governorates. Twelve Clinical Pharmacy teaching assistants conducted interviews; participants were recruited from universities, authorities, clubs, shops, workplaces, homes, and public places. No incentives were paid.

From July 2019 to March 2020, 1,378 interviews were conducted: 216 pilot, 75 incomplete, and 113 removed for poor protocol compliance, leaving 974 final interviews. COVID-19 interrupted recruitment before the planned 1,000 final interviews. Mean age was 36.9 years and 52.4% were male. Rural, illiterate, and age 65+ people were underrepresented, although geography and broad diversity were retained (Methods; Results, “Data Cleaning” and “Participants’ Characteristics”).

## Instruments, adaptations, and QC

EQ-5D-5L includes mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression at levels 1–5. Respondents completed self-report EQ-5D-5L and VAS, five practice cTTO tasks, ten cTTO health states, feedback module, seven forced-choice DCE pairs, and country-specific demographic/health/life/death questions. The cTTO design had 86 states in ten blocks; DCE had 196 pairs in 28 blocks of seven.

The pilot replaced the wheelchair example with a migraine example because many participants judged wheelchair worse than dead. Visual aids used traffic-light colours (green through dark crimson) for levels; interviewers read states aloud twice to illiterate respondents. The Egyptian Arabic EQ-VT software clarified that tasks need not conflict with religious beliefs. QC flagged short wheelchair explanation, short ten-task time, missing worse-than-dead explanation, 55555 inconsistencies, interviewer spikes at −1, −0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, and unusual DCE patterns. Weekly pilot and fortnightly main-data QC meetings supported retraining or removal (Methods, “Pilot Phase”; “Quality Control”).

## Models and selection

Models included generalized least squares random effects, Tobit, heteroskedastic, conditional logit, and hybrid models. Twenty main effects (MO2–AD5) used level 1 as reference. Tobit handled cTTO censoring at −1; heteroskedastic models addressed rising variance with severity; conditional logit modelled DCE choices; hybrids multiplied cTTO and DCE likelihoods. Performance used logical consistency, significance, RMSE, MAE, range, AIC/BIC, parsimony, and prediction accuracy. Flagged feedback states were excluded; sensitivity reintroduced them (Methods, “Data Analysis and Model Selection”).

## Findings and tariff

The final sample produced 9,740 cTTO and 6,818 DCE responses. Mean interview time was 41 ± 16 minutes and mean indifference steps 7.2 ± 3.2. Feedback flagged 898 cTTO responses; participants with an inconsistency fell from 26% to 12.5% after feedback. Of 8,842 unflagged cTTO observations, 40.9% were worse than dead; 13.3% were −1 and 12.3% were 1. Mean observed values for 86 states ranged 0.96 for 11211 to −0.83 for 55555 (Results, “Composite Time Trade-Off and Discrete-Choice Experiment Data”).

The preferred model was the constrained heteroskedastic cTTO model (model 4), selected for monotonic/significant parameters, handling heteroskedasticity, and lower MAE. It gives 11111 = 1, 11211 = 0.948, 55555 = −0.964, and 1,123/3,125 states (35.94%) worse than dead. Dimension ranking was mobility, pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression, self-care, usual activities. Values are calculated by subtracting the selected regular dummy decrements from 1 (Results, “Preferred Model and Value Set”).

The study reports that mobility had the largest impact, potentially reflecting limited access to social welfare and infrastructure. The high worse-than-dead share was linked to preferences to avoid being a burden. The authors discuss cultural appropriateness for Muslim and Arabic-speaking settings but recommend national value sets. Limitations include quota deviations, COVID interruption, underrepresentation of rural/illiterate participants, lack of fully validated visual tools, and no weighting analysis.
