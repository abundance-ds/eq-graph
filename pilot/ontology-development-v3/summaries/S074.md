## Aim, setting, and population

The study evaluated validity, responsiveness, minimal clinically important difference (MCID), and minimal detectable change (MDC) for the Amharic interviewer-administered EQ-5D-5L in Ethiopian cervical cancer patients. It used a longitudinal survey at Tikur Anbessa Specialized Hospital (TASH) and Saint Paul’s Hospital Millennium Medical College (SPHMMC), Ethiopia, from March 2022 to July 2023. Patients were newly diagnosed, aged over 18 years, and recruited consecutively. Exclusions were treatment already started, no consent, mental illness, or cognitive impairment. Four nurses received training; interviews were face-to-face, with questions read aloud and responses entered by interviewers (Abstract; Methods—Study setting and patient recruitment).

Of 422 baseline participants, 46 died and 5 were lost to follow-up. The final paired analysis included 371 patients, mean age 49.72 years (SD 10.80). Most were married (75%), had no formal education (60.6%), were unemployed (86.8%), and lived outside Addis Ababa (63.3%). Early-stage cancer (stage I or II) accounted for 73%; surgery alone was the most common treatment (34%). Thirty-nine percent reported improved health, 38% worsened health, and 22.6% no change at follow-up (Results—Participant characteristics; Table 1).

## Measures and analytic protocol

The Amharic EQ-5D-5L descriptive system contains mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each with no, slight, moderate, severe, and extreme problems. Profiles range from 11111 to 55555. The Ethiopian value set ranged from −0.718 to 1.0. EQ VAS asked for present health on a vertical 0 (worst imaginable health) to 100 (best imaginable health) scale. The EORTC QLQ-C30 was the disease-specific comparator, with functional and symptom domains. ECOG performance status ranged from 0 (fully active) to 5 (dead) (Methods—Instruments).

Descriptive statistics and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests compared baseline and three-month post-treatment index and EQ VAS scores. Convergent validity used Spearman correlations with EORTC QLQ-C30 dimensions. Discriminant validity used Kruskal–Wallis tests across cancer stage, ECOG status, age, and treatment modality. Responsiveness used EORTC QLQ-C30 global health as an anchor to define no change, improvement, or worsening; effect size (ES) and standardized response mean (SRM) followed Cohen thresholds. Distribution-based MCID used 0.5 baseline SD and one SEM; anchor-based MCID used EORTC QLQ-C30 global health changes and ROC/Youden analysis. MDC95% used a 1.96 multiplier, with individual and group estimates and MCID/MDC ratios (Methods—Statistical analyses, Responsiveness, MCID, MDC).

## Findings

After three months, the median EQ-5D index increased by 0.04 and EQ VAS by 7.0 (both *p* < 0.001). Baseline index and VAS were 0.81 and 65.31; follow-up values were 0.85 and 72.31. The improvement subgroup had index change 0.17, ES 0.60, and SRM 0.59; the worsening subgroup had −0.07, ES −0.53, and SRM −0.30. The no-change subgroup had change 0.005 and ES/SRM 0.03. EQ VAS improvement was 16.39 (ES 1.16; SRM 1.21), while worsening was −1.67 (ES/SRM −0.12) (Results—Responsiveness; Table 4).

Convergent validity was moderate to high. EORTC QLQ-C30 physical functioning correlated with EQ-5D mobility (−0.60), usual activities (−0.71), pain/discomfort (−0.63), EQ VAS (0.64), and index (0.70). Role functioning correlated with EQ-5D index (0.64) and VAS (0.58). Emotional functioning correlated with anxiety/depression (−0.64), index (0.58), and VAS (0.36). All reported correlations were statistically significant (Results—Convergent validity; Table 3).

The index and VAS discriminated by tumour stage (*p* = 0.001), ECOG status, and treatment modality. Median index was 0.91 in ECOG I versus 0.38 in ECOG III; EQ VAS was 42.5 for ECOG III. Stage I median index and VAS were 0.91 and 80, versus 0.82 and 60 in stage III (Results—Discriminate validity; Table 2).

Distribution-based index MCID was 0.10 using 0.5 SD and 0.15 using SEM; EQ VAS values were 4.01 and 7.56. Anchor-based MCID ranged from 0.02–0.09 for the index and 2.02–6.17 for VAS, with average estimates of 0.04 and 4.0. ROC-derived improvement MCID was 0.20 (sensitivity 34%, specificity 86%; AUC 0.59, 95% CI 0.50–0.68). The Results text reports MDC95% individual as 0.064 for index and 16.44 for VAS and group MDC as 0.42 and 20.95, but Table 6 labels the displayed individual/group rows differently (including 0.022 and 1.08). This MDC labeling is unclear in the source. Group-level MCID/MDC ratios exceeded 1, while individual ratios were below 1 (Results—MCID and MDC; Tables 5–6).

## Interpretation and limits

The authors conclude that the Amharic EQ-5D-5L detected changes and distinguished health groups, but the estimated MCID was clinically meaningful at group level and not separable from measurement error at individual level. Strengths were use of a disease-specific anchor and multiple MCID methods. Limits include three-month follow-up, patients still receiving treatment, deaths and loss to follow-up that may introduce bias, heterogeneous baseline disease duration, and limited generalisability beyond Ethiopian cervical cancer settings (Discussion; Conclusion).
