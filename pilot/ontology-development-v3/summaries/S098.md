# Longitudinal study of patients’ health-related quality of life using EQ-5D-3L in 11 Swedish National Quality Registers

## Aim and design

This study documents and compares EQ-5D-3L dimension problems and EQ VAS across patient groups and diagnoses in 11 Swedish National Quality Registers (NQRs), at baseline and one-year follow-up, with a Swedish general-population comparison. It also classifies change with the Paretian Classification of Health Change (PCHC), calculates TTO and VAS indices, and provides reference data for diagnoses. It is a longitudinal, descriptive study of routinely collected patient-reported outcomes (Abstract; Introduction; Methods—Study design).

The register data were collected from 2001–2019. One-year follow-up was defined as data within 90 days of one calendar year after baseline; fracture baseline data were within four weeks of fracture and recalled the week before fracture. After random de-duplication of people appearing in multiple registers, 266,241 patient records with complete EQ-5D-3L dimensions, age, sex, diagnosis and intervention were analysed; 209,247 had complete EQ VAS. The comparison comprised 49,169 general-population records with complete dimensions and 41,761 with EQ VAS. General-population data came from Scania (2004) and Stockholm (2006) surveys used for Swedish experience-based value sets (Methods—Sampling and data).

## Registers, population and measures

The 11 NQR areas were spine, hip, knee, ankle, cruciate ligament, Better management of patients with Osteoarthritis (BOA), fracture, heart failure, respiratory failure, psoriasis and rheumatology. Mean age ranged from 29.8 years for cruciate ligament injury to 73.7 years for respiratory failure; general population mean age was 46.2. EQ-5D-3L has mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, each with three levels; 11111 denotes no problems and the instrument describes 243 states. EQ VAS is 0 (“worst imaginable health”) to 100 (“best imaginable health”). Swedish experience-based EQ-5D-3L TTO and VAS value sets generated indices (Methods—Study design; Results—Demographic characteristics).

## Analyses

Descriptive statistics reported dimension problem prevalence, EQ VAS mean (SD), median (IQR), and changes by register, diagnosis, intervention, age, sex and BMI. ANCOVA compared sex-adjusted-for-age EQ VAS. Two-level random-intercept models estimated diagnosis effects against general population, adjusted for age group and sex. PCHC classified baseline-to-follow-up changes as no problem, no change, improved, worsened or mixed. “Improved” means at least one dimension improved without deterioration in another; “worsened” is the converse; mixed contains both. Sensitivity analyses compared people with complete follow-up with those without and separately used all baseline or all follow-up records (Methods—Data analysis).

## Findings

Pain/discomfort was the most frequently reported problem in most registers and the general population at both time points. Severe anxiety/depression was highest in COPD (9% baseline; 10% follow-up). Severe self-care problems were highest at follow-up in hip or femoral fracture (10%). “Confined to bed” mobility responses were uncommon, but reached 5% in disc hernia at baseline and in hip/thigh fracture, COPD and pulmonary fibrosis at follow-up. Mean EQ VAS across diagnoses ranged from 45.2 (SD 22.4) in disc hernia at baseline to 88.1 (15.3) in wrist/hand fracture; follow-up ranged from 48.9 (20.9) in pulmonary fibrosis to 83.3 (17.4) in wrist/hand fracture (Abstract).

The highest PCHC improvement proportions occurred in ankle, hip, knee and spine intervention registers. Most other diagnoses were also mainly improved. Respiratory failure was mainly worsened. Fracture was mainly worsened because baseline was recalled before the fracture, making direct comparison difficult. Heart failure had similar improved and worsened proportions (Results—PCHC).

Mean EQ VAS was highest in fracture patients (84.7 baseline; 77.7 follow-up) and lowest in spine at baseline (47.8) and respiratory failure at follow-up (49.1); general population mean was 79.5. Scores increased in almost all registers, with largest increases in hip (20.5) and spine surgery (20.1); fracture and respiratory failure declined. Higher dimension problem prevalence generally matched lower EQ VAS. Women generally had lower EQ VAS than men, but often greater improvement.

## Interpretation, limitations and use

The study supports EQ-5D-3L and EQ VAS as a common denominator for comparisons across diagnoses and NQRs, and shows the value of collecting both the descriptive system and overall self-assessed health. PCHC summarises direction of profile change without a value set, but does not quantify magnitude and is less useful when “mixed” changes are frequent. The authors note that EQ-5D-3L dimensions may not cover disease-specific symptoms and that the low prevalence of “confined to bed” may support applying EQ-5D-5L in some groups.

Different collection modes across NQRs, comorbidity, exclusion of incomplete/non-follow-up records and follow-up selection may introduce bias. The fracture recall baseline is not comparable with other registers. The general-population surveys were from two regions and years. Data sharing is not possible under Swedish law. The study reports ethics approvals from Gothenburg and the Swedish Ethical Review Authority and ClinicalTrials.gov registration NCT04359628 (Discussion; Ethics statements).
