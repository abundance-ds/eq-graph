## Aim, population and instrument

The study developed the first EQ-5D-5L value set based on Taiwanese general-population preferences, to support health technology assessment and Taiwan’s National Health Insurance. Adults were recruited from nine randomly selected cities in six geographic regions: Taipei, north, east, central, south and KauPing. Multi-stage stratified quota sampling targeted age, gender, education and living area. Eligible participants were aged 20 or older, able to understand the tasks and read/communicate in Chinese or Taiwanese, and gave consent. People with acute illness or cognitive problems, unwilling to discuss death, or rushing the interview were excluded. Ten interviewers collected data January–July 2017 in computer-based face-to-face interviews. Of 1123 interviewed, 1000 completed the study; 970 remained after 30 QC exclusions, but all 1000 were used in the final modelling. Participants received NT$200 (Participants; Results).

The official traditional Chinese Taiwan EQ-5D-5L was used. Five dimensions (mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression) and five levels describe 3125 states. Similar Chinese characters for severity levels were highlighted in red in the EQ-VT interface to aid discrimination. EQ VAS was collected. Each interview included EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS, background questions, a wheelchair cTTO example, three practice cTTO tasks, ten cTTO tasks, a cTTO feedback module, cTTO debriefing, seven DCE tasks, DCE debriefing and comments (Instruments; Valuation Procedures).

## Elicitation and QC

cTTO used conventional TTO for BTD states and lead-time TTO for WTD states; values ranged from −1 to 1. The 86 cTTO states were selected by Monte Carlo simulation over 10 blocks, each containing a mild state, 55555 and balanced intermediate states. DCE used 196 pairs in 28 blocks of seven, without duration, producing a latent scale. Standardised interviewer training, a 50-person pilot and weekly QA reports were used. QC flagged interviews for wheelchair explanation under three minutes, no WTD explanation, formal cTTO under five minutes, logical inconsistency, or flat-line DCE patterns. Materials were translated iteratively into traditional Chinese characters (Valuation Procedures; Quality Assurance).

The 1000-person full sample was similar to Taiwan in sex, age and living area but had more higher education and fewer primary-school participants. It generated 10,000 cTTO and 7000 DCE observations. The cTTO distribution was 55.9% positive, 38.5% negative, 7.7% equal to 1, 5.6% equal to 0 and 12.7% equal to −1. Flagged QC observations were retained after a matched subsample showed no significant distribution difference (p=0.384) (Results Data Characteristics).

## Analysis and tariff

Twenty incremental dummy variables represented each move from level 1 to levels 2–5. cTTO models were OLS, GLS, Tobit and Tobit-GLS; Tobit addressed left-censoring at −1. DCE used conditional logistic regression, with rescaling to the desired utility scale through the hybrid theta. Standard hybrid and hybrid-Tobit models maximised the joint likelihood. Selection used logical consistency/monotonicity first, then goodness of fit and the predicted-utility distribution. AIC/BIC assessed cTTO/DCE models; log likelihood assessed hybrids. STATA 14 estimated hybrids and SAS 9.4 estimated other models (Statistical Analysis).

The preferred model was the hybrid using all data with cTTO left-censored at −1, no heteroscedasticity and no constant. All main-effect coefficients were logically consistent. Predicted utility ranged from 1 for 11111 to −1.0259 for the worst state 55555. The example 12345 was 0.0395. Hybrid disutility sums were 0.4767 for mobility, 0.4534 for pain/discomfort and 0.4212 for anxiety/depression; these dimensions were close in influence, unlike some other Asian value sets. DCE scores tended to be higher than cTTO scores except for some poor states (Modelling Results; Tables 2–3).

The authors recommend the set for cost-utility analysis, HTA, new drugs and devices, formularies and insurance coverage. Important limitations are education imbalance, difficulty recruiting older/rural primary-education respondents, highlighted severity labels that differed from other countries, and interviewer effects. The unusually high negative cTTO proportion (38.5%) and a utility below −1 may affect QALY calculations. The discussion calls for research into cultural differences, the impact of colour coding and interviewer effects (Discussion; Conclusions).
