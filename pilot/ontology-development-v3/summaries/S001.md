## Aim, design and setting

The study aimed to produce a Swedish EQ-5D-5L value set based on hypothetical social preferences for use in Swedish decision making (Aim; Abstract Background). Data were collected from February 2020 to April 2021 in five regions: Västerbotten, Örebro, Skåne, Gothenburg and Stockholm. Adults aged above 18 years from the Swedish general public were eligible. Quota sampling used age, sex and rural/urban area, with Swedish census data as the target. Recruitment used local voluntary organisations, including pensioner associations, sports clubs and the Red Cross Association. The abstract reports 785 interviews; the Results report 784 completed interviews and 775 in the primary analysis. Mean interview time was 49 minutes (SD 14) (Study sample; Results Data).

The official Swedish EQ-5D-5L version was used. It has mobility (MO), self-care (SC), usual activities (UA), pain/discomfort (PD), and anxiety/depression (AD), each with five levels: no, slight, moderate, severe, and unable/extreme problems. The descriptive system has 3125 states, coded as five digits; EQ VAS ranges from 0 (worst health you can imagine) to 100 (best health you can imagine) (The EQ-5D-5L instrument).

## Protocol and elicitation

The interview used EQ-VT version 2.1. It comprised welcome, EQ-5D-5L self-report and background questions; c-TTO tasks, debriefing and feedback; DCE tasks and debriefing; a comment box; and further background questions. c-TTO included two wheelchair examples, three practice states and ten real tasks. The feedback module allowed a respondent to flag, but not revalue, a state. The c-TTO design used 86 states in 10 blocks: the five mildest imperfect states (21,111; 12,111; 11,211; 11,121; 11,112), 55,555 and 80 block-unique states. DCE used 196 pairs plus 10 mild-state pairs, allocated to 28 blocks of seven; order and left/right position were randomised (EQ VT valuation protocol version 2.1; Methods for eliciting preferences).

Initially 12 trained interviewers were recruited; 11 began fieldwork. COVID-19 required three administration categories: face-to-face, face-to-face with social distance, and online video conference with a shared EQ-VT screen. Interviewers with fewer than 10 main-survey interviews were excluded from analysis. The interviewer intraclass correlation coefficient was 1.3% (95% CI 0.0036–0.0467). A mixed-effects model found no mode effect on c-TTO responses (p=0.440), so data were pooled. QC checked completion times, inconsistency size and use of interview sections (Interviewer recruitments; Data quality).

## Analysis and findings

c-TTO values flagged by the feedback model were excluded; all DCE observations were retained. c-TTO models were generalized least squares (GLS), Tobit and heteroskedastic models. DCE used conditional logit. Hybrid models combined c-TTO and DCE. The main-effect specification used 20 dummy variables (levels 2–5 for each dimension, with level 1 as reference) and no constant. Model selection considered theoretical plausibility, monotonicity, p<0.05, AIC, BIC, mean absolute error (MAE), root mean squared error (RMSE), and predicted-versus-observed scatterplots (Data analysis and modelling; Model development and selection criteria). Sensitivity analyses removed non-traders, flat-lining DCE respondents, extreme response-time cases and the 10 mild-mild pairs.

The primary analysis retained 775 respondents. It contained 7750 c-TTO responses and 5425 DCE responses. Of c-TTO responses, 21% were 1 and 13% were below 0; 876 (11%) were flagged. After flagging, 6874 c-TTO values were modelled. The observed mean ranged from 0.982 for 11,121 to −0.285 for 55,555. Two respondents who valued almost all states at 1 were retained as non-traders (Results Data; Descriptive analysis).

The preferred product was the c-TTO-only heteroskedastic model. It produced 1 for 11111 and −0.314 for 55555 (reported abstract value −0.31). The example state 12,345 was 0.220, calculated as 1−0.019 (SC2)−0.048 (UA3)−0.319 (PD4)−0.394 (AD5). PD and AD were the largest dimensions in c-TTO models; DCE and hybrid models differed slightly in ordering. DCE and hybrid models had more logical inconsistencies, and DCE had a rescaling problem because death was not included. The authors did not select hybrid modelling because combining c-TTO and DCE lacked a theoretical foundation (The preferred value set).

The study contrasts hypothetical and Swedish experience-based values: the latter made all states better than dead and valued 11111 at 0.976, whereas the hypothetical set reached 1 at 11111 and assigned negative values to 4% of states. The authors state that hypothetical values can support reimbursement decisions, but recommend further work on how the choice of values changes QALYs and cost-effectiveness results. Limitations include the small primary sample (775), COVID-19 disruption, interviewer turnover, changes in mode, and possible pandemic effects. The paper states that this is the first c-TTO-based social value set for Swedish EQ-5D-5L (Discussion; Conclusions).
