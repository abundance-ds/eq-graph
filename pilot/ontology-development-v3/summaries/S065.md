## Objective and decision context

The protocol tests two properties of the experimental EQ-TIPS-5L V3.0 descriptive system: whether successive severity levels agree with preferences, and whether each attribute level has a non-zero main effect on choices. The common scenario is a one-month acute health episode for a one-year-old child. The respondent is the decision maker in the general community. The child recovers after one month. Wave 1 uses kaizen tasks; Wave 2 uses paired comparisons (Abstract; Introduction > Aims; Methods > Decision context).

EQ-TIPS was developed for children aged 0–36 months for clinical research, population health, and health technology assessment/economic analysis. V3.0 has seven attributes and both three-level and five-level versions. The EQ-TIPS-5L attributes are **Movement**, **Eating or drinking**, **Sleep**, **Pain**, **Managing emotions**, **Interacting with others**, and **Play**. Examples are included in the descriptive system, such as “brings hands to mouth, head control, reaching, sitting, crawling, walking, running” for Movement and “falling asleep, staying asleep, amount of sleep” for Sleep. The five response levels run from “No problems” or “No pain” through “A little bit”, “Some”, “A lot”, and “Extreme” problems (Introduction > Experimental EQ-TIPS V.3.0; Table 1).

## DCE waves and samples

Wave 1 will recruit 400 Australian adults. Each respondent will complete 14 kaizen tasks. A task starts with one EQ-TIPS-5L profile and asks the respondent to choose successive improvements. Four changes are improvements and one is a bad change, called *aku*. A bad change selected early is a type-1 discordance. An improvement that is skipped is a type-2 discordance. Fourteen tasks produce 14 choice probabilities and preference evidence from sequential improvements.

Wave 2 will recruit 1,000 Australian adults. Each respondent will complete 28 paired comparisons and choose between two EQ-TIPS profiles. Both profiles describe health problems for the one-month episode. The survey will use five blocks of 28 comparisons, based on wave-1 evidence. Wave 1 has two blocks of 200 respondents; Wave 2 has five blocks of 200 respondents. Recruitment uses a panel vendor and 32 quotas based on gender, age, and ancestry. The planned total is 1,400 adults (Abstract; Methods > Preference elicitation; Data collection).

## Experimental design and survey

Wave 1 will use two d-efficient blocks of 14 origin–destination pairs. Each task has five possible changes, four differential attributes, and three hold-outs. The full factorial set contains 350,000 origin–destination pairs. Candidate pairs exclude atypical severe combinations and alternatives with choice probability below 0.0833. Block construction balances all 28 incremental improvements, avoids duplicate origins and improvement pairs, and makes each attribute a hold-out six times. The designs were selected with R Statistical Software V.4.1.3 using d-error.

The survey has consent and demographics; respondent HRQoL and EQ-5D-5L plus 13 EQ-5D bolt-ons; a child-representation task; the EQ-TIPS-5L for the referent child; the DCE, checklist, and warm-up; and debriefing and socioeconomic questions. The referent child is someone known well to the respondent. An open description must include a child, toy or object, personalisation, and at least two complete sentences. Surveys should take less than 20 minutes. Attribute order and task sequence are randomised (Methods > Survey instrument; Data collection).

## Analysis plan

The primary model is a main-effects conditional logit model estimated by maximum likelihood. Wave 1 tests the frequency of type-1 and type-2 discordances and whether main effects are non-zero. Wave 2 estimates main effects and compares them with Wave 1. Differences use p<0.05, Lin’s concordance coefficient, and comparison of Wave-1 predictions with Wave-2 choices. Secondary analysis excludes respondents with high discordance. Sensitivity analyses test block, task sequence, and attribute order. P values, 95% confidence intervals, and standard errors use 1,000-iteration cluster bootstrap with block-specific strata. Future work may examine scale-adjusted latent-class models (Methods > Analysis; Table 2).

## Status, uses, and limitations

This paper reports a protocol and analysis plan. It reports no completed DCE results, value set, or final recommendation. Ethics approval was granted by the independent Includovate review board on 19 February 2025. Findings are intended to inform refinement of EQ-TIPS-5L and may also inform instruments using similar level descriptors, such as EQ-5D-Y-5L.

The online marketing-panel design may have quality and generalisability limits, especially for people with lower education or no panel participation. Wave-2 materials will be adapted after Wave 1, so the paper provides full survey, design, and analysis materials only for Wave 1. Paired comparisons may be more burdensome because respondents choose between profiles with “bads”; kaizen tasks use improvements or “goods” (Strengths and limitations; Ethics and dissemination).
