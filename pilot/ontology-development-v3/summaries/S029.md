## Aim and population

The study derived a societal Indonesian EQ-5D-5L value set for QALY-based economic evaluation. It recruited at least 1,000 people aged 17 years or older from the general population. A multi-stage stratified quota design covered residence (urban/rural), sex, age (17–30, 31–50, >50), education (basic, middle, high), religion (Islam, Christian, other) and ethnicity (Jawa, Sunda, Sumatera, Sulawesi, Madura-Bali, other). The first four variables formed 36 quota groups; religion and ethnicity were controlled independently. Interviews were conducted in six cities and surrounding areas: Jakarta, Bandung, Jogjakarta, Surabaya, Medan and Makassar. Recruitment used personal contact, local leaders, public places and respondent referrals. [Source: Abstract; Methods—Respondents]

The final analytic sample was 1,054 completed interviews. The official Bahasa Indonesia EQ-5D-5L version was used. The instrument has mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, each with no, slight, moderate, severe and unable/extreme problems, yielding 3,125 states; EQ VAS ranges 0–100. The protocol was computer-assisted face-to-face on EQ-VT version 2.0. It included informed consent, descriptive system/VAS and demographics, one wheelchair example, three practice states (21121, 35554, 15411), 10 C-TTO states, a feedback module and seven forced-pair DCE comparisons. [Source: Methods—Instruments; Valuation Protocol; Preference Elicitation Methods]

## Elicitation, exclusions and analysis

Conventional TTO valued better-than-dead (BTD) states: x years full health versus 10 years impaired health, utility x/10. Lead-time TTO valued worse-than-dead (WTD) states: x years full health versus 10 years full health followed by 10 years impaired health, utility (x−10)/10, with −1 the lowest possible value. The standard protocol assigned respondents to blocks of 10 states: 86 unique states included 80 Monte Carlo states, five mild states repeated in two blocks and 55555 in every block. DCE used 196 pairs in 28 blocks of seven. [Source: Methods—Preference Elicitation Methods]

Thirteen interviewers received a one-day workshop and at least five pilot interviews; three more received equivalent training. Interviews ran 9 March 2015–24 January 2016. After 102 interviews, quality was judged insufficient; these were treated as pilot interviews, excluded, and interviewers retrained. Exclusion criteria covered incomplete interviews, inability to distinguish dimensions/levels or understand C-TTO, irrational C-TTO choices, and flagged states. Final data included 9,462 C-TTO observations after removal of 45 irrational and 1,033 feedback-flagged responses, and 7,378 DCE observations. [Source: Methods—Data Collection; Exclusion Criteria; Results—Data Characteristics]

The study fitted a Tobit C-TTO model censored at −1, a conditional-logistic DCE model with rescaled coefficients, and a hybrid regression combining both. Logical consistency and coefficient significance were assessed. C-TTO and rescaled DCE predicted utilities for 3,125 states were highly correlated (r = 0.9881); hybrid versus C-TTO r = 0.995 and versus DCE r = 0.997. [Source: Results—Modelling Results; Figure 2; Table 3]

## Main findings and value set

The most self-reported problems were pain/discomfort (39.66%) and the fewest self-care (1.89%). Observed C-TTO means ranged from −0.719 for 55555 to 0.909 for 12111; 29 of 86 states had negative observed means and 657 observations (6.92%) were censored at −1. The final hybrid main-effects model was logically consistent. It gives 1.000 to 11111, 0.921 to 11112 and −0.865 to 55555; 1,108 of 3,125 states (35.46%) are negative. Mobility had the greatest effect on values and pain/discomfort the least. Example state 12345 receives 0.240 through the listed level-transition decrements. [Source: Abstract; Results—Data Characteristics; Modelling Results; Table 3]

The authors state that the sample was similar to the Indonesian population for residence, sex, age, education, ethnicity and religion, although 92.9% lived on Java. The rigorous quality-control process and retraining were considered important for logical and significant models. The high proportion of negative values may reflect valid administration of WTD tasks and cultural concerns about burden on family and friends, but the latter is presented as a possible explanation rather than a tested causal result. The national value set supports HTA, health economic evaluations, PROM studies, clinical trials and hospital-care research. Limitations include quota and personal-network recruitment, Java concentration, no weighting for small demographic deviations, and uncertainty about island-specific preferences. [Source: Discussion; Conclusion]
