# The performance relationship between the EQ-5D-5L composite “Anxiety/Depression” dimension and anxiety and depression symptoms in a large, general population sample

## Aim and population

The study assessed whether the EQ-5D-5L Anxiety/Depression (A/D) dimension captures anxiety, depression, or both, and whether performance differs by gender, age, education, or chronic conditions. It used secondary data from the POPCORN study. Web surveys were collected in April–May 2020 from Greece, Italy, The Netherlands, Sweden, the United Kingdom, and the United States, using official EQ-5D-5L translations. The survey did not allow skipped questions, so no missing values occurred (Abstract; Methods—Population and data collection).

The final sample contained 19,902 adults aged 18–75, median age 45 (IQR 26), with 46.8% reporting at least one chronic condition. Thirteen point six percent selected depression or anxiety disorder as a chronic condition; among 5,892 with one singular chronic condition, 1,248 (21.2%) selected anxiety or depression disorder (Results—Respondent characteristics; Table 1).

## Measures and analysis

The A/D dimension asks about “today” and uses five levels: none, slight, moderate, severe, and unable to/extreme. GAD-7 measured anxiety symptoms over two weeks, with anxiety defined as GAD-7 ≥8. PHQ-9 measured depression symptoms over two weeks, with depression defined as PHQ-9 ≥10. Co-morbid anxiety and depression meant both thresholds. These screening instruments were treated as “gold standards,” while the authors note that neither is a professional diagnostic interview (Methods—Measures).

AUROC quantified discriminatory performance. Analyses compared A/D against anxiety, depression, and both combined, overall and in age groups 18–30, 31–45, 46–60, and 61–75; gender; ISCED education (low 0–2, medium 3–4, high 5–8); no versus one-or-more chronic conditions; and singular anxiety/depression versus another singular chronic condition. Parametric AUROCs used smoothing and 2,000 stratified bootstrap repetitions. Non-parametric and higher-threshold sensitivity analyses used GAD-7 ≥10 and PHQ-9 ≥15. Sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, accuracy, and Youden index assessed A/D cut-offs (Methods—Data analysis).

## Findings

Across the total sample, A/D AUROC ranged 0.853–0.859 for anxiety, depression, and co-morbid groups, with no significant difference between diagnostic groups. This indicates equally good discrimination of anxiety, depression, and both (Results—Discriminatory performance; Table 3). Prevalence showed a severity gradient: severe GAD-7 anxiety ranged from 1.1% among respondents reporting no A/D problems to 64.7% among those reporting extreme A/D problems (Figure 2).

Performance differed strongly by age. AUROC was ≤0.823 in the two younger groups and ≥0.874 in the two older groups; it was consistently ≤0.804 under age 30 and ≥0.910 above age 61. Gender and total chronic-condition status did not produce significant differences. Highly educated respondents had marginally worse performance than low- and medium-educated respondents in two diagnostic groups.

Among participants with one singular chronic condition, A/D performed worse when the condition was an anxiety/depression disorder (AUROC 0.726–0.750) than when it was another condition (0.832–0.838). The authors suggest that treatment could reduce GAD-7/PHQ-9 symptoms while respondents still report having the disorder on A/D, or that the dimension is interpreted as having the condition rather than current symptoms (Results; Discussion—Singular chronic condition performance).

Optimal cut-off varied by subgroup. For the total population and younger groups, A/D ≥3 had the highest sensitivity or Youden index; older groups favoured ≥2, with Youden index 0.52–0.65. A/D ≥4 was more adequate for a singular anxiety/depression disorder group (Youden index 0.32–0.37). Non-parametric and higher-threshold analyses changed absolute AUROC values but retained the main age and chronic-condition conclusions (Results—Optimal A/D dimension cut-off).

## Interpretation and limits

The composite A/D dimension was equally sensitive to anxiety and depression symptoms overall, but performance was worse in younger adults and in respondents with a self-reported anxiety/depression disorder. The authors discuss possible age-related differential item functioning and caution that intra-age comparative results may be flawed. The A/D dimension was analysed alone, not as a full EQ-5D measure. A/D uses a “today” recall while GAD-7 and PHQ-9 use two weeks, and the screeners are imperfect self-rated comparators. Age effects may also reflect unmeasured cohort or living-standard factors (Discussion; Strengths and limitations).
