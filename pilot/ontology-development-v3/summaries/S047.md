# The use of EQ-5D-5L as a patient-reported outcome measure in evaluating community rehabilitation services in Alberta, Canada

## Aim, setting, and population

The study described HRQL and change among patients using Alberta Health Services community rehabilitation, and examined factors associated with impact. EQ-5D-5L was implemented through Alberta’s Rehabilitation Model of Care (R-MoC) across 152 sites, with intake and end-of-care surveys. Adults (18+) willing and able to complete surveys were included; cognitive impairment was excluded. Surveys used iPad, laptop, or paper. Only complete pre/post records were analyzed; same-day records (*n* = 107) were excluded (Methods, “Data source”). Three programs were analyzed: pulmonary rehabilitation (*n* = 542), group-based community exercise (*n* = 463), and physiotherapy for bone and joint care (*n* = 391).

## Instrument and analysis

EQ-5D-5L has mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each with levels 1 (none) to 5 (extreme), producing 3125 states. The Canadian EQ-5D-5L value set generated index scores; EQ-VAS recorded “today” health from 0 worst imaginable to 100 best imaginable. A total sum score ranged 5–25 (Methods, “Measures”).

Dimension change used PCHC categories: deteriorated, no change, maintained perfect health, or improved. Index MID was 0.04, VAS MID 7.0, and a one-point total-sum change was considered important. Effect sizes used mean change/baseline SD. Scores were compared across programs, age, gender, urban/rural region, and Alberta norms. Chi-squared tests examined change by age, gender, region, and anxiety/depression at intake; STATA 14.2 was used (Methods, “Statistical analysis”).

## Program results

- **Pulmonary rehabilitation:** 542 patients, 59.2% older adults, 50.9% male; 65.5% had chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and 28.0% another respiratory condition. Mean intervention duration was 47.0 days (SD 23.5). At intake, any level 2–5 problems were mobility 72.5%, self-care 29.9%, usual activities 77.1%, pain/discomfort 70.5%, and anxiety/depression 61.4%; mean index 0.73 (0.17), VAS 62.3 (19.4), total sum 10.2 (3.2). Index-based change: 38.4% improved, 24% deteriorated, and 38% no change. Largest improvement was usual activities, followed by mobility (Results, Sections “Pulmonary Rehabilitation” and “Rehabilitation patients…”).
- **Group-based community exercise:** 463 patients, 71.1% female; common conditions were chronic pain, osteoarthritis/inflammatory arthritis, and falls/balance. Mean duration 103.7 days (SD 51.4). Any problems were mobility 79.1%, self-care 33.5%, usual activities 79.3%, pain/discomfort 89.9%, anxiety/depression 59.2%; mean index 0.70 (0.18), VAS 65.3 (18.2), total sum 10.8 (3.1). Index-based change: 51.2% improved, 17% deteriorated, 32% no change. Largest improvements were mobility, usual activities, then pain/discomfort (Results, Section “Group-based Community Exercise”).
- **Physiotherapy:** 391 patients, 60.6% female; common conditions were orthopaedic surgery, musculoskeletal conditions, and fracture. Mean duration 67.3 days (SD 46.0). Any problems were mobility 67.3%, self-care 39.9%, usual activities 83.4%, pain/discomfort 96.2%, anxiety/depression 41.4%; mean index 0.71 (0.17), VAS 68.3 (18.9), total sum 10.6 (2.9). Index-based change: 72.4% improved, 5% deteriorated, 23% no change. Largest improvement was usual activities, followed by pain/discomfort and mobility (Results, Section “Physiotherapy”).

Across programs, intake HRQL was below Alberta general-population norms. Mean change in index/VAS/total sum was 0.02/6.0/−0.5 in pulmonary, 0.06/6.6/−1.2 in community exercise, and 0.13/1.2/−2.8 in physiotherapy (Abstract). The physiotherapy Results section instead reports a VAS change of 12.2 (SD 18.4), so the source is internally inconsistent on this value (Results, Section “EQ-5D-5L in Physiotherapy”). Older age, urban region, and anxiety/depression problems at intake were associated with positive change. In pulmonary rehabilitation, older groups improved more than ages 25–44; anxiety/depression at intake was associated with index and total-sum change. In community exercise, urban participants improved more on total sum (65% vs 52% rural), and anxiety/depression was associated with index/total-sum change. In physiotherapy, anxiety/depression at intake was associated with index, VAS, and total-sum change (Results, Section “Distribution…”).

## Interpretation and limitations

The study supports routine EQ-5D-5L for program/service decisions and quality improvement, while noting that no-control observational change cannot establish effectiveness. Maintenance may be successful; deterioration needs review. Limitations include selection/non-response bias, exclusion of cognitive impairment, incomplete site implementation, reversion to the mean, generic measure insensitivity, two timepoint “snapshots”, and no linkage to other patient data. PROMs should be used with other outcomes (Discussion; Conclusions).
