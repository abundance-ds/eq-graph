## Aim and sampling

The study derived Indonesian population norms and assessed test–retest reliability for EQ-5D-5L and WHOQOL-BREF. A multistage stratified quota sample of adults aged 17–75 years represented residence, gender, age, education, religion, and ethnicity. Six cities and surrounding areas were included: Jakarta, Bandung, Jogjakarta, Surabaya, Medan, and Makassar. Of 1,117 approached, 1,056 completed both instruments. A retest subgroup completed both twice; after excluding intervals >1 month, 206 remained, with mean interval 17.45 days (SD 4.71) (Abstract; Methods; Results, “Characteristics” and “Test-retest reliability”).

## Instruments and analysis

The official EQ-5D-5L Bahasa Indonesia version 1.0 was administered online through EQ-Valuation Technology (EQ-VT) platform version 2.0 with interviewer help. It assesses mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each from no problems to unable/extreme problems, plus EQ-VAS 0 (“The worst health you can imagine”) to 100 (“The best health you can imagine”). Index scores used the new Indonesian value set. WHOQOL-BREF is a 26-item paper instrument with physical, psychological, social relationships, and environmental domains plus overall QOL and general health; scores transform to 0–100 (Methods, “Instruments”).

Categorical responses used cross-tabulation and Chi-square tests; means/SD and t-tests or ANOVA described norms. Gwet’s agreement coefficient (AC) and percentage agreement assessed categorical test–retest reliability because of Kappa paradoxes in low-prevalence general-population samples. EQ-VAS, EQ-5D-5L index, and WHOQOL-BREF domain scores used two-way random-effects absolute-agreement ICC, transformations when needed, Lin concordance correlation coefficient, and Bland–Altman plots (Methods, “Statistical analysis”).

## Reliability and norms

For 206 retest respondents, the five EQ-5D-5L dimensions showed almost perfect agreement: Gwet’s AC 0.85–0.99 and agreement 90–99%. EQ-VAS ICC was 0.45 and index ICC 0.37, both poor. WHOQOL-BREF domain ICCs were 0.70–0.79, moderate to good; overall QOL and general health ACs were 0.91 and 0.86 (Results, “Test-retest reliability”; Table 2). Bland–Altman points outside limits were 5.3% for EQ-VAS, 7.3% for index, and 4.9–6.3% for WHOQOL-BREF domains.

In the full sample, 44.07% reported 11111 (no problems in all five dimensions). Problems were most frequent for pain/discomfort (39.7%) and anxiety/depression (34.3%), least frequent for self-care (1.9%). Females and older respondents had lower EQ index scores; rural, younger, and higher-educated respondents had higher EQ-VAS. Mean EQ-VAS was 79.39 (SD 14.01), mean index 0.91 (SD 0.11) (Results, “EQ-5D-5L population norms”; Table 8). WHOQOL-BREF generally showed higher scores for male, younger, higher-educated, and high-income respondents.

The study reports useful reference estimates for clinicians, researchers, public-health experts, epidemiologists, and intervention assessment. Limitations include concentration of respondents on Java, a retest interval overlapping the WHOQOL-BREF four-week frame, skewed low-variance EQ-5D data, interviewer administration, and poor stability of VAS/index summary scores despite stable dimensions (Discussion; Conclusion).
