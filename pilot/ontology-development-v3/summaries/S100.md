## Aim and eligibility

This systematic review summarises statistical methods used to estimate treatment effects on EQ-5D outcomes in randomised clinical trials (RCTs). MEDLINE and EMBASE were searched from inception through 15 November 2021; ClinicalTrials.gov was searched 16 August 2023, with PubMed searches for linked publications. When no PubMed publication was found for a registry record, methods and results were extracted from the registry. Eligible reports were RCTs analysing postbaseline EQ-5D by treatment group, including EQ-5D dimension responses, utilities or EQ VAS; quality-adjusted life years and quality-adjusted time without symptoms of disease or toxicity were explicitly excluded as composite outcomes. Peer-reviewed articles and health technology assessment (HTA) reports were eligible; pilot/feasibility studies, reviews, editorials, conference abstracts and non-English reports were excluded. Quality appraisal was not performed because the aim was methods description, not treatment-effect estimation (Abstract; Methods—Trial Identification, Eligibility and Quality Appraisal).

Eight reviewers screened titles/abstracts in duplicate; pairs screened full texts and resolved disagreements with a third reviewer. Multiple publications were mapped to one trial ID using registration number, indication, treatments and sample size. Four reviewer pairs independently extracted data with a standardised, pilot-tested Excel template. Extracted fields covered trial phase/design/therapeutic area/sample/follow-up; EQ-5D outcome role, time points, data type and format; models, assumptions, confounder adjustment and missing-data handling.

## Evidence base

Of 11,633 identified records, 9,030 abstracts/titles and 7,056 full texts were screened. The final analysis included 2,125 unique RCTs. Parallel treatment design was used in 2,044 (96.2%); 1,316 (61.9%) were nondrug trials without a specified phase and 574 of 809 phase-specified trials were phase 3. Main therapeutic areas were orthopedics (349, 16.4%), cardiology (296, 13.9%), oncology (265, 12.5%), psychiatry (172, 8.1%) and rheumatology (161, 7.6%). EQ-5D was primary in 131 trials (6.2%), secondary in 1,219 (57.4%) and exploratory in 775 (36.5%). Baseline EQ-5D was collected in 1,881 (88.5%); 855 (40.2%) had one postbaseline and 1,270 (59.8%) at least two (Results—Trial characteristics; Table 1).

## EQ-5D data and methods

Utilities were analysed in 1,592 trials (74.9%), EQ VAS in 1,197 (56.3%) and dimension responses in 385 (18.1%). Utility only occurred in 777 trials (36.6%); utility plus EQ VAS in 661 (31.1%). Numerical format dominated utilities (1,573/1,592, 98.8%) and EQ VAS (1,186/1,197, 99.1%). Dimension responses were categorical in 231 (60.0%) and numerical in 156 (40.5%); percentages were the common categorical representation (193, 50.1%). Categorical utilities and EQ VAS commonly represented stable/deterioration/improvement using a minimal important difference (MID), or good/bad health thresholds.

For single-postbaseline numerical utilities (589 trials), 273 (46.3%) used multivariable methods; the most common specific model was a linear fixed-effect model (192, 32.6%), followed by parametric tests (121, 20.5%) and nonparametric tests (83, 13.9%). For multiple postbaselines (984 trials), 612 (62.6%) used multivariable methods; linear mixed-effect models were most common (338, 34.3%), followed by parametric tests (220, 22.4%) and fixed-effect models (196, 19.9%). EQ VAS showed the same pattern: fixed-effect models dominated single postbaseline (137/489, 28.0%); mixed-effect models dominated multiple postbaseline (245/697, 35.2%).

Categorical analyses often used nonparametric tests (chi-square or Fisher’s exact), descriptive analysis, fixed-effect logistic models and Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel tests. Seventeen trials used survival analysis for time to utility deterioration and 18 for EQ VAS. Other models—Tobit, beta, Poisson and quantile regression—appeared in fewer than three studies per EQ-5D data type. Data synthesis and visualisation used R 4.3.3 and followed Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidance (Methods—Data Synthesis; Results—Statistical Methods).

## Reporting of assumptions, baseline and missing data

Among 2,054 studies analysing numerical EQ-5D, only 221 (10.8%) reported examining model assumptions and 438 (21.3%) adjusted for baseline EQ-5D. Only 54 (2.6%) used MID to interpret clinical meaning. Missing EQ-5D was explicitly assessed in 661 trials (31.1%); 347 (52.5% of 661) imputed missing data. The most common methods were multiple imputation (200, 57.6% of 347) and last observation carried forward (106, 30.5%), with mean, regression, Markov Chain Monte Carlo, nonresponder and worst-case imputations also reported.

## Interpretation and limitations

Utilities and EQ VAS were favoured over dimension responses, but value sets can alter utility estimates and dimensions can reveal treatment effects hidden in an overall utility. Linear models were common despite EQ-5D skewness, ceiling effects and discreteness; insufficient assumption checking can produce biased or inefficient estimates and reduce comparability. Mixed models can incorporate within-person correlation and missingness under missing-at-random, but the missingness mechanism may be non-random when patients with poor HRQoL stop completing questionnaires. The authors stress that methods should follow the estimand: for a longitudinal change estimand, a mixed model incorporating all time points is appropriate. The review may miss unpublished or non-English evidence and details were often sparse, so baseline-adjustment and missing-data use may be underestimated. No single best method emerged (Discussion; Conclusion).
