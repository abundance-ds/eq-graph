## Aim, setting and sample

The study produced Italian normative data for the EQ-5D-5L descriptive system, index value and EQ VAS, and compared them with other countries. Adults aged ≥18 were recruited through an online panel, local recruiters and quota sampling for age, sex and macro-area (north-east, north-west, centre, south and islands). Recruitment and Zoom interviews took place during the COVID-19 period, November 2020–February 2021 (the Methods also describe October 2020 as the start of data collection). Eleven trained interviewers at Bocconi University conducted the computer-assisted personal interviews through Lime Survey and Zoom under the EQ-VT protocol. A market-research company scheduled interviews and called participants the day before. [Source: Abstract; Methods—Sample Recruitment and Data Collection]

The sample included 1,182 adults aged 18–84, including 606 women (51.3%), representative of Italy by sex and geographical area but about four years younger than the national adult population. A total of 461 (39%) reported at least one chronic condition; cardiovascular disease (n = 180), arthritis (69), diabetes (62) and asthma/chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (58) were most frequent. The official Italian EQ-5D-5L version was used: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, each with five severity levels. Health states range from 11111 to 55555. Index values used the Italian value set derived from cTTO and DCE, ranging from −0.571 to 1. [Source: Methods—EQ-5D-5L; Results—Sample Characteristics]

## Analysis

The study described health-state frequencies, dimension severity levels, “no problems” versus “any problems” (levels 2–5), index and EQ VAS means, standard deviations, medians and ranges by sex, EuroQol age bands (18–24 through 75+), socioeconomic and health subgroups. Chi-square, t tests and ANOVA assessed group differences. OLS regression with robust standard errors and backward selection examined sociodemographic predictors. Comparisons used available international population norms. Excel and Stata 13 were used. [Source: Methods—Data Analysis]

## Results

Only 106 of 3,125 possible health states (3.4%) were observed; 11111 and 11112 were selected by about half of participants, and 34.7% reported 11111. At least one problem occurred in 12.1% for mobility, 4.2% self-care, 11.6% usual activities, 43.3% pain/discomfort and 41.2% anxiety/depression. Severe or extreme levels were uncommon (0.3% self-care to 1.3% pain/discomfort). Women reported more anxiety/depression problems than men (49.7% versus 32.3%). Anxiety/depression problems were highest among 18–24-year-olds (56.0%) and lowest in the oldest groups (30.0%); other dimensions generally worsened with age. [Source: Abstract; Results—EQ-5D-5L Dimensions; Figures 1–3]

Mean EQ-5D-5L index was 0.93 (SD 0.11; median 0.96; range −0.23 to 1); men averaged 0.94 and women 0.92 (p = 0.01). The mean decreased from 0.95 (SD 0.06) at 18–24 to 0.91 (SD 0.13) at ≥75. Mean EQ VAS was 81.8 (SD 13.5; median 85); it decreased from 87.0 (SD 8.9) to 75.1 (SD 16.4) across the same age groups. Index values were lower among participants with chronic illness (0.88), low education (0.89), low income (<€14,000; 0.90), pensioners (0.91), housewives (0.90), divorcees (0.89), widowed participants (0.91) and social-assistance recipients (0.85). Chronic conditions, female sex and social-assistance status were negative OLS predictors of index; higher income was positive. Chronic conditions and age >55 were negative predictors of EQ VAS, while income and previous serious illness were positive. [Source: Abstract; Results—EQ-5D-5L Index Value; EQ VAS; Multivariate Regression; Tables 3–4]

Italy’s mean index and EQ VAS were higher than most compared European countries, but cross-country differences may reflect culture, religion, survey timing and mode. The authors highlight the high anxiety/depression prevalence among young participants and women, and recommend these norms for patient benchmarking, public-health planning and future surveys.

## Limitations

The sample was younger than Italy’s population and had fewer participants aged >65, so overall values may be overestimated. Online videoconferencing required basic computer skills and may have introduced age selection and social-desirability bias, particularly for anxiety/depression. Data were collected during the second COVID-19 wave, and a simple linear model was used. The authors also note that the survey design came from an EQ-5D valuation study and that cross-country comparisons should be cautious.
