## Aim and design

EQ-DAPHNIE (EuroQol Data for Assessment of Population Health Needs and Instrument Evaluation) is infrastructure for large, representative and comparable population-health datasets across countries. The paper describes the rationale, design, survey content, sampling, translation, quality assurance and governance. It is a cross-sectional online survey programme, not an intervention study and not a report of comparative outcome results. Its two linked aims are population-health assessment and evaluation of standardised health-status, HRQoL and wellbeing instruments (Abstract; Background).

## Countries, population and sampling

The programme targets adults aged 18 years or older in 15 countries in two rounds: Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the UK and the US in round 1; Argentina, Brazil, Chile, China, France, Germany, Japan, Mexico, the Netherlands and Spain in round 2. Each country target is 4,500 complete surveys. A UK pilot ran 6–20 April 2023 with 3,012 participants. Round 1 data collection occurred February–May 2024 and round 2 May–December 2024; country collection is targeted to six weeks, with quota controls relaxed at week five if required, while aiming for at least 85% adherence to quota thresholds (Methods—Setting and population; Country selection; Study timelines).

Participants were Dynata online-panel members recruited through websites, social media, direct email and consumer loyalty programmes. Eligibility required age ≥18 in the country of residence and ability and willingness to complete the survey. Quota sampling uses census or national data from 2020–2023 for age, sex, household income, area of residence (rural/urban), and language where relevant. Enrollment is first-come-first-served within strata. Post-stratification weighting is planned to address representativeness. Surveys are anonymous, voluntary, consent-based and can be withdrawn before submission; submitted surveys cannot be retracted. Duplicate records are blocked.

## Survey and measures

The approximately 20-minute core survey has four sections: social determinants of health; health status and wellbeing; health behaviours and habits; and health services and insurance coverage. It contains around 50 online screens, with conditional questions used to limit burden. Selection used a modified Delphi process and local adaptation while retaining cross-country comparability. Measures include the EQ-5D-5L, EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons, EQ-HWB, PROMIS-10, ASCOT or ICECAP-A, WHO-5, OPQOL-brief, PHQ-2, GAD-2 and an EQ-5D-5L response-scale heterogeneity vignette. Social determinants include age, sex, gender, marital status, education, years of education, employment, income, financial deprivation, ethnicity, country born, years lived in country, region, area, household size and related variables (Methods—Study survey; Table 1).

The EQ-5D-5L describes mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression “today,” with responses from “no problems” (level 1) to “extreme problems/unable to” (level 5). EQ VAS rates overall health today from 0 (“the worst health you can imagine”) to 100 (“the best health you can imagine”). Bolt-ons vary by country and include vision, hearing, breathing, sleep, tiredness, social relationships, cognition, skin irritation and self-confidence. EQ-HWB is an experimental preference-based 25-dimension health-and-wellbeing instrument referring to “over the last 7 days”; its short version has nine dimensions. PROMIS-10 has nine five-point Likert questions and one numeric rating. ASCOT-SCT4 has nine preference-weighted four-point items. ICECAP-A covers stability, attachment, autonomy, achievement and enjoyment “at the moment.” WHO-5 concerns the last two weeks and has six responses from “all of the time” to “at no time.” OPQOL-brief has 14 items, with adaptive display at age 65+. PHQ-2 and GAD-2 assess depressive and anxiety symptoms over the last two weeks, with score ≥3 indicating symptoms (Methods—Study survey).

EQ-5D-5L and its response-scale heterogeneity vignette are fixed at the beginning, because the vignette asks respondents to rate “Alex’s health” while imagining Alex has the same age and background. Other standardised measures are randomised. Surveys were translated into Spanish, French, Portuguese, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Dutch, German and other required languages; native speakers and local researchers reviewed wording and response options. A soft launch of 250 responses was reviewed before full launch.

## Governance, data quality and intended products

The EQ-DAPHNIE Project Team has Population Health and Instrument Comparison sub-teams, a monthly Project Executive Committee, a Data Access Review Team (DART), and external advisors. The resulting datasets are intended to support population-health comparisons, social-determinant analyses, instrument comparison and future research. Data are currently restricted to EuroQol Group members or collaboration with members; the core survey is available on request. Funding is EuroQol Research Foundation grant 367-RA (Methods—Governance; Data availability).

## Limitations and stated gaps

Web surveys can exclude people with poor or no internet access and online panels can introduce selection and representativeness bias. Cultural differences can alter measure interpretation and cross-country comparability. The cross-sectional design limits longitudinal questions; the authors suggest longitudinal subsets and serial biennial or triennial panels. Future rounds were planned for additional regions. Local ethical approvals include University of Alberta Pro00123401, University of Otago H23/130 and Advarra Pro00077236 (Discussion—Limitations; Ethical considerations).
