# Exploring the relation between the EQ-5D-5L pain/discomfort and pain and itching in a sample of burn patients

## Aim, setting, and sample

The study examined whether the single EQ-5D-5L pain/discomfort (PD) domain captures pain and itching, and whether it reflects variability in these symptoms. Adult burn patients had been hospitalized for at least one day or had surgery in one of three Dutch burn centres between August 2011 and September 2012; an additional severe-burn sample was admitted January 2010–March 2013. Exclusions were pre-existing cognitive impairment and insufficient Dutch. The survey was mailed March 2017–March 2018, 5–7 years after injury. Of 517 eligible patients, 257 participated and 245 (47.4%) had complete EQ-5D-5L and POSAS data. Mean age was 47.4 years, 62.0% were male, median burned total body surface area was 6.0%, and mean hospital stay was 17.9 days. Subgroups were 183 without and 62 with pre-existing chronic disease; separate symptom groups included 10 with POSAS pain and no itching and 65 with itching and no pain. [Abstract; Methods—Participants; Results—Table 1]

## Measures and methods

Participants completed paper-and-pencil Dutch EQ-5D-5L and the patient part of POSAS 2.0. EQ-5D-5L dimensions were mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each with “no problems”, “slight problems”, “moderate problems”, “severe problems”, and “extreme problems”. The Dutch value set produced an index from 0 (death) to 1 (full health); EQ-VAS ranged 0–100. POSAS pain and itching were each scored on a 10-point scale from 1 (no pain/itch) to 10 (extreme pain/itch), for the patient’s most severe scar. The POSAS worst count variable used the higher pain or itching score. [Methods—Measures]

Descriptive and non-response analyses used Mann–Whitney U and chi-square tests. Head-to-head comparisons assessed corresponding answers (EQ-5D-5L PD >1 and POSAS >1) by chi-square. Convergent validity used Spearman rank correlations, with weak 0.10–0.29, moderate 0.30–0.49, and strong ≥0.50. Multivariable regression predicted EQ-5D-5L PD from POSAS pain and itching, with age, sex, comorbidity, hospital stay, %TBSA, surgeries, and aetiology. A second model predicted EQ-VAS. Hypotheses specified comparable PD associations with pain and itching, lower associations for other EQ-5D domains, and stronger pain association in patients without comorbidity. [Methods—Data analyses and Hypotheses]

## Findings

- Mean EQ-5D-5L index was 0.87 (SD 0.18), EQ-VAS 81.6 (15.7); 39.2% reported at least slight PD problems. POSAS identified pain in 25.7% and itching in 48.2%. Patients with comorbidity had worse EQ-5D-5L and POSAS outcomes. [Results—Table 1; EQ-5D-5L and POSAS outcomes]
- PD and POSAS pain responses corresponded in 73% of patients and were related (*χ*²=40.735, *p*<0.001); 27% were non-corresponding, and 75% of those reported no POSAS pain but slight-to-severe EQ-5D-5L PD. In the pain/no-itching subgroup, 70% reported EQ-5D-5L PD problems. [Results—Head-to-head comparisons; Table 2]
- PD and POSAS itching responses corresponded in 70%; 30% were non-corresponding, with 65% of the non-corresponding group reporting POSAS itching but no EQ-5D-5L PD. In the itching/no-pain subgroup, 48% reported EQ-5D-5L PD problems. PD and POSAS itching were still related (*χ*²=38.741, *p*<0.001). [Results—Head-to-head comparisons; Table 2]
- Spearman correlations were 0.468 for PD–pain and 0.473 for PD–itching (both *p*<0.001). In the pain/no-itching subgroup, PD–pain was 0.585 (*p*=0.076; strong but small subgroup); in itching/no-pain, PD–itching was 0.408 (*p*=0.001; moderate). The PD–POSAS worst-count correlation was 0.530. Other EQ-5D domains had lower correlations: pain 0.099–0.359 and itching 0.058–0.301. [Results—Table 3]
- In multivariable models, POSAS pain (unstandardized β=0.14) and itching (β=0.08) independently predicted PD (both *p*<0.001). With comorbidity, pain remained significant (β=0.15, *p*=0.040) but itching did not (β=0.08, *p*=0.155); without comorbidity, pain β=0.14 and itching β=0.09 (both *p*<0.001). EQ-VAS was associated with PD (β=−7.94, *p*<0.001), but not POSAS pain or itching. [Results—Variability in pain and itching]

## Interpretation and limits

The broader PD domain captures both pain and itching in this burn sample, but separate POSAS items are more sensitive. Pain appears sufficiently covered; itching evidence is less conclusive, and 48 POSAS-itching patients had no PD problem. The authors attribute discordance partly to different instructions: EQ-5D-5L asks about health “today”, whereas POSAS asks about the most severe scar and the past weeks; patients may not regard itching as discomfort or may overlook it. Other scar features were outside scope. Low symptom variability and few severe cases limit scale-correspondence analysis. EQ-5D-5L is not a replacement for POSAS when the primary aim is scar-quality assessment. [Discussion; Conclusions]
