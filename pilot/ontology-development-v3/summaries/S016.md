## Purpose and scale

The protocol addresses two innovations: valuing EQ-5D-Y-3L (Y-3L) profiles on an “experience scale” instead of a QALY scale, and comparing Kaizen tasks with paired comparisons (Abstract; Introduction). No scenario describes a child’s death. The experience scale sets 0 to “being in a coma” and 1 to full health for one week; positive values indicate experiences better than coma and negative values worse than coma. The authors note that this scale may not yet be suitable for cost-utility analyses because it does not trade quality against length of life.

The study concerns a generic 10-year-old child experiencing a one-week episode. The Y-3L has five three-level attributes: mobility (walking around), looking after myself, doing usual activities, having pain or discomfort, and feeling worried, sad or unhappy. A child is alternatively described as having “health problems” or being “in a coma.” The protocol includes a coma definition and a five-question coma quiz covering causes, self-care, duration, recovery, and outcomes (Methods, “Decision model and descriptive framework”). Respondents confirm that cause, recovery, the rest of the child’s life, and survival are the same in coma and health-problem scenarios.

## Sample, tasks, and design

The planned online survey recruits 600 US adults from a panel, with quotas for gender, age (18–34, 35–54, 55+), and race/ethnicity to approximate the 2021 American Community Survey. Eligibility requires US residence, English reading, internet/device access, and consent. The survey is programmed in LimeSurvey Community Edition V5.6; completion is expected to take about 20 minutes (Methods, “Data collection”; “Survey instrument”).

Each respondent is randomly assigned to one of four D-efficient blocks. Each block contains five coma comparisons (Y-3L versus coma), ten paired comparisons (Y-3L versus Y-3L), and ten Kaizen tasks. A coma comparison resolves one inequality. A paired comparison resolves one inequality between two profiles. In a Kaizen task, respondents sequentially select three of four possible improvements, generating one of 24 preference paths and resolving 11 inequalities. The coma component is presented first; the paired-comparison and Kaizen components are randomised in order, task order within each component is randomised, and paired-comparison left/right position is randomised. The coma component starts with a warm-up (33333 versus coma) and confirmation checklist; the Kaizen component starts with a warm-up (33331 to 11111).

The ten Kaizen origin–destination (OD) pairs were selected from 405 candidates (five possible hold-outs × 3^4 improvement combinations), reduced to 321 after excluding predicted probabilities below 0.15 under De Moivre’s NP5 rule and McFadden’s positivity assumption. Blocks were screened for unique origins, each attribute as hold-out twice, and each level 1–2 and 2–3 increment at least three times. Five hundred candidate blocks were sampled and the most D-efficient block selected, repeated four times. Hold-out levels were then reassigned for utility balance and unique OD pairs (Methods, “Experimental design”). Each pair has one hold-out to deter lexicographic preferences.

## Interview content and planned analysis

The instrument has consent/screener, background, coma comparisons, paired comparisons, Kaizen, and follow-up/debrief sections. Background includes US-English EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS plus the coma quiz. Debrief asks which task was easier, preferred, and easier to understand; it also collects socioeconomic, work, family, political, and open-text feedback.

Descriptive analyses examine sample selection, experimental design, response behavior, and response frequencies. Primary analysis estimates main-effect conditional-logit models by maximum likelihood and compares task evidence. Sensitivity analysis uses maximum simulated likelihood mixed logit. Secondary analyses are specified in online supplemental appendix 3 (Methods, “Data analysis”). The study hypothesizes significant Y-3L main effects, concordant Kaizen responses with smaller standard errors than paired comparisons, and coma better than 33333.

## Ethics, strengths, and limitations

Advarra IRB project Pro00072276 (27 June 2023) was exempt under 45 CFR 46.104(d)(2) and did not require continuing review. Beta-testing involved members of the general population. Data and code were planned for reasonable-request access and peer-reviewed dissemination.

Strengths include avoiding distressing child-death scenarios, collecting more preference evidence per Kaizen task, and deliberate over-powering to compare methods and simulate smaller samples. Limitations include online-panel generalisability, possible exclusion of less educated non-internet users, one child age and one-week duration, and uncertain suitability of experience-scale values for QALYs. The protocol does not report study findings because it is a protocol.
