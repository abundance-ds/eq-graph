# Comparison of the psychometric performance of experimental EuroQol Toddler and Infant Populations Instrument (EQ-TIPS) and Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory™ (PedsQL) in young children

## Aim and sample

The study compared validity, reliability, and responsiveness of experimental EQ-TIPS version 2.0 and PedsQL version 4.0 in Australian children aged 2–3 years. Data were from the Australian Paediatric Multi-Instrument Comparison (P-MIC) study, recruited at Royal Children’s Hospital Melbourne and through Pureprofile. Primary caregivers completed the instruments in randomised order from a proxy perspective at baseline and four-week follow-up. Baseline included 510 caregivers; 286 completed follow-up (Abstract; Methods, “Data and sample”).

## Instruments and analysis

EQ-TIPS is for children aged 1–36 months and has six descriptive items: Movement, Play, Pain, Communication, Social interaction, and Eating, plus EQ VAS 0–100. It uses “today” recall, three levels (no problems, some problems, a lot of problems), and LSS 6–18; lower LSS indicates better HRQOL. PedsQL toddler core generic version 4.0 uses 21 items across physical, emotional, social, and school functioning, with “during the past ONE month” recall and transformed 0–100 scores; higher scores indicate better HRQOL (Methods, “Measures”).

Known groups used general health (excellent, very good, good, fair/poor), special healthcare needs (SHCNs), atopy, neurological conditions, sleep problems, and a healthy reference group without conditions and EQ VAS ≥70. Analyses assessed acceptability/time and difficulty, distributions and ceiling/floor, Mann–Whitney/Kruskal–Wallis known-group tests and Cohen’s D, Spearman convergent validity, weighted kappa and ICC test–retest reliability, and standardised response means (SRM) for responsiveness. A PedsQL proxy minimal clinically important difference (MCID) was 4.5 points (Methods, “Data analysis”).

## Findings

EQ-TIPS had a 25.8% ceiling in children with SHCNs, but no ceiling in fair/poor-health children (12.9%); PedsQL had no ceiling. EQ-TIPS showed larger effect sizes for distinguishing health conditions and known groups. Item problems were most frequent for Eating, followed by Communication and Social interaction (Results; Discussion). Hypothesised EQ-TIPS–PedsQL pairs had moderate-to-strong correlations, including Movement with PedsQL walking/running, Play with active-play items, Pain with hurts/aches, and Social interaction with social functioning.

For 173 stable children, EQ-TIPS item weighted kappas ranged 0.48 (Pain) to 0.66 (Communication), indicating moderate to substantial agreement. EQ-TIPS LSS ICC was 0.89 (0.85–0.91), while PedsQL total ICC was 0.77 (0.70–0.82); PedsQL domain ICCs ranged 0.66–0.76 (Results, “Test-retest reliability”; Table 5). Among caregivers reporting “much better,” EQ-TIPS LSS changes were in the expected negative direction but not statistically significant (mean −0.42 for general health; −0.73 for main condition; small SRM). PedsQL changes were 4.27 and 4.65; the main-condition change was statistically significant and reached the 4.5 MCID (Results, “Responsiveness”; Table 6).

Both instruments performed comparably at ages 2 and 3. EQ-TIPS was quicker and easier to complete, stronger for known-group effect sizes and test–retest reliability, and more sensitive to pain/eating. PedsQL was more sensitive to emotional aspects and reported health improvements. Limitations include proxy-reported rather than clinically verified conditions, few severe cases, small responsiveness samples, no worsening-health analysis, four-week non-intervention follow-up, EQ-TIPS LSS without preference weights, and no infants under 24 months (Discussion; Conclusion).
