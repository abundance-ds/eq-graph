## Aim, instrument and samples

The study developed an Indonesian EQ-5D-Y-3L value set using adults’ preferences for a 10-year-old child. It tested whether expanding cTTO beyond the standard ten states improves mapping from DCE latent values to the full-health/dead scale. EQ-5D-Y-3L has mobility (walking about), looking after myself, doing usual activities, pain or discomfort, and feeling worried, sad or unhappy, with levels no, some and many problems. DCE used 1072 Indonesian adults in face-to-face interviews, each completing 15 pairs. cTTO was collected separately from 222 adults living in Java for 23 states. DCE and cTTO respondents were not the same (Abstract; Instrument; Valuation Tasks).

The DCE protocol used a paper-based survey with 10 blocks of 15 pairs, randomised order and left/right presentation, attribute-level overlap and colour coding. Its framing was: “Considering your views for a 10-year-old child, which health state do you prefer?”. cTTO used conventional TTO for BTD states and lead-time TTO for WTD states in computer-assisted personalised interviews using EuroQol Portable Valuation Technology. The 23 states comprised 18 orthogonal-array states, two added severe states (33332 and 32232), and five protocol states; 33333 appeared in both blocks. The design intentionally increased state coverage but reduced observations per state to approximately 100 rather than 200 (Valuation Tasks; Table 1; Data Collection; Quality Control).

## Analysis and data

The mixed-logit DCE model estimated ten main effects (levels 2 and 3, five dimensions), with level 1 as reference. Coefficients were logically ordered and significant at 0.01. Dimension order by weight was pain/discomfort, mobility, usual activities, worried/sad/unhappy and self-care (DCE Model). The final DCE sample comprised 1072 respondents after 18 of 1090 completed interviews failed the logically dominant-pair check; the final cTTO sample comprised 222 after six of 228 interviews were excluded for non-trading or inability to understand the task. The cTTO respondents supplied 2664 observations. The feedback module flagged 222 (8.3%). Four observations were 0 and 238 (8.9%) were negative; 76% of negative observations were in 33332 and 33333. Mean observed values ranged from 0.939 for 11121 to −0.185 for 33333 (Respondents; Composite TTO Data Characteristics).

Linear, linear-without-constant, power and power-without-constant mapping functions related DCE values to mean cTTO. Mapping performance used R-squared and mean absolute error. Linear mapping mispredicted mild or severe states; non-linear models fit better. Power mapping without a constant had coefficient 0.1048, power 1.9013, R-squared 0.9773 and MAE 0.0598. Although the power model with a constant had slightly better fit, the no-constant model was selected because it predicted mild states better (Anchoring Results).

The final value set is:

`U = 1 − (0.1317 MO2 + 0.2265 MO3 + 0.1017 SC2 + 0.1793 SC3 + 0.1441 UA2 + 0.2093 UA3 + 0.1256 PD2 + 0.2277 PD3 + 0.1283 AD2 + 0.2016 AD3)^1.9013`.

It ranges from 1.000 for 11111 to −0.086 for 33333. State 12312 is 0.791. Pain/discomfort had the largest effect and self-care the smallest. The value set is intended for Indonesian child health economic evaluations and HRQoL studies (Anchoring Results; Conclusions).

## Interpretation and limitations

The cTTO distribution was non-linear: the two worst states were much lower than a standard linear model predicted. A hybrid model was rejected because DCE observations (16,080) greatly outnumbered cTTO observations (2220) and would dominate. The selected power mapping violates the interval-scale assumption that would favour a linear transformation, but it better approximated observed cTTO across the severity range. The authors note that 23 cTTO states exposed this non-linearity more clearly than ten states.

Limitations include too few cTTO observations in the severe region, a non-representative Java cTTO sample, different sampling for DCE and cTTO, uncertainty from two outlier observations, and the selected mapping’s theoretical drawback. Compared with Indonesian adult EQ-5D-5L valuation, youth cTTO negative values were much smaller (2.5% versus 35.4%) and dimension importance differed: pain/discomfort was most important for youth but least important for adults. The authors state this supports separate adult and youth value sets (Discussion).
