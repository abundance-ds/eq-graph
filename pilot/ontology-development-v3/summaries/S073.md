# EQ-5D-5L measurement properties are superior to EQ-5D-3L across the continuum of health using US value sets

## Aim and data

The study compared three US EQ-5D value-set scoring approaches across the spectrum of health: the US EQ-5D-3L value set (3L), the US EQ-5D-5L value set (5L), and the EQ-5D-5L-to-EQ-5D-3L crosswalk value set (5L > 3L, called crosswalk). It examined range of scale, worse-than-dead (WTD) states, single-level transitions, discriminative ability, and responsiveness. The stated implication concerned health measurement and cost-effectiveness analysis, including potential effects on incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (Abstract; Background).

Two cross-sectional data sources included respondents who completed both descriptive systems and EQ VAS: the 2017 US 5L valuation study (1,133 respondents, quota-sampled for age, gender, race, and ethnicity to represent the 2017 US general population) and an international 3L/5L parallel fielding dataset (3,790 respondents from healthy and disease populations). Disease groups included diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, rheumatoid arthritis/arthritis, stroke, cardiovascular disease, depression, personality disorders, and a student group (Methods—Data sources; Table 1). The study used the US 3L tariff by Shaw et al., the US crosswalk by Van Hout et al., and the 2019 US 5L tariff by Pickard et al.

## Instruments, analysis, and measures

The EQ-5D-3L has mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, with three levels and 243 health states. The EQ-5D-5L retains these five dimensions with five levels and 3,125 health states. Both are preference-based scores anchored at 1 for full health and 0 for death; negative values represent WTD states. EQ VAS was used as an external health anchor (0–100) (Background; Methods—EQ-5D value sets).

Theoretical comparisons used range of scale, number and percentage of WTD states, utility difference between 11111 and the next-best state, mean single-dimension-level utility transition, scatterplots, and smoothed kernel density plots. A single-level transition averaged all possible one-level deteriorations and improvements. Empirical analyses used SAS 9.4 and R studio 1.3.1056. Discriminative ability used ANOVA F-statistic ratios across self-reported general-health groups and ten EQ VAS strata (0–10 through 91–100); a ratio above 1.0 indicated greater relative efficiency for the numerator (the 5L). Bootstrap resampling used 1,000 samples and 95% confidence intervals. A novel responsiveness analysis applied triangular-distribution weighted bootstrap sampling to generate 1,000 samples of 1,000 respondents over EQ VAS 0–100 (Methods—Analysis).

## Findings

The 5L had the broadest scale (1.573), compared with 1.109 for both 3L and crosswalk. It assigned 620/3,125 states (19.8%) as WTD, compared with 10/243 (4.1%) for 3L and 39/3,125 (1.2%) for crosswalk. The utility gap between 11111 and the next-best state was 0.057 for 5L, 0.140 for 3L, and 0.112 for crosswalk. Mean single-level transition was 0.078 (SD 0.014) for 5L, 0.111 (0.029) for 3L, and 0.061 (0.017) for crosswalk (Results—Theoretical value set characteristics; Table 2). Scatterplots showed more consistent transitions across severity for 5L. The 3L and crosswalk each had an outlier at 11111. The 5L kernel density was closest to a normal, single-maximum distribution; 3L had multiple local maxima and crosswalk was skewed (Figures 1–2).

In US valuation data, 5L tended to be more discriminative than crosswalk (F-statistic ratio 1.111, 95% CI 0.989–1.240) and 3L (1.102, 0.861–1.383) across general-health levels. Across EQ VAS categories, 5L was most discriminative in both datasets, with F-statistic ratios from 1.050 to 1.430. Within disease groups, 5L was usually more discriminative; crosswalk exceeded 5L in diabetes (0.981), rheumatoid arthritis/arthritis (0.935), and stroke (0.962), while other ratios ranged from 1.077 to 1.513 (Results—Discriminative ability).

In simulations using the US valuation sample, mean 5L utility ranged from 0.749 to 0.876, crosswalk from 0.790 to 0.871, and 3L from 0.806 to 0.889 across EQ VAS values. In the parallel fielding sample, 5L ranged from 0.489 to 0.734, crosswalk from 0.630 to 0.783, and 3L from 0.625 to 0.784. In disease groups, 5L generally produced lower values and steeper changes between EQ VAS 25 and 75. Differences were less clear in stroke and personality disorders (Results—Responsiveness; Figure 4).

## Interpretation and limits

The authors interpret 5L as having better interval properties, precision, discrimination, and responsiveness than 3L and crosswalk. Greater changes in 5L index values could produce lower incremental cost-effectiveness ratios when survival effects are similar. The evidence gap is greatest for very poor health because few patients had EQ VAS below 50. Limitations include few datasets with paired 3L/5L responses, no trial or longitudinal data, use of EQ VAS administered after 5L, and possible order effects. Responsiveness was anchored only to EQ VAS, and findings may depend on disease state and geography (Discussion; Limitations).
