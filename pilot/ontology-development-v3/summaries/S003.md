## Aim, population and protocol

The objective was to develop a Polish EQ-5D-5L utility tariff for cost-utility analysis. Poland’s AOTMiT recommends EQ-5D for valuing health states and QALYs. The study used quota sampling based on Polish census data for age, sex, education, geographical region and locality size. Adults aged 18 years and older were recruited in public venues and homes and received an approximately €8 voucher. Data were collected June–October 2016 by 15 interviewers. Of 1570 interviews, 1252 respondents remained after excluding flagged interviews and interviews failing the interviewer quality criterion. The analysis sample was 52.5% female, age 18–91 (mean 46.2, SD 17.6), and was reported as representative (Study Design; Sample Characteristics).

EQ-VT 2.0 computer-assisted personal interviews included self-reported EQ-5D-5L and background questions, cTTO, feedback, DCE and Polish country-specific questions. cTTO used conventional TTO for BTD states and lead-time TTO for WTD states; values ranged −1 to 1 in 0.05 increments. It included a wheelchair example, three practice states, ten real states, debriefing and a feedback module. The cTTO design had 86 states in 10 blocks: one of five mild states, 55555 and eight intermediate states per block. DCE used 196 pairs in 28 blocks of seven, with randomised order and side position (Valuation Interview).

## Data quality and modelling

Flagged interviews, the first ten interviews of an interviewer who did not meet a minimum of seven unflagged interviews, and individual cTTO values flagged by respondents were excluded. No individual DCE values were excluded. The Results report 12,520 available cTTO valuations, of which 1040 (8.3%) were removed by the feedback module, leaving 11,480 for modelling; DCE had 8764 pairs. In 10.7% of cTTO experiments no time was traded, 13.5% were WTD, 6.1% were reported as −1, and mean observed 55555 was −0.408. DCE suspicious patterns were retained (Data Characteristics).

The final analysis used a Bayesian hybrid approach with TTO and DCE dependent variables. JAGS within R used non-informative priors and Markov-chain Monte Carlo (four chains; 2000 adaptive, 30,000 burn-in and 20,000 actual iterations for the final model). Posterior medians were point estimates; 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles gave 95% credible intervals. Deviance information criterion (DIC) and potential scale reduction factors assessed fit and convergence. Models tested main effects only, no constant and no interactions. Random parameters allowed person-level differences in dimension importance; the error used a generalised t-Student distribution with scale increasing with theoretical disutility. cTTO was censored at −1. DCE used a Cauchy rather than logit distribution. A religion parameter scaled TTO disutilities for respondents whose religious beliefs could produce unwillingness to trade time (General Approach; Intermediate Models).

The paper reports intermediate models: M1 panel random effects; M2 fixed Bayesian; M3 random parameters; M4 M3 plus t-Student error scaling; and M5 M4 plus religion scaling. The final model added DCE and censoring. Comparisons used kernel density functions, matched median/worst states and scatterplots against Polish EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L cross-walk value sets (Value Set Comparison).

## Value set and findings

The final disutility at level 5 was 0.314 (MO), 0.264 (SC), 0.205 (UA), 0.575 (PD) and 0.232 (AD). Thus PD was most important, followed by MO, SC, AD and UA. Selected predicted utilities were u(22222)=0.873, u(33333)=0.800, u(44444)=0.296 and u(55555)=−0.590. The directly measured EQ-5D-5L set had a range comparable to the Polish EQ-5D-3L and cross-walk sets, but more values near 1 and greater sensitivity to moderate decline. The authors state that it supports continuity with past reimbursement decisions and can be considered by Central and Eastern European countries without national data (Preferred Model; Comparison of Polish Value Sets).

The authors describe three important modelling choices as statistically motivated but partly arbitrary: hybrid combination of TTO and DCE, a simple main-effects/no-constant/no-interactions structure, and religion scaling. The value −0.590 is below the observed 55555 mean because censoring treats observed −1 values as potentially lower, religion scaling increases true disutility, and severe-state error variance is larger. Limitations include sparse cTTO data for severe states, assumptions in religion correction, the trade-off between predictive fidelity and an easily usable tariff, and possible cultural or wording differences. The paper concludes that this is a Polish TTO/DCE-based EQ-5D-5L value set suitable for Polish economic evaluations and a possible option for CEE contexts (Discussion; Conclusions).
