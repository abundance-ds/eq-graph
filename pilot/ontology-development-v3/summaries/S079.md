# Validity, reliability, and responsiveness of the EQ-5D in diseases of the upper respiratory tract: a systematic review of measurement properties

## Aim and eligibility

The review synthesised construct validity, test-retest reliability, and responsiveness of EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L in adult diseases of the upper respiratory tract, including nose, sinus, pharynx, and larynx conditions. MEDLINE, EMBASE, and the EuroQol publication database were searched from inception to 21 November 2024. Original full-text English articles were eligible; experimental or unapproved EQ-5D versions, reviews, letters, abstracts, and non-empirical studies were excluded. The protocol was registered in PROSPERO (CRD42023481369) (Abstract; Methods—Guidelines, Eligibility, Search).

The search yielded 540 records. After duplicate removal, 382 abstracts were screened, 57 full texts assessed, and 23 publications included, representing 22 studies and 20–17,780 patients (Abstract; Results—Selection). Most evidence concerned chronic rhinosinusitis (10 articles) and allergic rhinitis (5). Sixteen studies used 5L, six used 3L, and one did not specify the version. English was used in 17 publications; German, Japanese, Spanish, and Thai each appeared once, with two international publications using 19 languages. Patients were adults (one study included age 16+), mean age 47.7; most studies were from the United States (Results—EQ-5D and study characteristics; Table 1).

## Methods and outcomes

EQ-5D consists of five dimensions—mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression—and EQ VAS 0–100. EQ-5D-3L has three levels and EQ-5D-5L five levels; 11111 is optimal health. Studies reported EQ VAS, EQ-5D Index, dimensions, or level sum scores. All included studies used patient self-report; no proxy reporting was found (Methods—EQ-5D questionnaire; Results—EQ-5D characteristics).

Two reviewers assessed measurement tests with the COSMIN Risk of Bias checklist and applied the “worst score counts” principle. Convergent validity hypotheses used correlations above 0.60 as strong, 0.40–0.59 moderate, 0.21–0.39 weak, and ≤0.20 no correlation. Known-groups validity was assessed by expected group differences. Responsiveness used standardized effect size (SES) or AUC, positive if ≥0.7. Test-retest reliability was planned with ICC ≥0.7. Summary ratings were sufficient when at least 75% of tests were positive; certainty used modified GRADE (Methods—Measurement properties and Certainty assessment).

## Findings

Construct validity was tested in all 23 publications: 18 examined convergent validity and eight known-groups validity. Across 78 convergent-validity tests, 77 (99%) met hypotheses. For EQ-5D-5L, 52/53 tests (98%) were positive; EQ-5D-3L had 23/23 positive. Across 63 known-groups tests, 61 (97%) were positive; EQ-5D-5L had 54/56 (96%) and EQ-5D-3L 7/7 (100%). The overall quality was sufficient for EQ VAS (99%) and EQ-5D Index (97%). Disease-specific rates included 92% positive EQ VAS results in allergic rhinitis and 92% positive EQ-5D Index results in allergic rhinitis; head and neck cancer EQ-5D Index was 88% positive (Results—Results of synthesis; Tables 2–4).

Version-specific EQ-5D Index construct validity was sufficient for 3L (100% positive) and 5L (96%). Certainty was high for both versions overall, with downgrades for imprecision in rhinitis, chronic rhinosinusitis, and head and neck cancer subgroups. EQ VAS construct validity had high certainty overall. There was no test-retest reliability evidence in any eligible publication.

Responsiveness appeared in only two publications, with four tests, all for EQ VAS. Three of four tests showed the expected directional change; overall responsiveness was sufficient by the review’s threshold, but certainty was moderate and downgraded for risk of bias. Evidence did not establish responsiveness of EQ-5D Index or either version specifically (Results—Responsiveness and certainty).

## Interpretation and limits

Both EQ-5D-3L and 5L have sufficient construct validity, especially convergent validity, in upper respiratory tract disease and can assess HRQoL in chronic rhinosinusitis and allergic rhinitis. Caution is needed for detecting clinically important change because responsiveness evidence is sparse and limited to EQ VAS. Test-retest reliability remains an evidence gap. Generalisability is limited by concentration on chronic rhinosinusitis and allergic rhinitis, mainly US samples, English-language eligibility, and populations generally over 40. Other gaps include asthma, nasal polyps, rhinitis, and wider geographic settings (Discussion; Conclusions).
