# Impact of mapped EQ-5D utilities on cost-effectiveness analysis: in the case of dialysis treatments

## Aim and data

The study tested whether EQ-5D utilities mapped from SF-12 produce comparable cost-utility results to directly observed EQ-5D, and assessed SF-6D as an alternative preference-based measure. It re-ran two 10-year Markov models for haemodialysis (HD) and peritoneal dialysis (PD) in non-diabetic and diabetic end-stage renal disease. The models used Singaporean local data, societal perspective and 2015 Singapore dollars. [Source: Abstract; Methods—Decision analytic models]

A consecutive cross-sectional sample contained 75 HD and 75 PD patients treated for at least three months. Respondents completed EQ-5D-5L, SF-12, disease-specific KDQOL-36 and sociodemographic questions. EQ-5D-5L assesses health on the survey day; SF-12 has a four-week recall and produces physical and mental component summaries. [Source: Methods—Quality of life data]

## Utility derivation and model analysis

Observed utilities used the England EQ-5D-5L value set (composite TTO and DCE; range −0.285 to 1). Five SF-12-to-EQ-5D-3L mappings produced “mEQ-5D”: three direct OLS utility mappings and two multinomial-logit response mappings. The mapping functions were developed mainly from US general-population samples; one used a low-income, predominantly minority community-health-centre sample. SF-6D utilities used seven SF-12 items and a UK standard-gamble value set (range 0.345–1). The mapped EQ-5D functions used UK EQ-5D-3L values. Individual utilities entered multivariate linear models controlling for sociodemographics to estimate mean HD, PD and transplant utilities. [Source: Methods—Estimation of utilities; Table 1]

Each model simulated a hypothetical cohort of 10,000. HD versus PD incremental costs, QALYs and ICERs were calculated with 3% annual discounting. Non-parametric bootstrap percentile confidence intervals used 1,000 replications. [Source: Methods—Analysis]

## Results

HD utility was higher than PD under every approach. Mapped EQ-5D and SF-6D values were lower than directly observed EQ-5D, and response mapping had wider error margins than direct mapping. Observed EQ-5D produced incremental QALYs of 2.011 in model 1 and 1.603 in model 2. mEQ-5D produced 1.343–1.399 in model 1 and 1.215–1.364 in model 2; SF-6D produced 1.427 and 1.364. Relative to observed EQ-5D, mapping reduced incremental QALYs by 14.9–33.2%; SF-6D reduced them by 29.0% in model 1 and 14.9% in model 2. [Source: Abstract; Results; Table 2]

Observed EQ-5D ICERs were SGD59,073 (model 1) and SGD70,193 (model 2). mEQ-5D ICERs were 43.7–49.7% higher in model 1 and 17.5–25.4% higher in model 2; SF-6D ICERs were 40.9% and 17.5% higher. Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals confirmed lower incremental QALYs and higher ICERs for all mapped and SF-6D estimates. Differences were driven mainly by smaller HD–PD utility differences under mapped measures. [Source: Abstract; Results—Table 2]

## Interpretation and limitations

The authors attribute discrepancies to differences between SF-12 and EQ-5D descriptive systems, recall periods, mismatch between US mapping-development samples and older/sicker dialysis patients, prediction bias and use of EQ-5D-5L values for observed data versus EQ-5D-3L values in mappings. Day-of-survey EQ-5D may reflect dialysis effects, whereas four-week SF-12 may average them. Response mapping did not clearly improve performance, although the summary-score-to-item-response function performed best among the five mappings. [Source: Discussion]

The findings imply that mapping can change reimbursement conclusions: a technology cost-effective with directly observed EQ-5D may appear not cost-effective when mapped data are used. The authors recommend directly collecting EQ-5D whenever possible and designating one preference-based measure as the jurisdictional reference case. SF-6D is not interchangeable with EQ-5D because descriptive systems, valuation methods (standard gamble versus TTO) and value ranges differ. Limitations are a single Singapore dialysis CUA, no Singapore value set for the observed data, and no primary EQ-5D-3L comparison. [Source: Discussion; Conclusions]
