# The psychometric performance of the EQ-5D-5L composite and component items in the U.S. General population and by age group

## Aim and design

The study examines whether three EQ-5D-5L composite dimensions—self-care (SC), pain/discomfort (PD), and anxiety/depression (AD)—capture the information in their six component items: washing self (WA), dressing self (DR), pain (PN), discomfort (DI), anxiety (AN), and depression (DE). It compares composites and components overall and across six age groups (18–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60–69, and 70+). The stated concern is interpretive ambiguity in composite labels and the limited evidence for SC, particularly whether splitting composites improves measurement precision. The paper reports ceiling/floor behaviour, response agreement, informativity, convergent validity, structural validity, and explanatory power for EQ VAS (Abstract; Background).

## Population, setting and data

This was an online cross-sectional US study conducted through Dynata from 27 August to 15 September 2024. The target was a representative sample of adults in the 50 states and Washington, D.C.; 18 quotas were stratified by gender, broad age (18–34, 35–54, 55+) and race/ethnicity to align with the 2020 US Census. Of 16,953 unique identifiers, 12,078 completed screening, 8,395 met eligibility, and 6,467 completed the survey. Excluding 449 respondents who completed in under 13 minutes produced 6,018 respondents. Age-group counts were 866 (18–29), 1,268 (30–39), 1,161 (40–49), 997 (50–59), 910 (60–69), and 802 (70+). Advarra determined the project exempt from IRB oversight (Pro00080475, 11 July 2024) (Methods—Data; Results—Characteristics; Appendix 1).

## Instruments and administration

The US EQ-5D-5L was administered online with its five dimensions: mobility (MO), self-care (washing self/dressing self [SC]), usual activities (UA), pain/discomfort (PD), and anxiety/depression (AD), each with five severity levels. EQ VAS asked respondents to rate health today from 0 (“the worst health you can imagine”) to 100 (“the best health you can imagine”). The six component items were shown in a fixed order in the same survey. The paper treats UA examples as illustrations, not separate components. Analyses also created module variants: EQ-4D-5L + one component, and EQ-4D-5L + both components for each composite (Background; Methods—Data and analyses).

## Analyses

R 4.4.2 was used with dplyr, vegan, EFA.dimensions, psych and ggplot2. McNemar’s test assessed composite/component response discrepancies; ceiling and floor percentages were calculated at item and instrument levels. Cross-tabulations compared each composite with the maximum response of its two components; percent agreement and Cohen’s weighted kappa were interpreted using standard thresholds. Shannon index (H′) and Shannon evenness (J′) measured absolute and relative informativity. Spearman correlations assessed convergent validity among composites, components and EQ VAS. Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) used scree inspection, Kaiser eigenvalue >1 and rotation to assess structural validity. Bivariate and multivariate linear regressions estimated explanatory power (adjusted R²) for EQ VAS and EQ-5D-5L level-sum scores; additive component terms and component interaction terms were compared with the standard instrument. All analyses were repeated by age group (Methods—Statistical analyses).

## Findings

At item level, composite ceiling percentages were lower than corresponding component ceilings, contrary to the hypothesised underreporting. SC had 9.60% reporting problems versus 8.62% for WA and 9.13% for DR; PD was 61.33% versus 61.17% for PN and 56.8% for DI; AD was 52.0% versus 49.1% for AN and 41.8% for DE. Instrument-level ceiling was 25.3% for EQ-5D-5L, 25.3% for EQ-4D-5L + WA or + DR, 25.2% for + WA + DR, 27.7% for + DI, 27.9% for + DE, 24.1% for + PN + DI, and 24.6% for + AN + DE (Results—Distributional characteristics).

Composite/component agreement was high: SC 94.5% (WA) and 94.3% (DR), PD 88.8% (PN) and 77.9% (DI), and AD 81.6% (AN) and 75.7% (DE). Weighted kappas were 0.78 (SC), 0.91 (PD), and 0.92 (AD), respectively, indicating substantial to almost perfect agreement. First-listed components had stronger agreement and correlations with their composites: WA, PN and AN exceeded DR, DI and DE. Individual informativity did not uniformly favour components. PN had H′=1.8383, J′=0.7917; DI H′=1.7403, J′=0.7495; AN H′=1.8182, J′=0.7830; and DE H′=1.6711, J′=0.7197, while SC H′=0.5643 and AD H′=1.8925. Component modules increased absolute informativity, with EQ-4D-5L + PN + DI H′=6.0431 and + AN + DE H′=6.0619 (Results—Informativity; Table 3).

EFA identified three factors: physical functioning (MO, UA and SC), PD, and AD. Components loaded with their composites, and all standardised loadings exceeded 0.60; the structure remained stable across age groups. The standard EQ-5D-5L adjusted R² for EQ VAS was 0.3956. Additive component models produced modest gains, while interaction terms increased explanatory power. EQ-4D-5L + WA×DR outperformed the standard instrument; AN×DE had the largest age-specific gain at 18–29, and PN×DI at 60–69 (Results—Structural validity and Explanatory power; Tables 5–6).

## Interpretation, limitations and implications

The authors interpret composites as generally capturing, or sometimes covering more than, component information in this sample. The fixed presentation order may explain the first-listed component effect; randomised ordering is a stated future need. Older respondents reported fewer ceiling responses for PD, PN and DI, whereas younger adults reported more mental-health problems and showed greater added value for AN×DE. Splitting SC into WA and DR produced little improvement. The online panel, quota bands that differed from reported age bands, and fixed item order limit generalisability and causal interpretation. Clinical populations with stroke, Parkinson’s disease or multiple sclerosis may differ. No utility analysis was performed. The authors also note language-version differences in the wording of SC (Discussion; Conclusions).
