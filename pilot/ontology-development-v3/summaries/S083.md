# The impact of demographic change on value set validity and obsolescence

## Aim and data

The study tested whether changes in age and gender composition contribute to value-set validity or obsolescence. It re-analysed time-trade-off (TTO) data for three EQ-5D-3L value sets at least 20 years old: United Kingdom, Japan, and United States. UK data had 3,395 respondents before exclusions (3,270 analysed), Japan 543 (536 analysed), and US 4,048 (3,915 analysed). Respondents completed 12 analysed TTO tasks in the UK and US or 17 tasks in Japan. Respondents with fewer than 10 observations or positive slopes against health-state misery index were excluded (Abstract; Methods; Results).

Demographic data came from the World Bank for the year of each TTO study and 2022. Age was grouped as 18–34, 35–54, 55–74, and 75+. The US data also contained race/ethnicity for sensitivity analysis. The national populations in all three countries became older over the intervening decades, most prominently Japan; original survey samples were not fully nationally representative (Methods—Data; Results—Table 1).

## Model and value-set comparison

Observed TTO values were censored at −1. The study fitted Bayesian heteroskedastic Tobit models with sex- and age-group-specific scale parameters. The mean model included EQ-5D-3L level-2 and level-3 decrements for mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. The standard deviation was a fourth-order polynomial of predicted health-state values. Uninformative normal priors were used for slope and variance parameters; mean-one constrained log-normal priors were used for scale parameters. Models used OpenBUGS, three chains, 10,000 burn-in iterations, and 30,000 MCMC iterations (Methods—Model specification).

Original value sets were weighted by demographic composition in the original data-collection year. Updated value sets used the same estimated preference structure weighted by 2022 age/sex composition. The difference between original and updated decrements was monitored with 95% credible intervals. The analysis therefore holds the preference structure constant and isolates demographic weighting (Methods—Value-set monitoring).

## Findings

Modelled preferences depended on age and sex. In all countries, some demographic-specific scale parameters differed from 1. In the UK, all five EQ domains had scale parameters significantly different from 1; in the US all except mobility did; in Japan pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression differed. However, the demographic reweighting had negligible impact on the final value sets.

Original and updated decrement differences were close to identical across countries. Most differences were 0.00 or smaller, maximum difference was 0.01, and all 95% credible intervals included 0. Therefore original and updated value sets were not significantly different (Results—Table 2). The US ethnicity sensitivity analysis also did not materially change validity (Abstract; Discussion).

## Interpretation and limits

The paper concludes that demographic change alone was not an important determinant of value-set validity or obsolescence, despite substantial ageing and sex-composition changes. Value sets may still become redundant or obsolete because of improved elicitation and modelling methods, changed preferences, HTA requirements, or other population changes. The conclusion depends on the assumption that the original preference structure remained constant. Only age and sex were included in the main analysis; ethnicity was tested only for the US sensitivity analysis. The authors call for research on temporal stability of preferences and accepted thresholds for value-set validity and obsolescence (Discussion; Conclusion).

The method is strong in using original TTO data from three continents and Bayesian heteroskedastic modelling. Limits include non-representative original samples, possible future demographic shifts beyond those observed, and inability to separate demographic change from changing health-state preferences. The paper does not propose an expiration date for value sets.
