## Aim, data source, and population

The study examined how sociodemographic characteristics and illness experience affect TTO-based utility scores from the Egyptian EQ-5D-5L valuation study (Abstract). Data came from an adapted translated EQ-VT study designed to develop the Egyptian Tariff for EQ-5D-5L. Trained interviewers recruited a multistratified quota sample from personal contacts and public places using Egyptian official statistics updated March 2019. The 972 respondents had mean age 36.9 years (SD 12.7), 47.7% female, 61.9% married, 59% with high-school education or more, and 75.2% employed (Methods, “Data Source”; Results, Table 1).

## Instrument and elicitation

EQ-5D-5L describes mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each at five levels: no, mild, moderate, severe, and unable/extreme. States range from 11111 to 55555. The EQ-VT design used cTTO and DCE. Eighty-six hypothetical states were assigned to 10 blocks; each block included 55555, one very mild state, and eight unique states. cTTO used conventional TTO for states better than dead and lead-time TTO (LT-TTO) for states considered worse than dead. Raw cTTO scores ranged from 1 to −1 and were the dependent variable (Methods, “Dependent Variable…”).

The country-specific questionnaire collected age, sex, education, region, dwelling, religion, marital status, household size, employment, insurance, chronic conditions, quality-versus-quantity preference, previous illness experience, beliefs about life/death, and self-rated EQ-5D-5L/VAS. The paper reports a pictorial tool for illiterate/less-educated participants in the underlying Egyptian valuation study in its Discussion.

## Statistical analysis

Univariate and multivariable censored linear regression models adjusted for health-state severity. Severity was represented by five dimension-specific ordinal variables. The final model included all examined independent variables; age/sex interactions with comorbidity and quality-versus-quantity preference were tested and not significant. STATA 15.0 `intreg` was used with point-data specification; approximate fit was the R² between predicted and observed values (Methods, “Data Analysis”).

## Findings

At baseline, 31.8% reported any mobility problems, 6.4% self-care, 31.5% usual activities, 60.5% pain/discomfort, and 64.2% anxiety/depression; 15.2% were 11111. Mean EQ-VAS was 77.0 (SD 16.7). Most respondents reported prior illness experience (69.7%), preference for quality over quantity (80.2%), and no religious conflict with tasks (95%) (Results, “General Characteristics”).

After severity adjustment, age, sex, education, marital status, geographical region, dwelling, health insurance, multimorbidity, and quality-versus-quantity preference were associated with TTO values in the study’s analyses. Employment, household size, religion, and previous illness experience were not significant (Abstract; Results, “Determinants…”). Respondents aged 31–45 and >60 had lower values than 18–30; females lower than males; divorced/widowed lower than single; literate respondents higher than illiterate; Alexandria and North Upper Egypt lower than Greater Cairo; insured respondents lower than uninsured; and respondents with two or more chronic conditions lower than those with none. The multivariable model R² was 54% (Discussion).

## Interpretation and limitations

The authors identify age, sex, and marital status as the main determinants, consistent with findings from other countries. They interpret lower older-age values through subjective life expectancy, dependency, and burden concerns; higher married values through longevity with family; lower female values through caring and social roles. They caution that education findings may relate to literacy, graphical state presentation, and numeracy. Results support examining subgroup differences and quotas or weighting in valuation work to inform fair policy.

Limitations were self-reported chronic conditions without severity/duration, unmeasured parenting, and limited assessment of religious adherence. The study is a secondary analysis of one Egyptian valuation sample; causal relationships are not stated. The paper recommends standardized protocols, quality control, and cross-country comparison (Discussion; Conclusions).
