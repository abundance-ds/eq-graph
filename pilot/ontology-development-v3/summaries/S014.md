## Aim, design, and participants

The study described interviewer and respondent problems in EQ-VT collection and tested whether a quality-control (QC) report, retraining, and individual feedback improved interviewer performance (Abstract; Introduction). It formed part of a national Indonesian EQ-5D-5L valuation study. Thirteen interviewers were hired to recruit 1,000 adults in Jakarta, Bandung, Jogjakarta, and surrounding areas using quota sampling for urban/rural location, gender, age, education, religion, and ethnicity (Methods, “Respondents and interviewers”). Eleven interviewers met the analysis criteria: attendance at initial training and retraining, and at least 15 post-retraining interviews. The process comparison used 266 respondent interviews: 98 before retraining and 168 after. Of 286 potential respondents, 266 participated (93% response rate).

## Instruments and QC indicators

The EQ-VT protocol was a computer-assisted face-to-face interview with five parts: welcome; EQ-5D-5L, VAS, and sociodemographics; cTTO; DCE; and round-up (Methods, “EQ-5D-5L valuation technology”). An open-ended questionnaire asked interviewers about their problems, respondents’ problems as perceived by interviewers, and solutions. Qualitative data were coded thematically in NVivo by the first author and interviewer coders.

The EuroQol QC report measured seven protocol indicators: zero TTO values; mean number of iterative steps in the wheelchair example; 55555 inconsistency; wheelchair explanation under 180 seconds; ten-task TTO duration under 5 minutes; missing wheelchair lead-time explanation; and percentage of flagged interviews. A flagged interview failed at least one criterion, and the overall target was below 40% (Methods, “Quality control (QC) report”).

Initial training covered HRQoL/EQ-5D-5L concepts, EQ-VT instructions, and group practice. After 98 interviews, 53% were flagged, so those interviews were discarded and a one-day retraining was held on 21–24 March 2015. QC reports were then produced every two days, with group and individual feedback (Methods, “Training and retraining”; “Procedure”).

## Problems and solutions

Thematic analysis identified interviewing problems/solutions and technical problems/solutions. Recruitment problems included finding suitable respondents, matching schedules, obtaining local government permissions, and locating older or minority-ethnicity respondents. Solutions were selective search, broader personal networks, convenient interview times, and detailed explanations of goals and benefits (Results, Tables 2 and 4).

Interview-process problems included explaining TTO and DCE, respondent fatigue or boredom, interviewer confidence, and adapting explanations to education and comprehension. Respondent problems, as perceived by interviewers, included cognitive difficulty distinguishing dimensions/levels and TTO versus DCE, emotional and physical burden, religious beliefs, and distraction by other people (Table 3). Some Indonesian respondents regarded “words as prayer,” resisted “instant death,” or rejected “worse-than-dead” choices. Interviewers used rephrasing, concrete examples, comparisons of each dimension, reassurance, rapport-building, encouragement, and explanation that TTO was a cognitive task rather than a prayer.

Technical problems concerned laptop battery/RAM, EQ-VT software and registration/offline upload, and weak internet. Interviewers solved these independently (charging, electrical sockets, mobile modem, regular upload) or sought help from colleagues and the EuroQol Office (Results, “Technical problems and solutions”).

## Quantitative findings and interpretation

All seven QC indicators improved significantly after retraining (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, *p* < 0.05; Table 7): zero TTO values fell from 275/98 interviews (28.1%) to 65/168 (3.9%); mean wheelchair moves rose from 14.66 to 58.98; inconsistencies fell from 6.1% to 1.2%; wheelchair explanation flags from 39.8% to 0.6%; TTO-time flags from 15.3% to 1.2%; missing lead-time explanation from 20.4% to 0.6%; and flagged interviews from 59.2% to 3.6%. The authors interpret the changes as stronger protocol compliance and engagement.

The process supports continuous QC, feedback, retraining, a small pilot for each interviewer, and academically skilled interviewers. Limitations include objective QC indicators that do not capture spoken or nonverbal interaction, possible network-based recruitment bias, unknown problems among nonparticipants, underrepresentation of older/lower-educated/rural people in the first 266 interviews, all interviews on Java, interviewer social-desirability effects, and retrospective recall bias (Discussion, “Strengths and limitations”).
