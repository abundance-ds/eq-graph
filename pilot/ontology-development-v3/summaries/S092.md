# A direct comparison of the measurement properties of the PROMIS-16 and EQ-5D-5L in the U.S. general population

## Aim, design, and sample

The study compared PROMIS-16 and EQ-5D-5L distribution, ceiling/floor percentages, informativity, construct/convergent/divergent validity, and combined latent structure. The second wave of a US online survey ran 18 March–6 April 2024. Dynata recruited adults aged ≥18 years to 18 quotas by gender (male, female, other), age (18–34, 35–54, ≥55), and race/ethnicity (Hispanic, non-Hispanic Black, non-Hispanic other), across all 50 states and Washington, D.C. Of 4989 recruits, 248 dropped before study-arm assignment, 1742 failed a three-attempt comprehension quiz about being in a coma, and 422 dropped during preference tasks/follow-up; the analytical sample was 2577 (52%). Characteristics matched the 2021 American Community Survey except never married, some college, and ≥$150,000 income. [Abstract; Methods—Data]

## Instruments and administration

PROMIS-16 has eight two-item, five-level dimensions: physical function (PF), social roles and activities (SOC), anxiety (ANX), depression (DEP), sleep disturbance (SLP), pain interference (PI), cognitive function (COG), and fatigue (FTG). EQ-5D-5L has single items for mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, plus EQ VAS. All were official US English versions, electronic, stand-alone, one item per screen; EQ-5D-5L came before choice tasks and PROMIS-16 after choice tasks. Except PF and SOC, PROMIS-16 uses a 7-day recall; EQ-5D-5L asks about health “today”. [Methods—Survey instrument; Background]

The survey also contained consent/screener, a five-question comprehension quiz, EQ-5D-Y-3L/coma paired tasks, 20 kaizen tasks, debriefing, and socioeconomic/health items. Data used the original February 2024 PROMIS-16 publication, not the August erratum: SLP item order and wording differ, and COG labels have reversed response ordering (first/last options are worst/best). This inconsistency was retained and may reveal inattentive responding. [Methods—Survey instrument]

## Analysis

Matched constructs were PF/Mobility and ANX+DEP/Anxiety/Depression; within PROMIS-16, ANX+DEP and SLP+FTG were also grouped. McNemar’s test compared paired ceiling/floor percentages. Item informativity used Shannon’s H′ (0–2.32 for five categories) and J′ (0–1). Construct validity used linear regression of EQ VAS on four indicators for incremental severity above levels 1–4, with dimension- and instrument-level sensitivity models. Polychoric correlations assessed cross-instrument, within-PROMIS, and within-EQ validity; very weak <0.20, weak 0.20–0.39, moderate 0.40–0.59, strong 0.60–0.79, and very strong ≥0.80. Exploratory factor analysis used parallel analysis and oblique Oblimin rotation; absolute loading ≥0.40 was meaningful. Analyses used R 4.4.2. [Methods—Statistical analyses; Ceiling/floor; Informativity; Validity; Structural validity]

## Findings

- **Ceiling/floor:** PROMIS item ceiling ranged 27.9% (SLP2) to 72.9% (PF2), floor 1.1% (PF1) to 24.7% (COG1). PROMIS construct ceiling/floor were PF 64.6/0.7%, SOC 50.8/0.8%, ANX 34.4/2.1%, DEP 35.5/2.5%, SLP 23.2/3.1%, PI 48.8/1.9%, COG 28.9/19.7%, FTG 25.4/4.5%. EQ-5D-5L ceiling ranged 42.7% (Pain/discomfort) to 92.2% (Self-care); floor 0.2% (Usual activities and Self-care) to 3.5% (Anxiety/depression). Matched PROMIS PF and Anxiety/Depression had significantly lower ceiling; PF had a higher floor than Mobility, but PROMIS Anxiety/Depression had a lower floor than EQ-5D-5L Anxiety/depression (*p*<0.001). [Results—Tables 1 and 2]
- **Informativity:** EQ-5D-5L item H′ ranged 0.48 (Self-care) to 1.92 (Anxiety/depression), J′ 0.21–0.83. PROMIS item H′ ranged 1.30 (PF2) to 2.20 (COG2), J′ 0.56–0.95. PROMIS items generally had greater dispersion and informativity. [Results—Informativity; Table 2]
- **Construct validity:** Greater severity generally predicted lower EQ VAS. Largest first-step declines were EQ Mobility −13.44, Self-care −20.76, and Usual activities −13.19, versus PROMIS PF1 −10.11 and PF2 −10.58 (all *p*<0.001). Positive, non-significant, or large Level-4-to-5 transitions occurred for COG1/COG2, Mobility, and Self-care. In multivariable models many effects diminished, changed sign, or lost significance; EQ-5D-5L retained stronger independent instrument-level associations. [Results—Construct validity; Table 3]
- **Convergent/divergent validity:** PROMIS PF1/PF2–Mobility was very strong (r=0.85). ANX/DEP items–EQ Anxiety/depression were strong (r=0.76–0.79). PF also correlated moderate-to-strongly with Self-care, Usual activities, and Pain/discomfort. COG had very weak or no correlations with EQ items and weak/no relations to other PROMIS items. SLP and FTG were most related to EQ Anxiety/depression. [Abstract; Results—Convergent and divergent validity; Table 4]
- **Structure:** EFA retained six factors: Physical Function, Social Roles & Activities, Anxiety / Depression, Pain Interference, Cognitive Function, and Sleep / Fatigue. Mobility, PF1/PF2, Self-care, Usual activities, and Pain/discomfort loaded on Physical Function; Anxiety/depression and ANX/DEP loaded together. SOC2 had loading 0.42 and uniqueness 0.64, unlike SOC1 (0.90; uniqueness 0.19). COG was not correlated with other factors; PI had negative factor correlations, interpreted as rotation/shared-variance effects rather than negative item relations. [Results—Structural validity; Tables 5–6]

The authors conclude that PROMIS-16 offers broader conceptual coverage and higher informativity, while EQ-5D-5L aligns more closely with EQ VAS global health. Concerns include reversed COG response framing, original-versus-erratum SLP wording, item complexity, possible satisficing after valuation tasks, non-random instrument order, commercial online-panel selection, and general-population rather than clinical sampling. Future work should test disease-specific populations, randomize order, and refine consistent labels, recall periods, and item wording. [Discussion; Conclusions]
