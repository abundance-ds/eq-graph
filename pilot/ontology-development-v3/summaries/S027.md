# Population norms for the EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L in Romania

## Aim, design and population

The study estimated Romanian population norms for the EQ-5D-5L, EQ-5D-3L, their index values and EQ VAS. It used an Omnibus cross-sectional survey in all Romanian regions from November 2018 to November 2019. A three-stage probability sample was stratified by region and settlement size: 32 settlements were selected, households by random walk from polling-station addresses, and adults within households by the next-birthday rule. Eligible respondents were adults older than 18 residing in Romania. The planned sample was 1,613 (±3% maximum error, 95% confidence); 1,674 interviews were conducted and 1,649 analysed, after 25 interviews by a non-compliant interviewer were excluded. [Source: Abstract; Methods—Study design and sample; Results]

Interviews were computer-assisted, face-to-face, at home, by trained interviewers selected from patient associations and the National Authority of Quality Management in Health. The software was EQ-VT v2.1. The five-block interview included background questions, EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS, warm-up and cTTO tasks for EQ-5D-5L and EQ-5D-3L, DCE tasks for EQ-5D-5L, the EQ-5D-3L questionnaire and a second EQ VAS, and sociodemographic questions. This paper analysed sections 1, 4 and 5 only. A random 61.5% was contacted by telephone for settlement, age and duration checks; the core questions used hard-choice format. [Source: Methods—Questionnaire; Data quality control]

## Measures and analysis

Both questionnaires describe mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression; EQ-5D-5L has five severity levels and EQ-5D-3L three. Indexes were scored with recently developed Romanian value sets. The study reported dimension frequencies, means, standard errors, standard deviations, percentiles, medians and health-state frequencies by age group, sex and residence. Standardised age groups were 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, 65–74 and 75+. Design, non-response and post-stratification weights were combined; raking used 2011 census controls for age, sex and residence. Survey-weighted estimates accounted for stratification and clustering, with bootstrapped sampling errors. STATA 18 was used. Cross-country comparisons with Central and Eastern Europe were qualitative. [Source: Methods—Statistical analysis]

## Main results

The weighted sample had mean age 47.4 years (SE 1.157); 50.3% reported good health. Women were 65% and urban residents 73.5% in the raw sample, versus 52% and 55.2% in national statistics; weighting corrected the principal sex and residence ratios, although high education remained overrepresented. Interview duration was about 47 minutes (SD 24), and there was no missing data for the five dimensions, EQ VAS, indexes, age, sex, residence or illness experience. [Source: Results; Table 1]

Pain/discomfort had the highest prevalence of problems in both instruments; self-care had the lowest. The most frequent health states were 11111 and 11121. Full health was reported by 61.2% on EQ-5D-3L and 50.3% on EQ-5D-5L. Health declined with age. Mean EQ-5D-5L index fell from 0.977 (SE 0.005) at age 18–24 to 0.765 (SE 0.017) at 75+; EQ-5D-3L fell from 0.981 (SE 0.005) to 0.784 (SE 0.019). EQ VAS decreased by 29.9 points between the youngest and oldest groups. Men aged over 35 reported fewer pain/discomfort problems than women; older women generally reported poorer health. Rural residents reported fewer “no problems” responses across dimensions than urban residents. [Source: Abstract—Results; Results—EQ-5D dimensions and Table 2; Supplementary Tables 1–9]

Young people had a relatively high frequency of anxiety/depression problems, unlike the generally age-increasing pattern for other dimensions. The authors note this pattern in other Eastern European populations and call for further research. The study provides reference values for clinicians, researchers, decision-makers, health-policy comparisons and patient-group benchmarking. [Source: Discussion; Conclusion]

## Limitations and interpretation

Potential limitations include the raw sample imbalance in sex and residence, incomplete correction for emigration in census controls, interviewer adherence to the next-birthday rule, and the long, fixed order of the interview. EQ-5D-3L was administered after EQ-5D-5L and valuation tasks, so fatigue and context bias may affect responses; EQ VAS was completed twice and the authors used the first administration for norms because exposure to severe states may change later ratings. Cross-country comparisons also mix survey years and administration modes. The authors state that the norms are the first for both Romanian instruments and their indexes and EQ VAS.
