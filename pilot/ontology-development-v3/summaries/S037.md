## Aim and scope

The paper compares the characteristics of EQ-5D-5L value sets and PROPr, the preference-based scoring system accompanying PROMIS. It asks how two systems that are, in principle, anchored on the same 0–1 utility scale describe and value health, and what the differences may mean for QALY estimation, incremental cost-effectiveness ratios, decision makers, and clinical triallists (Abstract; Introduction). The main comparison is the US EQ-5D-5L value set and US PROPr, both based on stated preferences of the US general public. England and Australia EQ-5D-5L value sets provide sensitivity context (Method, “Analysis of Theoretical Values”). No participant sample was recruited for this analysis; sample size for the theoretical-state analysis is not applicable/not reported.

## Instruments and value sets

EQ-5D-5L has five one-item dimensions—mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression—with five levels: no, slight, moderate, severe, and unable to/extreme problems. States are five-number strings from 11111 (best) to 55555 (worst). US and English value sets use the EQ-VT protocol, including cTTO and DCE; Australia used DCE with duration and an N5 interaction for any level-5 problem (Methods, “EQ-5D-5L Questionnaire and Value Sets”).

PROPr is based on multi-attribute utility theory and stated preferences elicited by standard gamble (SG). It uses seven PROMIS domains: Cognitive Function, Depression, Fatigue, Pain Interference, Physical Function, Sleep Disturbance, and Ability to Participate in Social Roles. PROMIS domains are IRT-calibrated T-scores (mean 50, SD 10); PROMIS-29 + 2 is the fixed-form measure used here, with four items per domain except two Cognitive Function items. CAT is recommended for domain scores; PROMIS-29 + 2 is the recommended fixed form (Introduction; Methods). “PROPr utilities” in this paper mean scores generated from PROMIS-29 + 2.

## Design and analysis

All theoretically possible health states were generated in Python v3.8. PROPr scores used code written in SAS 9.4 and transformed to Stata; analyses used Stata 14.2 (Method). Descriptive statistics compared range, percentage below zero, number of unique values, and kernel-density distributions. Domain importance was ranked by corner-state utility: the worst level in one domain with all others best. Matched severity states were full health, mild, moderate, severe, and worst: EQ-5D-5L 11111/22222/33333/44444/55555 versus PROMIS raw-score cut points, with symptom and function domains reversed as appropriate. Adjacent-state analysis compared a one-level EQ-5D-5L change with a four-point PROMIS-29 + 2 raw-score change while other dimensions were held at best, moderate, or worst (Methods, “Analysis of Theoretical Values”).

## Principal findings

- The US EQ-5D-5L range was −0.573 to 1; PROPr ranged from −0.022 to 0.954. PROPr therefore had a higher minimum and a narrower theoretical range. Its best PROMIS-29 + 2 state was below 1 (0.954), whereas EQ-5D-5L full health was 1 (Abstract; Results, Table 2).
- Pain was the most important US (and English) EQ-5D-5L dimension, while pain was among the least important PROPr domains, apart from Sleep Disturbance. Relative domain decrements were more similar to one another in PROPr (Results, “Comparing Value Set Characteristics”; Discussion).
- For descriptively matched states, PROPr was lower than EQ-5D-5L for mild states but higher for more severe states. Examples in Table 3: EQ-5D-5L 22222 had US value 0.630, while corresponding PROMIS states were 0.437, 0.391, and 0.358; EQ-5D-5L 55555 was −0.573 and PROPr was −0.022.
- EQ-5D-5L’s largest adjacent utility change occurred between levels 3 and 4 (moderate to severe). PROPr’s largest change occurred between the most severe states and their adjacent states. With all other domains at the worst level, PROPr showed no or almost no change across a dimension and became higher than EQ-5D-5L (Results, “Differences in Utility Between Adjacent States”, Fig. 2).

The authors attribute differences to both descriptive systems and valuation choices: administration mode, elicitation technique, valued-state selection, health-state display, number of states per respondent, anchoring and handling worse-than-dead values, quality control, exclusions, modelling, and final-model selection. They note no gold standard validates stated-preference value sets. Implications for QALYs are non-linear: PROPr may produce smaller changes across its narrower range, but larger gains from mild states and disutility corner states; an improvement in one domain from the all-worst PROPr state may produce no gain (Discussion; Conclusions).

## Limitations and implications

Matched PROMIS and EQ-5D-5L states were hypothesised, not empirically established as equivalent. The main analysis used PROMIS raw scores rather than the IRT descriptive system; other PROMIS instruments may have different ranges. The study did not apply either value set to patient data or directly compare QALYs. Further patient and longitudinal research is needed. The paper recommends awareness by decision makers and clinical triallists and international PROPr utilities, while noting PROPr was available only for the US at publication (Discussion; Conclusions).
