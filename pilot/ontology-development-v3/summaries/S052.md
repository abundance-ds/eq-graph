# Exploring the content validity of the EQ-5D-5L and four bolt-ons (skin irritation, self-confidence, sleep, social relationships) in atopic dermatitis and chronic urticaria

## Aim and design

The study assessed content validity—relevance, importance, comprehensiveness, and comprehensibility—of the EQ-5D-5L and four bolt-ons (skin irritation, self-confidence, sleep, social relationships) in adults with atopic dermatitis (AD) and chronic urticaria (CU). It was a UK qualitative study approved by the University of Portsmouth ethics committee (23/ETHICS/002), reported using Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research. Interviews occurred May–September 2023, in person or by Zoom, lasted mean 56 min (SD 13), and were audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, and de-identified (Methods).

## Population and methods

Thirty adults were interviewed: 15 AD and 15 CU. Recruitment used social media, a patient database, healthcare-professional referrals, patient associations, and a specialist agency. Eligibility was age ≥18, UK residence, English literacy, and self-reported AD or CU; other dermatological conditions or systemic diseases with dermatological symptoms were excluded. Soft quotas covered mild/moderate/severe self-reported severity and ethnicity. Most interviews were online (24/30), 23 participants were female, and 27 had college/university education. Twelve CU participants had chronic spontaneous urticaria. AD duration averaged 19.4 years and CU 8.3 years (Results, “Sample characteristics”; Table 1).

The semi-structured interview began with concept elicitation of symptoms and HRQOL impacts. Participants then completed EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS using think-aloud and retrospective probing, followed by EQ-5D-5L plus skin irritation and self-confidence bolt-ons. Sleep and social relationships were then explored as additional bolt-ons. Probes addressed relevance, importance, comprehensiveness, comprehensibility, wording, and recall period. POEM (AD), UCT (CU), and a 0–10 condition-severity NRS were also completed. Transcripts underwent content and thematic analysis in MAXQDA, with independent coding, reconciliation, and saturation-grid monitoring (Methods, “Procedures” and “Analysis”).

## Findings

Itch was spontaneously reported by 100% of AD and 93% of CU participants; pain/discomfort by 11 in each group. Self-confidence was reported by all AD and 47% of CU participants; sleep by 53% of each; social relationships by 27% of AD and 60% of CU (Abstract; Results, “Concept elicitation”). All EQ-5D-5L and bolt-on dimensions were spontaneously reported by at least 20% of each sample. Participants said the seven-dimension version was easy to complete and more comprehensive. Mobility was less relevant for five AD and six CU participants.

Skin irritation was relevant and important to all participants, and all preferred adding it. It was understood as itch, redness, raised skin, pain, hives, burning, and scratching. Self-confidence was also relevant and important to all; nine in each group preferred adding it. Sleep was relevant to 10 AD and 11 CU participants; nine AD and 10 CU preferred addition. Social relationships were relevant to eight AD and 10 CU, but only seven AD and six CU preferred adding it. When ranking bolt-ons, skin irritation was most important for 9 AD and 11 CU; social relationships was most often least relevant (7 in each group) (Results, “Cognitive debriefing” and “Ranking”).

Conceptual overlap was reported between skin irritation and pain/discomfort (7 AD, 7 CU) and between self-confidence and anxiety/depression (11 AD, 5 CU), but most participants considered the concepts sufficiently distinct. The authors support skin irritation, self-confidence, and sleep; evidence for social relationships is less clear. Limitations include self-reported diagnoses, modest purposive samples, mostly mild/moderate AD, CU subtype imbalance, and no quantitative psychometrics (Discussion; Conclusion).
