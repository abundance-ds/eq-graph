## Aim, setting, and population

The paper describes integration of EQ-5D-5L into evaluation of two Edmonton O-day’min Primary Care Network (EOPCN) programs in Alberta, Canada: exercise and active living and nutrition. EOPCN includes 230 family physicians and allied-health programs. Routine data were drawn from electronic medical records for adult patients between 1 January 2021 and 31 March 2022. All referred patients completed baseline EQ-5D-5L on a waiting-room tablet; repeat timing was individualized. Records with same-day pre/post assessments were excluded, and the last repeat was used (Methods, “Design and Setting”). Analytic samples were exercise and active living *n* = 301, nutrition *n* = 573; repeated measures were *n* = 112 and *n* = 212.

## Instrument and outcomes

EQ-5D-5L is a generic PROM with mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. Levels 1–5 are no, slight, moderate, severe, and extreme problems. Profiles have 3125 possible states. The Canadian EQ-5D-5L value set generated index scores. EQ-VAS captured health “today” from 0 poor health to 100 perfect health (Methods, “Measures”). Other variables included age, gender, visit type, history/primary concern, and BMI. Programs also collected internally developed indicators such as satisfaction, mobility/exercise capacity, dietary adherence, and qualitative feedback.

## Analysis

Dimension change used Pareto Classification of Health Change (PCHC): deteriorated, no change, maintained perfect health, improved, or mixed change. Index and VAS change used minimally important differences (MID) 0.04 and 7.0. Effect sizes were mean change divided by baseline SD; <0.2 was small, 0.5 moderate, >0.8 large. Distributions and scores were compared with age/sex-matched Alberta general-population norms. Analyses used STATA 14.2 (Methods, “Data Analysis”).

## Results

In exercise and active living, 72% were female, mean age 57 (SD 16), and mean care duration 73 days. Baseline any-problem levels 2–5 were mobility 60.7%, self-care 18.7%, usual activities 62.5%, pain/discomfort 90.2%, and anxiety/depression 61.6%. Mean index was 0.74 (0.18), VAS 66.5 (18.9), both below Alberta norms. Among repeat patients, PCHC classified 36.6% improved, 22.3% deteriorated, 23.2% mixed, and 17.9% no change. Mean index rose 0.01 (effect size 0.1); VAS rose 5.0 (effect size 0.3), neither reaching MID. Usual activities had the largest improvement (17%); pain/discomfort the largest deterioration (25%) (Abstract; Results, Section “Exercise…”).

In nutrition, 71% were female, mean age 50 (SD 17), and mean care duration 113.7 days. Any problems were mobility 34.9%, self-care 18.9%, usual activities 45.7%, pain/discomfort 75.9%, and anxiety/depression 67.0%. Mean index was 0.77 (0.19), VAS 70.1 (15.6), below norms. PCHC classified 37.3% improved, 24.5% deteriorated, 17.0% mixed, and 21.2% no change. Mean index rose 0.02 (effect size 0.1); VAS rose 1.0 (effect size 0.1), neither reaching MID. Anxiety/depression had largest improvement (16%); pain/discomfort largest deterioration (26%) (Abstract; Results, “Nutrition Program”).

## Interpretation and limitations

The authors stress that maintaining health can be a positive primary-care outcome, not only improvement. EQ-5D-5L supported meso-level program evaluation and comparisons across programs and population norms, but ceiling effects and limited responsiveness may obscure subtle change. There was no control arm, differential follow-up loss, no linkage to other patient data, and limited generalizability. The authors recommend combining generic and condition-specific PROMs and other validated outcomes (Discussion; Conclusions).
