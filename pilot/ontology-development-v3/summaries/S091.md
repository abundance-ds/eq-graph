## Aim, design, and population

The study compared health-related quality of life (HRQOL) and mental well-being among four mutually exclusive T2 groups: (I) post COVID-19 condition (PC), (II) acute COVID-19 infection (AC), (III) one or more chronic condition(s) but no COVID-19, and (IV) healthy (PH). It also compared T1-to-T2 change and examined which T1 group transitioned to PC. POPCORN used web surveys in Greece, Italy, the Netherlands, Sweden, the United Kingdom, and the United States. T1 was April–May 2020 (Sweden 26 May–1 June); T2 was 29 April–25 June 2022. Dynata recruited an age-, sex-, and education-representative panel. Participants were 18–75 at T1; 19,902 completed T1 and 4,999 completed both (25% response), including PC 240, AC 107, chronic condition(s) 1798, and healthy 2854. [Abstract; Methods—Study design and Data collection; Results—Study population]

PC meant likely or confirmed prior COVID-19 infection, ongoing symptoms, and infection not within the previous three months. AC used the same criteria but infection within the previous three months. Chronic condition(s) excluded PC and AC; healthy was the remainder. T1 had no exclusive PC group because early questions differed and PC was combined with likely/confirmed acute or past infection. Surveys had no skipped or missing questions; translations used computer-assisted translation, back-translation, and bilingual verification. [Methods—Questionnaire and Disease status categorisation]

## Measures and analysis

HRQOL used EQ-5D-5L descriptive system, index value, and EQ VAS. Dimensions were Mobility, Self-care, Usual activities, Pain/discomfort, and Depression/anxiety; levels ranged “no problems” (1) to “extreme problems/unable to” (5), referring to health today. A single US value set was used for cross-country comparison (range below 0 “worse than death” to 1 “full health”); EQ VAS ranged 0–100. Mental outcomes were WHO-5 Well-Being Index (0–100, last two weeks; poor ≤50), PHQ-9 (0–27; poor/depression ≥10), and GAD-7 (0–21; poor/anxiety ≥8). [Methods—Primary outcome measures]

Descriptive outcomes were stratified by T2 group. One-way ANOVA, Fisher’s exact and chi-square tests compared characteristics; one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc tests compared T2 outcomes. Stacked bars displayed dimension and poor mental-outcome levels; a Sankey plot displayed transitions. Paired-samples *t*-tests assessed T1-to-T2 change; positive change meant deterioration for EQ-5D-5L index, EQ VAS, and WHO-5, but improvement for PHQ-9 and GAD-7. Venn diagrams showed outcome overlap. [Methods—Statistical analyses]

## Findings

- **T2 cross-sectional:** Mean EQ-5D-5L index was 0.70 (PC), 0.73 (AC), 0.75 (chronic condition[s]), and 0.92 (healthy), all group differences *p*<0.001. EQ VAS means were 65.7, 65.1, 68.4, and 80.8. PC had the highest rate of any problems in all five EQ-5D-5L dimensions; AC had slightly lower EQ VAS than PC. [Abstract; Results—Primary outcomes at T2; Figs. 2 and 4]
- **Mental well-being at T2:** WHO-5 means were 47.7 (PC), 51.1 (AC), 56.1 (chronic), and 65.6 (healthy); PHQ-9 means 9.1, 7.7, 5.2, and 2.8; GAD-7 means 7.4, 5.7, 4.2, and 2.5. Poor mental well-being, depression, and anxiety were highest in PC and lowest in healthy; all outcome means differed by group (*p*<0.001). [Abstract; Results—Primary outcomes at T2; Figs. 3–4]
- **Transitions:** Of PC at T2, 116 (48.3%) were chronic condition(s) at T1, 80 (33.3%) healthy, and 44 (18.3%) possible acute/past infection. The probability of PC at T2 was 5.9% for chronic condition(s) at T1 versus 3.0% for healthy. Sixty percent of PC participants had one or more chronic condition(s) at T2. [Results—Description of respondents; Fig. 1]
- **Longitudinal change:** HRQOL deteriorated in all groups except healthy. EQ-5D-5L index deterioration was largest in chronic condition(s), Δ0.027, *p*<0.001; EQ VAS deterioration was largest in AC, Δ6.327, *p*<0.001, followed by PC Δ4.963, *p*<0.001. Healthy index change was −0.005 (*p*=0.035), while EQ VAS increased 1.402 (*p*<0.001). [Results—Change; Table 2]
- **Mental change and overlap:** WHO-5 deterioration was largest in AC, Δ4.785 (*p*=0.016); PHQ-9 worsened in AC, Δ−1.252 (*p*=0.012), while healthy PHQ-9 improved, Δ0.754 (*p*<0.001). GAD-7 improved significantly in healthy (Δ1.272) and chronic condition(s) (Δ0.561), both *p*<0.001; AC did not improve significantly. Unaffected respondents were 75% of healthy versus 35% of PC; all three poor mental outcomes overlapped in 4% healthy versus 27.5% PC. [Results—Change and Overlap; Figs. 3 and 5]

## Interpretation and limitations

PC had the worst HRQOL and mental well-being at T2. AC showed the largest deterioration since the pandemic start, whereas PC deterioration was lower, possibly because PC had pre-existing chronic disease and already poor T1 health. The authors caution against causal interpretation: PC and chronic conditions were self-reported, “probable” infections were included, types and counts of comorbidities were not compared, and disease classification differed between T1 and T2. T2 responders differed from non-responders in gender, age, education, country, and chronic conditions; attrition may have removed PC participants. Strengths were individual panel data, no recall-based T1 measures, general-population sampling, and three comparator groups. [Discussion—Key findings; Strengths and limitations; Conclusions]
