## Aim, design and participants

The study developed Singapore’s first EQ-5D-5L value set for health technology assessment and cost-utility analysis. It used the EuroQol Research Foundation “lite” valuation protocol and EQ-VT version 2.1. The lite design values 91 selected states rather than all 3,125 states: five blocks of 20 states are formed from standard blocks, replacing one repeated 55555 state with one of 45555, 54555, 55455, 55545 or 55554. Each respondent completed 20 cTTO tasks. [Source: Abstract; Methods—The ‘Lite’ EQ-5D-5L Valuation Protocol]

Five hundred adults were quota-sampled to resemble Singapore’s 2021/2022 population by age, sex, ethnicity and education. Recruitment began from the Singapore Multi-Ethnic Cohort (>20,000 residents) and used social-media advertisements to fill minority-group quotas. Eligibility was age ≥21, English or Chinese communication, and physical and mental ability to complete an interview in person or by Zoom; prior EQ-VT participation excluded. Participants received SGD40. Data were collected November 2023–April 2024; 533 registered and 500 completed (93.8% response). Mean age was 48.1 (SD 16.6), 49.8% female, 76.8% Chinese, and 93.8% completed in English. [Source: Methods—Sampling and Recruitment; Results; Table 2]

## Instruments, elicitation and quality control

The EQ-5D-5L describes mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression at five levels and defines 3,125 states. Interviews had four sections: introduction; demographics and EQ-5D-5L; a tutorial, three practices and 20 randomly assigned cTTO tasks; and closed/open questions about task reasoning. The cTTO combines conventional 10-year TTO for better-than-dead (BTD) states with lead-time TTO (10 years full health followed by 10 years in the target state) for worse-than-dead (WTD) states. Full health time varied in 1- or 0.5-year steps, giving 41 possible values from 1 to −1. Five trained research staff conducted in-person or Zoom interviews. Two quality specialists reviewed data for wheelchair-example explanation time (<3 min), failure to demonstrate WTD, 55555 value higher than another by 0.5, and cTTO completion under 5 min. [Source: Methods—Data Collection; Valuation Procedure]

## Statistical models and selection

Respondents valuing all 20 states as −1 or giving values that increased with severity were to be excluded; none met these criteria. Six tobit models regressed disutility (1 − cTTO) on EQ-5D-5L levels: ADD20 (20 main effects), ADD20-I (main effects plus two-way interactions), ADD20-P (power function), CALE (eight-parameter cross-attribute level effects), CALE-I and CALE-P. Tobit modelling addressed censoring at −1; no intercept ensured 11111 = 1. Ten dimension-pair interactions treated levels 0–4 as continuous. Five-fold block cross-validation compared coefficient significance/logical consistency and out-of-sample RMSE, MAE and ICC, with and without heteroskedasticity. Censoring-adjusted means were used for validation; backward selection retained significant interactions. Final models were refitted to all data and checked across 759,375 ordered state pairs. [Source: Methods—Data Analysis; Model Construction; Model Evaluation; Value Set Estimation]

## Findings and value set

No cTTO values were missing; observed values had spikes at −1 (16.1%) and 1 (14.3%). Only mobility × anxiety/depression and pain/discomfort × anxiety/depression were significant in the full ADD20-I fit. Accounting for heteroskedasticity improved all models. ADD20-I was best: RMSE 0.0782, MAE 0.0598 and ICC 0.9883; CALE-I was second. No model gave logically inconsistent predictions. The recommended 20-parameter main-effects-plus-two-interaction model produces values from 1.000 for 11111 to −0.851 for 55555; about 60% of states are between 0 and 1, and 1,197 states (38.3%) are negative. Pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression have the greatest disutility; level-5 dimension rank is PD–AD–MO–SC–UA. Full 3,125-state values and R, Stata, SPSS code are supplied as online resources. [Source: Results; Tables 3–4; Figures 1–3; Conclusions]

The authors interpret interaction models as evidence that compounded problems have a concave, lower-than-additive disutility. The lite protocol is feasible under resource constraints but may reduce precision because only 91 states are valued; the standard protocol is preferred when possible. The sample was mainly drawn from an existing cohort and underrepresented less educated and minority groups. Participants selected interview mode, and the two modes could introduce systematic differences, although Online Resource 7 found no evidence of mode differences. Because the local EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L value sets differ, the authors recommend mapping EQ-5D-3L data to EQ-5D-5L before scoring for economic evaluation.
