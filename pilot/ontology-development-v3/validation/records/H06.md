# H06 — Population health status based on the EQ-5D-Y-3L among adolescents in Sweden

Source publication: Åström et al., *Quality of Life Research* 27(11), 2018; DOI 10.1007/s11136-018-1985-2.
Source location: article abstract, sections, tables, and conclusion.
Extraction date and method: 2026-08-17; manual extraction from the assigned article file.

## 1. Identity and study type

- Study: cross-sectional population-health survey of adolescent health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in Sweden.
- Study types: population norms or population-health study; survey/data-quality study.
- Data source: Life & Health—young people survey, conducted in 2014 in Örebro County, Sweden.
- Funding: EuroQol Research Foundation research grant 2015400.
- Source: abstract; “Study design”; Funding.

## 2. Population and samples

- Target population: adolescents aged 13–18 years in the general population of Örebro County, Sweden.
- Recruitment population and frame: all adolescents in grade 7 and grade 9 of compulsory school and the second year of upper secondary school. The anonymous survey was completed during school hours after school staff invited participation.
- Survey respondents: 7,399 pupils; reported response rate 79.7%.
- Post-screening sample: 6,805 after exclusion for missing or ambiguous sex or age and failure to meet the age criterion.
- Analytic sample: 6,574 complete cases with answers for every EQ-5D-Y-3L dimension. Of these 6,574 participants, 106 had missing EQ VAS scores.
- Subgroups and covariates: age 13–14, 15–16, and 17–18 years; boys and girls; parents' occupational status; self-reported diseases; functional impairments; mental distress; self-rated health; and BMI.
- Analytic sample summary: mean age 15.9 years; 3,324 boys and 3,250 girls. The paper reports 4,805 with both parents working and 249 with one or both parents unemployed; 1,520 had missing parental occupational-status data.
- Source: “Study design”; “Data analyses”; Results, “Response rate” and “Characteristics of study participants”; Table 1.

## 3. Research concepts

- Population health status and self-reported HRQoL among adolescents.
- Differences in health status by sex, age, socioeconomic proxy, disease, functional impairment, mental distress, self-rated health, and BMI.
- Usefulness of EQ-5D-Y-3L for describing population health and guiding health-prioritisation decisions.
- Source: abstract; Introduction; Discussion; Conclusions.

## 4. Instruments and administration

- Instrument: “EQ-5D-Y-3L”; preferred label: EQ-5D-Y-3L, a youth version of EQ-5D-3L.
- Instrument role: administered as a self-reported HRQoL and health-status outcome measure. The survey included the Swedish version; this was the first year that EQ-5D-Y-3L was included.
- Descriptive system: mobility; looking after myself; doing usual activities; having pain or discomfort; and feeling worried, sad or unhappy. Each dimension has no, some, or a lot of problems.
- EQ VAS: respondent rated health today from 100 (“the best health you can imagine”) to 0 (“the worst health you can imagine”).
- Respondent and perspective: adolescent self-report; not proxy-reported.
- Interaction and channel: self-completed paper-and-pencil questionnaire in the classroom during school hours.
- Setting and time point: schools in Örebro County; survey year 2014.
- Recall periods: EQ-5D-Y-3L asks about health “today”. Stress and depression covariates asked about the past 3 months.
- Comparator and covariate questions: self-rated health, parents' occupational status, self-reported disease, functional impairment, BMI, stress, and depression. These were survey questions, not named measurement instruments in the paper.
- Source: Introduction; Methods, “Study design”, “Measures”, and “EQ-5D-Y-3L”.

## 5. Methods, protocol, and task design

- Research method: cross-sectional total survey using the Life & Health—young people questionnaire.
- Administration design: two survey versions were used (grade 7; grade 9 and second-year upper secondary school). Most questions were common, with some additional questions in the older group.
- Quality-control and participation design: participation was voluntary and anonymous; teachers or principals provided information; participants sealed completed surveys in envelopes.
- Named protocol: Life & Health—young people survey; no EQ-5D-specific protocol version or task block was reported.
- Inclusion criterion: age 13–18 at the end of 2014, born between 1996 and 2001. For the EQ-5D-Y-3L outcome, complete-case analysis was used.
- Source: Methods, “Study design”, “Measures”, and “Data analyses”.

## 6. Analysis and statistical models

- Descriptive analyses: proportions reporting no, some, or a lot of problems in each EQ-5D-Y-3L dimension and mean or median EQ VAS scores by subgroup.
- Data handling: some and a lot of problems were combined as “any” problems for significance tests; complete cases were used for EQ-5D-Y-3L dimensions.
- Group tests: Chi-square test or Fisher's Exact Test for proportions; Mann–Whitney U test for mean VAS-score differences.
- Multiple logistic regression analysis for associations between any reported dimension problems and sex, age, and parents' occupational status; results reported as odds ratios.
- Final models 1–7: multiple linear regression for EQ VAS score, with combinations of sex, age group, parents' occupational status, disease, functional impairment, mental distress, and BMI.
- Statistical software and threshold: IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 23.0; 5% significance level.
- Candidate, preferred, and sensitivity model roles: not reported. No value-set or preference-elicitation model was used.
- Source: Methods, “Data analyses”; Results, “Regression analyses”; Tables 5 and 6.

## 9. Principal findings and interpretation

1. The final analytic sample contained 6,574 adolescents; 94 unique EQ-5D-Y-3L health profiles were observed, and 44.9% reported profile 11111 (no problems in any dimension). (Source: Results, “Response rate” and “Health profiles”.)
2. Girls reported more problems than boys in doing usual activities, pain or discomfort, and feeling worried, sad or unhappy. Mean EQ VAS was 71.8 for girls and 78.9 for boys; adjusted odds for any mood-dimension problem were 3.44 for girls versus boys. (Source: Abstract; Results, “EQ-5D-Y-3L by sex and age group”; Table 5.)
3. Adolescents with one or both parents unemployed reported more problems with usual activities, pain or discomfort, and the mood dimension, and had lower mean VAS scores than those with both parents working. Mean VAS was 72.3 versus 76.1; adjusted odds ratios were 1.55, 1.52, and 1.68 for those three dimensions, respectively. (Source: Results, “EQ-5D-Y-3L by parents' occupational status”; Table 5.)
4. Self-reported disease, functional impairment, and mental distress were associated with more reported problems and lower VAS scores. Among adolescents who always felt depressed, 90.7% reported problems in the mood dimension; in the fully adjusted VAS model, the coefficient for always depressed was −28.55. (Source: Results, “EQ-5D-Y-3L by self-reported disease, functional impairment and mental distress”; “Regression analyses”; Table 6.)
5. The authors interpret the instrument's ability to distinguish these groups as support for EQ-5D-Y-3L use in adolescent population health assessment and as guidance for prioritisation. They also report few missing or ambiguous EQ-5D-Y-3L responses as an indication of feasibility. (Source: Abstract; Discussion; Conclusions.)

## 10. Limitations and source issues

- The authors note that adolescent reports of parents' occupational status may not be fully reliable, although earlier evidence showed agreement with parents' reports.
- The survey was cross-sectional; the paper does not present longitudinal follow-up or causal analysis.
- The EQ-5D-Y-3L was included for the first time in this survey. No value set was available for the instrument at the time.
- The paper reports 7,399 survey respondents, 6,805 after demographic screening, and 6,574 complete EQ-5D-Y-3L cases; these are different sample stages and must not be merged.
- The article does not report a named EQ-5D administration protocol, participant-level values, or a separate analytic sample for every subgroup.
- No direct source conflict is reported for the extracted study facts.
- Source: Methods, “Data analyses”; Discussion, paragraph on socioeconomic status; Results.
