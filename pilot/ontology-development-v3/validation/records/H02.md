# H02 — DCE task design, respondent engagement, and attribute non-attendance

## 1. Identity and study type

- **Publication:** “How do Design Characteristics Affect Respondent Engagement? Assessing Attribute Non-attendance in Discrete Choice Experiments Valuing the EQ-5D-5L”; DOI `10.1007/s40271-025-00735-9`.
- **Study type:** valuation-method or task-design study; valuation study using a discrete choice experiment; survey/data-quality study.
- **Source/provenance:** article file `corpus/2016260/doi_10.1007_s40271-025-00735-9.md`; extraction date 2026-08-17; manual Markdown extraction; review status: holdout extraction. Abstract, Methods—Choice Experiment, Sample Recruitment, Attribute Non-attendance, and Statistical Modelling provide the study scope.

## 2. Population and samples

- **Target/recruitment population:** Australian general population recruited through the Pureprofile online panel; quotas targeted age, gender, and region (Methods—Sample Recruitment).
- **Recruited sample:** 3,365 respondents. A subset of 1,432 respondents was used because this article selected 8 of the original 19 designs (Methods—Sample Recruitment).
- **Analytic design groups:** eight focal designs—four construction methods, each with no overlap and with overlap on two of the five attributes. The article reports 493 full attenders, 705 partial attenders, and 234 non-attenders in the external-validity analysis (Methods—Choice Experiment; Results—External Validity).
- **Representativeness:** the overall sample was similar to the Australian population by age, gender, and region; respondents were more highly educated. Forty-seven percent reported a long-term health condition and 22% reported EQ-5D-5L state 11111 (Results—Respondent Characteristics).
- **Respondent role/perspective:** general-population respondents making choices between hypothetical health states. Incentive: small payment for completing the survey (Methods—Sample Recruitment).

## 3. Research concepts

- Attribute non-attendance (ANA); respondent engagement and attentiveness; behavioural versus statistical efficiency of DCE designs; preference heterogeneity; health-state valuation (Introduction; Methods—Attribute Non-attendance).
- Attribute overlap means that a subset of attributes has the same level across the two options in a choice task (Introduction).

## 4. Instruments and administration

- **EQ-5D-5L:** five attributes—mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression—each with five levels. Roles: descriptive system in DCE choice tasks and self-reported health assessment before the DCE (Methods—Choice Experiment).
- **Administration:** online panel survey through Pureprofile; channel is online. The paper does not separately report interaction mode, survey language, setting, or recall period (Methods—Sample Recruitment; Choice Experiment).
- **Survey sequence:** demographics and consent; self-reported EQ-5D-5L; DCE instructions and 21 choice tasks; follow-up questions on survey difficulty and demographics (Methods—Choice Experiment).
- **Choice task:** two hypothetical EQ-5D-5L health states; respondents selected the preferred state (Methods—Choice Experiment).

## 5. Methods, protocol, and task design

- **Research method:** multi-arm discrete choice experiment using EQ-5D-5L health states; 21 choice sets per respondent (Abstract; Methods—Choice Experiment).
- **Design universe:** 19 designs from six construction methods, zero or non-zero priors, and no overlap or overlap on two attributes. This article restricts analysis to zero-prior designs and four methods capable of overlap: generator-developed, modified Fedorov in Ngene, modified Fedorov in SAS macros, and Bayesian D-efficient design in R (Methods—Choice Experiment).
- **Focal comparison:** each method generated one no-overlap design and one design with two overlapping attributes, producing eight designs. The Ngene and SAS modified Fedorov implementations used a candidate set of 40,000 two-attribute-overlap choice pairs (Methods—Choice Experiment).
- **Quality/engagement constructs:** inferred ANA strategy; straightlining, choice-task and total-survey completion times, task-difficulty feedback, and whether respondents considered the entire description (Methods—Attribute Non-attendance; Statistical Modelling).

## 6. Analysis and statistical models

- **Equality constrained latent class (ECLC) model:** primary ANA classification model used in the study for classifying respondents by attribute-attendance patterns. It allows 32 possible attendance strategies in principle, uses a five-step inclusion/exclusion process, and removes classes with membership probability below 5% (Methods—Attribute Non-attendance).
- **Multinomial logit (MNL) model:** candidate/comparator model for choice data, assuming preference homogeneity; used for main effects and interactions with full-attendance status (Methods—Statistical Modelling).
- **MNL main-effects comparisons:** fitted for all respondents and full attenders only; coefficients were anchored using state 55555 to compare utility decrements and relative attribute importance (Methods—Statistical Modelling).
- **Outcomes/derived analyses:** relative attribute importance (RAI) before and after exclusion; external-validity comparisons across full, partial, and non-attendance classes. ANA estimation used Latent GOLD 5.1; MNL models used R/RStudio and `gmnl` (Methods—Statistical Modelling).

## 7. Products

- **DCE design recommendation:** evidence-supported recommendation for modified Fedorov designs implemented in Ngene or SAS with two-attribute overlap. Status: design guidance, not a new EQ-5D-5L value set (Abstract; Conclusion).
- **ANA quality-control approach:** ECLC-based attendance classification is proposed as a quality-control tool for respondent inclusion/exclusion and robustness checks (Abstract; Key Points; Conclusion).
- No national tariff, value set, translation, or instrument version was produced by this study.

## 8. Outcomes or measurement properties

- Full, partial, and no attribute attendance; ANA class probabilities and confidence intervals (Results—ANA Probabilities).
- EQ-5D-5L utility decrements and relative attribute importance before and after exclusion of non-full attenders (Methods—Statistical Modelling; Results—Health State Valuation).
- Engagement indicators: straightlining, completion time, reported task difficulty, difficulty distinguishing options, and agreement that the entire description was considered (Results—External Validity).

## 9. Principal findings and interpretation

1. Attribute overlap increased estimated full attendance from 22.3–28.4% without overlap to 28.2–54.2% with overlap. Modified Fedorov designs in Ngene or SAS had the highest rates; Ngene increased from 24.6% to 54.2% and SAS from 22.3% to 49.4% (Abstract; Results—ANA Probabilities).
2. RAI differed between all-respondent and full-attender analyses, especially for mobility and anxiety/depression, but there was no clear pattern across designs. Mobility was more important before exclusion; anxiety/depression was more important among full attenders (Abstract; Results—Health State Valuation).
3. ECLC attendance classes aligned with external engagement indicators: non-attenders had more straightlining (3.9%) than partial (1.2%) or full attenders (0.4%), and median total-survey times were 7.6, 8.7, and 10.2 minutes, respectively (Results—External Validity).
4. Non-attenders more often reported task difficulty (17.3%) and difficulty distinguishing options (26.6%), and fewer agreed that they considered the entire description (55.6%, versus 71.3% partial and 82.5% full). The authors interpret this as support for ECLC classification as a measure of engagement (Results—External Validity).

## 10. Limitations and source issues

- The article analyzes only 8 of 19 designs and only zero-prior designs; the restriction was used to improve comparability and avoid prior misspecification (Methods—Choice Experiment).
- ECLC assumes common preference parameters apart from non-attended dimensions. The authors state that preference heterogeneity and ANA driven by genuine low importance cannot be fully disentangled (Methods—Attribute Non-attendance; Discussion).
- Partial non-attendance may reflect either inattention/heuristics or genuine indifference; the authors recommend further robustness checks rather than automatic exclusion of all partial attenders (Discussion).
- The study did not directly test dimension-order effects; this is discussed as a possible explanation for mobility and anxiety/depression differences (Discussion).
- Design construction methods are compared within this Australian online-panel sample; transfer to other populations or modes is not established.
