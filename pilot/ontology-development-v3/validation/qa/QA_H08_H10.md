## H10 — EQ-5D-5L visualization in a TKA decision aid

Verdict: PASS WITH REQUIRED CLARIFICATION.

The record correctly captures the TKA/OA clinic population, separate Part 1 and Part 2 samples, prototype versions, Pain/Discomfort dimension, visualization formats, custom checklist, descriptive tests, directed content analysis, findings, planned versus existing products, and implementation limitations. One instrument-use statement needs correction because it can imply that participants completed EQ-5D-5L.

Exact corrections or additions:

- Replace “administered PROM data were visualized” with “historical or routinely collected EQ-5D-5L PROM data were visualized in the decision-aid prototypes; participants reviewed hypothetical Pain/Discomfort level-4 presentations and did not complete the EQ-5D-5L in this study.” Source: Background, paragraph on historic population-level data; Methods, prototype and participant paragraphs.
- State that the prototypes represented a hypothetical patient with severe problems (level 4), rather than participants’ actual EQ-5D-5L scores. The record states this, but it should be adjacent to the instrument-use correction. Source: Methods, participant/prototype paragraph.
- Add that the checklist was developed by the study team because no suitable validated checklist was found, and that it was piloted internally and with 10 clinic patients. Source: Methods, participant/prototype and checklist paragraph.
- Add the named methodological guidance used for the decision aid and visualizations: International Patient Decision Aid Standards for the decision aid, plus data-visualization recommendations. This is separate from a EuroQol protocol. Source: Background, paragraph on decision-aid development; Methods, prototype paragraph.
- The study used female or male prototype variants based on participant self-identification. Add this only if the instrument-form field is intended to preserve the prototype distinction. Source: Methods, participant/prototype paragraph.
- `survey/interviewer or data-quality study` is a weak and potentially unnecessary study-type label. Prefer `qualitative or stakeholder study` and `implementation or routine-measurement study`; describe the researcher-administered checklist as a data-collection method. Source: Abstract, Methods, and Results.

Ontology gaps or unnecessary fields:

- Instrument administration fields need a distinction between administering an instrument and displaying previously collected instrument data. This paper is an important negative example.
- The ontology needs a product subtype for visualization/prototype and a status distinction for existing, revised, planned, and implemented products. The record already applies this distinction.
- Comprehension, usefulness, and visual appeal are feedback outcomes, not EQ-5D measurement properties. They should not be stored as validity, reliability, or responsiveness.
- A custom, non-validated checklist is a method artifact. A validated-instrument field would be misleading here.
- Prototype version, dimension, hypothetical level, display channel, and intended deployment channel are useful fields. No unnecessary ontology field was identified beyond the weak survey/data-quality study label noted above.
