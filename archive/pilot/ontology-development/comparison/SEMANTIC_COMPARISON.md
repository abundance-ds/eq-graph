# Anonymous semantic comparison of the three ontology records

## Scope and method

This comparison treats Candidate 1, Candidate 2, and Candidate 3 as anonymous records. It compares their meanings. It does not use name similarity, file layout, element counts, or a combined score to decide ontology content. It does not rank the candidates and does not select a candidate.

The main evidence is the three ontology records and their applications:

- Candidate 1: [ontology](inputs/candidate-1/ontology.md), [decisions and open cases](inputs/candidate-1/decisions-and-open-cases.md), and applications for [round 1](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-01.md), [round 2](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-02.md), and [round 3](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-03.md).
- Candidate 2: [ontology](inputs/candidate-2/ONTOLOGY.md), [decisions and open cases](inputs/candidate-2/DECISIONS_AND_OPEN_CASES.md), and [all applications](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md).
- Candidate 3: [ontology](inputs/candidate-3/ontology.md), [decisions](inputs/candidate-3/decisions.md), [unresolved cases](inputs/candidate-3/unresolved.md), and [all applications](inputs/candidate-3/applications.md).

Each record has an application for each of the 30 papers. The applications are the main evidence for coverage and consistency. One source paper was checked because the three records gave different levels of certainty about its retest subgroup. That check is stated in the applicable section.

## Overall semantic result

The three records have a large stable common core. They all separate the paper's contribution from its design, the focal measure from its role, the respondent from the person or object described, a descriptive system from its value set, direct responses from derived scores, property-specific evaluations from broad conclusions, and a research output from its maturity and use limits. They also reject a binary representativeness label and a single positive or negative psychometric judgment.

The main differences are ontology architecture and boundary placement. Candidate 1 uses a measurement chain and treats study activities, evaluations, and comparisons as explicit conceptual entities. Candidate 2 uses a ten-part paper description and adds a formal comparison procedure, typed uncertainty, and meta-research transparency. Candidate 3 uses questions and action relations, with the paper as the primary unit and study components below it. These are material alternatives for later review. They are not evidence that one record is correct and another is incorrect.

There is no direct conflict in the core distinctions. There are boundary conflicts about the status of a study component, comparison, data-quality assessment, population or burden work, and institutional relations. There are also differences in which source conflicts each record captures.

## Material boundary differences and unique elements

### 1. Main ontology backbone

Candidate 1 makes the measurement chain its main structure. It gives named layers from intended construct through observation, valuation, scoring, analyzed outcome, and decision use. It also provides graph-like relations between those layers. This makes the provenance of a quantity central to the ontology. See the [Candidate 1 measurement chain and main relations](inputs/candidate-1/ontology.md).

Candidate 2 does not select one domain chain as the main structure. It uses ten descriptive sections around contribution, object, question, people, design, derivation, evaluation, output, rigor, and uncertainty. It adds an ordered procedure for paper-to-paper comparison. See the [Candidate 2 core elements and comparison procedure](inputs/candidate-2/ONTOLOGY.md).

Candidate 3 also does not use a single chain. It uses research questions and relation patterns. This gives each application an open form while it keeps the action on the focal entity explicit. See the [Candidate 3 core research description and relation patterns](inputs/candidate-3/ontology.md).

This is a material alternative. A later representation must decide whether provenance through the measurement chain is the primary organizing principle, or whether it is one field in a broader paper description. This comparison does not make that decision.

### 2. Study activity versus study component

Candidate 1 treats **study activity** as a core entity with its own purpose, evidence, design, methods, comparison, and output. Candidates 2 and 3 keep the paper as the primary record and use **study component** when phases or sources differ. Candidate 2 defines a threshold for component use. Candidate 3 permits multiple components but does not define a fixed hierarchy.

The difference is visible in the multi-phase EQ-5D-Y-5L development paper, `10.1007/s11136-019-02115-x`, and in the two-sample valuation-method paper, `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.016`. Candidate 1 describes coherent activities and their roles. [Its applications](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-01.md) and [round-2 application](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-02.md) fit the activity model. Candidates 2 and 3 describe phases or samples as components in [Candidate 2 applications](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md) and [Candidate 3 applications](inputs/candidate-3/applications.md).

The paper evidence supports the need for a subordinate unit. It does not decide whether that unit must be a reusable ontology entity or a paper-local component.

### 3. Observation and transformation artifacts

Candidate 1 uniquely gives **observation** and **scoring or transformation artifact** explicit positions in the main chain. A profile, preference response, mapping model, value set, level sum score, population norm, DALY, and ICER do not share one data-product type.

Candidates 2 and 3 make the same distinctions, but they express them as focal objects, derivation steps, procedures, or products. The dialysis application, `10.1007/s10198-018-0987-x`, supports all three treatments. All records preserve the path from SF-12 or EQ-5D responses through mapping and value sets to utilities, QALYs, and ICERs. See [Candidate 1 round-2 application](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-02.md), [Candidate 2 application](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md), and [Candidate 3 application](inputs/candidate-3/applications.md).

This is a granularity difference, not a factual conflict.

### 4. Comparison as entity or relation

Candidate 1 makes comparison an explicit entity. It records within-person or between-sample design and alignment of population, language, timing, mode, source study, scoring, and criteria. Candidate 2 records these conditions under components and compatibility, then uses an ordered comparison procedure. Candidate 3 uses an action relation and records the applicable design details in prose.

The DCE-with-duration comparison, `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.016`, supports the detailed alignment fields because the two method outputs use different people, modes, periods, recruitment, and exclusion flows. The wording-by-perspective experiment, `10.1016/j.jval.2018.05.002`, supports a separate task-frame record because wording and perspective interact. All three applications capture these facts. The remaining alternative is structural: a comparison node, a comparison relation with fields, or a paper-local description.

### 5. Data quality, sample fitness, and rigor

Candidate 1 separates three related families:

- response-data integrity, such as bot, duplicate, speed, and repeated-item checks;
- data fitness, such as coverage, sampling, quotas, weighting, and target fit;
- analytic adequacy, such as missing-data handling and estimand alignment.

Candidate 2 places survey integrity in evaluation, while it places coverage, exclusion risks, and access under rigor, applicability, and transparency. Candidate 3 combines **data integrity and sample quality** in one evaluation family, while it also records related limits under scope and uncertainty.

The EQ-DAPHNIE quality paper, `10.1007/s11136-025-04074-y`, supports a distinction between record integrity and population representativeness. All three applications state that bot and duplicate controls do not establish representative population inference. See [Candidate 1 round-3 application](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-03.md), [Candidate 2 round-3 application](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md), and [Candidate 3 round-3 application](inputs/candidate-3/applications.md). The paper evidence does not decide whether these concepts must be separate evaluation families.

### 6. Population and burden work

Candidate 2 adds **population or burden estimation** as a contribution role. Candidate 3 keeps one broad **health, care, economic, or policy outcome analysis** role and states that a separate cost-of-illness role would add little for this corpus. Candidate 1 keeps population norms, inequalities, burden, resource use, and societal cost as study-activity purposes, derivation types, and outputs rather than one exclusive report class.

The vision-impairment paper, `10.1038/s41433-023-02860-x`, fits all three approaches. It is not an instrument study. It combines prevalence, service use, costs, disability weights, DALYs, and monetized wellbeing. The three applications agree on this semantic content. [C1](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-03.md) [C2](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md) [C3](inputs/candidate-3/applications.md)

The open boundary is whether population and burden estimation needs a named contribution role or can remain within a broad substantive-outcome role.

### 7. Transparency and institutional relations

Candidate 2 uniquely makes data and code access and institutional or funder relations an explicit optional meta-research element. It states that a disclosed relationship must not become an inferred bias label. Its applications record these items consistently. See [Candidate 2 rigor, applicability, and transparency](inputs/candidate-2/ONTOLOGY.md) and [Candidate 2 origin decision](inputs/candidate-2/DECISIONS_AND_OPEN_CASES.md).

Candidate 3 applications also record funding, access, and disclosure facts, but the current ontology does not name institutional relations as a separate element. Candidate 1 records access or metadata only when it affects a paper application or a source issue and does not include an institutional-relation field.

This is a unique coverage choice in Candidate 2 and a mild ontology-to-application boundary difference in Candidate 3. The corpus shows that the facts exist. It does not show how central they must be to this ontology's purpose.

### 8. Inferential aim and identification limits

Candidate 1 uniquely lists inferential aim as descriptive, associational, predictive, causal, psychometric, or methodological when the distinction changes interpretation. It also requires competing mechanisms when a design cannot identify one. Candidate 2 and Candidate 3 apply the same caution in their applications and limitation sections, but they do not make inferential aim an equally explicit controlled facet.

The cancer-equity paper, `10.3390/curroncol32110645`, and the within-profile inequality paper, `10.1007/s11136-026-04294-w`, support this distinction. All records state that the associations are not causal. All also keep omitted health content and response-scale heterogeneity open in the within-profile paper. See [C1 decisions D22](inputs/candidate-1/decisions-and-open-cases.md), [C2 application](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md), and [C3 unresolved case](inputs/candidate-3/unresolved.md).

### 9. Uncertainty types and source conflicts

Candidate 2 uniquely provides an explicit uncertainty type list. Candidate 1 instead gives detailed interpretation rules and a consolidated source-issue register. Candidate 3 uses a separate unresolved-case file and updates the status of inherited cases by round.

The records do not capture the same set of source conflicts:

- Candidates 1 and 2 record the arithmetic conflict between “68%” and `18/57` in `10.1007/s11136-025-04038-2`. Candidate 3 records the content-boundary issue but does not record this arithmetic conflict.
- Candidates 1 and 3 record the collection-period conflict in `10.1186/s12955-024-02323-1`. Candidate 2 instead records an inconsistency in the number of source studies or survey components.
- Candidate 1 also records a direction conflict for the income comparison in that population-norm paper. Candidates 2 and 3 do not record it.
- Candidate 2 records the `<0.5` versus `<=0.5` reCAPTCHA boundary in `10.1007/s11136-025-04074-y`. Candidates 1 and 3 record the response-rate terminology problem but not that boundary.

These differences do not change the core ontology. They show that source-conflict capture is not yet application-equivalent across the records. See [Candidate 1 source issues](inputs/candidate-1/decisions-and-open-cases.md), [Candidate 2 unresolved source conflicts](inputs/candidate-2/DECISIONS_AND_OPEN_CASES.md), and [Candidate 3 unresolved cases](inputs/candidate-3/unresolved.md).

## Application evidence for the common core

### Contribution, object, and role separation

All records distinguish papers that concern the same instrument but make different contributions:

- `10.1007/s40258-021-00639-3` develops a Danish EQ-5D-5L value set.
- `10.1007/s11136-020-02688-y` reviews measurement properties of EQ-5D-5L.
- `10.1007/s11136-025-03983-2` describes EQ-DAPHNIE data infrastructure.
- `10.1017/s0266462326103602` studies HTA practice and needs.
- `10.1016/j.jval.2025.02.001` reviews analysis practice in randomized trials.

This supports the shared rejection of one broad “EQ-5D study” class. The supporting applications are in all three records: [C1 round 1](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-01.md), [C2](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md), and [C3](inputs/candidate-3/applications.md).

### Respondent, referent, and target perspective

The China youth valuation paper, `10.1007/s40273-022-01216-9`, uses adults to value the health of a hypothetical 10-year-old. The EQ-TIPS paper, `10.1007/s11136-025-04150-3`, uses experts to assess a proxy-completed instrument for infants and toddlers. The HTA paper uses individual practitioners to describe practice associated with organizations. All three records keep these roles separate. This application consistency directly supports the shared perspective model.

The records also agree that proxy answers can include caregiver knowledge, developmental expectations, or spillover. None gives a rule that makes proxy and self-report evidence equivalent. [C1 open case](inputs/candidate-1/decisions-and-open-cases.md) [C2 open case](inputs/candidate-2/DECISIONS_AND_OPEN_CASES.md) [C3 open case](inputs/candidate-3/unresolved.md)

### Property-specific evaluation

The Brazilian youth paper, `10.1007/s10198-025-01770-x`, reports known-group discrimination together with poor-to-moderate item reliability. The Graves' disease paper, `10.1186/s12955-023-02177-z`, gives stronger responsiveness evidence for worsening than for improvement. The 3L-versus-5L review, `10.1007/s40273-018-0642-5`, gives clearer support for ceiling reduction and informativity than for reliability or responsiveness.

All three records preserve these property-level differences. None converts the paper conclusion into an unqualified “valid,” “reliable,” or “better” label. The applications give repeated support for this common idea.

There is one difference in uncertainty for the Graves' disease retest subgroup. Candidate 1 states that the paper does not clearly explain how it selected the 64-person stable subgroup. Candidates 2 and 3 state that these people self-reported unchanged health at one month. A selective check of the local source paper found both elements: the Results call the subgroup self-reported unchanged, but the Methods explicitly describe the global change question for the six-month comparison and do not clearly describe the one-month classification procedure. Candidate 1 preserves a procedure uncertainty. Candidates 2 and 3 preserve the label that the paper applies to the subgroup. These descriptions can coexist, but they give different certainty about subgroup construction.

### Derivation and compatibility

The dialysis paper, `10.1007/s10198-018-0987-x`, shows why direct, mapped, and modeled outcomes must remain separate. The EQ-HWB-S paper, `10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.007`, shows that agreement between scores also depends on two pooled datasets and different scoring routes. The Trinidad and Tobago norm paper, `10.1186/s12955-024-02323-1`, shows that a common value set can align scoring across years while sampling, mode, and context still differ.

All three records apply their derivation and compatibility concepts consistently to these cases. None calls a mapped EQ-5D value a direct EQ-5D response. None calls an equal-weight level sum score a utility. None treats a monetized DALY as a directly observed cost.

### Evidence lineage and nonindependence

All records added explicit evidence lineage in round 3. Their applications identify these connected evidence families:

- the Ontario oncology pilot and its later cancer-equity analysis;
- the Chinese youth hybrid valuation study and the later cTTO-only analysis;
- the Trinidad and Tobago EQ-VT, DCE-with-duration, anchoring, value-set, and population-norm papers;
- the EQ-DAPHNIE design report, quality-control report, and within-profile inequality analysis.

The applications agree that a new paper can make a new contribution without adding an independent respondent sample. See [C1 round-3 applications](inputs/candidate-1/applications-round-03.md), the [C2 shared-evidence map](inputs/candidate-2/APPLICATIONS.md), and [C3 round-3 applications](inputs/candidate-3/applications.md).

### Representativeness and data quality

The UAE value-set paper, `10.1016/j.jval.2025.01.003`, combines quotas with networks, snowball recruitment, public places, and social media. The EQ-DAPHNIE papers use non-probability online panels, quotas, integrity rules, and weighting. The Trinidad and Tobago papers mix household and panel sources.

All records retain the actual sampling evidence. None treats quota match, weighting, or response-integrity controls as proof of representativeness. The difference is only the internal classification of these facts.

The changes are mainly additive. None of the records reverses its original core distinction between contribution, focal object, people or perspective, property-specific evaluation, output, and limits. This is evidence of structural stability.

The records handle prior applications in different ways:

- Candidate 1 keeps one application file per round. Later decisions and round-3 applications add cross-round lineage links. It does not rewrite every earlier application into the final minimum template.
- Candidate 2 keeps one application file and adds an explicit shared-evidence map before round 3. It states why earlier applications remain semantically valid.
- Candidate 3 also keeps one application file. Round-3 applications add direct cross-paper lineage, and the decisions state why earlier applications did not need a rewrite.

No record leaves a paper without an application. The final-round concepts are clearest in round-3 applications, while earlier applications remain less explicit about features that did not yet exist, especially evidence lineage and analysis-population rules.

The applications also show a shared implementation limit. Candidate 1 defines study activity as a core entity, but its applications usually give one consolidated paper description instead of separate activity records. Candidates 2 and 3 also describe components inside one paper narrative. Thus, the applications support the need for activities or components, but they do not test a fixed repeated-component serialization. This is consistent with all three records because each rejects a fixed schema. It also means that the structural difference between activity and component remains partly untested.

## Coverage, consistency, redundancy, and clarity

### Candidate 1

Candidate 1 formalizes the measurement-chain semantics. Its named entities and relations make direct observation, transformation, comparison, evaluation, and downstream use easy to distinguish. Its batch maps and separate round files make development by round visible. Its decisions file gives a large set of open cases and source issues.

The same detail creates possible redundancy. Activity purpose, artifact role, evaluation, output type, and interpretation can repeat one contribution in several places. Long method and output lists can also become an informal schema even though the ontology says that it is not a fixed form. An implementation must define when a fact belongs to the activity, artifact, output, or interpretation field. This is a clarity and maintenance issue, not a coverage failure.

### Candidate 2

Candidate 2 gives a stable paper-comparison workflow. Its ten sections make participation flow, analysis population, compatibility, rigor, transparency, and typed uncertainty easy to find. The shared-evidence map gives clear cross-paper context. The explicit comparison procedure is unique among the records.

There is possible overlap between study contribution, focal-object role, evidence status, output, and conclusion scope. For example, a final value-set paper can be described as valuation, completed primary study, value-set focal object, selected output, and intended QALY use. Each statement has a different function, but an implementation needs rules to prevent repetitive records.

### Candidate 3

Candidate 3 has a compact question-and-relation style. It keeps focal entities tied to actions and makes nonexclusive contribution roles clear. Its separate decisions and unresolved files show which cases changed status. Its applications use a consistent prose pattern across the three rounds.

Some distinctions remain in prose rather than a formal element. Comparison alignment is less explicit than in Candidate 1. Institutional and funding facts appear in applications even though the ontology does not give them a separate slot. The broad substantive-outcome role also contains population health, inequality, cost, and policy work that Candidate 2 separates more clearly at the role level. These are boundary choices, not missing paper coverage.

## Stable common ideas for later review

The following ideas are stable across all three independent records and have repeated application support:

1. A paper can have more than one nonexclusive contribution or activity.
2. Contribution, design, evidence status, and focal object are different concepts.
3. A descriptive system, its component responses, a VAS, a value set, a mapped score, a utility, a QALY, and an ICER must remain distinct.
4. Respondent, referent, preference perspective, analysis unit, and target population must remain distinct when they differ.
5. Evaluation must attach to the correct target level and property. One broad “valid” or “high quality” judgment is not sufficient.
6. A comparison needs its objects, basis, population, methods, timing, scoring route, and important asymmetries.
7. Output type and output maturity are separate.
8. Sampling evidence must remain visible. Representativeness is not a binary property.
9. Reused respondents and supplied analytic inputs create evidence dependence across papers.
10. Author conclusions, curator uncertainty, source conflict, and unidentified competing mechanisms must not be merged.
11. Method agreement in one setting does not establish general interchangeability.
12. The ontology must preserve the main derivation chain without becoming a detailed claim-evidence graph.

## Important unresolved alternatives for human review

These alternatives remain explicit. This comparison does not resolve them:

1. **Primary architecture:** measurement-chain ontology, ten-part paper description, or question-and-relation description.
2. **Subordinate study unit:** reusable study activity or paper-local study component.
3. **Comparison representation:** explicit comparison entity or action relation with alignment fields.
4. **Data-quality boundary:** separate response integrity, population fitness, and analytic adequacy, or a broader data-integrity and rigor family.
5. **Population and burden boundary:** named contribution role or part of a broad substantive-outcome role.
6. **Transparency boundary:** whether data access, funder, membership, employment, and consultancy facts are core ontology elements or optional meta-research context.
7. **Inferential vocabulary:** whether inferential aim and identification limit need controlled terms or can remain in scoped conclusions.
8. **Derivation depth:** where the shortest material chain ends for decision models, burden estimates, costing, and monetized DALYs.
9. **Uncertainty representation:** explicit uncertainty types, a source-issue register, or prose in an unresolved-case record.
10. **Construct hierarchy:** whether health status, HRQoL, QoL, wellbeing, capability, and social-care-related QoL need a shared hierarchy. All records currently preserve paper terms and scope instead of imposing one.
11. **Transfer rules:** what evidence permits transfer of a translation, value set, proxy response, or method result across countries, languages, cultures, ages, and decision contexts.
12. **Threshold rules:** what level of agreement supports method or score interchangeability; what quality or completion evidence supports scale-up; and what sampling evidence supports national inference.
13. **Application maintenance:** whether earlier applications must be rewritten when new ontology concepts appear, or whether a later lineage map and change note are sufficient.

## Final comparison statement

The three records independently converge on the same main semantic safeguards. Their differences concern representation, granularity, and the boundary of optional meta-research information. The applications support all three architectures across the 30-paper corpus. They also show that no architecture removes the need for human judgment about construct boundaries, transfer, representativeness, method interchangeability, shared evidence, and derivation depth.

No candidate is ranked or selected here. No harmonized ontology is proposed.
