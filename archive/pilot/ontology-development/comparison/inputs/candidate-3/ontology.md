# Current ontology

## Purpose and unit

Use this ontology to describe what a paper contributes, how it produces evidence, what it studies, and where its conclusions apply. The primary unit is the paper. A paper can contain more than one study component.

Do not copy bibliographic data into the ontology. Use the supplied paper identifier to connect an application to its structured record.

## Core research description

For each paper, answer the applicable questions below. Omit a question when the paper does not give useful information. Add a new answer term when the existing terms do not fit.

### 1. Contribution role

What does the paper add for researchers?

Current roles are:

- **Instrument or descriptive-system development:** Creates or changes domains, items, wording, response levels, or a version of an instrument.
- **Translation and cultural adaptation:** Produces a language or setting-specific version and tests whether its meaning and response structure transfer.
- **Value-set development:** Converts health-state preferences into values on a utility scale.
- **Measurement-property evaluation:** Tests how an instrument, item, response scale, score, or extension performs.
- **Conceptual or content-validity evaluation:** Tests whether an instrument covers the intended construct and population perspective.
- **Method development or evaluation:** Develops or tests a preference-elicitation, mapping, scoring, statistical, or decision-analytic method. This role tests methods. It is different from an analysis-practice assessment, which describes which methods researchers use.
- **Health, care, economic, or policy outcome analysis:** Uses one or more measures to study a substantive population, treatment, cost-effectiveness, inequality, or policy question.
- **Implementation feasibility or acceptability assessment:** Tests whether a measure, data-collection process, or other research practice can work in a service or research workflow.
- **Evidence synthesis:** Reviews and combines evidence from prior studies.
- **Practice and needs assessment:** Describes current professional use, constraints, or research priorities.
- **Data-resource or study-infrastructure development:** Defines a reusable data collection system or data resource.
- **Analysis-practice assessment:** Describes the statistical methods that researchers use and the gaps in those methods.

These roles can overlap. For example, a health-inequality paper can both answer a population-health question and evaluate the discrimination of two outcome formats.

### 2. Study status, evidence origin, and evidence lineage

What is the maturity of the reported work, where does its evidence come from, and how does it depend on other studies?

- **Status:** planned protocol, design/resource report, completed empirical analysis, completed synthesis, or completed development stage.
- **Evidence origin:** new primary data, secondary analysis of one or more existing datasets or models, or synthesis of prior reports.

Keep status separate from design. A paper can describe completed data collection without reporting substantive analyses. If components have different evidence origins, record each origin. When a paper combines datasets, record important differences in collection period, mode, measure order, eligibility, variable definition, or available variables.

Also record an identifiable source study, dataset, model, value set, or participant sample when the paper reuses one. State whether papers share or can overlap in respondents, whether one paper supplies an input or comparator to another, and whether the new analysis replicates or extends earlier work. Useful relations are **reuses**, **shares a sample with**, **supplies an input to**, **replicates**, and **extends**. “Primary” or “secondary” alone does not show dependence between papers.

### 3. Study components, design, and time structure

What components produced the evidence?

Examples are a valuation experiment, cross-sectional survey, qualitative interview study, translation study with several stages, instrument-development study with multiple phases, longitudinal measurement study, secondary psychometric analysis, decision model, and systematic review.

Record important time features, such as a recall period, retest interval, repeated collection, model horizon, or literature-search end date, when they affect comparison. Do not collapse components when they use different samples or designs. State how components connect, such as survey utilities used as inputs to a decision model.

### 4. Focal entity and its role

What object does the paper study, and what does the paper do to it?

Current focal entities include:

- instrument, instrument version, translation, descriptive system, bolt-on, domain, item, response scale, or health-state profile;
- value set, tariff, utility score, level sum score, or other data product;
- preference-elicitation, mapping, scoring, statistical, or analysis method;
- intervention, comparator, decision model, implementation workflow, or data-collection process;
- conceptual framework, health determinant, inequality, or population outcome;
- dataset or data-collection infrastructure;
- professional practice, evidence need, or research priority.

Use a relation to state the role. Useful relations are **develops**, **adapts**, **uses**, **evaluates**, **compares**, **extends**, **values**, **derives**, **anchors**, **maps**, **estimates**, **associates**, **implements**, **reviews**, and **informs**. The relation is more informative than an unqualified list of instrument names.

### 5. Construct and intended use

What does the focal entity represent, and why can a researcher use it?

Current construct scopes are health status, health-related quality of life, broader quality of life or well-being, health-state preference and utility, population health and inequality, economic or policy consequence, implementation experience, and methods or evidence practice. Current use contexts include population-health assessment, clinical or trial outcome measurement, routine care, economic evaluation and QALY estimation, health technology assessment, social care, and instrument research.

Do not treat these terms as synonyms. In particular, a descriptive health profile, a level sum score, an EQ VAS response, a preference-weighted utility, a modeled QALY, and a cost-effectiveness ratio are different data products.

### 6. People, evidence units, and perspective

Who supplies the data, what does the data describe, and who is the intended population?

Record these as separate concepts when necessary:

- **Recruitment or sampling unit:** the unit selected for inclusion, such as person, dyad, publication, trial, country, or agency-associated respondent.
- **Data contributor or respondent:** for example, adult public respondent, child self-report respondent, caregiver proxy, patient, researcher, expert, or HTA practitioner.
- **Target of judgment:** the contributor's own health, another person's health, a hypothetical person's health, an instrument, a method, professional practice, or an implementation experience.
- **Analysis or evidence unit:** the unit used to estimate a result, such as person, health state, publication, trial, country, model scenario, or modeled cohort.
- **Target population or setting:** the population, country, language, culture, service, or decision setting to which the paper aims to apply.
- **Sampling basis:** probability sample, quota sample, convenience sample, panel sample, purposive professional network, or review inclusion process.

State the modeled unit separately from the source respondents when a decision model uses respondent data. State the proxy perspective separately from the target person's health and from caregiver spillover.

Do not convert a representativeness claim into a simple yes/no property. Record the sampling basis and the authors' reported shortfall.

### 7. Study procedures and data products

Which procedures and products are necessary to understand or compare the work?

Examples include cTTO, TTO, DCE, DCE with duration, VAS, sorting, response scaling, forward and back translation, cognitive interviews, thematic coding, questionnaires, mapping algorithms, Markov models, health-state profiles, level sum scores, utility scores, QALYs, cost-effectiveness ratios, direct and indirect costs, DALYs, population norms, and inequality indices. Survey quality procedures can include bot and duplicate detection, completion-time rules, missing-data checks, consistency checks, and quota monitoring.

Record which instrument component is administered, which scoring or mapping method creates a score, and which downstream analysis uses it. This prevents confusion between a descriptive system, a VAS, a derived utility, and a modeled decision outcome.

### 8. Comparison and evaluation basis

What is compared or tested, and by which criterion?

Current evaluation families are:

- **Distribution and feasibility:** missing responses, completion, floor, ceiling, response-category use, and respondent burden.
- **Reliability:** test-retest agreement for items, profiles, or scores.
- **Validity:** content, construct, convergent, known-group, structural, and other stated forms.
- **Responsiveness:** detection of improvement or worsening, the change anchor, and related effect measures.
- **Valuation and method performance:** coefficient order, statistical significance, logical consistency, fit, prediction error, anchoring, scale, time preference, censoring, heterogeneity, agreement, and data-quality rules.
- **Response-scale performance:** informativity, item discrimination, threshold coverage, and differential item functioning.
- **Translation and cultural adaptation:** conceptual or semantic equivalence, language clarity, response-order preservation, cultural relevance, and cognitive usability.
- **Incremental measurement value:** added construct coverage, ceiling reduction, discrimination, explanatory power, and burden when an item or bolt-on extends a base instrument.
- **Conceptual coverage:** theme or concept coverage, relevance, comprehensibility, comprehensiveness, the boundary of the intended construct, within-profile residual differences, and possible reporting heterogeneity.
- **Implementation feasibility and acceptability:** reach, initial and follow-up completion, willingness, workflow burden, mode, timing, accessibility, and user experience.
- **Data integrity and sample quality:** bot and duplicate detection, speeding, completion and dropout, missing data, outliers, repeated-item consistency, exclusion rules, quota achievement, coverage, and weighting limits.
- **Substantive outcome or decision comparison:** between-group or intervention difference, adjusted association, modeled QALY or cost difference, cost-effectiveness ratio, and possible decision consequence.
- **Practice profile:** frequency of use, data-source choice, data-quality concern, and stated priority.
- **Analysis adequacy:** data format, model choice, assumptions, baseline adjustment, missing-data method, and interpretation threshold.

Record a prespecified threshold, hypothesis, reference method, or decision rule when it controls the authors' conclusion. Do not assume that a failed hypothesis confirms that an instrument is invalid. Method agreement in one setting does not establish interchangeability in all settings.

### 9. Output and decision point

What reusable product or decision comes from the paper?

Examples are an instrument version, translated version, value set, conceptual framework, comparative performance result, method recommendation, modeled decision impact, population-outcome profile, implementation recommendation, evidence map, priority list, survey dataset, or recommendation for a method. Record the choice among alternatives when the paper makes one. Distinguish a final product from recommendations that inform a later development stage.

### 10. Scope, limitations, and uncertainty

What restricts interpretation or transfer?

Record important population, setting, cultural, language, sampling, measurement, analytic, implementation, data-integration, access, and reporting limits. Also record conflicts or gaps in the supplied metadata or article. Keep author-reported limitations separate from an ontology-review uncertainty.

## Useful relation patterns

The following patterns show how the concepts connect. They are examples, not a required syntax.

- A paper **develops** an instrument version **for** a target population **from** label-generation and cognitive-test evidence.
- A study **adapts** an instrument version **from** a source language **for** a target language and setting **with** translation and cognitive evidence.
- A study **derives** a value set **for** an instrument version **from** stated-preference data **with** a selected model.
- A study **evaluates** an instrument or item **for** a measurement property **in** a specified population.
- A study **compares** two methods **on** agreement, scale, prediction, or a downstream decision outcome.
- A study **estimates** a population or treatment outcome **from** an administered measure **with** a stated score or model.
- A study **implements** an instrument **in** a service workflow **and evaluates** feasibility and acceptability **from** patient and process evidence.
- A review **synthesizes** prior reports **about** an instrument or analysis practice **within** an inclusion scope.
- A survey **maps** individual professional practice **across** countries or regions **to inform** research priorities.
- A resource report **defines** a dataset or infrastructure **for** later population or instrument research.
- A secondary analysis **reuses** a named source study or dataset **and extends** it with a new question, model, or target output.
- A data-quality study **evaluates** a survey process **with** exclusion, consistency, missingness, and sample-composition indicators.

## Application boundary

This ontology records study intent, component designs, evidence roles, focal entities, comparisons, reusable outputs, and important limits. It does not encode detailed claims and supporting results. It does not duplicate authors, affiliations, journal data, or other structured metadata. It does not yet define database classes or a fixed serialization.
