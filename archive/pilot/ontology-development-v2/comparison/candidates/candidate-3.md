# Candidate 3: paper-first EuroQol ontology

Status: consolidated after four rounds and a 40-paper backward application review, 2026-08-16.

This record treats the paper as the main research record. Study components, evidence sources, instruments, methods, analyses, comparisons, products, and findings are subordinate to the paper. The record describes an ontology and extraction guidance. It is not a fixed data schema.

Paper numbers P1 to P10 refer to the order within each batch application section.

## Current ontology

### Core model

The main entity is a **paper research record**. It answers what the paper tried to do, what evidence and methods it used, what it produced, and what the authors concluded.

A paper can have one or more **study components**. Create a component only when a phase, sample, task, time point, or analysis path has a material difference. The difference must affect retrieval or interpretation. For example, batch-01 P6 needs separate DCE and cTTO components because the samples, designs, quality control, and roles in value-set estimation differ. Batch-01 P5 needs development, testing, and harmonization components. Do not create components only to copy section headings.

Use four forms of semantic information:

- A **controlled term** supports stable retrieval across papers.
- A **repeatable value** keeps exact paper-specific information, such as a language, country, condition, count, instrument version, or named model.
- A **relation** states a role or connection, such as an instrument being the target of validation or a sample providing preferences about a hypothetical child.
- A **concise narrative** preserves meaning that would become unclear in small labels.

The following ontology elements apply at paper level or component level as needed.

| Element | Preferred form | Extraction guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Research intent | Concise narrative | State the primary aim and any material secondary aim. Do not copy the abstract without synthesis. |
| Study family | Repeatable controlled term | Use one or more of: valuation and value-set development; valuation-methods study; social-value or priority-preference study; instrument development; translation or linguistic adaptation; cultural adaptation; psychometric or measurement-property study; qualitative concept or content-validity study; population-health description; population-norm study; health-inequality or equity analysis; mapping or crosswalk study; economic evaluation or decision-modeling study; cost-of-illness or burden study; implementation or use study; professional-practice or policy survey; survey-methods or data-quality study; systematic evidence review; statistical-methods study; conceptual or methodological guidance; research infrastructure or resource study; other EuroQol study. Terms are repeatable. For example, a Singapore language study can be both cultural adaptation and content-validity work. A valuation task does not by itself make a paper a value-set study. Protocol is an execution status, not a family. |
| Contribution | Controlled kind plus narrative | Use a small, repeatable kind such as empirical evidence, synthesized evidence, method evidence or guidance, conceptual framework, research product, practice or infrastructure description, or research-priority evidence. Then state what is new in this paper. Do not use the contribution kind as a second study-family field. |
| Research execution and evidence status | Controlled term | Distinguish planned protocol, pilot-informed protocol, ongoing empirical study, completed primary empirical study, completed secondary analysis, completed evidence synthesis, and conceptual or method-guidance article. Keep execution status separate from study family, product maturity, implementation state, and access. |
| Topic, construct, theory, or framework | Repeatable normalized term plus paper wording | Examples include HRQoL, quality of life, health-state utility, content validity, response-scale heterogeneity, QALY evidence, or the EQ-HWB conceptual framework. Keep a paper-local framework as a named object. Do not force all of its domains into universal ontology classes. |
| Study component | Relation from paper | Give the component a short functional name. Record its sample, task, instruments, methods, analysis, and findings only when these differ from other components. |
| Evidence source | Controlled type plus values | Identify people, documents, trial records, prior data sets, or other sources. For people, record the important selection method, inclusion and exclusion rules, recruitment channel, setting, and counts at meaningful stages. |
| Evidence roles | Explicit relations | Keep **evidence provider**, **answer referent**, **target population**, and **decision context** separate. A respondent can value another person's health. | Population and context | Repeatable values | Record country, language, clinical area or condition, age group, care or community setting, and policy setting when they define applicability. Record quota or representativeness targets and important transfer limits. |
| Equity or stratification factor | Normalized factor plus exact operationalization | Record the factor, unit, categories or cut points, reference group, and whether it is objective or subjective and individual, household, or area based. Type its role as a quota, covariate, subgroup, inequality rank, target of explanation, or other role. Keep missing, undisclosed, and inapplicable responses separate. A broad `income` or `socioeconomic status` tag is insufficient. |
| Instrument involvement | Typed relation plus exact identity | Record family, version, youth or adult form, level count, variant, language version, self or proxy form, and recall period when material. Type the role: instrument under development; target of valuation; target of validation; administered measure; comparator; convergent or anchor measure; source of health states; scoring route; survey topic; or product. A broad `used EQ-5D` label is insufficient. |
| Instrument configuration | Relation among an instrument and its modules | Record the exact base instrument and each attached bolt-on or module when the configuration is the object of a test. Keep a tested configuration separate from a finalized instrument version. Do not treat a list of possible bolt-ons as if all were administered together. |
| Score or outcome representation | Controlled type plus scoring relation | Distinguish item or dimension response, health profile, level sum score, value-set index or utility, VAS, mapped response, mapped utility, category, change score, QALY, DALY, years lived with disability, cost, inequality index, and other derived outcome. Link an index to its value set or mapping route. Link a derived outcome to the instrument data, economic inputs, and model that produced it. Do not treat a DALY, QALY, utility, or monetary burden as the same outcome. |
| Elicitation or study task | Controlled task plus narrative | For valuation, distinguish cTTO, conventional TTO, lead-time or lag-time TTO, DCE, DCE with duration, DCE with immediate death, VAS, PTO, and other tasks. Record health-state or health-gain framing, duration, design or block structure, and feedback when they affect comparison. For PTO, record the beneficiary groups, group-size path, forced choice or equivalence option, iteration rule, decision-maker perspective, and weighting output. Record the mathematical anchoring basis and the time-preference form separately from the task. For qualitative and development work, record interview, focus group, sorting, response scaling, cognitive interviewing, Delphi, co-design, coding, and harmonization paths. |
| Named protocol, platform, or design | Exact identity plus role | Record EQ-VT version, OPUF, survey platform, experimental design, reporting framework, or other named procedure when it changes conduct or comparison. A protocol or platform used by a paper is not automatically a product of that paper. |
| Administration | Several orthogonal values | Record medium or channel, human contact, response perspective, and setting separately. Examples are web survey, computer-assisted personal interview, paper-and-pencil self-completion, face-to-face qualitative interview, and proxy report. Do not compress these into one ambiguous mode term. |
| Analysis | Named method, purpose, and inference | Record the important statistical, modeling, psychometric, synthesis, or qualitative method and what it tests or estimates. Keep model families and material variants that change interpretation. Use narrative for detailed equations and diagnostics. |
| Assessment class | Controlled class | First classify an assessment as a measurement property, instrument-development criterion, valuation or mapping method-performance criterion, survey-process quality indicator, or implementation outcome. This prevents a bot check, a value-set prediction error, or response-level preference ordering from becoming a generic instrument-validity claim. |
| Measurement property | Controlled hierarchy plus operational test | Use specific properties. Keep feasibility or completion separate from item missingness. Treat ceiling and floor as distribution properties. Under content validity, distinguish relevance, comprehensiveness, and comprehensibility. Under construct validity, distinguish convergent, discriminant or divergent, and known-groups evidence. Also distinguish structural validity, test-retest reliability, measurement error, responsiveness, differential item functioning, inter-rater or proxy-self agreement, cross-instrument score agreement, informativity, and discrimination. Cross-version response consistency is a separate comparison property. Record a broad parent only when the paper gives no stable subtype. Link each property to its target item, score, instrument or configuration, comparator or anchor, subgroup, method, assessment level, and decision rule. State whether reliability applies to a response, task output, personal function, or aggregate product. |
| Comparison | Explicit relation with axes | State what is compared and on which axes: instrument version, language, population, condition, country, timing, administration, response scale, preference source, task, model, scoring route, or product. |
| Harmonization or calibration | Explicit relation plus transformation note | Record the original variables, response levels, value set, time period, or source definitions and the operation used to place them on a common basis. State what information the operation preserves or removes. Rescoring old profiles with a new value set, harmonizing country-specific education categories, and aligning 3L and 5L response levels are not generic data cleaning. |
| Product or research output | Controlled kind, identity, role, maturity, and access | Kinds include instrument, language version, value set, mapping, protocol, framework, data set, data infrastructure, implementation program or workflow, and method guidance. Record output role separately as the paper's main product, a reused input, a planned output, or a comparison-only analysis derivative. Record maturity separately as proposed, draft, experimental, tested, co-designed and mock-tested, pilot-ready, finalized or endorsed for the stated purpose, or superseded. Record release or access separately as available, restricted, not released, or planned. A restricted operating data resource can be mature. A generated comparison set is not an available scoring product. |
| Reuse or dependency | Explicit relation | State whether the paper reuses a sample, data set, protocol, value set, instrument, mapping, or model. If the source study is not identifiable from the supplied paper, preserve the authors' description and mark the identifier as unresolved. |
| Data-quality rule or indicator | Controlled indicator plus rule, stage, and action | Distinguish consent, link click, completion, dropout, item missingness, bot detection, speed, duplicate response, internal consistency, outlier, quota achievement, interviewer compliance, and other indicators. Record the operational threshold, collection stage, affected component, and whether the result caused a flag, exclusion, retraining, retention, or only a warning. Do not treat survey-process quality as an instrument measurement property. |
| Uncertainty source and handling | Controlled type, scope, and propagation relation | Distinguish variability, observed or latent heterogeneity, statistical uncertainty, and methodological variation when uncertainty is a study object or changes interpretation. Link it to the instrument, task, sample, model, mapping, value set, outcome, or decision model where it arises. Record how it is measured, reported, propagated, or tested. Keep parameter uncertainty separate from model specification and from source-data variability. Do not create this structure for every ordinary limitation. |
| Implementation state | Controlled stage plus workflow narrative | Distinguish proposed use, co-designed or pre-implementation prototype, feasibility or pilot use, local routine use, multi-site scale-up, and established system use. Record the site, encounter or workflow trigger, collection schedule, responsible actors, result display, review duty, feedback or flag rule, action pathway, completion and burden measures, barriers, facilitators, and integration target when material. Do not convert a prototype, successful pilot, or scale-up plan into documented system adoption. |
| Finding | Short scoped statement | Capture principal results only. Link them to the component, instrument, property, comparison, or product that they concern. Do not extract every estimate. Keep a few values when they define the result or product. |
| Interpretation | Attributed narrative | Record the authors' explanation or synthesis separately from the empirical finding. Mark it as author-reported. |
| Limitation or unresolved evidence | Attributed narrative plus scope | Record important author-reported limitations, source conflicts, extraction uncertainty, and transfer limits. Keep these narrative statements separate from structured uncertainty when uncertainty is itself a research object or a propagated method input. Do not turn a limitation into a corpus-level gap. |
| Implication or documented use | Attributed narrative with status | Separate a recommended or expected use from an actual documented use or effect. Do not infer policy impact. |
| Future work or gap | Attributed narrative | Record author-stated research needs. Keep these separate from later corpus-derived gaps. |

### Family-specific depth

The common core does not require the same method depth for every family.

Study-family terms describe what kind of research the paper does. Research execution status describes whether that work is planned, ongoing, primary, secondary, synthesized, or conceptual. Product maturity describes the state of an output. Use these three axes independently. Translation or linguistic adaptation records work on wording between languages or locales. Cultural adaptation records changes for local concepts or use. Content-validity work records evidence about relevance, comprehensiveness, or comprehensibility. These terms can all apply to one paper, but they are not synonyms.

For a valuation paper, record the target descriptive system and language, respondent and referent, preference source, experienced-own-health or hypothetical perspective, task form, duration and worse-than-dead framing, scale anchors, experimental design, protocol or software version, interview and quality-control process, exclusion rules, model families, mathematical anchoring basis, linear or nonlinear time-preference form, hybrid strategy, model-selection basis, final product, and intended decision setting. An EQ VAS score used as an experience-based valuation does not become a QALY-ready utility unless the paper supplies the required scoring and dead-state anchor. Do not infer the anchor from the presence of an immediate-death task.

For a social-value or priority-preference paper, record the decision-maker role, beneficiary groups and ages, health gain and duration, without-treatment counterfactual, attributes held equal, group sizes, forced choice or equivalence option, iterative search rule, randomization, weighting output, consistency tests, and comparison with attitudinal or qualitative evidence. Keep a social weight for a health gain separate from a health-state utility or value set.

For a measurement-property paper, record each property separately. Connect it to the target instrument, score or item, comparator or anchor, population subgroup, time interval, statistic, and stated criterion. A single `psychometric study` label cannot answer which property succeeded in which population.

For instrument development or cultural adaptation, preserve the sequence from concept or label generation through selection, testing, harmonization, and final version. Record source and target languages, participant groups, the role of target-user input, material protocol differences by country, and the product stage. When preference tasks test a developing descriptive system, record response-level ordering, non-zero decision relevance, task burden, and the effect of the task evidence on item retention separately from later value-set estimation.

For a qualitative concept or content-validity paper, record the theoretical position, sampling, interview form, language, coding approach, inductive and deductive inputs, coder process, framework construction, comparison target, saturation claim, and which aspect of content validity was assessed.

For a systematic review, record sources and search dates, eligibility boundaries, duplicate-study or shared-data handling, screening, extraction, synthesis, appraisal status, target instrument and outcomes, and principal coverage gaps. The reviewed populations are evidence context. They are not direct respondents to the review paper.

For a population survey, professional-practice survey, or research infrastructure paper, record the sampling frame, quota or recruitment strategy, survey administration, respondent role, instrument or topic battery, order and randomization, language adaptation, quality checks, analysis level, and whether the product is a survey result, a reusable data resource, or only a planned resource. For a time comparison, record the source sample and mode for each period and the common scoring or calibration route. Separate changes in responses from changes in preference weights or data collection.

For a cost-of-illness or burden paper, record the condition and prevalence basis, population, perspective, cost year and currency, resource-use sources, unit-cost sources, direct and indirect cost categories, productivity method, health-loss outcome, monetization rule, cost bearer, included and excluded categories, assumptions, and sensitivity analyses. Distinguish utility, QALY, DALY, years lived with disability, and monetary burden. The citation of a prior QALY analysis does not show that the current paper administered or scored a EuroQol instrument.

For a survey-methods or data-quality paper, record each indicator, its operational rule, the stage when it applies, its result, and the action taken. Keep invitation, link click, consent, completion, and item response as separate stages. Keep bot, speed, duplicate, consistency, missingness, outlier, and quota checks separate. State whether a rule flags or excludes a record and whether it was defined before or after pilot evidence.

For a mapping or crosswalk paper, record the source instrument and source variables, target instrument and target output, direct or response mapping, model family, development population, value set, and the population to which the mapping is applied. Preserve source and target response labels when a semantic difference can invalidate the assumed correspondence. Keep mapping development, generated analysis derivatives, application evidence, and performance evidence separate. If the mapping feeds an economic model, also record the model type, population, alternatives, perspective, time horizon, utility route, QALY and cost-effectiveness outcomes, and uncertainty analysis.

For an implementation or routine-collection paper, record the implementation stage, site and care workflow, eligible population, encounter or schedule, actual administration and data-capture modes, result display, scoring or non-scoring route, responsible reviewer, patient-selected or automated flags, response and escalation pathway, staff and patient burden, completion and retention measures, barriers, facilitators, and system integration. Separate a co-designed and mock-tested workflow from a clinical pilot, and separate the instrument used in a pilot from an instrument recommended for the next stage.

For a conceptual or methodological guidance paper, record its defined terms, framework or causal chain, scope, evidence basis, proposed practices, intended users, and unresolved method needs. State whether it used a systematic search, a non-systematic literature synthesis, worked examples, or author reasoning. Do not represent a guidance framework as completed empirical validation.

For a secondary analysis that combines source data sets, record each data source, original purpose, sample contribution, instrument order, mode and collection period. Record variable harmonization and any source-specific definition that limits a pooled result. State which subset or component overlaps with another paper. Do not reduce evidence independence to one paper-level yes or no value.

### Extraction sequence and checks

1. Write the paper aim, family, status, and contribution.
2. Decide whether paper-local components are necessary.
3. Identify evidence sources and separate provider, referent, target population, and decision context.
4. Add exact instruments and type each instrument's role.
5. Add methods, tasks, administration, score or output routes, analyses, comparisons, harmonization, and data-quality rules at the lowest useful component.
6. Add products and their maturity or access state.
7. Add principal findings, then author interpretation, limitations, implications, and future work as separate statements.
8. Add reuse or dependency relations and any source conflicts.
9. Check that a corpus query can combine instrument, version, language, population, country, condition, method, mode, and purpose without reading the full paper.
10. Check that the record does not claim use, effect, or impact that the paper did not document.

Deterministic title, author, identifier, affiliation, date, journal, licence, funding, and reference facts stay in the linked JATS or source layer. A semantic extraction can refer to the DOI for identification, but it does not reconstruct that metadata.

## Applications to batch 01

### P1 — `10.1007/s40258-021-00639-3`

**Intent, family, and contribution.** This completed valuation and value-set-development study aimed to create the first Danish EQ-5D-5L value set from adult general-population preferences and to select a suitable model from cTTO-only, DCE-only, and combined approaches. It contributes a Danish EQ-5D-5L value set and evidence about a heteroscedastic censored hybrid model. Topics include health-state utility, QALYs, Danish priority setting, preference elicitation, anchoring, logical consistency, and cross-country transferability.

**Components and evidence roles.** One recruitment and interview component supplied both valuation tasks. Statistics Denmark first supplied a random population sample. A market-research panel later supplemented recruitment. Of 4,585 invited people, 1,052 interviews took place and 1,014 interviews were analyzed after interviewer, software, withdrawal, health, and incomplete-task exclusions. The evidence providers were adults in Denmark. Their cTTO and DCE answers referred to hypothetical EQ-5D-5L health states. Their self-reported EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS answers referred to themselves. The value set represents Danish adult general-population preferences and is intended for Danish healthcare and medicine-prioritization decisions.

**Instruments, tasks, and administration.** The target of valuation was the Danish-language EQ-5D-5L descriptive system. EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS were also administered as self-report measures. EQ-VT version 2.1 was the named valuation and administration protocol. Computer-assisted personal interviews took place face to face. Each respondent valued 10 states by cTTO and seven pairs by DCE. The cTTO used conventional TTO for better-than-dead states and lead-time TTO for worse-than-dead states. The DCE had no duration attribute. The EQ-VT blocked design covered 86 cTTO states and 196 DCE pairs, with randomized blocks and order. A cTTO feedback module let respondents flag an unwanted rank order. The interview also contained extra experimental DCE and priority-setting questions that this paper did not report.

**Selection and quality control.** Recruitment monitored age, gender, education, and region against national data. Interviewers received 2.5 days of training. The EQ-VT quality-control tool checked worse-than-dead explanation, example and task time, and a severe-state consistency indicator. Fortnightly reports supported interviewer feedback. States that respondents flagged in feedback were removed from the main cTTO analysis. Respondents without both task types were excluded. Non-trading and other logical inconsistencies did not cause respondent exclusion.

**Analysis and comparisons.** Important analysis paths were generalized least-squares random-intercept and random-effects Tobit models for cTTO; conditional and heteroscedastic conditional logit models for DCE; and two hybrid combinations. Censoring, respondent heterogeneity, and heteroscedasticity were material model features. Logical ordering was the main model-selection criterion. A sensitivity analysis restored 712 feedback-removed observations. The final value set was compared with the direct Danish EQ-5D-3L value set and the Danish 3L-to-5L crosswalk. This comparison changes interpretation because these are different instrument versions and scoring routes. The paper also identifies response-label differences: 3L `confined to bed` versus 5L `unable to walk about`, and 3L `some problems` versus 5L `moderate problems` in three dimensions. Keep these semantics in the comparison provenance. Do not treat level numbers as equivalent meanings.

**Product and findings.** The paper finalized a Danish EQ-5D-5L value set for use. The heteroscedastic censored hybrid model was the only tested model with fully consistent parameters and was selected. It predicts all 3,125 states, with a reported range from 1 to -0.757. Anxiety/depression had the largest decrement, followed by pain/discomfort. About 22% of directly observed states were rated worse than dead. The sensitivity analysis did not materially change estimates. Compared with the Danish 3L and crosswalk sets, the 5L set had a lower worst-state value and a different dimension ranking.

**Author interpretation, limits, and implications.** The authors attribute data quality to the detailed protocol, interviewer training, and continued quality control. They recommend the new EQ-5D-5L value set for Danish QALY estimation. This is an author-reported recommendation and expected decision use; the paper does not document a later policy effect. Reported limits include the mid-study recruitment-source change, slight under-representation of young adults and lower education groups, and unresolved theoretical foundations for combining TTO and DCE. The authors call for more work on the utility-theory basis of hybrid models and on DCE anchoring. Country differences in dimension weights limit transfer of foreign value sets.

### P2 — `10.1007/s11136-020-02688-y`

**Intent, family, and contribution.** This completed systematic evidence review summarized psychometric properties of the official EQ-5D-5L and identified evidence gaps. It contributes a property-by-property evidence synthesis, with limited random-effects pooling. It is both a systematic review and a statistical synthesis study.

**Evidence source and coverage.** The evidence sources were 99 publications from 32 countries, not the people in those source studies. Searches covered MEDLINE/PubMed, PsycINFO, EMBASE, and the EuroQol website through January 2019. Two reviewers independently screened titles, abstracts, and full text, with senior adjudication. The review included human studies of adults, in German or English, that explicitly assessed psychometric or measurement properties of the official EQ-5D-5L. It excluded experimental 5L versions and studies that only applied the instrument without a psychometric aim. When publications shared an underlying data set, the review extracted the data once. This is an important evidence-reuse and de-duplication relation.

**Instrument roles and properties.** The EQ-5D-5L descriptive system was the validation target. The EQ VAS was outside the main target because the version was often unclear. EQ-5D-3L, other multi-attribute utility instruments, global health, function, pain, mental health, satisfaction, cognition, clinical measures, and change anchors served as comparators or anchors in source studies. Extracted properties were missingness or feasibility, floor and ceiling distributions, test-retest reliability, content and construct validity, convergent validity, known-group validity, and responsiveness. Internal consistency was explicitly treated as not applicable to the EQ-5D framework.

**Methods.** The review extracted predefined study and property information. It pooled full-health proportions, mean index values, and correlation coefficients with random-effects models when heterogeneity and available data allowed. It used Fisher transformation for correlations and logit transformation for proportions. Property thresholds and interpretation rules were stated, including missingness, floor effects, kappa or ICC, and effect-size conventions.

**Findings.** Missing values were acceptable in all 17 studies that reported them, and floor effects were usually acceptable. All nine reliability papers supported index-score reliability, but several showed weaker item-level stability, especially for pain/discomfort. The EQ-5D-5L index and dimensions generally showed expected known-group patterns and moderate-to-strong convergence with multi-attribute utility, physical or functional, pain, activities-of-daily-living, and clinical measures. Correlations were weak for life satisfaction and cognition or communication. Fifteen studies addressed responsiveness. Effects were generally moderate in groups expected to improve, but methods and anchors were heterogeneous. General-population samples had substantial full-health ceilings. The self-care dimension had a high ceiling in many populations, but it remained relevant in groups with expected self-care limitations.

**Author interpretation, limits, and gaps.** The authors conclude that EQ-5D-5L is reliable and valid across many populations and settings. They do not claim that every property works in every condition. They identify responsiveness as the main unresolved property and call for clearer relevant-change definitions and anchors. They also call for validation outside Western Europe, OECD countries, and East Asia, and for setting-specific assessment. Reported limits are the exclusion of experimental versions and application-only evidence, no review of valuation methods, and heterogeneity that prevented wider pooling. Choice of value set can alter index distributions and responsiveness, but this review did not have enough evidence to quantify that effect. There is a count inconsistency in the paper: the abstract reports 889 identified publications, while the body reports 496 initial and 397 update records, which sum to 893. Preserve the published counts with an extraction warning.

### P3 — `10.1007/s11136-025-03983-2`

**Intent, family, status, and contribution.** This paper describes the rationale, design, governance, and data-collection methods of EQ-DAPHNIE. The study families are protocol or research infrastructure, multi-country population health survey, instrument comparison, and future population-norm production. The project aims to create comparable general-population data across countries and a reusable infrastructure for population-health assessment and instrument evaluation. The paper reports design and completed collection dates and counts, but it does not report health-status results.

**Components, countries, and evidence roles.** The paper separates a 2023 United Kingdom pilot, a 2024 round in Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and the United States, and a 2024 second round in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, China, France, Germany, Japan, Mexico, the Netherlands, and Spain. Further geographic expansion was planned. Adult online-panel members were the evidence providers and the referents for most self-report measures. In one vignette component, respondents rated the hypothetical person Alex, who was framed as the same age and background as the respondent. The target is each country's general adult population and cross-country comparison.

**Selection, administration, and sample stages.** Dynata recruited panel members through several online channels. Eligibility required age 18 or older, panel membership, consent, and survey completion. Quotas used age, sex, income, urban or rural residence, and official language where relevant. The target was 4,500 completed surveys per country. Country collections reported about 4,500 completions each after the UK pilot of 3,012. Quota controls could relax after five of the planned six weeks, with a minimum goal of 85% adherence. The project planned post-stratification weighting. The survey used LimeSurvey version 5 or later, was self-administered on the web, took about 20 minutes, and used approximately 50 screens. A 250-response soft launch supported review before full release. Duplicate participation was prevented. Age and marital-status questions were repeated as quality checks. No item was mandatory.

**Instrument battery and roles.** EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS were administered measures and central population-health outcomes. Country-specific EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons included vision, hearing, breathing, sleep, tiredness, social relationships, cognition, skin irritation, and self-confidence. Other administered comparator measures included the experimental EQ-HWB or EQ-HWB-S, PROMIS-10, ASCOT SCT4 or ICECAP-A, WHO-5, OPQOL-brief for respondents aged 65 or older, PHQ-2, and GAD-2. The author-developed response-scale-heterogeneity vignette used EQ-5D-5L as the rating instrument. EQ-5D-5L self-report came before the vignette. Remaining standardized measures were randomized. The battery varied by country, so instrument-by-country involvement must remain repeatable and must not be reduced to one global list.

**Language and adaptation.** Survey languages included English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Dutch, and German. Standardized instrument translations came from developers when available. A professional vendor translated other survey content. Native speakers and local researchers reviewed language and response options. Some questions were adapted to country context. This is survey translation and local adaptation. It is not development of new official instrument language versions.

**Product and maturity.** The product is an operating multi-country survey infrastructure and a set of country data collections. The paper reports completed collection for the pilot and first 15 countries, with future rounds planned. Data access was restricted to EuroQol members or collaboration with a member at publication. The project had executive, population-health, instrument-comparison, and data-access governance groups. Record the resource as collected and governed, not as fully open and not as evidence that all planned longitudinal work exists.

**Author interpretation, limits, and gaps.** The authors expect the standardized infrastructure to support cross-country population assessment, instrument comparison, and policy research. This is intended use, not documented downstream policy effect. They report web coverage and panel representativeness as limits, along with cultural differences in item interpretation and the cross-sectional design. They propose tests of other sampling modes, longitudinal follow-up, and serial panels. The paper switches between past and future tense for ethics and study conduct even though round dates and achieved counts are reported. The extraction therefore marks the project as operational with completed cross-sectional rounds and planned expansion, rather than treating all described future work as complete.

### P4 — `10.1017/s0266462326103602`

**Intent, family, and contribution.** This completed cross-sectional professional-practice and policy survey examined how HTA practitioners use and judge HRQoL instruments, elicitation methods, preference sources, and utility data, and which research they prioritize. It contributes global practice and needs evidence rather than a new HRQoL instrument or value set.

**Evidence providers and decision context.** Evidence providers were personnel or contracted experts who worked with national HTA bodies. The sampling process approached 60 countries, distributed the survey in 49, and obtained 238 completed surveys from 45 countries and 65 agencies. Respondents covered Asia, Central and Eastern Europe, Western Europe, Latin America, the Middle East and Africa, and a Commonwealth group. Most worked in the public sector, most had QALY-related responsibilities, and many reviewed industry cost-effectiveness evidence. Their answers described personal practice and views. They were not official agency positions. The target decision context was QALY-based cost-effectiveness assessment, reimbursement, listing, and pricing.

**Recruitment and administration.** The authors used purposive, network-based, two-stage recruitment. They identified HTA agencies, then used EuroQol members or their contacts as country recruiters. Eligible respondents had relevant national HTA employment or advisory roles, could understand the English form, and consented. Qualtrics delivered an anonymous web survey from April 2023 to January 2024. The form had six topic sections, four-point frequency scales, and open-text fields. Respondents could answer open text in another language; the team used forward and backward translation. Several international pilot rounds refined the form.

**Tasks, design, and analysis.** In each kaizen task, respondents select a sequence of four improvements from four good changes and one worsening change. A selected worsening is type-1 discordance, and an unselected improvement is type-2 discordance. The task yields a preference path and more comparisons than one paired choice. Wave 2 presents two impaired profiles. Both waves randomize attribute order and task sequence. The planned main-effects conditional-logit models test response-level decision relevance. Cluster bootstraps will compare uncertainty and main-effect agreement. Lin concordance will compare waves, and wave-1 predictions will be tested against wave-2 choices. Discordance thresholds of 10%, 20%, and 30% are study hypotheses, not universal instrument-validity rules.

**Recruitment, quality, product, and unresolved quota.** The online panel uses gender, age, and ancestry quotas. Exclusions cover withdrawal, invalid open-text child descriptions, completion below 10 minutes, and retake attempts. Checklists and warm-up tasks support comprehension. Only wave-1 materials are complete because wave 2 will adapt to wave-1 evidence. Panel selection, English and internet requirements, and greater burden from choosing between impaired child profiles limit transfer. The age quota list contains `45–64` and `55+`, which overlap. The intended upper age bands are unresolved and require source correction.

### P8 — `10.1007/s40258-025-00954-z`

**Intent, family, status, and contribution.** This completed secondary mapping, value-set comparison, and statistical-methods study compared direct EQ-VT EQ-5D-5L value sets with same-country five-level crosswalk sets. It contributes country-level agreement evidence, a same-respondent test in three countries, and application evidence in a multi-condition data set. It reuses an existing crosswalk algorithm and does not develop a new mapping function.

**Components, sources, and dependencies.** The main component paired 19 three-level source sets with 19 direct five-level sets from 18 countries; the United States contributed two pairs. Each three-level set generated an EQ-5D-5L crosswalk derivative with the Van Hout response mapping. A stronger comparison component used Hungary, Romania, and United States parallel studies in which the same respondents supplied three-level and five-level EQ-VT data. A reverse-crosswalk component mapped those direct five-level sets back to 3L. An application component applied all 19 pairs to 7,933 EQ-5D-5L profiles from the multi-instrument comparison data set, with severe-profile and mobility-level-5 subsets. Published and unpublished value sets and the patient data set are evidence sources; the source-study respondents are not new respondents to this paper.

**Mapping provenance and comparisons.** Each path retains country, source EQ-5D-3L value set, valuation method and date, crosswalk algorithm, target EQ-5D-5L output, and direct EQ-VT comparator. The most severe mobility labels are also retained: `confined to bed` in 3L and `unable to walk about` in 5L. Rank, range, state 55555, percentage below zero, full-set and severe-state means, Spearman and Pearson correlations, Lin concordance, mean absolute difference, scatterplots, and Bland-Altman plots assessed agreement. Publication interval was a proxy for elapsed time and cannot by itself measure preference change.

**Findings.** Spearman correlations ranged from 0.831 to 0.989 and were below 0.9 in 11 of 19 pairs. Mean absolute difference averaged 0.149 and was below 0.1 in only five pairs. No country preserved the rank of the five single-severe-dimension states. In 15 pairs, mobility-level-5 states formed a visible group in Bland-Altman plots; ten formed a separate band. This pattern remained in the three same-respondent studies and in reverse crosswalks. In the applied data, mean utility differed for every pair, including all severe-profile comparisons. The direction and size varied by country.

**Interpretation, derivative boundary, and limits.** The authors advise direct five-level valuation when feasible and restrict crosswalk use to cases where direct studies are impractical. This is method guidance, not evidence of a reimbursement change. The generated crosswalk sets are analysis derivatives and must not be listed as newly released national products. Differences can arise from valuation method, source-set age, population, crosswalk-development population, model choice, and response-label meaning. The 19 pairs are too few to isolate the contribution of each source. Even the parallel component does not prove that label change is the only cause, so the label explanation remains the authors' strongly supported interpretation.

### P9 — `10.1177/0272989x251380556`

**Intent, family, status, and contribution.** This conceptual and methodological guidance article explains how uncertainty in health-state values arises and propagates into QALYs and cost-effectiveness analysis. It contributes a four-type uncertainty vocabulary, a dependency framework from valuation to decision model, and reporting recommendations. It is not a systematic review, valuation study, mapping study, or economic evaluation, and it reports no new participant data.

**Evidence basis and framework.** The authors use prior literature, methodological reasoning, and an illustrative EQ-5D-3L variance calculation. No systematic search, eligibility process, or formal appraisal is reported. The framework distinguishes intrinsic individual variability, observed or latent heterogeneity, statistical uncertainty, and methodological variation. It traces uncertainty through valuation studies, health-state descriptive-system profile data, other descriptive-system data, response or direct mapping, disease-state studies, meta-analysis, and cost-effectiveness models. Each downstream use inherits uncertainty from upstream inputs and adds its own.

**Valuation-specific sources and handling.** Design sources include whose preferences count, sample frame, selected health states, and prior scale constraints. Elicitation sources include comprehension, engagement, constructed preferences, heuristics, task and mode choice, interviewer effects, and fraud. Modeling sources include summary statistic, interpolation model, parameter uncertainty, and misspecification. Proposed handling includes representative-sample checks, state-design and order controls, quality processes, alternative models, deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analysis, state-level standard errors, and reporting a coefficient variance-covariance matrix. These are framework elements and recommendations, not empirical estimates of each source's importance.

**Instrument roles, product, and finding boundary.** EQ-5D, SF-6D, HUI, and mapping examples are objects in the framework; none is administered. The paper's product is method guidance for researchers, modelers, instrument developers, HTA bodies, and value-set users. Its main conclusion is that point estimates hide uncertainty that can affect QALYs and ICERs, especially after mapping or meta-analysis. An illustrative prior-data calculation gives small profile-level standard errors for one EQ-5D-3L study, but it does not establish small uncertainty for other sets or methods.

**Limits and gaps.** The source list is explicitly not exhaustive, the relative size of most uncertainty sources is not quantified, and difficult normative questions about whose preferences should guide decisions remain outside statistical resolution. The authors call for state-level uncertainty or variance-covariance reporting, better guidance, propagation into economic models, and research on unresolved methodological uncertainty. The ontology retains this uncertainty framework because it supports retrieval and interpretation, but it does not require an uncertainty object for every ordinary paper limitation.

### P10 — `10.1007/s11136-025-03996-x`

**Intent, family, status, and contribution.** This completed co-design and pre-implementation product-development study created the P-PROM ROCK program for use of EQ-5D-Y-5L in outpatient care at one Australian tertiary pediatric hospital. ### Why these details are controlled, repeated, related, or narrated

Study family, task type, property, administration axis, product kind, maturity, implication status, and evidence-source type need controlled terms because researchers will filter on them across the corpus. Countries, languages, conditions, instrument versions, counts, named protocols, model names, and frameworks need repeatable values because one paper can contain several.

Instrument role, evidence role, component membership, comparison, production, and reuse need relations because their meaning depends on both ends. For example, `EQ-5D-Y-3L — target of valuation — P6 cTTO component` is different from `EQ-5D-Y-3L — administered self-report — P6 interview context`.

Research intent, detailed recruitment, method rationale, principal finding, interpretation, limitation, implication, and future work need concise narrative. Their meaning is often lost when split into small fixed labels. Narrative stays scoped to the paper or component and is still supported by controlled topic tags.

Round 2 adds controlled score forms, mapping types, implementation stages, measurement properties, and module roles. It adds relations for configuration membership, score derivation, mapping provenance, decision-model input, and source-data harmonization. Detailed workflow barriers, construct debates, linguistic choices, and causal cautions remain narrative because a small label would remove their context.

Round 3 adds controlled equity-factor roles, cost-of-illness methods, survey-quality indicators, cross-version response consistency, anchoring bases, and time-preference forms. It adds relations for calibration, source-component overlap, cost inputs, and quality-rule actions. Detailed causal explanations, country-specific quota failures, cost assumptions, and reasons for within-profile gradients remain narrative.

Round 4 adds controlled social-priority study, PTO task, conceptual-guidance, proxy-self agreement, response-level preference concordance, implementation-prototype, uncertainty-type, and analysis-derivative terms. It adds relations for uncertainty propagation, response-label mapping provenance, proxy perspective, aggregation level, review duty, result display, and workflow action. ## Consolidation decision record

### Consolidated boundary decisions

The 40-paper review retained the paper-first core and the four information forms. It made the following boundary changes.

1. **Study family, research execution status, product maturity, implementation state, and access are independent.** The earlier `protocol or research infrastructure` family mixed a planned-study status with a type of research. The consolidated terms let batch-01 P3 be a completed infrastructure description with collected but restricted data, batch-04 P1 and P7 be pilot-informed protocols, batch-04 P10 be a completed co-design study with a pilot-ready prototype, and batch-02 P9 be a completed one-site pilot with only planned scale-up. ## Rejected distinctions and structures

- **A fixed nested JSON contract was rejected.** The batch needs different component depth by study family. A fixed slot set would either hide P5 and P6 phase structure or produce many empty fields in P4 and P8.
- **A single primary-method or primary-family value was rejected.** P3 is both research infrastructure and a population or instrument-comparison survey. P8 is qualitative concept work and content-validity assessment. Repeatable family labels preserve both purposes.
- **A generic `uses instrument` relation was rejected.** It confuses administration, development, valuation, validation, comparison, scoring, and professional discussion.
- **A one-value administration mode was rejected.** `Digital`, `interview`, `self-report`, and `proxy` are not alternatives on one scale. They describe different axes.
- **A universal hierarchy of every QoL domain and subtheme was rejected.** P8 gives evidence for a named local framework. Converting all 57 subthemes into global controlled classes after one paper would add unstable detail and erase framework provenance.
- **A controlled label for every survey item, every regression variant, or every table estimate was rejected.** P3's full battery, P4's full survey form, P7's coefficients, and P9's long method inventory are useful only at selected depth. Important named methods and items are retained when they change retrieval or inference; exhaustive tables remain in the paper.
- **A detailed claim–evidence graph was rejected.** Principal findings are linked to their component and subject. Every coefficient, quotation, and table cell is not represented as a separate claim.
- **A broad `representative sample` flag was rejected.** P1 uses probability recruitment plus a later panel, P3 uses online quota sampling and weighting, P6 uses nonprobability quotas, and P4 uses network recruitment. Preserve the authors' representativeness claim with the actual method and transfer limits.
- **An inferred impact field was rejected.** Expected HTA use, author recommendation, research priority, actual data access, and documented adoption are different states. Only the state supported by the paper is recorded.
- **Deep general disease, geography, and statistical taxonomies were rejected for this round.** Exact paper terms and a small normalized grouping are enough for retrieval. A general ontology of medicine, geography, or statistics is outside the EuroQol paper-first purpose.
- **Deterministic bibliographic reconstruction was rejected.** The semantic record links to DOI and paper number only. JATS and the source layer remain canonical for title, author, date, funding, licence, and references.
- **A generic `utility score` field without derivation was rejected.** Batch-02 P7 and P10 show that direct value-set scoring, crosswalk scoring, direct mapping, response mapping, and another preference-based instrument can give different ranges and decisions.
- **A new instrument version for every tested bolt-on combination was rejected.** Batch-02 P6 tests configurations. It does not name, finalize, or value each combination as a product.
- **One `validated` flag was rejected.** A paper can support one property, output, comparator, or subgroup and fail or not test another. Batch-02 P3, P6, and P10 require property-scoped findings.
- **A binary `implemented` field was rejected.** Batch-02 P9 distinguishes a completed pilot, actual one-site collection, planned platform integration, and proposed provincial scale-up.
- **A mapping label without source and target detail was rejected.** Batch-02 P7 needs the source instrument, variable form, target version and output, model, development population, value set, and applied population.
- **Collapsing pooled data into one homogeneous sample was rejected.** Batch-02 P10 has material differences in source-study questions, instrument order, mode, period, and covariate availability.
- **Automatic transfer of a language version to all countries that use that language was rejected.** Batch-02 P5 finalizes Modern Standard Arabic for Egypt but calls for local validation elsewhere. P1 shows why language can also mark different lived experience.
- **Availability of a proxy form was rejected as proof of proxy evidence.** Batch-02 P9 made proxy version 1 available but did not report its use. An instrument option is not an observed respondent role.
- **A generic equity tag without factor definition was rejected.** Batch-03 P1, P8, and P10 use different income and education constructs, units, categories, and analytic roles. Combining them as one socioeconomic exposure would create false comparisons.
- **A data-quality flag as proof of instrument validity was rejected.** Batch-03 P9 evaluates survey records and sampling processes. Bot, speed, missingness, and quota results do not establish content validity, reliability, or responsiveness of EQ-5D-5L.
- **One universal good-quality threshold was rejected.** Batch-03 P7 and P9 use project-specific interviewer, bot, speed, and quota rules. Preserve the rule and consequence. Do not infer a corpus-wide pass or fail label.
- **Immediate-death task and immediate-death anchor were not merged.** Batch-03 P2 asks respondents to compare states with death. P6 shows that the mathematical decision to set immediate death to zero is a separate and consequential model choice.
- **DALY, QALY, utility, and monetary burden were not merged into a health-economic outcome.** Batch-03 P5 uses disability weights, DALYs, and a value-of-statistical-life monetization route. This route cannot answer a query for EQ-5D utility, QALY, or cost-effectiveness.
- **A detailed universal cost ledger was rejected.** Batch-03 P5 supports controlled top-level cost categories and linked source assumptions. Every service, drug, transport mode, wage input, and table cell remains paper-level detail unless it changes a comparison.
- **A paper-level independent-evidence flag was rejected.** Batch-03 P6 reuses one DCE sample and an independent cTTO benchmark. P8 combines three source samples. Independence differs by component and inference.
- **A temporal difference as a causal event effect was rejected.** Batch-03 P8 reports lower outcomes in 2022–2023 than in 2012, but sampling, mode, reporting, and social conditions also differ. The paper does not identify a pandemic effect.
- **A within-profile EQ VAS gradient as proof of an omitted dimension was rejected.** Batch-03 P10 cannot distinguish omitted health content from response-scale heterogeneity. Both mechanisms remain author interpretations.
- **Background QALY discussion as EuroQol instrument use was rejected.** Batch-03 P5 cites a prior QALY analysis but does not administer or score EQ-5D in the current paper.
- **A PTO social weight as a health-state value or utility was rejected.** Batch-04 P1 weights equal gains for beneficiary age groups. The output cannot score an EQ profile or be inserted into a QALY without an additional normative and modeling path.
- **Every EQ VAS score as a QALY-ready utility was rejected.** Batch-04 P2 uses EQ VAS for experienced-own-health valuation, but dead is not anchored and the broader VAS construct is not the same as a value-set index.
- **A causal online-mode effect from the batch-04 P3 comparison was rejected.** Interviewer presence, panel source, payment, tutorial, feedback, and quality review differ with mode. The bundled comparison supports a practical contrast, not isolation of one cause.
- **Proxy-self agreement as proof that a proxy can replace self-report was rejected.** Batch-04 P4 shows condition-, dimension-, and time-specific agreement. It does not establish interchangeability in all pediatric use.
- **Aggregate OPUF reliability as personal-function reliability was rejected.** Batch-04 P6 has similar group decrements but weak individual task and decrement agreement.
- **A DCE in instrument development as a completed value-set study was rejected.** Batch-04 P7 plans preference tests of response ordering and attribute relevance. It does not estimate a scoring set.
- **Numeric response-level equivalence across mapped versions was rejected.** Batch-04 P8 shows that different mobility labels at the mapped severe levels can change values systematically.
- **A generated comparison set as a released scoring product was rejected.** The online, crosswalk, reverse-crosswalk, test, and retest sets in batch-04 P3, P6, and P8 are analysis derivatives.
- **An uncertainty object for every limitation was rejected.** Batch-04 P9 supports structure when uncertainty is the study topic, a quantified result, or part of a dependency chain. Ordinary transfer limits remain attributed narrative.
- **A co-designed and mock-tested program as implemented routine care was rejected.** Batch-04 P10 is a pre-implementation prototype. Phase 3 must still test real workflow use and effects.

### Consolidation rejections

- **One exclusive study-family term was rejected.** The consolidated list is more precise, but it remains repeatable. Batch-04 P5 requires cultural adaptation, instrument development, and content-validity terms at the same time.
- **Protocol, study family, and product kind were not merged.** A protocol states planned or governed conduct. A research-infrastructure paper can describe completed collections. A paper can also produce a protocol as its research output. The relation and execution status decide the meaning.
- **Product maturity and availability were not merged.** Batch-01 P3 has an operating resource with restricted access. Batch-02 P5 and batch-04 P5 have locale-endorsed language products that still need psychometric evidence. Access and validation need are not one stage.
- **Every broad and narrow measurement-property term was not retained as a duplicate tag.** Use `content validity—comprehensiveness` instead of adding both unqualified `content validity` and `comprehensiveness`. Use the parent only when the subtype is not stable from the paper.
- **Response-level ordering, value-set prediction, survey quality, and implementation completion were not forced into measurement-property vocabulary.** They belong to different assessment classes and answer different user questions.
- **All alternative model outputs were not treated as products.** An unselected model path becomes an analysis derivative only when its output matters to a comparison. Model specifications that only support selection remain analysis detail.
- **A universal semantic crosswalk between 3L and 5L level numbers was rejected.** Keep paper-supported response wording in provenance. Do not infer equivalence from ordinal position.
- **A universal causal explanation for crosswalk, mode, time, or equity differences was rejected.** The applications keep the observed comparison and the authors' candidate explanations separate.
- **A universal threshold for quality, reliability, agreement, saturation, or implementation success was rejected.** Preserve the study rule, assessment level, and consequence.
