# H07 — Measurement Properties of Commonly Used Generic Preference-Based Measures in East and South-East Asia

Source publication: Qian et al., *PharmacoEconomics* 38(2), 2020; DOI 10.1007/s40273-019-00854-w. The article was published online 25 November 2019 and gives issue date 2020.
Source location: article abstract, methods, results tables, discussion, and conclusion.
Extraction date and method: 2026-08-17; manual extraction from the assigned article file.
Review status: validation holdout extraction.

## 1. Identity and study type

- Study: systematic review of measurement-property evidence for generic preference-based measures (PBMs) in East and South-East Asian populations.
- Study types: systematic review or evidence synthesis; measurement-property or psychometric study.
- Review scope: published evidence on construct validity, test-retest reliability, and responsiveness.
- Geographic scope: China, Hong Kong, Japan, Singapore, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia, South Korea, Thailand, Vietnam, and the Philippines were searched as country or district terms. No relevant Philippine studies were found.
- Funding: EuroQol Research Foundation, grant EQ Project 2016230.
- Source: abstract; Methods, “Identification and Selection of Studies”; Funding.

## 2. Population and samples

- Target evidence population: people in East and South-East Asian countries or districts represented in validation studies of generic PBMs.
- Recruitment source: MEDLINE (OvidSP), EMBASE (OvidSP), PsycINFO (OvidSP), and PubMed, searched up to August 2019.
- Review sample: 79 retained papers containing 1,504 individual studies. The review treats each tested hypothesis, ICC, or SES value as one individual study; one paper can contain several studies.
- Selection flow: 1,710 records initially identified, 735 after duplicate removal, 114 after title/abstract assessment, and 79 after full-text assessment.
- Populations represented: general population and disease groups including cancer, developmental, diabetes, eye, gastric, genitourinary, heart, hepatitis, HIV, injury, kidney, mental, musculoskeletal, neurological, respiratory, rheumatic, skin, stroke, thyroid, and multiple conditions.
- Participant sample sizes, mean ages, sex distributions, and administration languages were extracted from the included studies, but the review does not report one pooled participant count.
- Source: Methods, “Identification and Selection of Studies” and “Data Extraction”; Results, opening paragraphs and Table 1.

## 3. Research concepts

- Measurement properties of generic PBMs: construct validity, test-retest reliability, and responsiveness.
- Health-related quality of life and preference-based health-state measurement for quality-adjusted life-year and cost-utility applications.
- Cross-population, disease-group, country, language, and administration-mode performance of PBMs.
- Methodological quality and quality of evidence supporting each population-specific measurement-property assessment.
- Source: Introduction; Abstract; Methods, “Assessment of the Preference-Based Measures”.

## 4. Instruments and administration

- Instruments searched and assessed: EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L (treated together as EQ-5D); EQ-VAS; SF-6D; HUI2 and HUI3 (treated together as HUI); and QWB. The search also included 15D and AQOL, but no relevant studies were found for them. EQ-VAS is not a PBM; the review included it because it is part of EQ-5D.
- Instrument roles: the included studies used these measures for construct-validity, test-retest-reliability, or responsiveness assessment.
- Exact instrument terms retained by the review include “EuroQol-5 Dimensions (EQ-5D)”, “EuroQol-Visual Analog Scale (EQ-VAS)”, “Short Form-6 Dimensions (SF-6D)”, “Health Utilities Index (HUI)”, and “Quality of Well-Being (QWB)”.
- Language, form, and administration: source studies reported language and study design, but this review did not analyse different language versions or self- versus interviewer-administration modes separately.
- The review does not report pooled values for recall period, setting, respondent perspective, or time point. Individual source-study details are not reproduced in this review-level record.
- Source: Methods, “Identification and Selection of Studies”, “Data Extraction”, and “Assessment of the Preference-Based Measures”; Table 1.

## 5. Methods, protocol, and task design

- Research method: systematic literature review guided by the COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN) guideline.
- Search method: database searches in August 2019 using country/district, PBM, and measurement-property terms, including spelling variations, acronyms, related terms, and an adapted measurement-properties search filter.
- Selection criteria: original research with primary data assessing construct validity, test-retest reliability, and/or responsiveness of a PBM in a country or district of interest. Reviews, mapping reports, non-English reports, commentaries, and conference abstracts were excluded.
- Review process: two review-team members worked independently through all phases; discrepancies were resolved in consensus meetings with the other two members.
- Extraction fields: PBM, sampling country or district, medical condition, sample size, mean age, sex distribution, administration language, study design, and result.
- Named protocol or registration: not reported. The COSMIN guideline was used as methodological guidance; a protocol version or task-design block was not reported.
- Source: Methods, “Identification and Selection of Studies” and “Data Extraction”.

## 6. Analysis and statistical models

- Individual-study result grading:
  - Construct validity: each result was rated positive or negative against a review-team hypothesis, including known-groups and convergent-validity hypotheses.
  - Test-retest reliability: ICC of at least 0.70 was rated positive; lower ICC was rated negative.
  - Responsiveness: standardized effect size (SES) of at least 0.20 was rated positive; lower SES was rated negative. The team used SES because all included responsiveness studies reported SES or data usable to calculate it; only one reported both AUC and SES.
- Methodological-quality assessment: COSMIN Risk of Bias categories were “very good”, “adequate”, “doubtful”, or “inadequate”, with standards adapted for the property being assessed.
- Population-specific synthesis: a property was “sufficient” when at least 75% of relevant studies were positive, “inconsistent” when 25–74% were positive, and “insufficient” when fewer than 25% were positive.
- Evidence quality: GRADE categories “high”, “moderate”, “low”, and “very low”; evidence was downgraded for methodological quality and sample-size imprecision. Inconsistency and indirectness were not used as downgrading factors.
- Statistical model: no pooled meta-analysis model or regression model was reported. The synthesis was rule-based grading of individual study results and evidence quality.
- Source: Methods, “Assessment of Individual Studies” and “Assessment of the Preference-Based Measures”; Results Tables 1–5.

## 7. Products

- Produced product: systematic evidence synthesis of PBM measurement properties by country or district and disease group.
- Product status: produced in this publication, with population-specific property ratings and quality-of-evidence ratings.
- Product recommendation: the authors identify EQ-5D as the preferred generic PBM when one is needed in Asia, while recommending continued testing and more research on rarely tested PBMs.
- Not produced: no new instrument, value set, tariff, scoring algorithm, or participant-level dataset.
- Source: Abstract; Results Tables 1–5; Discussion; Conclusions.

## 9. Principal findings and interpretation

1. The review retained 79 papers containing 1,504 individual studies. Positive results occurred in 80% of construct-validity studies, 79% of test-retest-reliability studies, and 57% of responsiveness studies. Methodological quality was “very good” or “adequate” for 99% of construct-validity studies, 61% of responsiveness studies, and 23% of test-retest-reliability studies. (Source: Abstract; Results after Table 1.)
2. EQ-5D was the most studied PBM. Its construct validity was rated “sufficient” in 6 of 10 countries or districts and 17 of 20 disease groups; responsiveness was “sufficient” in 5 of 6 countries or districts and 8 of 11 disease groups. Test-retest reliability was “sufficient” in none of 8 countries or districts and in 3 of 10 disease groups assessed. (Source: Results, EQ-5D paragraph; Table 2.)
3. EQ-VAS showed inconsistent construct validity in several populations, while its responsiveness was sufficient in all 4 countries or districts and 6 of 7 disease groups assessed. SF-6D construct validity was sufficient in 2 of 5 countries or districts and 6 of 11 disease groups. (Source: Abstract; Results, EQ-VAS and SF-6D paragraphs; Tables 3 and 4.)
4. Evidence for HUI and QWB was scarce. The available HUI evidence supported sufficient construct validity in all three countries or districts and all four disease groups assessed, with more limited evidence for reliability and responsiveness. QWB construct validity was sufficient in China and Japan and in neurological and respiratory disease groups. (Source: Results, HUI and QWB paragraphs; Table 5.)
5. The authors interpret the evidence as supporting EQ-5D as the preferred generic PBM in Asia, while recommending better test-retest and responsiveness designs and expanded study of HUI, QWB, and AQOL. (Source: Discussion; Conclusions.)

## 10. Limitations and source issues

- Some COSMIN methods and tools did not apply directly to a review covering multiple measures and populations, so the authors modified them. They caution that comparisons with reviews using the original COSMIN methods may not be meaningful.
- Non-English papers were excluded because of limited resources; the authors state that this may under-represent evidence from China, Japan, and South Korea.
- Different language versions were not differentiated, so aggregate results for Singapore and Malaysia may not apply equally to all language versions.
- The review combines EQ-5D-3L with EQ-5D-5L and HUI2 with HUI3, and does not analyse administration modes separately. These are deliberate review decisions, not source conflicts.
- The review reports paper-level and individual-study-level counts, not a pooled participant sample. No single target population, administration setting, or exact instrument version applies to all included evidence.
- No direct conflict between reported values is identified in the article. Aggregate ratings are review results and must not be treated as a result from one underlying study.
- Source: Discussion, limitations paragraphs; Methods, “Data Extraction” and “Assessment of the Preference-Based Measures”.
