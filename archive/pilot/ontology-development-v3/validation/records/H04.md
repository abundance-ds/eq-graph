# H04 — Proxy quality-of-life instrument development and item reduction

## 1. Identity and study type

- **Publication:** “From QI-disability to QID-12: creating a brief proxy-report measure of quality of life for children with intellectual disability”; DOI `10.1007/s11136-026-04223-x`.
- **Study type:** instrument or form development; measurement-property or psychometric study; proxy-report short-form/item-reduction study.
- **Source/provenance:** article file `corpus/429-RA/doi_10.1007_s11136-026-04223-x.md`; extraction date 2026-08-17; manual Markdown extraction; review status: holdout extraction. Abstract, Introduction, Methods—Sources of data, Variables, Statistical methods, and Results provide the extraction basis.

## 2. Population and samples

- **Target population:** children aged 3–18 years with intellectual disability ranging from mild to profound functional impairment; associated conditions were included (Abstract; Methods—Sources of data).
- **Recruitment/data sources:** completed QI-Disability datasets collected 2016–2023. Sources were The Kids Research Institute Australia datasets, Simons Searchlight online registry, and the Inchstone DEE Parents Speak online survey; duplicate cases were checked and excluded (Methods—Sources of data).
- **Completed analytic sample:** 1,699 unique and complete QI-Disability measures. Components were Simons Searchlight (*N* = 953), The Kids (*N* = 603), and Inchstone (*N* = 143) (Methods—Sources of data; Table 1).
- **The Kids source cohorts:** 418 participants from the initial QI-Disability validation/determinants dataset, 76 participants from the CDKL5 dataset, and 109 unique participants from the EQ-5D-Y-5L evaluation dataset. These total 603. Table 1 gives a different diagnostic grouping of the same The Kids total: autism 152, CDKL5 deficiency disorder 76, cerebral palsy 180, Down syndrome 94, Rett syndrome 68, and other 33 (Methods—Sources of data; Table 1).
- **Age:** median 9.4 years; range 3.0–18.3 years. The Simons Searchlight group was global with anonymous data; among participants with known country, most were from Oceania/Australia, followed by North America/United States (Results).
- **Respondent:** parent or primary caregiver proxy report of the child’s quality of life. Child self-report, interviewer interaction, common language, and a common administration setting were not reported (Abstract; Introduction; Methods—Variables).
- **Subgroups/conditions:** autism, CDKL5 deficiency disorder, cerebral palsy, Down syndrome, Rett syndrome, other genetic/neurodevelopmental conditions, and DEE community participants; functional abilities varied in mobility, communication, and independence (Methods—Sources of data; Results).

## 3. Research concepts

- Child quality of life; intellectual disability; caregiver-proxy measurement; conceptual breadth; respondent burden; short-form development; clinical monitoring and population research (Introduction; Discussion).
- The source QI-Disability domains are physical health, positive emotions, negative emotions, social interactions, leisure and outdoors, and independence (Abstract; Variables).

## 4. Instruments and administration

- **QI-Disability:** 32-item parent-report measure of child quality of life; five-point response scale; six domains; score scaled 0–100 with higher scores indicating better quality of life. Role: source instrument and comparator for item reduction (Abstract; Variables).
- **QID-12:** 12-item short form derived from QI-Disability. Role: developed and psychometrically evaluated; retained at least one item from each domain and no more than three from any one domain (Methods—Statistical methods; Results).
- **Administration/channel:** source datasets included an online registry and online survey plus other study datasets; a single administration mode is not reported. Respondent interaction is caregiver proxy report. Recall period and language/version details are not reported (Methods—Sources of data; Variables).
- The final item set included two Physical Health items, three Positive Emotions, one Negative Emotions, one Social Interactions, three Leisure and the Outdoors, and two Independence items (Results; Table 2).

## 5. Methods, protocol, and task design

- **Item-reduction method:** Genetic Algorithm (`GA`) implemented with R package `GAabbreviate`. The algorithm generated and evaluated short forms using a fitness function; the number of retained items was fixed at 12 (Methods—Statistical methods).
- **GA design:** run 500 times with different seeds; final items were those appearing most often in optimum solutions. Constraints required at least one item per QI-Disability domain and no more than three items per domain (Methods—Statistical methods).
- **Psychometric evaluation:** Pearson correlation with the 32-item total; Cronbach’s alpha; Rasch partial credit model; Infit/Outfit mean-square item fit; person separation reliability; Martin-Löf unidimensionality test; person-item targeting; disordered category-threshold review (Methods—Statistical methods).

## 6. Analysis and statistical models

- **Genetic Algorithm:** optimization/item-selection model used to select the 12-item product (Methods—Statistical methods).
- **Rasch partial credit model:** final QID-12 item-fit and measurement analysis. Infit and Outfit mean-square values below 1.5 were treated as satisfactory (Methods—Statistical methods; Results).
- **Classical psychometric analyses:** Pearson correlation and Cronbach’s alpha evaluated agreement with the full measure and internal consistency (Methods—Statistical methods).
- **Unidimensionality/targeting:** Martin-Löf test and person-item map evaluated dimensionality and ability/item targeting (Methods—Statistical methods; Results).
- **Software:** R packages `GAabbreviate` and `eRm`; package/version details beyond these names are not reported (Methods—Statistical methods).

## 7. Products

- **QID-12:** produced 12-item proxy-report short form of QI-Disability. Status: developed and validated within this 1,699-child dataset; intended for selected clinical and research settings where respondent burden is important (Abstract; Discussion; Conclusions).
- **QI-Disability:** retained as the full 32-item measure and is not replaced when detailed domain-level information is needed (Discussion).
- No value set, scoring algorithm beyond the source scale’s 0–100 scoring, translation, or cultural adaptation was produced.

## 8. Outcomes or measurement properties

- Correlation with QI-Disability total: Pearson’s *r* = 0.970 (Abstract; Results).
- Internal consistency: Cronbach’s alpha is reported as 0.85 in the Abstract and 0.84 in Results; both values are retained as a source conflict.
- Rasch item fit: all Infit and Outfit mean-square values below 1.5; person separation reliability 0.84 (Abstract; Results).
- Dimensionality and targeting: abstract reports no evidence of multidimensionality (*p* > 0.99); Results reports Martin-Löf *p* = 0.12. Item targeting was reasonable across the ability range, with better coverage at the lower quality-of-life end (Abstract; Results).
- Three items showed disordered category thresholds: “enjoyed feeling steady or stable during physical activities,” “made their own choices for activities or things they enjoy,” and “helped to complete routine activities” (Results).

## 9. Principal findings and interpretation

1. The GA selected QID-12, a 12-item set covering all six QI-Disability domains, from the 32-item source measure (Abstract; Methods—Statistical methods; Results).
2. QID-12 total scores correlated highly with the full QI-Disability score (*r* = 0.97), and internal consistency was acceptable (alpha 0.84 in Results; 0.85 in Abstract) (Abstract; Results).
3. Rasch analysis showed satisfactory partial-credit-model fit, person separation reliability 0.84, and reasonable targeting across the ability range (Abstract; Results).
4. Three items had reversed intermediate category thresholds, but the authors judged overall psychometric performance satisfactory; they state that QID-12 is an efficient overall quality-of-life index, not a replacement when detailed domain-level assessment is needed (Results; Discussion).
5. The authors interpret QID-12 as reducing respondent burden while retaining conceptual breadth, with possible use in clinical practice, large surveys, longitudinal studies, registries, and population research (Discussion; Conclusions).

## 10. Limitations and source issues

- The QID-12 was developed and validated in the same large dataset; the high correlation may therefore be inflated. Independent-cohort validation is required (Discussion).
- Three items had disordered category thresholds; the authors suggest this may reflect low frequency of intermediate responses but note it as a limitation (Results; Discussion).
- Only a limited set of descriptive variables was common across data sources (Discussion).
- The short form may miss detailed domain-level information; the full QI-Disability remains preferable when granular domain assessment is needed (Discussion).
- **Source conflicts preserved:** Cronbach’s alpha is 0.85 in the Abstract versus 0.84 in Results; Martin-Löf/unidimensionality is reported as *p* > 0.99 in the Abstract versus *p* = 0.12 in Results. The record does not resolve these conflicts.
- Dataset administration modes and common languages are not reported across all sources, so no common channel or language is inferred.
