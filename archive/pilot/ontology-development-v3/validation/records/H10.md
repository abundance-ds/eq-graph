# H10 — EQ-5D-5L visualization in a TKA decision aid

## 1. Identity and study type

- **Title:** *Exploring patient perspectives on EQ-5D-5L data visualization within an individualized decision aid for total knee arthroplasty (TKA) in Alberta, Canada* (article title, source title).
- **DOI:** `10.1186/s12891-024-07304-5`; project ID `445-RA`.
- **Study type:** qualitative or stakeholder study; implementation or routine-measurement study focused on visualization feedback.
- **Source verification:** article file SHA-256 `866438ce4bdc844c0cc7a3929828e48acfe2aab09c5d6b7461a5fec9219f4997`; byte count `57114` (manifest and file match).

## 2. Population and samples

- **Target population:** adults considering TKA for knee osteoarthritis (OA) and referred to an urban orthopedic clinic as possible surgery candidates (Methods).
- **Recruitment source:** a high-volume urban hip and knee clinic in Alberta, Canada, after the surgical screening appointment, from 6 April to 11 May 2023. Participants were recruited until a diverse sample by age, sex, and ethnic background was obtained (Methods).
- **Eligibility:** age ≥18; diagnosed knee OA; referred as a possible surgery candidate; able to read, speak, and understand English; and able to provide informed consent. Soft-tissue knee injury, rheumatoid arthritis, and other non-OA knee conditions were exclusion criteria (Methods).
- **Samples:** Part 1 (EQ-5D-5L pre-surgery) had 24 participants; mean age 67.7 (SD 8.8), 62.5% female, and 75% White. Part 2 (EQ-5D-5L one-year post-surgery) had 25 participants; mean age 69.0 (SD 7.9), 44% female, and 76% White (Results, Table 2). The paper does not state whether the two part samples were the same people.
- Prototypes represented a hypothetical patient with severe problems (EQ-5D-5L level 4) and were not participants’ actual EQ-5D-5L scores (Methods).

## 3. Research concepts

- Patient comprehension, usefulness, visual appeal, usability, and interpretation of EQ-5D-5L patient-reported outcomes (PROMs) within an individualized decision aid for TKA (Abstract; Introduction; Methods).
- Other concepts include patient comparison with similar people, possible outcomes and risks of surgery, expectation setting, shared decision-making, and routine clinical implementation of PROMs (Introduction; Discussion).

## 4. Instruments and administration

- **EQ-5D-5L:** exact instrument and descriptive system; historical population-level dimension-level data were integrated into the online individualized decision aid. Data comparisons were based in part on age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) (Background).
- **Instrument use:** historical or routinely collected EQ-5D-5L PROM data were visualized as decision-aid content. The prototypes used the Pain/Discomfort dimension and a hypothetical level-4 severe-problem state. Participants evaluated the presentation and did not complete the EQ-5D-5L in this study (Background; Methods).
- **Prototype administration:** paper copies were shown separately from the complete online decision aid for feasibility. A researcher administered a checklist and recorded closed-ended ratings and open-ended comments. Participants compared two versions for each part (Methods).
- **Language and interaction:** English-speaking participants; researcher-administered checklist. Prototype channel was paper in landscape orientation, although intended deployment was online. Setting was an urban orthopedic clinic after screening (Methods; Limitations).
- No EQ-5D-5L recall period, value set, scoring model, or respondent language version was reported.

## 5. Methods, protocol, and task design

- **Research methods:** visualization prototype comparison; patient feedback elicitation; closed-ended checklist; open-ended comments; and directed content analysis.
- The study had two parts: pre-surgery EQ-5D-5L data and one-year post-surgery EQ-5D-5L data. Each part had two revised visual versions. The checklist covered comprehension (9 items), usefulness (5 items), and visual appeal (4 items) (Methods).
- **Part 1 prototypes:** Version 1 used one line of gender-neutral icons, with each icon representing two people; Version 2 used a waffle chart with one icon per person. Both used descriptive labels, simplified language, linked icons/text, and clearer instructions.
- **Part 2 prototypes:** both used a Sankey Diagram to represent longitudinal change before and after surgery. Version 2 also used stacked bars and visual recall of pre-surgery status; Version 1 used narrative recall. Changes also included label placement, less curve slope, and clearer instructions (Methods, Table 1).
- The team completed three feedback rounds and used consensus to finalize prototypes. It also piloted the checklist internally and with 10 clinic patients before the study (Methods).
- The study team developed the checklist because it found no suitable validated checklist. It piloted the checklist internally and with 10 clinic patients (Methods).
- The decision aid used International Patient Decision Aid Standards and the prototypes used published data-visualization recommendations. No named EuroQol protocol was reported (Background; Methods).

## 6. Analysis and statistical models

- Closed-ended data were managed with Microsoft Excel and R version 3.6.1. Descriptive statistics used means and SDs for age and frequencies/percentages for categorical outcomes (Methods).
- Version ratings were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and McNemar’s test. Open-ended comments were managed with ATLAS.ti Version 8 and analysed using directed content analysis, coded by comprehension, usefulness, and visual appeal (Methods).
- No regression, psychometric model, or other fitted statistical model was reported. The prototype versions are visualization alternatives, not statistical-model candidates.

## 7. Products

- **Existing product:** an online individualized patient decision aid for TKA containing EQ-5D-5L comparisons based on similar people, including pre-surgery and one-year post-surgery data (Background).
- **Prototype products assessed:** two pre-surgery and two one-year post-surgery visualization versions, including icon arrays, waffle chart, Sankey Diagram, stacked bars, labels, instructions, and colour choices (Methods, Table 1; Figures 2–3).
- **Planned product:** an enhanced presentation of EQ-5D-5L data within the decision aid. Further usability testing of the complete online decision aid and routine clinical implementation were planned; they were not products completed by this study (Abstract; Conclusions).

## 8. Outcomes or measurement properties

- Primary feedback outcomes were comprehension, usefulness, and visual appeal of each visualization version (Abstract; Methods).
- Comprehension outcomes included clarity of instructions, understanding of the message, understanding comparisons with similar people, and interpretation of possible before/after outcomes. Usefulness included value for treatment decisions, risk/benefit assessment, and expectation setting. Visual appeal included ratings, readability, font/image size, and colour contrast (Results).
- The study did not evaluate EQ-5D-5L measurement properties. It evaluated presentation of EQ-5D-5L data.

## 9. Principal findings and interpretation

- Part 1 had a slight preference for Version 1 (14/24, 58.3%); Part 2 had a preference for Version 2 (16/25, 64%). No statistical differences between versions were reported for variables or categories (Abstract; Results; Table 3).
- Most participants showed adequate comprehension. For Part 1, 54.2% rated Version 1 and 50.0% Version 2 at 4 or 5 for understanding. For Part 2, the corresponding values were 68% and 72% (Results; Table 3).
- About half rated Part 1 content 4 or 5 for usefulness (50% for each version). For Part 2, 60% rated Version 1 and 64% Version 2 at 4 or 5. Some participants found the information interesting but were unsure how to use it or had already decided on treatment (Results; Table 3; Part 1/2 sections).
- Participants generally found Part 2 information about possible outcomes useful for assessing risks and benefits and setting realistic expectations. Some preferred Version 2’s complete before-and-after information, while others found it busy or distracting (Results, “Part 2…”).
- Participants rated visual appeal favourably overall, but requested larger fonts and images and greater colour contrast. The authors interpret the feedback as support for combining preferred elements, clarifying purpose and interpretation, improving readability, and continuing usability testing before implementation (Results; Discussion; Conclusions).

## 10. Limitations and source issues

- Participants viewed paper prototypes rather than the complete online decision aid. They usually reviewed Part 1 or Part 2 separately, not sequentially, which may have changed feedback (Limitations).
- Prototype presentation may have influenced visual-appeal feedback. Participants were recruited after surgical screening, when a treatment decision may already have been made, which may have reduced reported usefulness (Limitations).
- The samples were small (24 and 25) and drawn from one urban clinic. The paper does not state whether Part 1 and Part 2 participants overlap.
- Feedback on EQ-5D-5L descriptive labels was not reported because of the instrument trademark (Results). No EQ-5D-5L value set, scoring algorithm, recall period, or actual patient score analysis was reported.
- The decision aid and enhanced visualization were intended for future routine implementation. The current study reports prototype feedback, not completed implementation outcomes.
