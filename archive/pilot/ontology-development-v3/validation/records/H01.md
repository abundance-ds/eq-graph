# H01 — German Value Set for the EQ-5D-5L

## 1. Identity and study type

- **Publication:** “German Value Set for the EQ-5D-5L”; DOI `10.1007/s40273-018-0615-8`.
- **Study type:** national valuation study; value-set development.
- **Source/provenance:** article file `corpus/2012020/doi_10.1007_s40273-018-0615-8.md`; extraction date 2026-08-17; manual Markdown extraction; review status: holdout extraction. Abstract, Introduction, Methods—Study Design, and Results—Preferred Model (Value Set) provide the identity and study purpose.

## 2. Population and samples

- **Target population:** German general population aged over 18 years; the objective was societal preferences of the German population (Abstract; Methods—Study Design).
- **Recruitment source and frame:** quota-based sampling by age, sex, educational level, and employment status, using German official statistics. Recruitment used personal contact and public locations (Methods—Study Design).
- **Geography and setting:** six cities and surrounding areas—Berlin, Leipzig, Hamburg, Bielefeld, Munich, and Frankfurt. Interviews took place in public venues or at participants’ or interviewers’ homes (Methods—Study Design).
- **Completed sample:** 1,158 interviews, conducted nationwide from December 2014 to March 2015; mean interview time about 48 minutes (Results; Characteristics of the Sample).
- **Representativeness:** sample was representative for age, sex, education, and employment status; the paper reports a small middle-class bias and clustering within six regions (Results—Characteristics of the Sample; Discussion).
- **Analytic samples:** 1,158 interviews were retained for the value-set analysis, including flagged interviews; 11,580 cTTO observations were reduced by 713 respondent-flagged observations to 10,867 cTTO observations after the feedback module. No DCE data were excluded (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling; Results—Data Characteristics).
- **Subgroups:** not reported as analytic arms. Self-reported health was collected; 421 respondents (36.4%) reported state 11111 (Results—Characteristics of the Sample).

## 3. Research concepts

- Health-related quality of life; societal health-state preferences; health-state utility; preference-based measurement; national value set; quality control for valuation interviews (Introduction; Methods; Discussion).
- The instrument describes mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each at five levels; 3,125 health states are possible (Introduction).

## 4. Instruments and administration

- **EQ-5D-5L:** German version; five-dimension, five-level descriptive system. Roles: administered for self-reported health/background description and valued in the preference tasks (Introduction; Methods—Valuation Interview and Methods of Eliciting Preferences).
- **EQ VAS:** used as a self-rated health/background measure; mean score 79.45 (SD 17.05) in the sample (Results—Characteristics of the Sample).
- **Protocol/software:** EuroQol Valuation Technology, `EQ-VT Version 2.0`, including a feedback module and continuous quality-control process (Methods; Abstract).
- **Interaction/channel:** computer-assisted personal interview (CAPI), interviewer-administered. Interview language is not reported (Methods—Study Design).
- **Respondent/perspective:** adult members of the German general population; societal preference perspective. Setting was public venue or home. Recall period and repeated time point were not reported (Methods—Study Design).
- **Interview sequence:** EQ-5D-5L self-report and background questions; cTTO tasks with training, debriefing, and feedback; DCE tasks with debriefing; comment box and further background questions (Methods—Valuation Interview and Methods of Eliciting Preferences).

## 5. Methods, protocol, and task design

- **Preference-elicitation methods:** composite time trade-off (`cTTO`) and discrete choice experiment (`DCE`) without duration (Methods—Valuation Interview and Methods of Eliciting Preferences).
- **Design:** 86 EQ-5D-5L health states in ten cTTO blocks of ten states (some repeated across blocks); 196 health-state pairs in 28 DCE blocks of seven pairs. Each respondent received one cTTO block and one DCE block (Methods—Valuation Interview and Methods of Eliciting Preferences).
- **cTTO tasks:** wheelchair example, three practice states, ten real tasks, debriefing, and feedback module. Conventional TTO was used first; lead-time TTO was used for states judged worse than dead. Values ranged from −1 to 1 in 0.05 increments (Methods—Valuation Interview and Methods of Eliciting Preferences).
- **DCE tasks:** seven forced-choice health-state comparisons, plus six duration/death methodological add-on pairs. The add-on data were not reported in this article’s value-set estimation (Methods—Valuation Interview and Methods of Eliciting Preferences).
- **Quality control:** 32 professional interviewers were trained. The QC criteria covered wheelchair-example duration, lead-time explanation, cTTO ordering, and cTTO task duration; 83 interviews (7.17%) were flagged. Daily QC feedback and interviewer exclusion rules were used (Methods—Data Collection and Quality Control Process; Results).

## 6. Analysis and statistical models

- **Analysis:** descriptive sample/task analysis; main-effects modeling of the 20 EQ-5D-5L level indicators; comparison of predictions, rankings, relative importance, and value distributions across models (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling).
- **Model 1, candidate/comparator:** Tobit model for cTTO, with observations left-censored at −1; the constant was suppressed (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling; Results—Modeling).
- **Model 2, candidate/comparator:** conditional logit model for DCE choices. DCE coefficients were rescaled to the 1-to-0 utility scale using the hybrid model’s theta parameter for comparison (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling).
- **Model 3a, candidate:** hybrid model combining cTTO and DCE likelihoods (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling).
- **Model 3b, preferred/final:** hybrid model combining cTTO and DCE, censoring at −1 and allowing heteroskedastic cTTO error variance. It had the highest precision and best model fit (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling; Results—Preferred Model (Value Set)).
- **Model selection:** theoretical considerations, logical ordering of level decrements, parameter significance, value range, dimension ranking, relative importance, and prediction comparisons. A sensitivity analysis tested exclusion of single persons, non-traders, suspect DCE patterns, and QC-flagged interviews; the paper reports only marginal non-significant differences and retained all available information (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling).
- **Software:** STATA Version 14 (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling).

## 7. Products

- **German EQ-5D-5L value set:** produced national value set/tariff for the German EQ-5D-5L, derived from the combined cTTO and DCE data using preferred Model 3b. Status: final value set and recommended as the preferred value set for Germany (Abstract; Results—Preferred Model (Value Set); Conclusions).
- **Detailed artifact:** predicted values for all 3,125 EQ-5D-5L health states were supplied in Electronic Supplementary Material 3 (Results—Preferred Model (Value Set); Electronic supplementary material).
- The article does not report a new translation, crosswalk value set, or bolt-on instrument.

## 8. Outcomes or measurement properties

- Health-state utility/index value on a 1 (full health) to 0 (dead) scale, including values below zero; the final predicted range was −0.661 to 1 (Abstract; Results—Comparison of EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L Values).
- Utility decrements by EQ-5D-5L dimension and level; logical consistency, statistical significance, value range, dimension ranking, and relative importance were evaluated (Methods—Data Analysis and Modeling; Results—Modeling and Preferred Model).
- Data-quality outcomes included cTTO inconsistency, feedback-module removals, suspect DCE patterns, and QC flags (Results—Data Characteristics).

## 9. Principal findings and interpretation

1. The preferred Model 3b produced logically consistent parameter estimates, with *p* < 0.001 for all coefficients; the value set used all available cTTO and DCE information (Results—Modeling; Preferred Model (Value Set)).
2. Predicted German EQ-5D-5L utilities ranged from −0.661 to 1, covering a larger evaluation space than the German EQ-5D-3L value set and the crosswalk (Abstract; Results—Comparison of EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L Values).
3. Dimension importance was ordered pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression, self-care, mobility, and usual activities. Pain/discomfort had by far the greatest impact; all within-dimension level moves were significant except level 2 to 3 in the functional dimensions (Results—Modeling; Preferred Model (Value Set)).
4. The refined `EQ-VT 2.0` protocol, feedback module, interviewer training, and QC were judged a solid basis for national value-set estimation; the authors recommend this value set for German economic evaluation and clinical assessment (Discussion; Conclusions).

## 10. Limitations and source issues

- The sample was clustered in six regions and had a small middle-class bias, although it was predominantly representative on the reported quota variables (Discussion).
- The authors state that further research is needed to examine the practical evaluation space and discriminative properties in patient populations (Discussion).
- The article reports both a minimum target of 1,000 and a completed sample of 1,158; these are target versus completed counts, not a conflict (Methods—Study Design; Results).
- The six duration/death DCE add-on pairs were not analyzed in this article and are not treated as a separate product (Methods—Valuation Interview and Methods of Eliciting Preferences).
