# Holdout question test

## Method

- Answer each question from the ten extracted records.
- Verify the answer against the named source sections.
- Mark `pass`, `partial`, or `fail`.
- A pass means that the record makes the answer easy to find and does not distort the source.
- A partial result means that the record supports an answer but loses useful context or uses an unclear distinction.
- A failure means that an important answer is missing or wrong.

Required facts: study type, population, sample, instrument, administration, groups, outcomes, findings, and interpretation.

## Results

### T1 result — pass

H01 is the German national EQ-5D-5L valuation and value-set study.

- Population: 1,158 German adults.
- Protocol: EQ-VT 2.0.
- Valuation methods: cTTO and DCE.
- Candidate models: censored Tobit, conditional logit, and two hybrid models.
- Preferred model: hybrid model 3b, with cTTO censoring at -1 and heteroskedastic error variance.
- Product: German EQ-5D-5L value set, with a predicted range of -0.661 to 1.

The record makes the study type, methods, model roles, and product directly searchable. Source check: H01 Abstract; Methods; Results, “Preferred Model (Value Set)”; Conclusions.

### T2 result — pass

Two records use DCE for different purposes.

- H01 uses DCE as one valuation method. The final value set combines DCE with cTTO in a hybrid model. Six duration/death add-on tasks were collected but not used in this paper's value-set analysis.
- H02 uses a multi-arm DCE to test task-design effects on respondent engagement and attribute non-attendance. It compares design construction methods and attribute overlap. It produces design guidance, not a value set.

The records do not confuse DCE method use with a statistical model or product. Source check: H01 and H02 Methods and Conclusions.

### T3 result — pass

H03 evaluates the test-retest reliability of Online Elicitation of Personal Utility Functions for EQ-HWB-S.

- Participants completed the online survey twice, two weeks apart.
- Final analytic sample: 220 German adults, including general-population, diabetes, and rheumatic-disease groups.
- Method parts: dimension ranking, swing weighting, level rating, pairwise comparison, and anchoring.
- Individual responses had poor or moderate reliability. The anchoring-factor ICC was 0.12.
- Aggregate utility decrements were similar at test and retest.
- Interpretation: OPUF supported an aggregate EQ-HWB-S value set, but it did not produce stable personal utility functions at the individual level.

The record keeps method, instrument, individual product, aggregate product, and reliability level separate. Source check: H03 Abstract; Methods; Discussion; Conclusion.

### T4 result — pass

H08 evaluates Chichewa EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L among 289 healthy and sick participants aged 8–17 years in Blantyre, Malawi.

- Respondents self-completed both EQ-5D-Y versions and an age-appropriate Chichewa PedsQL form.
- The study evaluated missingness, ceiling and floor effects, redistribution, informativity, convergent validity, discriminant validity, known-group validity, and empirical validity.
- Younger children had more missing responses, especially for EQ-5D-Y-5L.
- The authors judged EQ-5D-Y-3L more suitable for ages 8–12 and EQ-5D-Y-5L more suitable for ages 13–17 in this setting.
- Test-retest reliability and responsiveness remain evidence gaps.

The record distinguishes language version, age group, scoring source, and evaluated properties. Source check: H08 Abstract; Methods; Discussion; Conclusion.

### T5 result — pass

H05 studies the 25-item EQ-HWB proxy version 2 in three residential aged-care facilities in Melbourne.

- Participants: nine family proxies and twenty staff proxies.
- Perspective: proxy-person, with observed drift toward proxy-proxy judgment.
- Method: cognitive think-aloud and semi-structured interviews, followed by thematic analysis.
- Findings: physical and observable items were easier than psychological items. Proxies reported ambiguity, double-barrelled items, unsuitable examples, repetition, recall-period problems, and difficulty maintaining the stated perspective.
- Interpretation: self-report should remain the default, but proxy reporting is sometimes necessary.

The record shows why proxy form, proxy type, and proxy perspective must be independent fields. Source check: H05 Methods; Results themes 1–2; Conclusions.

### T6 result — pass

H04 develops QID-12, a 12-item caregiver-proxy short form derived from the 32-item QI-Disability.

- Population: 1,699 children aged 3–18 years with intellectual disability, assembled from several datasets.
- Development method: a Genetic Algorithm run 500 times with domain constraints.
- Validation: Pearson correlation, Cronbach's alpha, Rasch partial credit model, item-fit statistics, person separation reliability, and the Martin-Lof test.
- Product: QID-12, validated in the development dataset and intended for settings where respondent burden is important.
- Caution: independent validation and responsiveness testing remain necessary.

The record keeps the source instrument, development method, statistical model, new product, and its status distinct. Source check: H04 Abstract; Methods; Results; Discussion.

### T7 result — pass

H06 is a cross-sectional population-health study of 6,574 complete EQ-5D-Y-3L respondents aged 13–18 years in Sweden.

- Girls reported more problems than boys in usual activities, pain/discomfort, and mood and had lower EQ VAS scores.
- Adolescents with one or both parents unemployed reported poorer health on several dimensions.
- Disease, functional impairment, and mental distress were associated with more problems and lower EQ VAS scores.
- The authors interpret the instrument as useful for population-health assessment and priority setting.

The record preserves the 7,399 survey respondents, 6,805 screened respondents, and 6,574 complete EQ-5D-Y-3L cases as different sample stages. Source check: H06 Abstract; Results; Discussion; Conclusions.

### T8 result — partial

H07 reviews construct validity, test-retest reliability, and responsiveness for generic preference-based measures in East and South-East Asia.

- Search: four databases through August 2019, guided by COSMIN.
- Evidence: 79 publications and 1,504 source-defined assessment units.
- Instruments: EQ-5D, EQ VAS, SF-6D, HUI, and QWB; the search also covered 15D and AQOL.
- Main result: EQ-5D had the broadest evidence and was the preferred generic measure, but test-retest evidence was weak or inconsistent in many populations.
- Gaps: better reliability and responsiveness designs, non-English evidence, and more study of less-used measures.

The record answers the question. The ontology needs one small addition: a `Review evidence unit` with a source-defined unit type. This prevents 79 publications and 1,504 hypothesis, ICC, or SES assessments from being called the same kind of study. Source check: H07 Methods; Results; Discussion; Conclusion.

### T9 result — funding-boundary pass

H09 is a population-based cost-of-illness and economic-burden study. It reports no EQ instrument. Its Funding section names other funders, and EuroQol grants occur only in one author's competing-interest statement. The project link is not verified.

- Population: adults aged 40 years and above in Trinidad and Tobago.
- Data: National Eye Survey, a health-system survey, population prevalence, utilization, and unit costs.
- Perspective: societal.
- Costs: direct medical, direct non-medical, indirect, and intangible loss of wellbeing.
- Main result: 2014 societal cost was TT$3.84 billion; loss of wellbeing was 73.3%.
- The paper also reports population extrapolation, DALYs, human-capital costing, informal-care opportunity cost, and sensitivity analyses.

The scientific extraction is accurate. It shows the fields that an economic profile could use: perspective, cost year, currency, cost category, costing method, burden measure, and population extrapolation. Do not add that profile from H09 alone because this paper is not yet an accepted EuroQol output. The correct system behavior is to keep the project link in review and exclude it from funded-output counts. Source check: H09 Abstract; Methods; Results; Discussion; Conclusion; Funding; Competing interests.

### T10 result — pass

H10 uses historical EQ-5D-5L outcomes as content in a knee-replacement decision aid. It does not evaluate the instrument's measurement properties.

- Participants reviewed paper prototypes in an orthopedic clinic.
- Products included icon arrays, a waffle chart, a Sankey diagram, stacked bars, and explanatory text.
- Methods included a researcher-administered checklist, descriptive statistics, Wilcoxon and McNemar tests, and directed content analysis.
- Outcomes were comprehension, usefulness, and visual appeal.
- An improved online decision aid and routine implementation were planned, not completed.

The record separates instrument use, prototype product, feedback outcome, and implementation status. Source check: H10 Abstract; Methods; Results; Discussion; Conclusions.

### T11 result — pass

The records preserve the corner-piece distinctions.

- H01: instrument `EQ-5D-5L`; methods `cTTO` and `DCE`; final model `hybrid model 3b`; product `German EQ-5D-5L value set`; principal findings about range and dimension importance.
- H02: instrument `EQ-5D-5L`; method `DCE`; task-design variants; model `equality constrained latent class model`; product `DCE design guidance`.
- H04: source instrument `QI-Disability`; development method `Genetic Algorithm`; model `Rasch partial credit model`; product `QID-12`.
- H10: instrument use is visualization content; prototype charts are products; comprehension and usefulness are outcomes.

No generic `component` is needed. The word `method` is not used as a substitute for an exact method value.

### T12 result — pass

The records support a research-gap search with source context.

- H01: test discrimination and practical evaluation space in patient populations.
- H02: separate genuine indifference from inattention and study dimension-order effects.
- H03: improve individual OPUF reliability and test observed or interviewer-assisted administration.
- H04: validate QID-12 in independent cohorts and test responsiveness.
- H05: compare proxy perspectives, proxy types, and self-report.
- H06: develop and apply suitable youth value sets and extend population comparisons.
- H07: improve test-retest and responsiveness study design and include non-English evidence.
- H08: test reliability and responsiveness of the Chichewa youth versions.
- H09: standardize cost-of-vision-loss methods and fill omitted cost and service data.
- H10: test the complete online decision aid before routine implementation.

Each gap remains linked to its population, instrument, method, or product. It is not stored as an unsupported generic gap label.
