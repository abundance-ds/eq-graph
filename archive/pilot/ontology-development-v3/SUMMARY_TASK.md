# Paper-summary task

Read the assigned paper. Write one dense Markdown summary of approximately one or two pages.

The summary is evidence for later ontology design. Do not propose an ontology. Do not normalize the paper into a predefined schema.

Preserve the paper's exact terms, abbreviations, capitalization, version labels, and study labels. Do not add synonyms, canonical labels, or ontology classes. If a paraphrase is necessary, retain the paper wording in parentheses.

Capture the important facts that a EuroQol researcher could search, filter, compare, or synthesize. Use exact terms from the paper when possible. Include:

- publication identity and publication type;
- research aim and study type;
- design, setting, country, and study period;
- population, eligibility, sample source, sample size, and relevant subgroups;
- each instrument, exact version, language, form, role, perspective, and administration mode;
- each elicitation method, task, protocol, and experimental variation;
- each analysis, statistical method, model, model-selection method, and its purpose;
- outcomes, constructs, score or value derivation, and comparison context;
- products such as a value set, mapping function, translation, protocol, instrument version, or recommendation;
- principal findings, direction, interpretation, and selected aggregate values when they are important;
- stated implications, uses, limitations, gaps, and conflicts in the source;
- source-reported labels and keywords, with their wording preserved. Do not invent labels.

This list is a reading checklist, not an output schema. Organize the summary in the clearest form for the paper. Prefer compact bullets. Do not omit an important fact because it does not fit a heading.

Do not include participant-level data. Do not copy large tables. Do not infer an unreported fact. For every high-value fact, provide a short source pointer when available, such as a page, section, table, or figure. Mark absent information `not reported`, ambiguity `unclear`, and unsupported relationships `not stated`.

End with `High-value query terms`: verbatim terms and phrases found in the source. Do not add synonyms or ontology labels.
