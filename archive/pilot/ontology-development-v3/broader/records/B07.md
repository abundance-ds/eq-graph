# B07: recalled compared with direct HRQoL after trauma

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1007/s11136-019-02256-z`.
- Purposes: recall-method research; measurement correspondence; longitudinal
  clinical outcomes.
- Design: prospective longitudinal cohort with repeated direct and recalled
  assessments.
- EuroQol support: study funding, project `2016160`.

## Population and samples

- Adult trauma patients admitted through emergency departments at ten hospitals
  in Noord-Brabant, the Netherlands, March to November 2016.
- Excluded people unable to answer Dutch questionnaires and people who did not
  survive discharge.
- Complete-case analytic sample: 446 participants at one week, three months,
  and 12 months after trauma.

## Concepts and themes

- recall bias; response shift; retrospective measurement; longitudinal
  recovery; trauma severity; patient memory; QALY measurement error.

## Instruments and administration

- Dutch EQ-5D-3L and EQ VAS, scored with the Dutch value set.
- Postal, self-completed surveys at one week, three months, and 12 months.
- Current health was reported at every time point. At later assessments,
  participants also recalled one-week or three-month health.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Direct and recalled summary scores, dimensions, and EQ VAS were compared
  across three-, nine-, and 12-month recall intervals.
- Prespecified subgroups covered age, sex, education, comorbidity, and Injury
  Severity Score. Major trauma was ISS 16 or higher.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Paired t-test; intraclass correlation coefficient; Bland-Altman plots;
  chi-square and Mann-Whitney U tests for nonresponse analysis.
- ICC categories: poor below 0.40, fair 0.40 to 0.59, good 0.60 to 0.74, and
  excellent 0.75 or higher.

## Products

- Evidence and guidance for retrospective EQ-5D use; no new instrument.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Direct-recall mean difference; individual agreement; recall correspondence;
  dimension-specific distortion; subgroup correspondence.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Recalled EQ-5D-3L and EQ VAS values were systematically lower than direct
  reports.
- Direct one-week utility was 0.482; recalled values were 0.453 at three months
  and 0.363 at 12 months. Direct three-month utility was 0.737; its 12-month
  recall was 0.713.
- All summary-score and EQ VAS correspondence was fair, with ICC 0.518 to
  0.598.
- Anxiety/depression and pain/discomfort had the most recall distortion.
  Mobility had the best dimension agreement.
- Longer intervals and recall of the acute phase generally reduced agreement.
  Major trauma and middle education had low correspondence.
- The authors conclude that recalled HRQoL cannot replace prospective
  measurement and can bias cost-effectiveness results.

## Limitations and source issues

- Fewer than 10% of invited patients supplied complete data, with possible
  selection and participation bias.
- Results might not represent the wider Dutch trauma population.
- The study used EQ-5D-3L; recall behavior can differ with EQ-5D-5L.
