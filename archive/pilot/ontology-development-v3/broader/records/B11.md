# B11: PROM-enhanced hospital readmission prediction

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1097/mlr.0000000000002315`.
- Purposes: clinical prediction; secondary use of routine PROM data;
  implementation evidence.
- Design: population-based retrospective cohort with linked survey and
  administrative data.
- JATS funding metadata links EuroQol project `1787-RA` to Nick Bansback. The
  Markdown article shows only a training acknowledgement.

## Population and samples

- Adults discharged from acute care in British Columbia, Canada, in 2016/17.
- Survey source: 78 acute hospitals and two rehabilitation hospitals; 47%
  response rate.
- Eligible survey respondents: 16,318. Analytic sample: 11,177; complete data
  for all predictors: 6,253.
- PROM responses used in prediction were available 23 to 60 days after
  discharge.

## Concepts and themes

- routine PROM reuse; readmission risk; clinical prediction; postdischarge
  care; patient-centered data; data linkage; value-based care; clinical
  decision support.

## Instruments and administration

- EQ-5D-5L dimensions and EQ VAS; Veterans RAND 12-Item Health Survey.
- Patient self-completion online or on paper after discharge.
- Instruments supplied predictors, not study endpoints.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Linked Acute Inpatient Survey data to provincial hospital, emergency, and
  demographic databases.
- Prediction horizons: 30, 180, and 360 days after weekly landmarks from day 23
  to day 60.
- Compared administrative-only, EQ-5D-5L-enhanced, and VR-12-enhanced models.
- Included an ambulatory-care-sensitive-condition subgroup and several
  sensitivity analyses.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Aggregated Cox landmark supermodel with landmark interactions.
- Multiple imputation by chained equations; five-fold patient-level cross-
  validation; 1,000 bootstrap replications; Rubin pooling.
- Performance: C-index, time-dependent AUC, calibration slope, Brier score, and
  Wald chi-square variable importance.
- Sensitivity models included time-dependent Cox and logistic regression.

## Products

- Validated-internal prediction models for research use. No external validation
  or implemented clinical tool is reported.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- First unplanned emergency visit or acute readmission; discrimination;
  calibration; predictive accuracy; incremental PROM value.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Observed readmission was 5.6% by 30 days, 18.4% by 180 days, and 25.0% by 360
  days.
- At 180 days, C-index increased from 0.762 for administrative data to 0.774
  with EQ-5D-5L and 0.782 with VR-12.
- PROMs gave similar modest gains at other horizons. In the ACSC subgroup,
  VR-12 improved discrimination by 2.4% to 3.0%; EQ-5D-5L did not give a
  significant subgroup gain.
- EQ-5D-5L mobility and EQ VAS ranked among important predictors.
- The authors propose reuse where PROM systems already exist, but require
  external validation and impact and economic evaluation before deployment.

## Limitations and source issues

- Survey respondents can differ in health literacy and socioeconomic status.
- Postdischarge PROM timing misses early readmission and cannot support the
  discharge decision itself.
- PROMs were measured once and carried forward.
- No external validation or downstream clinical-impact test was done.
- The main article Markdown and JATS funding metadata differ in funding detail.
