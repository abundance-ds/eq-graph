# B13: uncertainty in health-state values

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1177/0272989X251380556`.
- Purposes: methodological framework; reporting guidance; economic-model input
  analysis.
- Design: conceptual and methodological synthesis, not an empirical review or
  valuation study.
- EuroQol support: study funding reported in Acknowledgments, project `399-RA`.

## Population and samples

- No participant sample.
- Intended users: valuation researchers, economic modelers, clinical-trial
  researchers, guideline developers, and decision makers.

## Concepts and themes

- uncertainty quantification; health-state values; QALY; cost-effectiveness;
  evidence lineage; inherited uncertainty; sensitivity analysis; reporting
  standards.

## Instruments and administration

- Discusses EQ-5D-3L, EQ-5D-5L, SF-6D, HUI, other health-state descriptive
  systems, mappings, and disease states.
- No instrument was administered.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Defines four uncertainty types: variability, heterogeneity, statistical
  uncertainty, and methodological variation.
- Traces uncertainty from valuation, profile data, mapping, disease-state
  studies, and meta-analysis into cost-effectiveness models.
- Gives a worked EQ-5D-3L example for deriving state-value standard errors from
  a model variance-covariance matrix.

## Analysis and statistical models

- No new statistical model was fitted for the main article.
- Discussed methods include regression variance-covariance propagation,
  deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analysis, mapping, and
  meta-analysis.

## Products

- Conceptual taxonomy and evidence-flow model for health-state-value
  uncertainty.
- Reporting recommendations for value-set producers and users.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Types, sources, accumulation, reporting, and decision effect of uncertainty.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Health-state values are variables, but valuation publications usually report
  point estimates without the uncertainty needed by cost-effectiveness models.
- Uncertainty accumulates when values pass through valuation, mapping,
  meta-analysis, and economic modeling.
- Coefficient standard errors do not directly give state-value standard errors;
  a variance-covariance matrix can support their calculation.
- The worked EQ-5D-3L example produced state-value SEs from 0.008171 to
  0.012203.
- The authors recommend state-value SEs or a model variance-covariance matrix,
  wider sensitivity analysis, and explicit uncertainty guidance.

## Limitations and source issues

- The paper gives a conceptual account, not an empirical estimate of the
  relative size of each uncertainty source.
- Some methodological variation cannot be removed by statistical methods.
- Several recommendations depend on reporting data that current value-set
  publications do not supply.
