# B01: peripheral nerve block use in joint arthroplasty

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1213/ANE.0000000000007107`.
- Purpose: determinants of health-care use; disparities; comparative clinical
  outcomes.
- Design: retrospective cohort study of administrative claims.
- EuroQol support: travel grant, project `1483-TVG`. The paper reports no EQ
  instrument.

## Population and samples

- Population: US Medicare beneficiaries who had primary total hip arthroplasty
  (THA) or total knee arthroplasty (TKA), 2013 to September 2021.
- Source: 5% Medicare Limited Dataset.
- Initial procedures: 241,326. Final complete-case sample: 147,721.
- Analyses: 52,926 THA cases and 94,795 TKA cases.
- The sample mostly represents people aged 66 years or older and people with
  disabilities who have Medicare coverage.

## Concepts and themes

- health-care access and use; practice variation; socioeconomic inequality;
  regional variation; standardization of care; administrative claims.

## Instruments and administration

- No EQ or other PROM was administered.
- Claims, hospital, and area-level data supplied the study variables.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Identified peripheral nerve blocks (PNBs), procedures, diagnoses,
  complications, and readmissions from CPT and ICD codes.
- Grouped determinants as demographic, clinical, socioeconomic, and hospital
  variables.
- Used confounder-adjusted population-attributable risk (PAR) to estimate the
  variation in PNB use associated with each determinant group.
- Analyzed THA and TKA separately. Used complete cases and two sensitivity
  analyses.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Mixed-effects logistic regression with a hospital random intercept and fixed
  patient and hospital effects.
- Secondary mixed-effects logistic models for complications, 90-day all-cause
  readmission, and length of stay over three days.
- Model discrimination: c-statistic 0.94 for THA PNB use and 0.86 for TKA PNB
  use.

## Products

- No reusable EuroQol product.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Primary: receipt of any PNB around the day of surgery.
- Secondary: CMS-defined complications, 90-day all-cause readmission, and
  hospital stay longer than three days.
- Analytic outcome: determinant-specific and group-specific PAR.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Clinical factors explained 19% to 46% of THA variation and 32% to 34% of TKA
  variation. Hospital factors explained 31% to 64% and 22% to 25%,
  respectively.
- Socioeconomic factors explained only 2% to 3% of THA variation and 3% of TKA
  variation.
- Non-osteoarthritis indications predicted less PNB use. In THA, outpatient
  care and prior hospitalizations also predicted less use.
- In TKA, PNB use was associated with fewer complications and less prolonged
  stay, but not with readmission. THA outcome associations were not
  statistically significant.
- The authors interpret provider and hospital practice differences as the main
  source of variation and recommend more standardized PNB provision.

## Limitations and source issues

- The observational design cannot establish causation, and residual
  confounding can remain.
- Results do not necessarily generalize beyond Medicare.
- Area deprivation can differ from individual deprivation.
- PAR is a theoretical maximum reduction, not an expected practice effect.
