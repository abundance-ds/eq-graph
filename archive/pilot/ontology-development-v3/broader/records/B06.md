# B06: video compared with face-to-face cTTO

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1007/s40271-022-00573-z`.
- Purposes: valuation-method research; administration-mode comparison;
  interviewer and data-quality research.
- Design: nonrandomized comparison inside two national valuation studies.
- EuroQol support: study funding, projects `20180510` and `124-2020VS`.

## Population and samples

- General adult population respondents in Spain and Belgium.
- Spain analytic sample: 184 interviews, including 123 face-to-face and 61 by
  Skype.
- Belgium: 218 interviews, including 120 face-to-face and 98 by Zoom.
- All valuations used the perspective of a hypothetical ten-year-old child.

## Concepts and themes

- remote valuation; digital research; interviewer effects; respondent
  engagement; data quality; states worse than dead; pandemic adaptation;
  selection by digital access.

## Instruments and administration

- EQ-5D-Y-3L health states were valued. The instrument was not used to measure
  respondents' own health.
- Method: interviewer-administered cTTO.
- Channels: in-person face-to-face, Skype video, and Zoom video with screen
  sharing. Interviewers operated the software; respondents answered verbally.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- International EQ-5D-Y-3L valuation protocol, cTTO component.
- Ten health states per respondent: three mild, two moderate, and five severe.
- Better-than-dead and lead-time worse-than-dead tasks; feedback module.
- Weekly EuroQol quality control and trained interviewers.
- To reduce learning effects, the comparison separated each interviewer's first
  20 face-to-face interviews from later face-to-face interviews.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Descriptive distributions and engagement metrics.
- Z-tests for proportions; unequal-variance unpaired t-tests for duration and
  move counts; Bonferroni-adjusted and unadjusted results.
- No utility model or value set was estimated in this paper.

## Products

- Feasibility and data-quality evidence for video-administered cTTO; no new
  value set.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Interviewer and respondent engagement; task and interview duration; number
  of iterative moves; exact and half-year values; negative values;
  inconsistencies; interviewer effects; face validity of valuation data.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- Interviewer engagement did not materially decline by video.
- Respondent task duration and exact or half-year response patterns showed no
  consistent adverse video effect in either country.
- Video interviews took longer overall; the difference was significant only in
  Spain.
- Inconsistency differences between modes were not statistically significant.
- The authors found no evidence that video reduced cTTO data quality. They call
  for randomized mode-comparison studies.

## Limitations and source issues

- Mode was not randomized and video data came later.
- Learning, the pandemic, demographic imbalance, and digital access can
  confound the comparison.
- Separating early interviews reduced statistical power.
- Small interviewer samples prevent strong interviewer-level inference.

## High-value exact terms

- cTTO; EQ-5D-Y-3L; videoconferencing; face-to-face interview; Skype; Zoom;
  ten-year-old child perspective; interviewer engagement; feedback module;
  worse than dead; Bonferroni correction.

## Extraction fit

- Method, channel, interaction, perspective, and instrument role must stay
  separate. `Video` is not a valuation method.
