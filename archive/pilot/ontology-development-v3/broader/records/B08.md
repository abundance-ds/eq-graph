# B08: routine paediatric PROM implementation

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.1007/s11136-025-03990-3`.
- Purposes: implementation research; content and acceptability evaluation;
  co-design input.
- Design: qualitative semi-structured interview study, phase 1 of P-PROM ROCK.
- EuroQol support: study funding, grant `330-PhD`.

## Population and samples

- Setting: paediatric outpatient care at the Royal Children's Hospital,
  Melbourne, Australia.
- Participants: six service providers, five caregivers, and three adolescents.
- Eligible adolescents were 12 to 18 years; caregivers represented children
  aged 2 to 18 years.
- Purposive selection favored relevant experience and clinics planned for later
  implementation work.

## Concepts and themes

- routine PROM implementation; child and adolescent health; patient-centered
  care; clinical workflow; digital integration; clinician response; co-design;
  acceptability; negative framing.

## Instruments and administration

- EQ-5D-Y-5L and EQ VAS were shown and discussed, not used as participant
  outcome measures.
- Individual 30-minute video interviews; English; adolescent, caregiver, and
  clinician perspectives.
- Possible future modes, scoring, display, and workflow were research topics.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- COREQ-reported semi-structured interviews.
- Public-service co-design framework informed the larger project.
- Interviews were recorded, transcribed, de-identified, and checked.
- Themes were mapped after coding to the Theoretical Framework of Acceptability.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Inductive and deductive qualitative framework analysis in NVivo 12.
- Two researchers double-coded nine interviews over three rounds. One
  researcher applied the final framework to all records.
- No statistical model.

## Products

- Barriers and enablers for P-PROM ROCK phase 2 co-design.
- No implemented workflow or validated clinical display was produced in this
  phase.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Stakeholder acceptability; perceived usefulness; workflow requirements;
  display preference; perceived instrument relevance; barriers and enablers.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- All groups were open to generic P-PROM use, but they expected clinicians to
  use and respond to the information.
- Potential benefits included communication, problem detection, shared
  decisions, longitudinal tracking, and more holistic care.
- Main enablers were completion before the visit, clear purpose, trusted-team
  introduction, existing IT integration, training, and family access to results.
- Most participants preferred item-level and longitudinal display to one
  preference-weighted score.
- Concerns included the short `today` recall period, negative framing,
  ambiguous levels, missing context, clinician capacity, and problems outside
  specialist scope.
- The authors recommend co-design and prototype testing before routine use.

## Limitations and source issues

- Only three adolescents participated; one parent dominated one adolescent
  interview.
- Younger children, fathers, and non-English speakers were absent.
- Clinic prioritization limits transfer to other services.
- Participants evaluated EQ-5D-Y-5L rather than choosing among instruments.
