# B05: responsiveness after breast-cancer surgery

## Identity and study type

- DOI: `10.3390/cancers16111952`.
- Purposes: measurement-property evaluation; longitudinal clinical outcomes.
- Design: single-center longitudinal cohort using routine PROM data.
- The article does not report EuroQol funding. Any project link needs separate
  authoritative portfolio evidence.

## Population and samples

- Women with invasive breast cancer who had curative surgery at Erasmus MC
  Cancer Institute, the Netherlands, from November 2015 to January 2022.
- Complete-case analytic sample: 333 women with all three PROM assessments.
- Median age: 54 years. Most had early-stage disease; 41.1% received
  chemotherapy.

## Concepts and themes

- breast cancer; patient-reported outcomes; routine outcome measurement;
  longitudinal recovery; clinically important change; responsiveness.

## Instruments and administration

- Dutch EQ-5D-5L, including EQ VAS; index scored with the Dutch value set.
- EORTC QLQ-C30 version 3.0.
- Administration: Erasmus MC online PROM collection platform.
- Time points: preoperative baseline, six months, and 12 months after surgery.

## Methods, protocol, and task design

- Compared within-person change from baseline to each follow-up.
- Compared mean changes with an EQ-5D-5L MCID of 0.08 and an EORTC QLQ-C30
  MCID of 5.
- Prespecified subgroup: people who received chemotherapy.

## Analysis and statistical models

- Paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test after normality testing.
- Effect size: mean change divided by baseline standard deviation.
- Standardized response mean: mean change divided by change-score standard
  deviation.
- No regression model.

## Products

- Responsiveness evidence to guide PROM interpretation and study planning; no
  new instrument or value set.

## Outcomes or measurement properties

- Internal responsiveness; mean change; MCID comparison; effect size; SRM;
  health status; global HRQoL; functional and symptom scales.

## Principal findings and interpretation

- EQ-5D-5L index fell by 0.05 at six months and 0.03 at 12 months. Both changes
  were below the selected 0.08 MCID.
- EORTC global-HRQoL changes were also below its selected MCID.
- All EQ-5D-5L and EORTC effect sizes and SRMs were below 0.5 in the full
  sample.
- Responsiveness was greater at six than at 12 months. EQ-5D-5L index
  responsiveness exceeded the EORTC global and summary scores.
- Chemotherapy patients showed greater early responsiveness for some outcomes.
- The authors recommend careful interpretation, other assessment times, and
  possible disease-specific PROMs.

## Limitations and source issues

- Only internal responsiveness was assessed; no external reference standard
  was available.
- Small true change can make a responsive instrument appear unresponsive.
- The selected MCID thresholds are uncertain and affect the conclusion.
- Complete cases can differ from people with missing follow-up assessments.
- The subgroup analysis covered chemotherapy only.
