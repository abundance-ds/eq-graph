BackgroundPopulation health norms derived from EQ-5D instruments support health technology assessments, burden-of-disease studies, and economic evaluations. Random probability sampling remains the gold standard for generating representative population estimates, but rising costs and declining response rates have driven increasing use of online panel quota sampling. The EQ-DAPHNIE initiative uses online panel methods across multiple countries. Whether EQ-DAPHNIE samples are as representative as probability-based surveys is not yet established. A 2025 study of 53,000 randomly sampled adult individuals in the Norwegian population, recruited through the national health app, offers a unique opportunity for comparison.ObjectiveTo compare the representativeness and data quality of two Norwegian population health surveys using different recruitment strategies: (1) a large-scale random probability sample (N=53,000) recruited via national registries through Helsenorge, Norway's national digital health portal; and (2) a newly collected Norwegian EQ-DAPHNIE dataset using online panel quota sampling.MethodsCross-sectional comparison of survey performance metrics (response rates, completion rates, missingness, completion time, speeding), EQ-5D-5L outcomes (dimension scores, EQ VAS, index values, Level Sum Score), and chronic condition prevalence. Regression models will quantify unadjusted differences between samples. Sequential adjustment for sociodemographic and socioeconomic covariates (age, sex, education, income) will assess whether differences persist after controlling for composition. Post-stratification weighting, propensity score weighting, and calibration weighting will be explored as harmonization approaches.Expected outcomesThe study will produce methodological evidence on how sampling strategy affects data quality and population health estimates in a Norwegian context. Findings will directly inform the EuroQol Population Health Systems Working Group on sample representativeness, adjustment methods, and guidance for future EQ-5D population surveys and ongoing EQ-DAPHNIE data collection. Moreover, the Norwegian EQ-DAPHNIE data will be a valuable addition to the EQ-DAPHNIE data archive, providing a useful resource for population health research, instrument comparison and cross-country comparisons.
