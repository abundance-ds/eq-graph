Accurate measurement of health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in older adults is essential for fair and valid economic evaluation, clinical assessment, and population health monitoring. The EQ-5D-5L is widely used for these purposes, yet evidence on its validity and performance among the oldest age groups remains limited. While existing studies suggest that the EQ-5D-5L performs adequately overall, it is unclear whether its measurement properties differ across subgroups of older age or whether older respondents can reliably distinguish between all five severity levels. Moreover, aspects of life that gain importance in later years—such as autonomy, social participation, and cognition—are not directly represented within the EQ-5D descriptive system, raising questions about the need for age-relevant “bolt-on” dimensions.This project will examine the validity and psychometric performance of the EQ-5D-5L among older adults, with particular attention to differences between the “old” and “very old.” Using the UK SIPHER-HWMIC dataset (n≈12,000; ages 18–97), analyses will compare the performance of the EQ-5D-5L across the adult age range. These analyses will assess predictive validity for EQ-VAS and life satisfaction, evaluate ceiling and floor effects, convergent and construct validity, and explore age-related differences in the latent factor structure of health and wellbeing. The study will also test whether potential bolt-on domains (e.g., vision, hearing, cognition, fatigue) improve predictive performance for older adults, and whether such improvements differ from those observed in younger age groups.Findings will provide new empirical evidence on how the EQ-5D-5L functions across later life stages, informing EuroQol’s work on descriptive system validity and the potential development of age-relevant extensions.
