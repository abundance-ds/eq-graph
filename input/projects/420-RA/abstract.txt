Study aims: This study aimed to examine the long-term impact of COVID-19, with a specific focus on symptoms and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) up to two years post-infection. Using pooled data from seven cohorts, including the POPCORN cohort, two key objectives were addressed: (1) to describe the prevalence and nature of persistent complaints following COVID-19 and their relationship to HRQoL, and (2) to develop a prediction model to estimate an individual’s risk of experiencing long-term complaints using routinely available clinical and demographic data. Additional data collection: Participants in the POPCORN study completed three survey waves (2020–2022). As part of the CORFU project, a follow-up survey (POPCORN-CORFU) was administered in mid-2022 to participants in the Netherlands and the UK who had indicated prior COVID-19 infection. A total of 363 respondents completed the POPCORN-CORFU survey. Data from POPCORN and six additional cohorts were pooled and stratified by COVID-19 severity. Results: Results showed that over 50% of formerly hospitalized patients and 40% of non-hospitalized patients met criteria for post-COVID condition (PCC) at 24 months. The most prevalent symptoms included fatigue, sleep problems, muscle weakness, and muscle pain. PCC was significantly associated with reduced HRQoL across all severity groups, as measured by the EQ-5D-5L. A prediction model developed using logistic regression identified sex, BMI, initial disease severity, and presence of chronic conditions as significant predictors of PCC at two years. The model demonstrated moderate discriminatory performance and was internally validated using bootstrapping.
