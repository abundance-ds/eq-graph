This study examined whether people involved in health valuation research would allow their own children to participate in health state valuation tasks. An online survey of 166 respondents, including EuroQol members and non-members, asked about consent for children aged 8–17 years to complete discrete choice experiment (DCE) and time trade-off (TTO) tasks, alongside questions on professional experience and views about child involvement in valuation. Willingness to consent increased with child age and was consistently higher for DCE than TTO. For DCE, consent rose from 53.4% among children aged 8–10 years to 95.3% among those aged 16–17 years; for TTO, it increased from 29.9% to 88.0%. Most respondents preferred valuation studies to include both adults and children rather than children alone. EuroQol members were more likely to support taxpayer-based arguments in valuation. Having a child in the relevant age range was strongly associated with willingness to consent to DCE participation, whereas being a parent alone was not. Respondents generally supported involving children in decisions about their health and quality of life, but those who viewed valuation tasks as ethically inappropriate for children or supported taxpayer arguments were less likely to consent. Overall, support for involving children in valuation research was substantial but depended on child age, valuation method, and respondents’ experience and views. The findings suggest that child health valuation studies should use age-appropriate, method-sensitive approaches and focus particularly on older adolescents, while also indicating that concerns about involving children may be less pronounced than often assumed.
