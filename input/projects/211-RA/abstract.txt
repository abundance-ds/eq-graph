As the Discrete Choice Experiment (DCE) heavily relies on respondents to imagine hypothetical health states with five Health-related Quality of Life (HRQoL) dimensions combined, the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) may serve as an alternative which allows participants to compare dimensions in pairs, and in turn potentially imposing lower cognitive burden on the sample population. The project intended to conduct a head-on methodological comparison between the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and Discrete Choice Experiment (DCE) in EQ-5D-5L health state preference elicitations. To address its primary objective, a cross-sectional survey (n=504) was conducted in 2022-2023 with referenced to the local gender and age group composition of the Hong Kong SAR, China community. Participants were invited to finish 15 AHP tasks and 14 DCE tasks, which the DCE design was referenced to the EQ-VT protocol. The sample population reflected that the AHP method was easier to understand (AHP: 51.8%; DCE 36.8%), and was a more preferred health state valuation method. (AHP: 51.4%; DCE: 41.3%) In terms of predicting health state preferences, both DCE (90.00% - 93.88%) and AHP (85.19% - 94.76%) methods demonstrated high predictive ability in the settings under study. Findings uncovered in this project may hint that AHP could be of an useful alternative in health state preference elicitations. With the potential feasibility of generating Personal Utility Function (PUF) with AHP, further research could be conducted to explore its full potential such as settings with limited resources and biased samples.
