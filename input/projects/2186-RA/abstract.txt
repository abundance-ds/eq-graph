This study will provide the first systematic comparison of the EQ Health and Wellbeing (EQ-HWB) with widely used subjective wellbeing (SWB) instruments from happiness research, including single-item measures of happiness and life satisfaction, and validated affect scales. We will examine the extent to which these measures are linearly or non-linearly related, how relationships differ across population subgroups (e.g., health status, personality traits, cultural contexts), and how both respond to major life events. By integrating perspectives from health economics and happiness economics, the study will directly address EuroQol’s strategic goal to advance the EQ-HWB from experimental to approved status through robust conceptual and empirical comparison with other quality-of-life measures. Findings will be translated into practical EQ-HWB user guidelines, enabling researchers and policymakers to better interpret and apply EQ-HWB results in diverse settings. This work will enhance the comparability of quality-of-life evidence across sectors, improving the ability to evaluate interventions that span health, social care, and broader wellbeing domains.
