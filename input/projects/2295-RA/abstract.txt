Globally, older people (aged 65 and over) are the fastest growing age cohort, with 830 million older people, currently representing 10% of the world’s population expected to double to approximately 1.5 billion, representing 17% of the world’s population by 2050. Older people are heavy users of health and social care services, already accounting for nearly half of total healthcare expenditures in OECD countries. The EQ-5D-5L has been used extensively to measure and value health related quality of life in economic evaluation and is increasingly being used in quality assessment with older populations across health and social care settings. This research project builds upon our team’s previous qualitative work to assess older aged care residents' understanding of the EQ-5D-5L, funded by the EuroQol group, and our collective extensive experience in the measurement and valuation of quality of life in older people using the EQ-5D-5L, Quality of Life-Aged Care Consumers (QOL-ACC) and other preference-based measures. By use of a qualitative think-aloud protocol with older people of different age groups in community settings, we aim to [1] investigate face and content validity of the EQ-5D-5L in older people, and inform which age-subgroup(s) to target for any further analysis, and [2] subsequently investigate how older people assess bolt-ons in the Toolbox when presented alongside the EQ-5D-5L. An initial deductive framework analysis will be undertaken using the well-established Tourangeau framework.
