1. What is already known about the topic? Currently, a 3-level (3L) and a 5-level (5L) version of the EQ-5D is available. Both EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L health states can be converted to utility scores using either a value set or an interim (reverse) crosswalk. A recent study suggested that country-specific EQ-5D-5L crosswalks and value sets produce similar cost-utility outcomes. This study, however, only used empirical data of two trial-based economic evaluations and cost-utility outcomes derived using the EQ-5D-3L were not compared with those derived using the reverse crosswalk. Therefore, it remains unclear whether (reverse) crosswalks and value sets can also be used interchangeably in other patient populations. 2. What does the paper add to existing knowledge? This study provides novel information on the impact of using EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L value sets or (reverse) crosswalks on cost-utility outcomes for different health conditions (i.e., depression, low back pain, osteoarthritis, and cancer), different disease severity levels (i.e., mild, moderate, and severe), different treatment effect sizes (i.e., small, medium, and large), and different countries (i.e., the Netherlands, the United States, and Japan). The results indicate that the choice of utility scoring method can have a relevant impact on the probability of cost-effectiveness, especially when treatment effect sizes are small. 3. What insights does the paper provide for informing health care-related decision making? Crosswalks may be used when value sets are missing for a specific country or jurisdiction. However, our findings indicate that reimbursement decisions may change depending on the use of either value sets or (reverse) crosswalks. Therefore, when EQ-5D value sets are not available, rresearchers and decision-makers should be aware that the use of (reverse) crosswalk can impact the decision, especially in situations with small effect sizes.
