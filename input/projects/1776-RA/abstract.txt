Background: Assessing Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQoL) in children and adolescents is crucial for clinical and healthcare decision-making. The EQ-5D, developed by the EuroQol Group to assess HRQoL, is a simple and well-established instrument. The youth version of EQ-5D-3L (EQ-5D-Y-3L) was introduced in 2010 for self-completion by children from 8 years. Available evidence demonstrated the feasibility and validity of EQ-5D-Y-3L in diverse population groups with various clinical conditions, mostly carried out in high-income countries with limited evidence of the use if the instrument in low-and-middle-income countries (LMICs). Bangladesh is a LMIC, and the Bangla version of EQ-5D-Y-3L is currently piloted in Bangladesh among healthy school children. Aim: This project will aim to explore the psychometric properties of the instrument among children and adolescents across various acute and chronic health conditions, using both cross-sectional and longitudinal study designs. It will address several research questions, including feasibility, reliability, validity, responsiveness, and variation in problems reported across dimensions in different clinical conditions. Methods: The study will be conducted in Bangladesh and will be facilitated by the International Centre for Diarrhoeal Disease Research, Bangladesh (icddr,b). Hospitals under icddr,b treat about 0.2 million patients each year, mostly children (>65%) with various acute and chronic disease conditions. A list of prevalent acute conditions (e.g., pneumonia, diarrhea) and emerging chronic conditions (e.g., asthma, heart disease) will be determined. Children aged 4-17 years old with any acute/chronic health conditions will be asked to participate in the study. Ethical approval will be applied for, and recruitment of participants will be started in May 2024. The sample size will be varied depending on the health conditions. The study will employ short-term follow-ups (one month) for acute conditions, while participants with chronic conditions will be monitored for up to one year. Various statistical analysis methods will be employed to assess outcomes, including measuring feasibility, reliability, and variation across dimensions and disease groups. This comprehensive approach will provide valuable insights into the HRQoL of children and adolescents in Bangladesh, contributing significantly to healthcare decision-making and policy development in the context of LMICs.
