The funded project was successfully completed, supporting international dissemination and academic exchange around EuroQol-funded research on  EQ-HWB instrument and its application in elderly rehabilitation care.A central component of the grant was participation in the 2025 iHEA World Congress, where Haode Wang delivered an oral presentation on EuroQol-funded work related to preference elicitation using discrete choice experiments (DCE), and also co-chaired a session on QoL and PROMs research. This provided an important platform to disseminate findings, engage with leading international experts in health economics, and obtain high-quality feedback to refine ongoing and future research. The presentation has been arranged as the first presentation of QoL valuation and its methodology section. Haode was the only ECR researcher presented on the session. Around 47 international scholars, including Nancy Delvin, participated in the session activity. Apart from QoL researchers, the conference also facilitated networking with researchers and stakeholders interested in the application of EQ instruments in both clinical and policy settings.Haode also participated in the EuroQol-funded young children QoL pre-session, hosted by Nan Luo, where Haode and Nan had a thorough discussion about project 1963-RA. Although the originally planned institutional visit to the National University of Singapore (NUS) was not completed, key objectives related to academic exchange were still achieved. This iHEA interaction enabled meaningful discussion on the ongoing EQ-HWB (1963-RA) project, including methodological considerations such as the use of proxy versions, longitudinal data collection design, and potential approaches to estimating minimally important differences (MID). These discussions provided valuable expert input that will directly inform the next phase of the project.Importantly, the revision of the travel plan—specifically the cancellation of the NUS visit—resulted in a reduced overall expenditure, thereby generating cost savings for the EuroQol Group while still achieving the majority of the intended scientific and collaborative objectives. Overall, the project contributed to strengthening international academic exchange, enhancing methodological rigor in EQ-HWB research, and supporting the broader goal of promoting the application of EQ instruments. The knowledge gained and connections established are expected to lead to further collaborations, improved study implementation, and future research outputs, including publications and potential joint grant applications.
