Background. The EQ-HWB-9 is a 9-item instrument developed to capture a broader concept of health and wellbeing across emotional, physical, and social domains of patients, social care users and carers. To address limitations identified through several adaptations and qualitative studies, a modified version of EQ-HWB-9 was created. The EQ-DAPHNIE dataset, an international health survey, includes responses to both the experimental and modified versions of EQ-HWB-9 from Argentina and Germany, along with a range of validated health outcomes. This dataset presents a unique opportunity to conduct comparative psychometric testing of the experimental and modified EQ-HWB-9 using real-world population data.Objective. To quantitatively evaluate and compare the performance of the experimental and modified EQ-HWB-9 using existing EQ-DAPHNIE data from Argentina and Germany.Methods. This study analysed respondents who completed both versions of EQ-HWB-9 (Germany n=1,569; Argentina n=1,514; total N=3,083). We examined item- and scale-level floors/ceilings, feasibility (missingness), Spearman/Pearson correlations with EQ-5D-5L, EQ-VAS, PROMIS Global Health, PHQ-2, and GAD-2, and known-group validity (physical/mental disability, caregiver status, EQ-VAS <80, PHQ-2 ≥3, GAD-2 ≥3) with cohen’s d effect sizes.Results. Missingness was very low (<0.6% per item; scale-level 2.9% experimental, 4.0% modified). Ceiling effects concentrated in functional items: Mobility (experimental 76.3%) and Activities (modified 75.7%); floors were absent. Item-level response shifts were systematic (χ² significant for all items; McNemar significant for several domains), with 22-30% inconsistent pairs in Germany and 25-34% in Argentina. Convergent patterns were as expected: high with EQ-5D-5L LSS (~.58), moderate and negative with EQ-VAS (~-.47), and moderate with PROMIS domains (strongest for Social Activities ~.51). The modified Mobility item aligned more closely with EQ-5D-5L Usual Activities and PROMIS Physical/Self-rated Health. Known-group validity was significant across all contrasts (all p<.001), with large effects for mental-health screens and EQ-VAS, large/medium for physical disability, and small/medium for caregiving. Two contrasts showed a category improvement with the modified version (physical disability in Argentina: small to medium; caregiver status in Germany: small to medium).Conclusion. The experimental and modified EQ-HWB-9 versions showed broadly comparable psychometric performance. Some wording and order changes improved distributional properties, while others had minimal impact, providing preliminary evidence to guide further refinement of the measure
