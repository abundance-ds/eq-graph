Purpose The EuroQol Health and Wellbeing Short (EQ-HWB-S) is a new, generic 9-item instrument, suitable for evaluating interventions in health and social-care settings for patients and caregivers. The instrument now requires validation across a range of general and caregiver populations. We aimed to examine the psychometric performance of the EQ-HWB-S in a general population dataset that included rates of caregivers of persons with disability/chronic illness. Methods We used a nationally representative survey of the Australian and New Zealand populations. The main aim of the survey was to estimate the population prevalence of the use of meditation and other contemplative practices. We investigated EQ-HWB-S item distribution and known-group validity (t-tests; Cohen’s d for effect size, with sub-group analysis by country, gender and age) across sum- and preference-weighted scores (UK pilot). Item scores were compared across caregiver groups. Convergent validity was assessed between the EQ-HWB-S and the Kessler-6 using Spearman’s Rho. Results The sample included 2542 participants, 2018 from Australia and 524 from New Zealand. Item distribution was similar to previous studies. Caregivers had significantly higher scores (when scoring each item from 1 (best level) to 5 (worse level)) across each item than their counterparts; thus, the item distribution for caregivers was less skewed. Known-group validity results aligned to a priori hypotheses for being a caregiver, mental health, physical health and disability and sleep issues variables. Convergent validity conformed to a priori expectations. Conclusion Results from the psychometric tests used in this study suggested that the EQ-HWB-S was a suitable instrument for measuring health and wellbeing in a representative sample of the Australian and New Zealand population, building the evidence for the use of the instrument across a range of settings. An Australian-specific value-set would be a good future addition.
