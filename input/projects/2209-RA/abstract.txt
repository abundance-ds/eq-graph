Objective Adolescence is a transitional period in which both youth-specific and adult health-related quality-of-life instruments may be appropriate. The EQ-5D-Y-5L is a new child-friendly HRQoL instrument with improvements in responsiveness and known-groups validity compared to EQ-5D-Y-3L. Both EQ-5D-5L and EQ-5D-Y (-3L and -5L) are suitable for use in older adolescents. However, evidence remains limited on transitions and comparative measurement properties across these instruments. This study aimed to examine the measurement properties and response redistribution of the EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, and EQ-5D-5L among adolescents aged 12-18 years in China.Methods Data were obtained from the first wave of the Comprehensive Health and Relevant Factors Measurement among Chinese Children and Adolescents (CHARMING) study, a nationwide, geographically diverse survey using stratified quota sampling and paired parent-adolescent questionnaires. The EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, EQ-5D-5L, CHU-9D, and EQ VAS were administered in randomized order. Level sum scores (LSS) were calculated for all EQ-5D instruments. Measurement properties were assessed using ceiling effects, Shannon’s index (H’), Shannon’s evenness index (J’), convergent validity, and known-groups validity. Paired response redistribution was examined between EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L to assess the impact of expanding response levels, and between EQ-5D-Y-5L and EQ-5D-5L to evaluate wording-related differences. Response redistribution was further compared between adolescents aged 12-15 years and 16-18 years, following the age ranges specified in the EuroQol user guides. Results This analysis included 5,062 adolescents (mean age, 14.9 years; 53.9% boys). Mean LSS were 5.337, 6.342, and 6.121 for the EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, and EQ-5D-5L, respectively. Ceiling effects were highest for the EQ-5D-Y-3L (73.2%), followed by the EQ-5D-5L (48.6%) and EQ-5D-Y-5L (41.2%). The EQ-5D-Y-5L had higher mean H’ (0.823) and J’ (0.355) than EQ-5D-Y-3L (H’: 0.294, J’: 0.186) and EQ-5D-5L (H’: 0.731, J’: 0.315). Correlations with EQ VAS and CHU-9D utility were strongest for the EQ-5D-5L LSS (r=-0.631 and -0.624), followed by the EQ-5D-Y-5L LSS (r=-0.589 and -0.599) and EQ-5D-Y-3L LSS (r=-0.319 and -0.367). All three instruments distinguished the prespecified MHS-CA, EQ-VAS, and BMI groups, with the strongest gradients across EQ-VAS groups. Large-step discordance between the youth versions was low for mobility (0.51%) and self-care (0.22%) but higher for usual activities (7.05%), pain/discomfort (7.35%), and emotional health (6.60%). For EQ-5D-Y-5L versus EQ-5D-5L, non-agreement ranged from 0.32% for MO to 28.09% for WSU. Compared with those aged 12-15 years, adolescents aged 16-18 years showed larger inconsistency or non-agreement in WSU.Conclusions Three EQ-5D instruments showed varied measurement performance across criteria among adolescents aged 12-18 years. EQ-5D-Y-5L showed the most favorable distributional properties and informativity, whereas EQ-5D-5L performed best in convergent validity. All three instruments demonstrated known-groups validity. Response redistribution analyses suggested that expanded response levels and wording differences mainly affected UA, PD and WSU. Adolescents aged 16-18 years showed greater divergence in WSU than those aged 12-15 years, possibly because richer health experiences made them more sensitive to wording and response-level differences in subjective dimensions.
