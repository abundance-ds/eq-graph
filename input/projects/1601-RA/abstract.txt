Objective: This study examined the psychometric performance of six EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons - vision, breathing, tiredness, sleep, social relationships, and self-confidence. Two research questions: (1) What are the psychometric properties of the six EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons in patients with myasthenia gravis (MG)? and (2) What are the psychometric properties of the six EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons among members of the general population and in comparison to their 3L versions? Datasets: Data from two international digital surveys were used: one in the general population and one in MG patients. Both included the EQ-5D-5L with bolt-ons, HUI3, HADS, MG-ADL, and MG-QoL. The MG study additionally included PROMIS Sleep-Disturbance, PROMIS Dyspnea, and FACIT-Fatigue; the general population study also included the EQ-5D-3L bolt-ons. Methods: Psychometric properties included distribution characteristics (ceiling/floor), informativity, validity (divergent, convergent, and known-groups), and explanatory power, reliability, and responsiveness. Main findings:Findings indicated that the EQ-5D-5L performs well in capturing MG-related health issues, and its sensitivity improves further with the addition of vision and breathing bolt-ons. The six 5-level EQ-5D bolt-ons outperformed the 3-level bolt-ons by reducing ceiling and floor, enhancing informativity, demonstrating divergent validity, and improving responsiveness.
