Oftentimes, disabled individuals require assistive devices or aids (ADs) to enhance their functional capabilities. The growing demand and variety of ADs necessitate methods for decision-makers to measure the outcomes of disabled individuals effectively. Understanding the conceptualization of health and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) among people with physical impairments can help identify opportunities for improving outcomes. This study aimed to assess the feasibility, acceptability, and content validity of the EQ-5D-5L among disabled Ethiopians. A qualitative approach, using in-depth interviews, was employed to explore: the feasibility of administering and completing the EQ-5D-5L questionnaire, the acceptability of its items for disabled individuals, and feedback on its content. Two groups of participants were interviewed: (1) individuals disabled due to vision loss or mobility impairments, and (2) clinicians working with disabled individuals or leaders of disability organizations. All interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, and analyzed using NVivo software. An inductive thematic analysis approach was applied to the transcripts. The findings revealed distinct differences in how disabled individuals perceive health and quality of life. Quality of life was understood as encompassing more than health, including personal achievements, financial stability, social relationships, and self-perception. Respondents emphasized that being disabled does not necessarily equate to poor health. The EQ-5D-5L was found to inadequately address critical dimensions such as vision, hearing, and social interactions. Respondents recommended incorporating these elements to better reflect the health experiences of disabled individuals in Ethiopia. While most participants found the EQ-5D-5L generally acceptable, they particularly appreciated its mental health dimensions (anxiety/depression), which encouraged reflection on their health conditions. In Ethiopian settings, in-person interviews were preferred as the primary mode of administration, especially for rural or less literate populations. For urban and more educated groups, a mixed-methods approach—combining technology-enabled options (e.g., phone or tablet-based interviews) with self-completion—was suggested to enhance accessibility and feasibility. Overall, this study highlighted the relevance and acceptability of the EQ-5D-5L as a health outcome measure among disabled Ethiopians. The findings suggested that incorporating additional dimensions, such as vision, hearing, and social interaction, would enhance its validity for health economic evaluations of AD technologies in Ethiopia.
