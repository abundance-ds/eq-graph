Background: The digital administration of the EQ-HWB-9 introduces potential methodological variation due to device-dependent layout differences. On mobile devices, the instrument is displayed in an item-by-item format, while on larger screens (desktop or tablet), items appear simultaneously in a grid format. These presentation modes may influence usability, cognitive load, response behavior, and data quality, particularly among vulnerable respondents such as older adults and individuals with disabilities. Although such layout effects are well documented in web-survey research, their implications for EQ-HWB instruments remain unexplored. The availability of device metadata within the EQ-DAPHNIE dataset offers a unique opportunity to investigate whether layout-related differences influence data integrity and comparability in digital administration of the EQ-HWB-9.Objectives: This study aims to examine whether the presentation format of the EQ-HWB-9 affects respondent experience and data quality. Specifically, it seeks to (1) compare completion characteristics (e.g., completion time, break-offs, and item nonresponse) between item-by-item and grid layouts; (2) assess response quality and response pattern indicators (variance, straight-lining, and distributional shifts) across device types; (3) examine subgroup differences by age, health status, and disability to identify populations most affected by layout format; and (4) evaluate whether layout-related differences influence the consistency between the EQ-HWB-9 and EQ-HWB long form scores, thereby testing the construct validity of the short version across devices.Methods: Secondary analyses will use EQ-DAPHNIE data from Germany (n=1569) and Argentina (n=1514), which include both the modified versions of  EQ-HWB-9 and EQ-HWB long-form responses. Respondents will be classified as mobile (item-by-item) or non-mobile (grid) users based on device metadata. Weighted and unweighted analyses will be performed separately by country. Data quality indicators including missingness, variance, completion time, and straight-lining will be compared using descriptive and multivariable regression analyses controlling for demographics and health variables (EQ VAS). Sensitivity analyses will evaluate robustness across weighting schemes and subpopulations. Findings will provide evidence to guide the final digital layout standard for the EQ-HWB-9.
