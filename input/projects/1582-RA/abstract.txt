This research examined discrepancies between self- and proxy-rated health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in people living with dementia (PlwD), using data from the InDePendent cluster-randomized controlled trial initiated in Germany in 2020. The analysis focused on the EQ-5D-5L and EQ-VAS instruments, administered at baseline. At baseline, we conducted a detailed comparison of EQ-5D-5L ratings from 174 dyads to explore the degree and nature of discrepancies on an individual response level. The agreement was assessed using weighted Kappa and the Paretian Classification of Health Change (PCHC) and the presence and degree of inconsistencies between ratings. Results revealed substantial disagreement: 95% of PlwD rated their health differently than their caregivers, with 66% showing divergent responses in at least three EQ-5D-5L dimensions. Caregivers tended to rate PlwD health more conservatively, particularly in observable domains like usual activities (lowest agreement; weighted kappa = 0.23), whereas slightly higher agreement was observed in subjective dimensions such as pain/discomfort (0.40) and anxiety/depression (0.37). Although PlwD rated the health higher on average than proxies (mean utility index: 0.75 vs. 0.68), we could also observe that proxies rated the health of PlwD better than the self-ratings. This could be caused by the situation in which proxies tend to choose the mid-response options more often (avoidance of the extreme response options). This tendency toward moderate responses in proxy ratings may reflect uncertainty in assessment, potentially leading to underestimation or overestimation depending on the self-reported health status. These findings expand on previous research and highlight the need for further methodological investigation into self- and proxy-reported HRQoL in dementia.
