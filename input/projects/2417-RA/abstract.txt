Background:Measuring health-related quality of life (HRQoL) among children and adolescents is essential for understanding their physical, mental, and social well-being from their own perspectives. The EQ-5D-Y, developed by the EuroQol Group, is a validated, child-friendly, preference-based instrument that captures HRQoL across five dimensions. The instrument has been recently piloted in Bangladesh and found to be feasible and acceptable. Establishing national reference data is therefore crucial to support policy development, economic evaluations, and child health planning.Objectives:This study aims to (1) generate EQ-5D-Y-3L population reference data for Bangladeshi children and adolescents, (2) examine variations in HRQoL reporting by age, sex, and socio-economic characteristics, and (3) explore the impact of applying different international value sets on the derived utility scores.Methods:A cross-sectional study was conducted between December 2024 and February 2025 alongside the adult EQ-5D valuation study in Bangladesh, implemented by the ARK Foundation following the EuroQol Group’s EQ-VT protocol. Using a multistage stratified quota sampling method, 1,023 households were surveyed across the eight administrative divisions. Of these, 535 households with children aged 8 to 17 years were eligible, yielding 712 valid EQ-5D-Y-3L responses.  The Bangla version of the EQ-5D-Y-3L and visual analogue scale (VAS) were completed by children, while adult respondents provided socio-demographic data.Descriptive statistics will summarise socio-demographic characteristics and HRQoL responses across dimensions. Differences by demographic variables will be tested using chi-square, t-tests, Mann-Whitney U, and ANOVA, as appropriate. Logistic regression models will assess associations between socio-demographic factors and reporting problems in each EQ-5D-Y-3L dimension.  As Bangladesh lacks a country-specific value set, available international EQ-5D-Y-3L value sets will be applied to estimate health state utilities. Additional analyses will compare results across 10 available international value sets using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) and Bland-Altman plots to assess agreement. Expected Outcomes:This study will provide the first nationally representative EQ-5D-Y-3L reference data for Bangladeshi children and adolescents and offer methodological insights for developing a local value set. Findings will inform HRQoL research, child health policy, and future economic evaluations in Bangladesh.
