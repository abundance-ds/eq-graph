Both EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9 are generic, preference-accompanied instruments by EuroQol that use different recall periods. This study aimed to explore the impact of recall period on the measurement properties of EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9, including a possible EQ-HWB VAS. An online cross-sectional survey was conducted with a representative general population sample in Hong Kong (n=1262). Respondents completed both a ‘today’ and a ‘last week’ version of the EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9, including the EQ VAS and a ‘health and wellbeing’ VAS for EQ-HWB-9. Psychometric properties (e.g., ceiling, convergent validity with ReQoL-10 and PROMIS-29+2, and known-group validity) were assessed for both the descriptive systems and the VAS versions. The ‘last week’ versions of EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9 (28.8% and 14.6%) showed lower ceiling than the ‘today’ versions (34.8% and 18.2%). More respondents had identical ‘today’ and ‘last week’ profiles on EQ-5D-5L (52.4%) than on EQ-HWB-9 (17.7%). The longer recall improved convergent validity for most EQ-HWB-9 items. Both instruments’ ‘last week’ versions showed slightly better known-group validity. The mean ‘today’ EQ VAS and ‘last week’ EQ-HWB VAS scores were 76.7 and 73.5 (p<0.001), with a strong correlation between them (r=0.88). EQ-HWB-9 items explained more variance in EQ-HWB VAS than EQ-5D-5L items in EQ VAS. Recall periods affect EQ-HWB-9 more than EQ-5D-5L; however, overall effects are small. ‘Last week’ versions perform slightly better in the general population. We provide the first evidence of the EQ-HWB VAS’s validity and its ability to capture distinct concepts from the EQ VAS, supporting its potential added value to the EQ-HWB-9.
