Objective: This study aimed to evaluate the psychometric properties—feasibility, reliability, and construct validity—of the EQ-5D-Y in children and adolescents with ADHD. A secondary objective was to assess the performance of four cognition-related bolt-on items for the EQ-5D-Y. Methods: Data were collected through a web-based, cross-sectional survey in China, in collaboration with Lanzhou Children’s Hospital. All participants completed the EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, and four cognition-related bolt-on items, alongside providing sociodemographic and health condition information. Measurement properties were evaluated using ceiling and floor effects, convergent validity, classification efficiency, known-group validity, explanatory power, and test-retest reliability. Results: A total of 188 ADHD patients completed both Y-3L and Y-5L. The Y-5L dimensions showed high ceiling effects for self- and proxy-reports, ranging from 50.0–53.7% (anxiety/depression) to 89.4–93.2% (mobility). Ceiling effects for bolt-on items were lower, ranging from 5.3% (concentration) to 50.5% (school performance) for proxy-reports and 31.4% (concentration) to 59.0% (cognitive/mental abilities) for self-reports. Correlations between Y-5L dimensions, bolt-on items, and CHU-9D items assessing similar constructs were weak to moderate (rho: 0.23–0.49). Adding the four bolt-on items increased the Y-5L’s explanatory power by 15–33% for both self- and proxy-completed versions. The Y-5L dimensions demonstrated moderate to good test-retest reliability, while bolt-on items showed fair to moderate reliability. Conclusion: Both self- and proxy-completed Y-5L exhibited acceptable psychometric properties in ADHD patients. Including cognition-related bolt-on items enhanced the Y-5L’s psychometric performance.
