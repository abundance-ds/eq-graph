Background: Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death in China and worldwide, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for 85% of cases. In recent years, numerous innovative NSCLC drugs (e.g., targeted therapies, immune checkpoint inhibitors) have been launched. China requires economic evaluations, preferably cost-utility analysis, to inform new drug reimbursement decisions. CUA relies on health utility values, but the EQ-5D-5L and SF-6D, i.e., the only preference-based measures with Chinese value sets, lack reliability and validity evidence in NSCLC populations. This study aimed to examine the measurement properties of EQ-5D-5L in comparison with SF-6D in patients with NSCLC in China. Methods: A cross-sectional survey was conducted in 19 provinces across China from October 2022 to March 2024, recruiting NSCLC patients from 20 hospitals. Demographic and clinical data were collected, and participants completed the EQ-5D-5L, SF-6D, and QLQ-C30. The measurement properties were evaluated using response patterns (ceiling/floor effects), convergent validity with the QLQ-C30, known-groups validity (gender, age, cancer stage, surgery, brain metastasis), and Shannon (H’) and evenness (J’) indices. Results: Data from 947 eligible patients were analyzed (36.4% female; mean age 61.6 years; mean duration since diagnosis 1.81 years). Sixty-four percent were stage IV, 31.4% had surgery, and 18.9% had brain metastasis. The EQ-5D-5L ceiling effect was 24.87% vs 3.39% for the SF-6D. Moderate-to-strong correlations existed between EQ-5D-5L and SF-6D and QLQ-C30 dimensions with similar constructs (ρ 0.58–0.83, all p < 0.001). Known-groups validity hypotheses based on age, cancer stage, and surgery status were validated. The EQ-5D-5L showed strongest informativity and discriminatory power in “Pain/discomfort” (H’ = 1.75, J’ = 0.75) and weakest in “Self-care” (H’ = 1.10, J’ = 0.48). For the SF-6D dimensions, the Shannon index ranged from 1.93 for “mental health” to 2.23 for “social functioning”, and the Shannon evenness index ranged from 0.83 for “mental health” to 0.96 for “social functioning”.  Conclusions: The EQ-5D-5L demonstrated robust measurement properties, supporting its use in assessing health status of Chinese patients with NSCLC. Its performance was comparable with that of the SF-6D, though the EQ-5D-5L exhibited a higher ceiling effect.
