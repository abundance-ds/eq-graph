Objective: The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the impact of incorporating interaction terms in EQ-5D valuation models and to examine the implications of health state coverage and sample size allocation for future valuation studies. By leveraging three saturated datasets—EQ-5D-3L datasets from the Netherlands and the UK, and an EQ-5D-5L dataset from China—we aimed to determine optimal model specifications and assess whether expanding the number of health states in the design improves predictive accuracy. Methods: We first estimated main-effects models and models with interaction terms to assess their predictive performance, using root mean squared error (RMSE), Akaike information criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC), and R² as model evaluation metrics. We then systematically tested different health state selection strategies, including reducing the number of health states or observations per state, to examine the impact on model accuracy and feasibility. The study also explored whether specific universal interaction effects could be identified across the three datasets. Results: The inclusion of interaction terms significantly improved model performance, with RMSE reductions of 51% in the Netherlands dataset, 37% in the UK dataset, and 17% in the China dataset. However, the effect of interactions varied across datasets, with no universal interaction term consistently identified, except for PD3AD3, which captures the combined effect of severe pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression. Additionally, we found that increasing health state coverage (rather than increasing the number of responses per health state) yielded better prediction accuracy. Specifically, when the total sample size was fixed, doubling the number of health states while halving the number of responses per state improved RMSE without compromising model validity. Discussions: These findings suggest that the optimal model specifications differ by EQ-5D version: for EQ-5D-3L, the best-performing model includes N3 and D1, while for EQ-5D-5L, N45 and D1 provide the best fit. The results also highlight the importance of broader health state coverage in experimental design—current valuation studies that allocate 100 observations per health state may achieve better predictive accuracy by halving observations per state and doubling the number of health states in the design. The study demonstrates that interaction effects play a crucial role in EQ-5D valuation, particularly for severe health impairments. Future valuation studies should consider incorporating key interaction terms while optimizing health state selection strategies to balance model parsimony and predictive accuracy. These findings have practical implications for the design of future EQ-5D valuation studies and may inform methodological refinements for valuing newer instruments such as EQ-HWB-S, where health state coverage is a critical concern.
