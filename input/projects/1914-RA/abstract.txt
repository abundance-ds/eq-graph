Objectives: We aimed to evaluate the psychometric performance of existing EQ-5D-5L-format bolt-ons and selected EQ-HWB items as bolt-ons, comparing them in patients with breast cancer. Methods: A secondary analysis was conducted on a longitudinal dataset of 300 female patients with breast cancer. Outcome measures included EQ-HWB, EQ-5D-5L, and four EQ-5D-5L-format bolt-ons (bodily appearance [BA], social relationships, sleep [SL], fatigue [FA]). Fourteen EQ-HWB items were selected for testing as potential bolt-ons: sleep (SP), exhaustion (EX), loneliness (LO), unsupported (UN), memory (ME), cognition (CO), unsafe (US), frustration (FR), look forward (LF), no control (NC), coping (CP), accepted (AC), feel good (FG), and wanted to (WA). Psychometric properties assessed included ceiling reduction, validity (convergent/divergent and known-group), explanatory power, test-retest reliability, and responsiveness. Results: The EQ-5D-5L ceiling (35.0%) was reduced by adding EQ-5D-5L-format bolt-ons (0.7%-10.7%) or EQ-HWB items (0.3%-18.3%). No strong correlations were found between bolt-ons and core EQ-5D-5L dimensions. Adding FA, SL, EX, SP, CO, FG, or WA (or combinations) improved EQ-5D-5L’s known-group validity. In direct comparisons, EX and SP outperformed FA and SL in relative efficiency for distinguishing known-groups. EX outperformed FA in increasing the explanatory power of EQ-5D-5L for EQ VAS, Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy-General and Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale scores. Adding BA, SL, CO, LF, NC, or US slightly improved EQ-5D-5L test-retest reliability, while WA slightly increased the responsiveness of EQ-5D-5L in patients with worsened health. Conclusion: EQ-HWB items outperformed existing EQ-5D-5L-format bolt-ons in overall psychometric performance, with sleep and exhaustion emerging as promising bolt-on candidates in breast cancer. Further research is recommended to guide their adoption.
