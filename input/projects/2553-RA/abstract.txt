Accurate prediction of health outcomes such as mortality, hospitalizations, and healthcare utilization is essential for effective health system planning, risk stratification, and patient-centered care. Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs), including the EQ-5D, may capture dimensions of health not fully reflected in clinical or administrative data, thereby offering potential value in predictive modeling. While a growing body of literature suggests that PROMs are associated with future health outcomes, the extent to which EQ-5D contributes independently to prediction—and how it is operationalized within predictive models—remains unclear.This project will conduct a comprehensive systematic review to evaluate the predictive and prognostic performance of all versions of the EQ-5D instrument (EQ-5D-3L, EQ-5D-5L, and EQ-5D-Y) across diverse populations, clinical conditions, and health system contexts. The review will follow PRISMA guidelines and will be registered in PROSPERO. A systematic search will be conducted across major bibliographic databases (including MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, PsycINFO, and the Cochrane Library). Findings will be synthesized using a structured narrative approach, with quantitative meta-analysis conducted where appropriate.This work will provide the first comprehensive synthesis of evidence on the predictive and prognostic utility of EQ-5D. Findings will support the EuroQol Group in understanding the potential role of EQ-5D as a decision-support tool in health systems, informing applications in risk stratification, case-mix adjustment, and performance measurement. By clarifying where and how EQ-5D adds value, this project will contribute to advancing its use within routine data systems and learning health system environments.
