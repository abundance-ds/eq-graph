Objectives: To investigate whether the same health state results in the same distribution of responses on the youth and adult descriptive systems (Y-3L vs 3L and 5L, and Y-5L vs 5L) Methods: Adolescents with and without a health condition were recruited from South Africa (SA) (3L, Y-3L and 5L) and Ethiopia (ET) (5L and Y-5L). The order of questionnaires was randomised and separated by a cognitive task. Adult and youth descriptive systems were compared by analysing differences in unique health states, redistribution of dimension responses, inconsistencies, discriminative power by Shannon index (H’), agreement of dimension scores with Gwet’s AC, and known-group validity on the level sum score (LSS). Results: Data from paired adult and youth EQ versions from 592 SA (3L, Y-3L and 5L) and 693 Ethiopian (5L and Y-5L) adolescent respondents were analysed. There were slightly more unique health states reported on 3L (n=64) compared to Y-3L (n=61) (X2=0.097, p=0.755). Conversely Y-5L (n=133) had more unique health states than 5L (n=120 (X2= 0.696, p=0.040). There were 10.5% inconsistent responses between SA 3L versions, 35.1% between Y-3L and 5L and 12.3% between Ethiopian 5L versions. The highest % inconsistencies were reported between pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression. In those with a health condition the ceiling (percentage 11111) was lower on Y-3L (37.2%) than 3L (45.0%) (X2=3.67, p=0.056) whereas Y-5L (43.2%) was slightly higher than 5L (42.3%) (X2=0.055, p=0.815). In SA discriminatory power was higher for Y-3L compared to 3L for pain/discomfort (Δ H’=0.11) and anxiety/depression (Δ H’=0.04) and across all dimensions for Y-3L compared to 5L with the largest differences for usual activities (ΔH’=0.32) and anxiety/depression (Δ H’=0.38). Similarly, in ET discriminatory power was higher across all dimensions for Y-5L compared to 5L (Δ H’ range 0.05-0.09). Gwet’s AC showed very good agreement across all paired (SA) 3L and (ET) 5L dimensions except for anxiety/depression on 3L which showed good agreement [0.68 (0.63, 0.73)]. The LSS of all versions were able to differentiate between known disease groups (school, cardiac, diabetes, functional disability, HIV and respiratory disease). In SA there were significant differences on the 3L, Y-3L and 5L LSS between the general school group and those living with functional disability and HIV. Additionally the 5L could differentiate between the school and diabetes mellitus groups. In ET both the 5L and Y-5L LSS could significantly differentiate between the general school group and all other condition groups except those living with HIV. Conclusion: Despite the generally mild self-reported health state of the sample, both the Y-3L and Y-5L showed a more severe health state compared to the corresponding adult descriptive systems under the same conditions. The differences were most notable for pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression. When comparing the transition between the Y-3L and 5L versions there is a high percentage of inconsistencies, particularly between anxiety/depression and worried/sad/unhappy. The difference in dimension headings and level qualifiers between the adult and youth versions yielded different results. These differences in the descriptive systems need to be taken into consideration for descriptive analysis and comparison of preference-weighted scores.
