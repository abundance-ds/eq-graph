In health valuation, respondents are commonly asked to choose between episodes with problems described by EQ-5D-5L attributes. A chronic episode describes health problems until the person dies. Preferences over chronic episodes may differ from those of acute episodes (i.e., when a person returns to their original health trajectory). Currently, the paired comparison tasks in the EQ-VT describe health states (i.e., generic episodes), omitting the timing attributes such as when they start, their duration and how they end. The aim of the proposed study is to examine whether preferences differ when the 1-year episodes are acute, chronic, or generic. Differences in value between acute, chronic, and generic episodes are relevant for health technology assessments (HTAs). HTA agencies may evaluate chronic interventions separately (i.e., persons who are likely to die within 12 months). They may also consider evidence on acute episodes when assessing curative therapies. In the future, EQ-5D studies may commonly assess acute and chronic episodes.
