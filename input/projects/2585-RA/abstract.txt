Aim: This study's primary aim is to examine the validity of the EQ-HWB-9 and EQ-5D-5L proxy versions in neuropsychiatric illness by assessing patient-proxy agreement, where the proxy is an informal caregiver. Secondary aims are to examine the extent to which the proxy-proxy perspective (caregiver rating from their own viewpoint) differs from the proxy-patient perspective (caregiver rating standing in the patient's shoes), with particular attention to conditions where the patient may not be engaged with reality, and to examine the role of caregiver burden in modifying the level of patient-proxy agreement.Methods: We will conduct a cross-sectional study involving patient-caregiver dyads plus healthcare provider assessments, recruiting 300 patient-caregiver dyads (neurology/ psychiatry departments from two academic medical centers in Lebanon). Patients will complete self-assessments using the EQ-HWB-9, EQ-5D-5L, and self-perceived burden. Their informal caregivers will complete both proxy-patient assessments and proxy-proxy assessments and a caregiver burden instrument. Primary analyses will assess item-level and scale-level agreement between patient self-reports and caregiver proxy-patient assessments using weighted kappa, intra-class correlation coefficients, and Bland-Altman plots. Secondary analyses will quantify differences between proxy-proxy and proxy-patient perspectives, and will examine how caregiver burden modifies proxy-patient agreement through regression models, including whether patient self-perceived burden moderates these associations. Significance: This project addresses a critical gap in health and wellbeing measurement in neuropsychiatric populations where proxy reporting is essential. By validating the EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9 proxy versions and distinguishing between two conceptually distinct proxy perspectives and the influence of caregiver burden on assessment accuracy,  findings will inform best practices for proxy use in clinical and research settings, supporting more accurate health measurement in populations least able to self-report.
