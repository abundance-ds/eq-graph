Research into the Pain/Discomfortdimensionof EQ-5D instruments has identified problems with interpretation because two different aspects of health are combined in a single question. Studies have also shown that interpretation difficulties may arise because of the wording of response options, particularly concerning the differences between ‘slight’ and ‘moderate’ (levels 2 and 3), and ‘severe’ and ‘extreme’ (levels 4 and 5). This study will explore what is behind the Pain/Discomfort dimension of the EQ-5D-5L, drawing comparisons to other pain and discomfort items collected in the Extending the Quality-Adjusted Life Year (E-QALY) psychometric study in Australia. Analyses will include: descriptive statistics of response data; correlation analyses 2exploring the associations between EQ-5D-5L Pain/Discomfort and other E-QALY pain and discomfort questions; regression analyses assessing the extentto whichthe E-QALY pain and discomfort items are captured by EQ-5D-5L Pain/Discomfort; and discriminatory power analyses of the EQ-5D-5L with and without other E-QALY pain and discomfort questions. Furthermore, we will explore variation in reporting patterns for pain and discomfort questions across individuals living with mental health and physical health conditions, respectively. This sub-analysis arose from findings of the face validity study of the E-QALY project in Australia, which indicated that people with a mental health condition associated the Pain/Discomfort questions with emotionalpain or discomfort rather thanphysicalsensations. Overall, findings from this study will provide a better understanding of the Pain/Discomfortdimensionof the EQ-5D-5L, including implications for the complementary use of the EQ-5D-5L and E-QALY, and the future bolt-on agenda.
