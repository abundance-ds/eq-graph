Objectives The DCETTO is a popular method to create value sets for the EQ-5D. There are two main approaches to the DCETTO, where either immediate death or full health for a shorter duration is included as the third option in a choice set. There are also two main DCETTO choice set construction methods i.e. generator-developed or efficient DCE designs. There is a lack of studies that directly compare the two approaches and two construction methods and their impact on value sets. It is also unclear whether alternative methods would have an impact on nonlinear time preferences and whether the impact would vary in value sets from different countries. This study seeks to investigate these issues. Methods A 4-arm study was used to provide a comparison of the two approaches and two choice set construction methods of the DCETTO. This was tested in a sample of respondents from Peru (n = 942) and Denmark (n = 988). Results There was evidence that DCETTO approach and construction method can impact value sets, particularly for more severe health states. Respondents generally exhibited nonlinear time preferences. Results were noted to be more variable in the Peruvian data set. Conclusion This is the first study to provide a comparison of the two commonly used DCETTO approaches and associated construction methods in valuation studies across two countries. Efficient DCE designs had slightly better performance across arms but require priors and are less convenient than generator-developed designs. Directions for future research are discussed.
