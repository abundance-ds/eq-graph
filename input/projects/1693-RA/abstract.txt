OBJECTIVES: To evaluate two methodological extensions of the split-triplet discrete choice experiment (DCE) format for health state valuations: first, randomization of the choice task order, and second, the inclusion of additional pairwise choice tasks for improved anchoring of latent DCE results onto the quality-adjusted life year (QALY) scale. METHODS: Three study arms with increasing levels of choice task randomization were implemented in a Dutch DCE (n=1,522). Each DCE respondent completed 15 split-triplet and 8 additional pairwise choice tasks. A parallel composite time trade-off (cTTO) study (n=411) provided external benchmark values. Value sets were compared based on QALY decrements, pit state values, percentage of health states worse than dead, estimated discount rates, and relative choice consistency. RESULTS: Choice task randomization increased task complexity, led to more negative pit state values, and resulted in higher percentages of states worse than dead. The original non-randomized split-triplet format showed closest agreement with cTTO results. The additional pairwise choice tasks improve statistical identification of discount rates and produced more reliably anchored value sets that better corresponded to the cTTO benchmark. CONCLUSIONS: Our findings recommend against choice task randomization in split-triplet choice tasks while supporting the inclusion of supplemental pairwise tasks for improved anchoring. The study advances methods for standalone DCE health state valuations and expands the evidence base in support of an official EuroQol-approved DCE-based health state valuation protocol.
