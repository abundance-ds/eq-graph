This project aimed to disseminate findings from two studies at the ISPOR 2024 Conference through poster presentations in the "Pediatrics" and "Patient-Centered Research" sections. The first study, titled "Construct Validity and Test-Retest Reliability of the Three-level and Five-level Versions of the EQ-5D-Y" (SA65), systematically reviewed the psychometric performance of the three-level (Y-3L) and five-level (Y-5L) versions of the EQ-5D-Y instrument. Using a head-to-head comparison approach, the study assessed key properties such as construct validity and test-retest reliability. The findings support the adoption of the Y-5L for paediatric health assessments, especially in contexts requiring more precise measurement of health-related quality of life (HRQoL). These results align with EuroQol’s goal to enhance the applicability of its tools and were strategically timed to complement the launch of the Y-5L version. The second study, "Who Prefers Death to Life in Composite Time Trade-off Interviews and Why" (PCR43), is a secondary analysis of data collected from the Singapore EQ-5D-5L valuation study. It explored the motivations and characteristics of respondents who expressed a preference for death over living in states of poor health during composite time trade-off interviews. The analysis uncovered that middle-aged individuals, particularly those with dual caregiving roles, were more likely to consider health states as worse than death. These findings offer critical insights into the socio-demographic and cultural factors influencing valuation preferences, which can be used to refine valuation methods and inform resource allocation decisions. Both studies demonstrate strategic value to the EuroQol Group by advancing the scientific robustness, cultural sensitivity, and practical utility of EQ-5D instruments.
