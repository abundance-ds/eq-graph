As medical technology has advanced, the population of children with special healthcare needs has steadily grown to nearly 20%. These children: “have or are at increased risk for chronic physical, developmental, behavioral or emotional conditions and who also require health and related services of a type or amount beyond that required by children generally”.  At Children’s Hospital Los Angeles (CHLA), the Center for Healthy Adolescent Transition (CHAT) is developing a registry to track pediatric patients with special healthcare needs as they age from pediatric (age 16) to adult care (age 21) with a 5-year follow-up. Medical specialties include, but are not limited to, hematology, rheumatology, medical genetics, cardiology, endocrinology, neurology, nephrology, and oncology. The CHAT registry presents a unique opportunity to assess children with special healthcare needs over time, including their health-related quality-of-life (HRQoL). The objectives of this study are to use the EQ-5D-5L in the CHAT registry to 1) assess the HRQoL of the medically complex children in Los Angeles County; 2) evaluate the sensitivity of EQ-5D-5L in detecting changes in HRQoL among these children transitioning to adult care; and 3) compare the sensitivity of the EQ-5D-5L to that of the PedsQL in measuring HRQoL. In doing so, we hope to establish health state utility values among children with special healthcare needs; assess how HRQoL changes over time as adolescents transition into adulthood; and validate the EQ-5D-5L as an appropriate measure to assess health status in a transitioning pediatric population with special healthcare needs.
