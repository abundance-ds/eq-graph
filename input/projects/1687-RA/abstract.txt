This project translated and validated the EQ-HWB instrument in Slovenia and Hungary to support its use in social care and long-term care contexts. The self-complete, interviewer-administered and proxy 1 versions of the EQ-HWB long form (25 items) were translated into Slovenian and Hungarian following the EuroQol VMC protocol, including forward–back translation, reconciliation, and cognitive debriefing. Sixteen cognitive debriefing interviews were conducted in each country, confirming conceptual equivalence and identifying minor wording refinements to improve clarity and cultural acceptability.Content validity of the EQ-HWB-9 (both ’experimental modified’ 2024 and ’experimental’ 2022 versions with selected additional items from the long form) was then examined through qualitative interviews with social care recipients and carers in Hungary and long-term care recipients and carers in Slovenia, most recruited as dyads. In 2025, one-to-one semi-structured interviews with item-level probing were conducted using purposive sampling. Care recipients reviewed the self-complete version, while carers reviewed Proxy 1. A standardised topic guide, developed by members of the EQ-HWB WG, focused on relevance, comprehensiveness, and comprehensibility, and included targeted probes to assess selected proposed modifications to item wording, response scales, item order, and layout. The Hungarian study found the EQ-HWB-9 acceptable, relevant, and broadly comprehensive for use in social care, with very high comprehensibility, only isolated misunderstandings, and few suggestions for additional content, mainly related to broader social life. The Slovenian study identified comprehension difficulties, particularly for more abstract concepts such as control, concentrating or thinking clearly, and anxiety, with challenges most evident among care recipients with complex needs. Participants in both studies agreed that the instrument captures key aspects of wellbeing and supported refinements such as placing day-to-day activities before mobility and using the response level “moderate difficulty.”Overall, the translated versions and qualitative findings align with the growing international evidence base and support further psychometric validation, valuation studies, and the use of the EQ-HWB in economic evaluation and outcome measurement across social care, and long-term care settings.
