Background The choice of perspective in valuation tasks is likely to impact the scale of EQ-5D-Y-3L value sets, but less is known about how it impacts the relative importance of different dimensions. The aim of this study was to examine how preferences for EQ-5D-Y-3L health states differ using different perspectives, utilising two methods: the Online elicitation of Personal Utility Functions (OPUF) tool and a discrete choice experiment (DCE). An additional objective was to compare results from the OPUF tool and the DCE. Methods An online survey was designed containing the OPUF tool and a DCE. Adult respondents from the United Kingdom were randomised to one of five different arms: (1) 4-year-old child; (2) 10-year-old child; (3) a child of unspecified age; (4) another adult; and (5) own health. The characteristics of the OPUF value sets for the five perspectives, and relative importance scores from the DCE data were compared. Results Results differed by perspective in both valuation tasks. In both tasks, ‘looking after myself’ was less important and ‘pain or discomfort’ was more important in the child perspectives compared to the adult perspectives. Relative importance scores were far more varied in the DCE results compared to the OPUF results. The scale of the value sets produced by the OPUF tool differed by perspective, with the value of the worst health state being significantly lower in the adult perspectives compared to the child perspectives. Conclusion Our results suggest that the valuation of EQ-5D-Y-3L is impacted by the perspective that adult respondents are required to take. It is also clear that DCE and the OPUF tool produce different results.
