The EQ‑HWB is a comprehensive tool designed to capture key aspects of health and wellbeing, but it remains an experimental instrument with limited evidence on its real‑world applicability, particularly in disability service settings. People living with disabilities represent a population for whom broader measures of quality of life (QoL) and wellbeing are highly relevant and the EQ-HWB has the potential to be a valuable routine QoL outcome measure in this space.This project aims to evaluate the comparative advantage of the interviewer‑administered EQ‑HWB‑9 as a routine outcome measure within a real‑world disability service context. Using an Australian not‑for‑profit disability service provider, Scope Australia, as a test case, the study will compare the EQ‑HWB‑9 with the WHOQOL‑BREF with Disability (DIS) addition, an instrument currently being trialled at Scope Australia. Mixed methods will be employed, including stakeholder workshops, longitudinal routine data collection, and qualitative interviews with clients, clinicians and support workers.Quantitative analyses will assess response rates, missing data, response distributions and reasons for non‑completion to determine comparative feasibility and data quality. Qualitative analyses will explore stakeholder perspectives on relevance, burden, and usability of the EQ‑HWB‑9, as well as its perceived value in informing care planning and service delivery. The project will also examine how EQ‑HWB‑9 data can be summarised and presented to support decision making in disability services, including potential dashboard approaches.Findings will inform practical guidance on the routine use of the EQ‑HWB‑9 in disability support settings, with relevance for value‑based health decision making and broader applicability to public and non‑for‑profit disability services internationally.
