Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are tools used to capture a patient's self-reported health status, quality of life, and functional outcomes, providing insights into the impact of health conditions from the patient's perspective. Unlike clinical data collected by professionals, PROMs reflect the patient’s direct experience, helping to inform treatment decisions and track progress. The EQ-5D, a widely used PROM, assesses health-related quality of life (HRQoL) across various patient populations and is increasingly integrated into clinical practice. This research proposal aims to explore how healthcare professionals in community settings in a large London based Community Trust, use the EQ-5D to support clinical decision-making. Despite its broad application, limited research exists on how clinicians incorporate EQ-5D data into practice and their decision making, particularly in resource-constrained environments. The study will investigate clinicians' experiences, challenges, and perceptions of the EQ-5D, focusing on its role in guiding treatment, its limitations, and its potential for improving patient care. The research will involve semi-structured interviews with at least 20 healthcare professionals from a range of services within the Central London Community Healthcare Trust. Thematic analysis will be used to analyze the qualitative data, with the goal of understanding how EQ-5D data is interpreted and applied in clinical settings. The study will also explore potential training needs and suggest ways to improve EQ-5D integration into clinical workflows. Insights gained may lead to enhanced personalized care and a greater focus on patient-centered outcomes in community healthcare as well as ways for the EuroQol Working Groups to improve current and future instruments.
