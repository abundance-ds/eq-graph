Evidence regarding parent-child agreement in pediatric health-related quality-of-life assessments is scarce. This study aimed to explore the agreement between child/adolescent self-reports and parent proxy reports using the EQ-5D-Y-3L tool, along with the tool’s convergent validity and responsiveness. For younger participants aged 4-7 years, administer-assisted surveys were implemented to ensure accurate and child-friendly administration. The follow-up interviews were conducted before patient discharge, typically about 2-4 weeks after treatment. The repeated measure designed was intended to observe changes in health status over time and to assess the responsiveness of the EQ-5D-Y-3L measure. Separate interviews were conducted for caregivers and children/adolescents in distinct settings - nurses' offices for caregivers and pediatric wards for children/adolescents- to ensure comfort and privacy, thereby enhancing the quality of the data collected. The analysis involved examining the differences in EQ-5D-Y-3L health profiles and EQ VAS scores between self-complete and proxy reports using Chi-square tests for categorical data and t-tests or ANOVA for continuous EQ VAS scores, focusing on sociodemographic groups with known health disparities. Weighted kappa agreement coefficient and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) were employed to assess the level of agreement between self-complete and proxy versions of the EQ-5D-Y-3L, with subgroup analysis by sociodemographic characteristics like age and gender. The psychometric performance of both versions was evaluated in terms of convergent validity (correlation of EQ-5D-Y-3L index scores with EQ VAS scores using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient), and the responsiveness to health status changes over time. Responsiveness analysis was assessed at the dimension level using percent reduction of problems across each dimension. At individual level, the Paretian Classification of Health Change (PCHC) was employed. According to PCHC analysis, an EQ-5D-Y-3L health state is considered 'better' than another if it is better on at least one dimension and no worse on any other dimension. And an EQ-5D-Y-3L health state is considered 'worse' than another if it is worse in at least one dimension and no better in any other dimension. Furthermore, the change in EQ-5D-Y-3L index (computed using the Zimbabwe value set), and the EQ VAS scores were examined using a paired t-test analysis to assess the instruments performance in detecting change in health status. Participant characteristics o A total of 985 pediatric inpatients and their caregivers participated in baseline and follow-up interviews. 28 dyad responses were excluded due to incomplete data, resulting in analysis with 957 (97.1%) dyads. o Boys: 504 (52.7%), average age 10.7 years (SD = 4.4 years, range 4–18 years) o Girls: 453 (47.3%), average age 10.6 years (SD = 4.2 years, range 4–18 years) o Various health conditions identified including respiratory infections (e.g., pneumonia, pharyngitis), urinary tract conditions (e.g., UTIs, glomerulonephritis), neurological conditions (e.g., meningitis), vector-borne diseases (e.g., malaria), and nutritional problems (e.g., severe and moderate acute malnutrition). o Median length of hospital: 10 days (IQR 7-15 days), consistent for both boys and girls. o HRQoL problems increased with age, especially in dimensions of Looking After Myself (LAM), Doing Usual Activities (UA), Pain/Discomfort (PD), and Worried, Sad, or Unhappy (WSU). o Oldest age group (12-18) reported the highest frequency of problems in these dimensions. o Gender differences were not significant in most dimensions. o Family income significantly impacted emotional well-being, with children from families with inadequate income reporting more problems in the WSU dimension. o Urban versus rural residence showed a statistically significant difference in the mobility dimension, suggesting environmental factors may influence physical health components. Agreement between children/adolescents and caregiver reported measures o Significant trends noted in agreement over time across the EQ-5D-Y-3L dimensions, index, and EQ VAS scores:  Fair agreement on the EQ-5D-Y-3L dimension levels at baseline/admission and follow-up/discharge, with exception of poor agreement for WSU dimension at discharge.  Increase in exact agreement percentage observed from baseline/admission to follow-up/discharge.  Weighted kappa coefficients indicate consistent convergence over time. o Agreement level on index, and EQ VAS scores.  EQ-5D-Y-3L utility values and EQ VAS scores at admission and discharge within acceptable range.  EQ-5D-Y-3L index ICCs: 0.582 (admission), 0.498 (discharge).  EQ VAS scores ICCs: 0.671 (admission), 0.676 (discharge).  Gender-Based Analysis:  Boys exhibit higher ICC values compared to girls for both EQ-5D-Y-3L index and EQ VAS scores.  Boys: ICC values of 0.625 (admission), 0.493 (discharge) for index; ICC values of 0.689 (admission), 0.688 (discharge) for EQ VAS scores.  Age-Stratified Analysis:  Younger children (ages 4-6) and older adolescents (ages 16-18) showed lower ICC values, indicating lower agreement between child and caregiver reports.  Children aged 7 to 15 years exhibited higher ICC values, suggesting more consistent agreement between child and caregiver reports.  Residence-based comparisons:  Rural residents displayed slightly higher ICCs compared to urban counterparts, particularly at discharge, indicating more consistent health status reporting in rural settings. Convergent validity and responsiveness o Significant correlations observed between child/adolescent and caregiver reported EQ-5D-Y-3L index and EQ VAS scores at admission and discharge (p < 0.01).  Child/adolescent reports: correlation coefficient of 0.453 at admission, increasing to 0.514 at discharge.  Caregiver reports: correlation coefficients of 0.512 at admission and 0.520 at discharge. o Marked decrease in reported problems across all dimensions of EQ-5D-Y-3L from admission to discharge was observed for both child/adolescent and parent/caregiver reported measures. Dimensional improvements o Children/adolescents reported most pronounced improvement in 'Looking After Myself (LAM)' dimension (76.2% decrease), followed by 'Mobility (MO)' and 'Worried, Sad or Unhappy (WSU)' dimensions (75.2% and 75.4% reduction respectively), compared with caregiver reported outcomes. o Caregiver reports highlighted 'Worried, Sad or Unhappy (WSU)' dimension as most significantly improved (85.1% reduction), followed by 'Looking After Myself' (83.5%) and 'Mobility' (82.0%) dimensions. Paretian Classification of Health Change (PCHC) Analysis o Significant majority (91.4%) of children/adolescents demonstrated health improvement according to PCHC criteria in both measures, which was as expected as their treatment at the hospital was effective upon discharge.  Hence, instances of unchanged health status was rare (1.4% in children/adolescents' reports, 2.1% in caregivers' reports), and declines in health status were remarkably low (2.9% in children/adolescents' reports, 0.4% in caregivers' reports). EQ-5D-Y-3L Index and EQ VAS Scores o Substantial increments in the EQ-5D-Y-3L index scores from admission to follow-up for both measures. o Mean scores significantly increased for children/adolescents (from 0.422 to 0.810) and caregivers (from 0.350 to 0.844). o From admission to discharge, the EQ VAS scores also exhibited significant changes, increasing for both children/adolescents (from 36.2 to 72.1) and caregivers (from 36.3 to 79.5).
