Background:Although a vision bolt-on has been developed to enhance the sensitivity of the EQ-5D-5L in populations with visual impairment, evidence on its psychometric properties in relevant clinical populations remains limited. In particular, there is insufficient evidence on how well the bolt-on captures variation in vision-related health across different levels of impairment or between self- and proxy reports. Furthermore, uncertainties remain regarding the optimal wording (e.g., vision vs. seeing), the inclusion of visual aids (e.g., glasses or contact lenses), the use of examples or definitions to aid interpretation.Objectives:This study aims to evaluate the content and construct validity, known-group validity, responsiveness, test–retest reliability, and proxy–patient agreement of alternative versions of the vision bolt-on in patients with age-related macular degeneration (AMD), cataract, and visual complications of diabetes, supported by insights from caregivers and clinicians.Methods:A mixed-methods design will be employed. The qualitative phase will involve approximately 21 patients (7 for each condition), 21 caregivers (7 for each condition), and 7 clinicians, using the topic guide to explore interpretation, relevance, and preferences concerning wording, aids, examples. The quantitative phase will include about 300 patients (≈100 AMD, ≈100 cataract, ≈100 visual complications of diabetes) assessed at two time points. Caregivers will be invited to provide proxy assessments, enabling patient–proxy agreement analyses assessed. Data will be collected at two time points: before and after intervention (anti-VEGF therapy or cataract surgery); two visits for managing visual complications of diabetes. Psychometric evaluation will cover construct validity, known-group validity, responsiveness and proxy–patient agreement, using the Chinese NEI-VFQ-9 and clinical indicators such as visual acuity and contrast sensitivity.
