This project aims to co-develop and validate the Bangla version of the EuroQol Toddler and Infant Populations instrument (EQ-TIPS) for the first time in Bangladesh. In collaboration with the EuroQol Youth Working Group, the proposed study will adapt the EQ-TIPS-5L version to ensure cultural relevance and assess its psychometric performance in both healthy and ill children aged 2-4 years. In addition, caregivers' HRQoL will be measured using the EQ-5D-Y-5L and explore the relevance of potential sleep bolt-ons for improved content validity. The study will be prospective and longitudinal in design, with data collection conducted in collaboration with local partners: the International Centre for Diarrhoeal Disease Research, Bangladesh (icddr,b), and the ARK Foundation. Approximately 750 caregivers will be recruited, with 500 from hospital settings (ill children) and 250 from community settings (healthy children). The study will focus on two common infectious conditions: diarrhoea and pneumonia, in children under five. Recruitment will ensure a balanced representation across four age groups: 0-12 months, 13-24 months, 24-36 months, and 36-59 months, with at least 70 children per group to facilitate meaningful subgroup analyses. Quantitative data will include HRQoL measures and socio-demographic characteristics, while qualitative feedback will be gathered through Likert-scale ratings and open-ended questions. Psychometric properties will include feasibility (response rate, missing data, time taken), test-retest reliability, known-group validity (e.g., compare EQ-TIPS scores between children with mild versus severe cases), and responsiveness to clinical outcomes (comparing changes before and after clinical treatment). Data will be disaggregated by age groups, health status, and other relevant variables. Additionally, the spillover effect of the child’s health on the caregiver will be assessed using the EQ-5D-Y-5L with sleep bolt-ons. This study will provide critical insights into the psychometric properties of the Bangla EQ-TIPS and its potential for use in Bangladesh and similar contexts.
