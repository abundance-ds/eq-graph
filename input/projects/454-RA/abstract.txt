Background: Genomic testing is starting to be translated into clinical practice for people with rare genetic diseases. In England, the National Health Service launched a new Genomic Medicine Service. In Australia, Australian and Melbourne Genomics are supporting the Federal and State Governments in the translation of genomics. However, data on the outcomes of genomic testing is sparse, and there is uncertainty regarding the appropriate outcome measures to use. Data on the use of both established and new instruments, such as the EQ-5D-5L, EQ-HWB and EQ-5D-Y-5L, would inform more robust health technology assessment decisions in this context. Aims: Assess the psychometric properties of the EQ-5D-5L, EQ-HWB, and EQ-5D-Y-5L instruments in adult patients and parents of children with lived experience of rare genetic diseases in Australia and England using mixed methods. Methods: The project comprises three phases:  Assessment of content validity, face validity, and feasibility of various instruments using think-aloud interviews, with semi-structured follow-up discussions; Assessment of convergent and known group validity in a large baseline sample of participants; Assessment of responsiveness in a sample of participants who will undergo genomic testing, with or without subsequent medical treatment or gene therapy.  Recruitment for all phases will take place in both Australia and England, leveraging large established rare disease cohorts and patient support networks via our partner institutions. Deliverables: Study report presented to the EuroQol Descriptive System Working Group and EuroQoL Group; 2-3 articles for submission to academic journals; presentations/dissemination of findings through the iHEA Econ-Omics group and collaborating institutions.
