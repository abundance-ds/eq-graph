Background: Measuring health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in early childhood presents important methodological challenges due to rapid developmental changes, reliance on proxy respondents, and the limited availability of age-appropriate instruments. Within the EuroQol family of instruments, the EQ-5D-Y is currently recommended for children aged 4–18 years, while the new instrument being developed, the EQ-TIPS, is designed for children aged 0–4 years. However, the conceptual overlap and transition between these instruments in early childhood remain poorly understood. This gap is particularly relevant for children with severe health conditions whose developmental trajectories may extend the applicability of instruments designed for younger ages. Objectives: To explore  how primary caregivers of children aged 4–6 years with severe health conditions perceive the relevance of the EQ-TIPS and EQ-5D-Y descriptive systems for capturing key aspects of HRQoL,and to inform recommendations on  on which instrument is more appropriate at each developmental stage, particularly for children with severe health conditions. Methods: A qualitative study using cognitive interviews will be conducted with approximately 21–30 primary caregivers recruited from a tertiary pediatric hospital in Argentina. A purposive quota sampling strategy will ensure variation by age (4, 5, and 6 years) and condition (cerebral palsy, neuromuscular disorders, and oncological conditions). Participants will complete both instruments in randomized order using think-aloud techniques and targeted probing. Interviews will explore  instruments' relevance, comprehensiveness, and caregiver preferences, as well as which instrument is perceived as more relevant and suitable according to the child's health condition and developmental stage. Data will be audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, and analyzed using thematic content analysis with a directed coding approach informed by content validity frameworks. Expected Results and Impact: This study will generate novel evidence on how caregivers interpret and value HRQoL domains across early childhood in children with severe conditions, identifying gaps, overlaps, and factors influencing perceived relevance. Findings will directly inform EuroQol guidance on the upper age limit of EQ-TIPS and the lower age limit of EQ-5D-Y, particularly for children with severe health conditions. By providing evidence from a Spanish-speaking, low- and middle-income country context, this research will also contribute to improving the cross-cultural validity and global applicability of pediatric HRQoL instruments.
