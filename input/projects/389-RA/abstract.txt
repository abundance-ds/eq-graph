Background: Congenital heart disease (CHD) is one of the most common birth defects globally, with a long-term impact on health-related quality of life (HRQoL). Despite the great need, no validated generic preference-based HRQoL instruments are available for pediatric patients with CHD. Recently, EQ-5D-Y-5L was developed to reduce the ceiling effects and to enhance the sensitivity of EQ-5D-Y-3L. However, the evidence comparing the measurement properties of these two instruments is limited, with mixed findings. We aimed to compare the measurement properties of EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L in pediatric patients (age 8-18 years) with heart diseases. Methods: The study included a total of 221 pediatric outpatients with heart diseases from the two largest public hospitals in Singapore. Patients self-administered EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, and PedsQL Generic Core at baseline, 1 month (for test-rest reliability), and 12 months (for responsiveness) from the baseline. The convergent validity of baseline EQ-5D-Y-3L scores (utility index and sum score) and EQ-5D-Y-5L score (sum score) was evaluated with Spearman correlation (r) with PedsQL Generic Core total score. The discriminatory validity of the baseline scores was assessed by comparing the standardized effect size (SES) for the mean difference in scores between heart disease severity levels. Test-retest reliability between baseline and 1-month scores was evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Responsiveness of the scores was assessed using standardized response mean (SRM) separately for patients whose general health improved and worsened over 12 months. Results: Among 221 recruited patients, 188 and 169 patients completed 1-month and 12-month follow-ups. The mean age of the patients was 12.8 years, with a similar proportion of boys and girls (47.5% vs 52.5%), and their ethnic distribution was similar to the Singapore general population. The majority of the patients had congenital heart disease (85.1%), followed by heart failure (8.6%), and arrhythmia (8.6%). The distribution of patients’ disease severity was Level 1 (Mild): 38.5%, Level 2: 36.7%, and Level 3/4 (Severe): 24.9%. At the baseline, the ceiling effect was slightly lower in the EQ-5D-Y-5L score (47.5%) than in EQ-5D-Y-3L scores (49.3%). EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L scores were moderate-to-strongly correlated with the PedsQL Generic Core total score (r 0.55 to 0.60, each p0.65 for EQ-5D-Y-3L scores and >0.70 for the EQ-5D-Y-5L score. Among the improved cohorts based on general health, the EQ-5D-Y-5L score (SRM 0.50) appears to have higher responsiveness than EQ-5D-Y-3L scores (SRM 0.27 to 0.30). However, there was no meaningful change in both EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L scores for worsened cohorts (each SRM <0.05). Conclusions: Both EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L have acceptable and comparable measurement properties in terms of discriminative ability, convergent validity, and test-retest reliability to measure HRQoL among pediatric patients with heart diseases. EQ-5D-Y-5L has a slightly lower ceiling effect and slightly higher responsiveness to detect an improvement in health than EQ-5D-Y-3L. Further studies will be required to evaluate responsiveness among patients with deteriorating health over time.
