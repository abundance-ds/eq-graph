The global demographic shift towards an aging population poses significant challenges for healthcare systems, particularly as older adults often face complex health issues that impact their quality of life (QoL). Traditional measures like the EQ-5D-5L may overlook broader dimensions of well-being crucial for this demographic, which led the EuroQol Group to develop the EQ-HWB, to capture broader aspects of quality of life.  This study aims to evaluate and compare the content validity of the EQ-HWB-Short (EQ-HWB-S) and the EQ-5D-5L along with five bolt-ons (cognition, vision, hearing, tiredness, and social relationships) in community-dwelling older adults and those in assistive living environments. The study will explore three primary questions: (1) What HRQL and well-being domains are most important for older adults? (2) Which EQ instrument best captures these critical domains? (3) Are there significant domains inadequately addressed by existing measures? Utilizing a mixed methods approach, we will conduct semi-structured interviews using cognitive debriefing method, and following the COSMIN guidelines, with 30 older adults in the Edmonton area, Alberta, Canada. Participants will engage in open-ended discussions about their definitions of QoL and well-being. Following this, they will complete the EQ-HWB-S, the EQ-5D-5L and the 5 bolt-ons, and evaluate the relevance, importance, and comprehensibility (in terms of understanding and easiness to respond) of the items in each of the instruments as well as the bolt-ons. Finally, we will gather demographic and medical history information to contextualize responses. By evaluating the content validity of existing measures and identifying any potential gaps, this research seeks to inform the use of EuroQol instruments among older adults, and contribute to the development and/or modification of EuroQol instruments to better capture the multifaceted nature of health and well-being in older populations.
