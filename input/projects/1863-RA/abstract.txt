Background: Population ageing is a major public health concern. Preference-based instruments are widely used to assess health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and evaluate the cost-effectiveness of interventions. With multiple instruments available, it is important to identify the most suitable for older adults. This study systematically reviewed evidence on the psychometric properties of preference-based measures in older adults.Methods: Four databases (Medline, Embase, PsycINFO, CINAHL) were searched, supplemented by citation tracking. Peer-reviewed studies assessing the psychometric properties of preference-based measures in adults aged ≥60 were included. Two reviewers independently screened and extracted data. Methodological quality was assessed using the COSMIN risk of bias checklist, and evidence was graded using a modified GRADE approach.Results: A total of 106 studies met the criteria, covering EQ-5D-3L (48), EQ-5D-5L (34), ICECAP-O (29), ASCOT (15), QOL-ACC (9), HUI3 (8), HUI2 (7), QWB (5), EQ-HWB-9 (5), WOOP (3), SF-6D (3), AQoL-8D (2), AQoL-4D (1), and 15D (1). EQ-5D versions were most frequently evaluated and generally performed well, though ceiling effects, inter-rater reliability, and responsiveness issues were reported. Strong evidence supported ICECAP-O and ASCOT, but both need further testing on responsiveness. Limited and mixed findings were available for other measures. Early evidence for QOL-ACC and WOOP, both developed for older adults, was promising but incomplete.Conclusions: Preference-based measures are widely used with older adults, but psychometric evidence remains incomplete. Further research, particularly head-to-head comparisons using consistent methods, is needed to guide instrument selection in economic evaluations.
