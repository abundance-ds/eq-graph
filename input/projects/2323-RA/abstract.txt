Objectives: This study compared the psychometric performance of alternative item wording and presentation formats in the experimental EQ-HWB long form: 1) three different presentation formats for positively worded items; 2) positively worded (v1.1) items versus negatively worded counterparts (v1.2); 3) ‘hopelessness’ (v1.2) versus ‘nothing to look forward to’ (v1.1); and 4) ‘vision’ item with versus without reference to aids.Methods: Overall, 1,200 general-population adults completed the experimental modified EQ-HWB (v1.2) item-by-item with additional item variants and were randomized to three presentation formats for positively worded items: text warning, reversed response scale, or unmodified. Positively worded items (‘accepted’, ‘feeling good’, ‘do things one wanted to’) were compared with negatively worded counterparts (‘excluded’, ‘negative feelings’, ‘difficulties with enjoyable activities’). To identify response errors from negative-to-positive wording switches, positive-item responses were benchmarked against external measures (e.g., happiness). ‘Hopelessness’ was compared with ‘nothing to look forward to’, and vision items with and without reference to aids. Psychometric performance was assessed using distributional properties, and convergent and known-group validity analysis. Results: Response errors for positively worded items occurred across formats but were higher in the unmodified format (5.8-6.8%) than across alternative formats (3.3-4.5%). These items showed lower ceilings versus negatively worded counterparts and slightly improved instrument-level known-group validity. ‘Hopelessness’ showed stronger known-group validity than ‘nothing to look forward to’. Referencing aids slightly reduced reported difficulties, while both vision versions similarly distinguished those with chronic eye disease.Conclusions: Our findings can inform key design decisions on item wording and presentation in the EQ-HWB long form and should be considered alongside qualitative evidence from the literature.
