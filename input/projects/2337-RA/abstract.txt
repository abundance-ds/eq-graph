Background: The EuroQol Toddler and Infant Populations (EQ-TIPS ) is a proxy-completed, experimental measure developed by the EuroQol Group to measure and value health in children aged 0-3 years.Objective: To explore the effects of child age and task design on EQ-TIPS preferences.  More specifically, we will test the relevance of each EQ-TIPS attribute (Aim 1; indicating non-zero effects), whether these effects are consistent by child age (Aim 2; 1, 2, 3, and 4 years), and whether these effects remain stable by task design (Aim 3; paired comparisons and kaizen tasks).Methods: In this exploratory study, a nationally representative sample of 3,600 US adults (Dynata) will be recruited for an online survey (LimeSurvey) with geographic validation, automation detection, and attentiveness checks, followed by a latent-scale discrete choice experiment (DCE) with four arms (a child age 1, 2, 3, and 4 years; 900 respondents per arm). Under a shared base scenario (a 1-year episode), each DCE will include two sections in random order (14 paired comparisons and 14 kaizen tasks) and seven attributes taken from the EQ-TIPS version 3 descriptive system.  Each arm will have the same d-efficient experimental design (18 blocks; 50 respondents per block). For each arm and section, we will estimate conditional and mixed logit models on a latent scale (i.e., log odds)  and test the three hypotheses (Aims 1, 2, and 3) using cluster bootstrap techniques. We will also assess whether response burden and quality differ by child age and task design. The proposed study was developed in collaboration with the Younger Population Working Group and the Valuation Working Group. While the EQ-TIPS is under development, the preference evidence from this proposed study may inform and expedite its approval as well as aid in the design of future studies.
