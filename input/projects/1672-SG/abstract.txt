This research explores an empirical ethical framework for evaluating population health. We apply sequential stochastic dominance to compare population health distributions across five countries, using EQ-5D health-related quality of life data and life table mortality statistics. Sequential stochastic dominance relies on ethical principles — anonymity, Pareto efficiency,  diminishing positive welfare returns to quality of life that decline by age — without imposing restrictive functional forms. In comparing health distributions, it allows to take an ethical stance to age-prioritization of health improvements. Our findings show that sequential dominance provides a nuanced comparison of population health between countries, highlighting differences that are obscured by averages. The graphical exposition of sequential stochastic dominance offers a transparent and ethically grounded tool for health policy evaluation, showing why population health in one country is preferred. Sequential dominance therefore complements existing summary measures.
