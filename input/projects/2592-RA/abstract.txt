Background: Response scale heterogeneity (RSH) in the EQ-5D occurs when individuals systematically differ in how they interpret and use response categories, producing health self-assessments that are not comparable even when underlying health is equivalent. Qualitative evidence on the mechanisms underlying RSH remains sparse, and is virtually absent from low- and middle-income countries and non-English-speaking populations. Chronic conditions represent a particularly important context, as prolonged illness exposure and recalibration of internal standards can systematically alter how patients interpret response categories over time.Aim: This study aims to explore how patients with type 2 diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and heart failure in Argentina interpret the response categories of the validated Spanish-language EQ-5D-5L, and to identify the individual and illness-related factors — including disease severity, disease duration, and age — that contribute to heterogeneity in this interpretation.Methods: An interpretive qualitative design will be used, comprising individual semi-structured interviews (n = 30–36) integrating an illness narrative, concurrent think-aloud EQ-5D-5L completion, and anchoring vignette exploration. Purposive sampling will ensure variation in disease severity, duration, and age, with at least 40% of participants aged 65 or older. We will use thematic analysis with a hybrid a priori and inductive codebook, conduct a systematic comparison across conditions, severity strata, disease duration, and age as a central analytical objective.Expected contribution: This study will generate the first qualitative evidence on EQ-5D-5L response category interpretation from a Spanish-speaking, Latin American population, with direct implications for data comparability, instrument administration, and the generalisability of EQ-5D value sets.
