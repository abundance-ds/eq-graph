This research proposal investigates whether health state valuations should account for duration and sequence effects, rather than assuming values are independent of temporal context as current QALY calculations do. While standard practice values health states over fixed 10-year periods followed by death, real patient journeys involve dynamic health fluctuations, and evidence suggests people value health states differently depending on their duration and what follows them.The study proposes a mathematical framework where health state values depend on duration, sequence (improvement, deterioration, or death), severity, and potential interactions. The research comprises four stages: (1) developing a theoretical framework through systematic literature review, (2) conducting a discrete choice experiment across Netherlands and Australia to estimate temporal adjustment parameters, (3) validating findings against modified time trade-off studies, and (4) applying the framework to health economic models to assess impacts on cost-effectiveness analyses.This work aims to improve descriptive accuracy by capturing how people actually value health trajectories, while examining normative implications for resource allocation decisions. The framework could enable more realistic QALY calculations that better reflect preferences, though it may introduce challenges like path-dependent valuations requiring careful ethical consideration.
