This qualitative study assessed the content validity of the EQ-HWB instrument among informal carers of people living with dementia in Ireland. Seventeen family carers (13 current, 4 former) participated in cognitive interviews between May and July 2025, using think-aloud protocols and verbal probing to evaluate comprehension, relevance, and comprehensiveness of the 25-item EQ-HWB. A second phase tested 10 wording and format modifications.Results showed the EQ-HWB was generally answerable, though several challenges emerged. The most prevalent issue was perspective ambiguity; carers were uncertain whether items referred to their own health or the care recipient's, particularly for physical function items. Caregiving phase effects influenced responses, with former carers contrasting current answers against active caregiving experiences. Response mapping difficulties arose from double-barrelled phrasing and ambiguous severity quantifiers. While emotional items (loneliness, anxiety, frustration) were universally relevant, physical health items were often judged irrelevant by those in good health. Carers identified missing constructs including identity loss, anticipatory worry, and complex grief. Modification testing revealed strong preferences for less severe wording ("lacked control" over "no control"), frequency-based scales, and plainer language ("some" over "moderate").The EQ-HWB demonstrates promising content and face validity for dementia carers but would benefit from targeted refinements, for example adding perspective anchors to clarify self-report focus, addressing caregiving phase in instructions, reducing double-barrelled phrasing, refining response labels, and considering a caregiver-specific supplement to capture domains such as fatigue, autonomy constraints, and anticipatory worry. These findings support the use of EQ-HWB with dementia carers while highlighting the importance of population-specific adaptation to optimise measurement validity.
