Background:The EQ-5D-5L is a widely used measure of health-related quality of life; however, it may not fully capture the impact of vision problems on daily functioning and well-being. To address this limitation, vision and vision-related activities bolt-ons have been proposed to enhance its sensitivity in populations with visual impairment. Further work is needed to adapt these bolt-ons across different cultures, assess their relevance, comprehensiveness, and comprehensibility, and determine whether alternative wording (e.g., “vision” vs. “seeing”) affects their interpretation. Cultural adaptation is particularly important in Arabic-speaking settings, where language and context may influence how individuals perceive and report visual difficulties. Aim:This study aims to conduct cross-cultural adaptation and explore the content validity of vision bolt-ons from the EQ-5D Bolt-on Toolbox. Objectives include exploring wording variations, assessing cultural relevance, importance, comprehensiveness, comprehensibility and examining potential conceptual overlaps between these bolt-ons and the existing EQ-5D-5L dimensions. Methods:Vision bolt-ons (best available and exploratory) will first be translated following the EuroQol VMC translation and adaptation guidelines as two forward and two back translations, followed by cognitive debriefing interviews to establish consensus on conceptual and linguistic equivalence of the final Arabic version of the tested bolt-ons. Afterwards, semi-structured interviews will be conducted with adults diagnosed with cataract, age-related macular degeneration, or diabetic retinopathy. Using a think-aloud approach and retrospective probing, participants will complete the EQ-5D-5L and the four bolt-ons while providing feedback on clarity, relevance, comprehensiveness, and preferences for wording alternatives. All interviews will be recorded, transcribed, and analyzed using content and thematic analysis to identify key concepts, cultural relevance, and potential overlaps. Findings will inform final recommendations on item wording and the appropriate use of the bolt-ons in populations with visual impairment.
