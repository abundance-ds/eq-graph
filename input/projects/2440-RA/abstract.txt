The EuroQol Health and Wellbeing Full instrument (EQ-HWB) is an internationally developed instrument for evaluating the impact of health, public health and social care interventions on patients and carers.(1,2)  Whilst the wording of the EQ-HWB-9 short-form is now finalised, research is needed to evaluate the performance of the other 16 items and the proposed study aims to address this evidence gap.The Meditation Engagement Study (MES) study was undertaken by the Contemplative Studies Centre (CSC), University of Melbourne, and sought to measure engagement and utility in the use of mindfulness apps for addressing mental health concerns use for people who seek to improve their mental health.  In this study we included the modified and experimental versions of the EQ-HWB-9, randomised with more than 400 cases in each arm. We analysed this data with a EuroQol grant – 1872-RA and presented the findings at the EQ-HWB WG at the Vienna Plenary pre-meeting, thus contributing to the finalisation of the short form.In this study, we also included four extra hopelessness questions, the experimental and modified versions, plus a test item and an item from the WHOQol. An extra two hopelessness variables were contained in the SWEMWBS and K10 instruments, giving us 6 items suitable to measure the latent variable in IRT analysis. The study for which EuroQol funding is sought aims to examine the psychometric performance of the experimental and modified hopelessness questions. Specifically, we aim to conduct psychometric analysis on the experimental and modified hopelessness questions compared to 4 possible alternative questions, and conduct IRT and DIF analysis on the 6 questions.
