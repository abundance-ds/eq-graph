This study provided the first comprehensive psychometric evaluation of frequency versus severity response scales in pain and discomfort assessment using the EQ-HWB instrument. Analysis of data from 1,008 U.S. participants employed multiple complementary methods to examine scale properties and performance. Frequency scales demonstrated superior informativity (Pain: H'=2.28 vs H'=2.06; Discomfort: H'=2.18 vs H'=2.00) and better measurement across trait levels, while severity scales showed higher discrimination at upper trait levels (a=6.23 for pain severity). DIF analysis revealed significant scale differences for pain (ΔR²=0.24) but not discomfort (ΔR²=0.001), suggesting different measurement approaches may be needed for these constructs. Health condition-specific analyses showed distinct patterns of scale performance, with frequency scales being particularly informative for conditions characterized by recurring symptoms (e.g., immunologic conditions OR=3.21, 95% CI: 1.97-5.24). These findings support using frequency scales in shorter instruments like EQ-HWB-S, while suggesting both scale types provide valuable information in comprehensive assessment. The results offer important empirical evidence for response scale selection in health measurement instrument development and highlight opportunities for condition-specific measurement optimization.
