This project explored the use of the EQ-5D-Y as an outcome measure in pediatric gastroenterology, focusing on children and adolescents (8–15 years) with functional constipation (FC) and functional abdominal pain disorders (FAPDs), including irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and functional abdominal pain not otherwise specified (FAP-NOS). In a monocenter longitudinal pilot study, 141 patients were recruited and completed the EQ-5D-Y-3L, EQ-5D-Y-5L, and PedsQL™ 4.0 Generic Core Scales at two time points, three months apart. The study assessed the feasibility of longitudinal data collection and compared the psychometric performance of the EQ-5D-Y instruments with the PedsQL™. Of 141 included children, 104 (73.8%) completed the first survey and 85 (81.7%) completed both surveys. The mean age was 11 years, with 53% female; 66% had FC and 34% had FAPD. Both EQ-5D-Y versions and the PedsQL™ demonstrated good feasibility. Over 70% of children required caregiver assistance. At baseline, most children reported no problems with mobility and self-care, while limitations were more common in daily activities, pain, and emotions. Mean health scores were similar across instruments (EQ-5D-Y-3L: 72.2; EQ-5D-Y-5L: 72.8; PedsQL™: 69.2). Ceiling effects were prominent in the mobility domain for EQ-5D-Y, but minimal for PedsQL™. The EQ-5D-Y-5L showed higher discriminatory power than the 3L version, especially in pain and daily activities. Both EQ-5D-Y versions correlated moderately to strongly with PedsQL™ and negatively with clinical severity measures. PedsQL™ scores increased significantly over time, while changes in EQ-5D-Y scores were not statistically significant.Results demonstrated the feasibility of this pilot study, providing a foundation for larger-scale multicenter research and contributes to the evidence base for the use of EQ-5D-Y in pediatric populations with gastrointestinal disorders.
