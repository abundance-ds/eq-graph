Background: Health fluctuations affect the variability and reliability of outcome measurement. Recent evidence suggests that EQ-5D-5L's "TODAY" recall period may not capture recurrent health fluctuations, causing higher score variability and recall-period nonadherence when health fluctuates (Grand No: 244-2020RA). The EQ-HWB-9 uses a recall period of 7 days, potentially allowing better inclusion of health fluctuations, resulting in more robust results in fluctuating conditions, such as dementia diseases. Therefore, this study aimed to (i) evaluate if the EQ-HWB-9 with a "7 DAY" vs "TODAY" recall period captures better recurrent fluctuation with a higher recall period adherence in patients with dementia (PwD). This study also aimed to (ii) assess the face validity of the EQ Health and Wellbeing 9 (EQ-HWB-9) for use as a proxy by informal caregivers of community-dwelling people living with dementia (PlwD). It explored how caregivers understand and interpret the EQ-HWB-9 items when using two different recall periods (today vs 7-days).Methods: Within this mixed-method study, n=74 caregivers of PwD completed a daily diary for 14 days, documenting day-to-day health changes in PwD. The EQ-HWB-9 was administered as a patient self- and caregiver proxy-proxy rating on days one, seven and fourteen with the original recall period "7 days" (group 1, n=37 dyads) and an adapted recall period of "TODAY" (group 2, n=37 dyads). Subsequently, qualitative interviews were conducted with caregivers to determine whether recurrent fluctuations were considered in the EQ-HWB-S assessment of "TODAY's" health vs. "LAST 7 DAYS" health (recall period adherence), and to evaluate the appropriateness of the EQ-HWB-9. Interviews were then audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, and analyzed using qualitative content analysis. Based on the Level Sum Scores (LSS), both recall period versions were analyzed descriptively and in terms of variability (variance of LSS mean change, s2) considering the documented health fluctuations based on the daily diary entries (low fluctuation: 0-4 fluctuations in 14 days, moderate to high fluctuation: more than five fluctuations in 14 days). Results: Patients were, on average, 77 years old, more likely female (58%) and mildly to moderately cognitively impaired. (i, quantitative part) Fluctuations were reported for 92% of PlwD, with 20.2% of daily ratings indicating better and 19.1% worse health. Mean EQ-HWB-9 proxy ratings were higher compared to patient self-ratings (mean LSS day 1, 7 & 14: 20.7 vs. 14.6). Also, the "7-DAY" recall period had higher mean LSS (self: 15.4 vs. 13.9, proxy: 21.2 vs. 20.0) than the recall period of "TODAY", indicating worse health. Variance of LSS changes from day one to seven and seven to 14 was lower for the "7-DAY" recall period compared to the "TODAY" recall period (self: 5.5 vs. 10.8; proxy: 3.2 vs. 5.6), irrespectively of patients' health fluctuation (no-low: self-rating 1.1 vs 13.9; moderate-high: 6.8 vs 9.9). Interestingly, recall period adherence of caregivers was higher with the TODAY recall period (87%) than with the "7-DAY" recall period (76%), where 71% of non-adhered caregivers stated that the 7-day recall period was too long. (ii, qualitative part) Most caregivers found the EQ-HWB-9 acceptable, highlighting its broad health coverage, clear structure, and five-point response scale. Criticisms included the brevity and insufficient depth to capture the PlwD's health status fully. Caregivers tended to focus on observable aspects, such as daily activities, concentration, and mobility, while internal states, like loneliness, sadness, and anxiety, were more challenging to assess, often relying on indirect cues. The 7-day recall period was associated with lower acceptability and more frequent deviations from recall instructions compared to the "Today" recall. Overall, caregivers adhered well to the instructed proxy-proxy perspective but reported uncertainty in selecting the most appropriate responses.Conclusions: This study found that the "7-DAY" recall period of the EQ-HWB-9 may better capture recurrent health fluctuations through more stable scores. However, it was also associated with a higher rate of recall-period nonadherence. Conversely, although the "TODAY" recall period shows greater score variability, it may offer better adherence in practice, highlighting a critical trade-off between measurement stability and respondent adherence when selecting appropriate recall periods in fluctuating health conditions. The qualitative analyses demonstrated that the EQ-HWB-9 is generally acceptable and feasible for proxy assessments in PlwD. However, challenges regarding recall periods, proxy interpretation, and response certainty still require further investigation..
