Background: Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) like the EQ-5D-5L are increasingly used to monitor healthcare performance and inform clinical decision-making. However, longitudinal validity is often threatened by Response Shift and Response Scale Heterogeneity. This is especially critical in heart failure  patients, who undergo long-term adaptation to chronic symptoms. Beyond technical validity, this study views these phenomena as meaningful indicators of patient adaptation. Despite increasing theoretical recognition, empirical evidence examining these mechanisms remains limited, especially in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) such as Ethiopia, where sociocultural factors significantly influence health reporting during the transition from acute to chronic care.  Using a mixed-methods approach, this research combines psychometric modeling with qualitative inquiry to capture the psychological and social realities of patient adaptation in a resource-limited setting. Objective: To assess how response shift and response scale heterogeneity influence the longitudinal validity and clinical interpretation of EQ-5D-5L outcomes among Ethiopian heart failure patients transitioning from hospital discharge through the 6-month recovery phase. Methods: A prospective multi-center longitudinal observational cohort study will be conducted at Ayder, Aksum, and Adigrat University Comprehensive Specialized Hospitals. A sample of 300 adult patients will be recruited at discharge (T0) and followed at 3 months (T1) and 6 months (T2). Response shift will be analyzed using a combined framework of then-test assessments, longitudinal measurement invariance testing, and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), while scale heterogeneity will be evaluated via Latent Class Analysis (LCA). Clinical indicators and global rating of change measures will serve as external anchors to distinguish true health change from psychological adaptation and to provide a clinical context for score interpretation. A nested qualitative sub-study using cognitive and semi-structured interviews will triangulate these findings to explore the sociocultural drivers of health reconceptualization and response scale interpretation. All data will be captured electronically via KoboToolbox on encrypted tablets to ensure real-time validation and high data quality in a resource-limited setting.Expected Outcomes: This study will provide the first empirical quantification of response shift and scale heterogeneity in an Ethiopian HF cohort, assessing their impact on longitudinal EQ-5D-5L utility scores. By distinguishing true clinical change from measurement artifacts, and establishing how these shifts reflect patient adaptation, the findings will establish a methodological framework for the comprehensive interpretation of routine outcome monitoring. Ultimately, this research will provide the evidence-based guidance required to integrate the EQ-5D-5L into Ethiopia’s National non-communicable disease  Strategy, ensuring resource allocation reflects true health gains.Project duration (months):  24 months
