Respondents may make comparisons to various reference groups when self-reporting own health; for example, as a form of cognitive adaptation to the age-related negative physical changes, potentially leading to response heterogeneity. This project aims to investigate whether respondents tend to compare their health with their peers, e.g. others of the same age or social status (social comparisons) or their younger selves (temporal comparisons) when completing the EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS. Additionally, we will explore whether using an age- or self-comparative modified EQ-5D-5L and EQ VAS yields different outcomes. We will investigate whether social and temporal comparisons, as well as potential differences across standard and age- or self-comparative EQ-5D-5L, are associated with the respondents’ sociodemographic characteristics (e.g. age, gender, level of education), health-related characteristics (e.g. history of chronic disease) and self-esteem. The project involves conducting an online cross-sectional survey among a representative sample of 2000 members of the general population in Hungary. The project will contribute to the psychological literature on health measurement and inform future instrument development and refinement work on addressing unintended age- or self-referencing. The results may also shed light on the potential benefits of using age- or self-comparative instrument modifications in specific contexts or populations. Given that comparative health perceptions can influence health behaviour, including individual strategies for maintaining health and youthfulness, understanding routine comparisons made by respondents may also have public health implications.
