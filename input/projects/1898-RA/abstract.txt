To address the EuroQol strategic goals of understanding the measurement properties of its suite of instruments, increasing international relevance, and the call by the PHS WG to understand how these instruments can be used to assess health inequalities, we propose a **systematic literature review aiming to**: 1. systematically search and review published and grey literature on that used (approved) EuroQol suite of instruments to assess social inequalities in health; 2. evaluate how these instruments have been applied to assess health inequalities in both population and clinical settings; 3. synthesize the evidence on the sensitivity of these instruments for inequalities including potential differences across EQ-measures and socioeconomic indicators; and 4. (when feasible) conduct meta-analysis on particular inequality stratifiers. Understanding social inequalities in health is an important research topic and the EuroQol suite of instruments can play an important role as outcome measures. This review will provide the state of play of the use of these instruments in this field of research and identify knowledge gaps. Project results will include the existing evidence on the sensitivity of EQ-instruments to capture health inequalities across sociodemographic/-economic subgroups and identify populations (across and within cultures/countries) for which EQ-instruments have been used to address inequalities. Crucially, gaps in the existing literature will be identified. We will also assess the level of quality of existing published and grey literature. These results are relevant for the EuroQol Group as population level use of its instruments become more prominent (e.g. POPCORN and DAPHNIE studies).
