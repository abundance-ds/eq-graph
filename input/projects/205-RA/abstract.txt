Background: In EQ-5D valuation, time trade-off (TTO) is applied under strict assumptions about decision-making about health that are violated for many individuals, e.g. each year has the same value. Earlier work has shown that when these assumptions are relaxed, e.g. by applying cumulative prospect theory (CPT), TTO utilities are similar to those derived with standard gamble (SG). This work, referred to as the corrective approach, requires measuring or approximating differences between individuals’ decision-making for life duration, such as differences in sensitivity to losses and discounting life years. These CPT measurements are used to estimate parameters reflecting this loss aversion and discounting, used for ‘correcting’ TTO utilities Aim. When CPT parameters are used for correction, it is assumed that the decision-making processes they capture are also apparent in TTO. For example, for an elicited loss aversion parameter, which captures increased sensitivity to losses in life duration in CPT measurement, to be applicable to TTO, we should see similar sensitivity to giving up life duration in TTO. We aim to explore if this is true. Methods: We will study decision processes by using mouse tracking software. By tracking which information is studied and for how long, we can get insight into how people decide in TTO. Mouse tracking will be used when eliciting TTO utilities for 4 health states and CPT measurement. We also incorporate SG tasks for the same 4 health states, as this will allow us to evaluate the validity of corrected TTO utilities.
