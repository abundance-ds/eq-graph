Background: Informal caregivers provide essential support for people with chronic conditions, yet capturing their health and well-being in economic and outcomes research remains challenging. The EQ-HWB-9 is a recently developed instrument designed to measure broader health and well-being beyond traditional health-related quality of life. This study assessed the psychometric properties of the EQ-HWB-9 in informal caregivers and explored the performance of an EQ-HWB-9 visual analogue scale (VAS).Methods: An online survey was conducted among adults providing informal care to individuals with stroke, cancer, or dementia. Participants were recruited through an online panel and completed four instruments: EQ-HWB-9, EQ-5D-5L, CarerQol, and ASCOT-Carer. Baseline characteristics of caregivers, care recipients, and caring situations were collected. Psychometric evaluation included assessment of floor and ceiling effects, convergent and divergent validity, discriminatory ability using Shannon indices, known-group validity, and test–retest reliability. Exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses examined relationships among items across instruments. A subset of participants completed a follow-up survey after 14 days to assess reliability.Results: A total of 487 caregivers participated, with similar representation across the three caregiving groups. The mean EQ-HWB-9 utility score was 0.801, lower than EQ-5D-5L (0.907) but higher than ASCOT-Carer (0.791). Ceiling effects for EQ-HWB-9 (12%) were lower than those observed for EQ-5D-5L. EQ-HWB-9 scores were moderately correlated with other caregiver and health-related quality-of-life measures, supporting convergent validity. Factor analysis identified five underlying constructs relating to mental health, psychosocial well-being, physical functioning and pain, negative psychosocial health, and support and fulfilment. Known-group analysis showed lower well-being among caregivers providing more hours of care and those caring for individuals with greater disease severity. Test–retest reliability was acceptable (ICC=0.69), with moderate item-level agreement.Conclusions: The EQ-HWB-9 demonstrates acceptable validity, reliability, and discriminatory ability among informal caregivers and may provide a useful instrument for capturing broader caregiver well-being in research and evaluation.
