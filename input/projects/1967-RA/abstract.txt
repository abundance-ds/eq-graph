Purpose This study assessed the psychometric performance and response burden of five cognition bolt-ons for the EQ-5D-5L - one composite item (CO) and four components (Concentration (CC), Memory (ME), Confusion (CF) and Reasoning (RE)) in a U.S. general population sample. We aimed to determine whether specific cognition items or combinations offer meaningful psychometric improvements relative to added response burden. Methods A cross-sectional online survey collected data from 6,018 adults. Item-level properties included informativity (Shannon’s indices), ceiling reduction and divergent validity (polychoric correlations). Instrument-level properties assessed explanatory power (R² for EQ-VAS prediction) and dimensionality using exploratory factor analysis (EFA). Response burden was estimated from median item completion times. Bolt-ons were presented in fixed order after EQ-5D-5L dimensions. Results J' values ranged from 0.403 (RE) to 0.629 (ME), showing diverse response distributions. Cognition bolt-ons moderately correlated with EQ-5D-5L items (highest with Anxiety/Depression, r=0.722 for CC). R² values for EQ VAS prediction ranged from 0.0855 (RE) to 0.1575 (CC). Adding cognition items reduced the ceiling effect by up to 3.63% (ME-CC module). Median response times increased modestly: by 9 seconds for CO and around 13 seconds for ME-CC. EFA identified three factors, with cognition items clustering separately from physical and subjective health dimensions. Conclusion The ME-CC module demonstrated slightly better psychometric performance and ceiling reduction than the composite item, with minimal added burden. These findings support further qualitative and valuation research to refine cognition bolt-ons for integration into generic preference-based measures like the EQ-5D-5L.
