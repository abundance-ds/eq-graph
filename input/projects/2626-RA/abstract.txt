No changes to the abstract]Online health preference surveys using Discrete Choice Experiments (DCEs) are increasingly threatened by automated response systems. While traditional bots and browser add-ins have long posed a data quality risk, large language models (LLMs) now represent a qualitatively new category of threat: automated responders capable of generating contextually plausible, human-like responses to complex health scenarios in ways that standard detection systems such as reCAPTCHA are unlikely to identify. Unlike classical automated responders, which produce random noise or simple patterns, LLM-based systems process the semantic content of survey items and produce responses whose statistical properties may closely resemble those of genuine respondents. Two complementary categories of LLM-based tooling amplify this threat: AI coding assistants (e.g. Claude Code, OpenAI Codex) that enable rapid development of custom survey bots, and fully autonomous LLM-powered agent systems (e.g. OpenClaw, ClawdBot) that can complete surveys directly without custom development.This project aims to (1) characterise how currently available LLMs respond to DCE-based health valuation tasks and associated DAPHNIE background questions, and (2) evaluate candidate methods for detecting LLM-based automated respondents in online health preference surveys. This is not an investigation of AI preferences per se, but a methodological study to protect the integrity of online preference research.Methods include: development of one or more bespoke surveys on the EQ-VT platform with DCE valuation and DAPHNIE elements; deployment of multiple automated response systems (autonomous LLM agents, a bespoke LimeSurvey API-based responder, and non-LLM browser bots as a baseline); embedding of candidate detection mechanisms; and analysis of response patterns by system and model. Outputs are (a) a scientific paper characterising LLM response patterns across models and (b) a confidential report for the EuroQol Group on the effectiveness of specific detection strategies and recommended next steps. Together, these outputs will equip EuroQol with evidence to evaluate the growing threat from LLM-based automated responders and to guide investment in bespoke, durable countermeasures.
