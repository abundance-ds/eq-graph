Order bias in survey question sequencing can influence responses, potentially distorting data and limiting comparability across studies. The EQ-5D, a widely used health-related quality of life measure, traditionally places the EQ VAS after the descriptive system, a legacy design that may introduce context effects. With the EuroQol Group developing the EQ-5D Bolt-on Toolbox, the placement of bolt-on items relative to the EQ VAS remains unstandardized and underexplored. Limited evidence suggests that bolt-on positioning may affect EQ VAS scores, yet systematic investigation is lacking. This study aims to address this gap by examining order effects in a general population sample. We propose a cross-sectional study in China with 1,200 participants, randomized into four arms with varying sequences of the EQ-5D-5L descriptive system, EQ VAS, and three bolt-on items (cognition, fatigue, vision). Participants will be recruited via a survey company’s online panel, with soft quotas ensuring demographic representativeness. Data quality will be ensured through CAPTCHA, time analysis, and cross-validation. EQ VAS score differences across arms will be analyzed using ANOVA, ANCOVA, and regression models, with sensitivity analyses to assess robustness. This study will provide evidence on whether bolt-on placement impacts EQ VAS scores, offering initial evidence for optimal sequencing. Findings will inform recommendations for the EQ-5D Bolt-on Toolbox user guide, enhancing data consistency and comparability. By funding this project, the EuroQol Group will gain critical insights into order bias, supporting the strategic development and standardization of bolt-ons as outlined in the 2024-28 Strategic Plan, ultimately strengthening the utility and reliability of EuroQol instruments.
