The existence of socioeconomic inequalities in health and health related quality of life is well known. EuroQol instruments are increasingly being implemented in several large epidemiological, household and cohort studies. Several studies have shown that the EuroQol instruments are sensitive to detect socioeconomic inequalities in health. The concentration index (CI) based indices are a popular suite of methods in measuring socioeconomic inequalities in health, with a variety of indices available depending on the measurement properties of the health variable and the ethical response to inequality. To date, there has been relatively limited use of the CI in relation to the EuroQol instruments, even less so using CI based measures alongside simpler measures of inequality. Using data from UK Household Longitudinal Survey, this project has several aims: a) to use the CI based indices to measure the level of socioeconomic inequality in the EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L in the UK, b) to compare the results from the different versions of CI, c) to decompose the inequalities into its contributing explanatory factors. This will provide insight into how different CI variants respond to inequalities measured using EuroQol instruments, and whether they provide additional insights into inequalities.
