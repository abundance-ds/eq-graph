Informal caregiving constitutes a cornerstone of long-term care systems, particularly in Germany, where millions of individuals provide unpaid care with substantial implications for caregivers’ health and wellbeing. The EQ Health and Wellbeing (EQ-HWB) instruments have been developed to capture broader aspects of quality of life across health, social care, and caregiving contexts. However, unlike other EuroQol instruments, the EQ-HWB currently lacks a visual analogue scale (VAS), and the measurement properties of a proposed EQ-HWB VAS remain insufficiently understood, particularly among informal caregivers.This study aims to evaluate the psychometric performance of a proposed EQ-HWB VAS using a ‘health and wellbeing’ frame with a 7-day recall period in a German caregiver population, while examining the impact of key design features, namely the inclusion of a preamble and no specified recall period (as opposed to the default 7-days). A cross-sectional online survey will be conducted among approximately 600 informal caregivers recruited via a panel provider, using a two-arm design with stratified randomisation by caregiving intensity. Participants will complete the EQ-HWB-9 descriptive system followed by two EQ-HWB VAS variants, alongside established caregiver-relevant measures (ASCOT-Carer, ICECAP-A, CarerQol).Analyses will assess distributional characteristics, convergent and known-groups validity, and the extent to which EQ-HWB VAS scores reflect the EQ-HWB-9 descriptive system. Multivariable regression models, including within-subject comparisons and pooled analyses with interaction terms, will be used to evaluate the independent and combined effects of design features. Secondary analyses will examine the psychometric performance of the EQ-HWB-9 and explore conceptual overlap with external measures using exploratory factor analysis.The study will provide novel evidence on the validity and interpretation of an EQ-HWB VAS and inform its design and integration into the EQ-HWB framework, supporting its use in caregiver populations and health technology assessment contexts.
