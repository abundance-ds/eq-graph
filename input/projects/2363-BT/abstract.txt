BackgroundCognitive impairment is common after stroke and substantially affects patients’ independence and quality of life. While the EQ-5D-5L is widely used for assessing health-related quality of life (HRQoL), it does not explicitly capture cognitive functioning, sleep disturbance, and fatigue. The recently developed global cognition bolt-on, along with sleep and fatigue items, may enhance measurement precision in this population. However, stroke often limits patients’ ability to self-report, necessitating proxy assessment. To date, no study has compared patient and proxy reporting using the EQ-5D-5L with the cognition and additional bolt-ons in stroke.Objective This study aims to quantify agreement and systematic differences between patient and proxy assessments of health-related quality of life measured using the EQ-5D-5L with the cognition, sleep, and fatigue bolt-ons, recognising that proxy ratings represent a distinct but complementary perspective. The secondary aim is to validate the bolt-ons by rater-type (self vs proxy).Methods Patients will complete the EQ‑5D‑5L with cognition, sleep, and fatigue bolt‑ons, while proxies will complete the proxy–proxy perspective (their own judgement of the patient’s HRQoL). Agreement will be assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) for continuous outcomes, weighted Cohen’s κ for dimension‑level agreement, Bland–Altman plots for summary scores, correlations, and mean absolute error (MAE). Differences in mean Level Sum Scores (LSS) will be examined. Ceiling and floor effects will be evaluated. Exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses will be conducted on the core EQ‑5D‑5L dimensions and bolt‑on items to assess construct validity.Expected outcomesThe study will provide the first evidence on the reliability and validity of proxy-reported EQ-5D-5L instruments incorporating cognition, sleep, and fatigue bolt-ons in stroke. Findings will establish baseline agreement between patient and proxy HRQoL assessments and evaluate the added value of these bolt‑ons for capturing broader aspects of post‑stroke HRQoL.ImpactResults will guide EuroQol’s further development of the cognition, sleep, and fatigue bolt-ons; strengthen their applicability in clinical and health technology assessment settings; and enhance the precision and comprehensiveness of HRQoL measurement in stroke and other conditions associated with cognitive or communication impairment.
