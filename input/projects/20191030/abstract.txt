Systematic differences in the ways that individuals interpret response categories may introduce bias when analysing self-reports to compare health or quality-of-life measures across heterogeneous patient and population groups. Inter-group comparisons using these self-reported measures which experience such variation may bring into question any resulting findings. Self-reported health measures can also be affected by response style; a respondent’s tendency to systematically respond to questionnaire items in a given way regardless of item content. Given the increased interest in the routine collection of self-reported health measures, such as the EQ-5D in PROMs programmes, registries, cohort studies and national surveys, there is value in understanding whether, and to what extent, the EQ-5D suffers from such response heterogeneity/bias. Moreover, such biases may vary widely across response items from the same individual, due to question wording, for example. This study developed open-source Julia package (MIOP.jl), which we have applied to the Multi-Instrument Comparison (MIC) data to evaluate response heterogeneity. The package MIOP.jl has been designed to estimate extensions of the generic inflation model, which consists of a single binary splitting equation – which in this context corresponds to mis-reporting – and then both inflation (into one, all, or a subset of the observed outcomes) and true health equation. In this way, the approach allows observed outcomes to arise from two sources: mis-reported and true. This nests the usual zero-and middle-inflated ordered probit models. These can be estimated on both cross-sectional and/or longitudinal data. Importantly such a range of bespoke models is not currently available in any other statistical software. In developing the package and applying it to the MIC data we are able to showcase the use of open-source software to explore response heterogeneity in EQ-5D self-reports.
