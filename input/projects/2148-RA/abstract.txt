Background: Engaging and involving key collaborators beyond the research team is viewed as increasingly important in health research and practice. In health state valuation, non-researcher collaborators may include patients, members of the public, caregivers, clinical experts, and decision-makers, amongst others. Several benefits associated with collaborative involvement include enhanced outputs, increased buy-in from affiliated parties, and superior research impact. Best practice guidance on how to effectively involve and engage collaborators in health state valuation studies and to comprehensively and transparently report this process is lacking. Aim: Developing best practice methods guidelines is iterative. This project represents ‘Phase 2’ (building on project 1711-RA) and aims to develop guidelines for conducting and reporting collaborative involvement (CI) in health state valuation studies, as an extension to the EuroQol-funded Reporting Valuation Studies of Multi-Attribute Utility-Based Instruments (CREATE) reporting checklist (i.e., CREATE-CI). Methods: A three-stage, consensus-building methodology is proposed. First, a targeted review of associated literature (e.g., in comparative effectiveness research) will be combined with a framework of best practices for collaborative involvement derived from review project 1711-RA and draft recommendations developed. Second, focus groups will be held with EuroQol-affiliated experts, non-EuroQol experts, and collaborator representatives to refine the draft recommendations. Third, a modified international Delphi survey will be conducted to derive consensus on the guidelines. The guidelines will be finalised by a development committee, who will have oversight of the guideline development process. Key outputs include the CREATE-CI reporting guidelines and an aligned manuscript detailing best practice in collaborative involvement in health state valuation.
