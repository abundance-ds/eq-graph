Aim: Given the substantial disease burden among Egyptian children under five—particularly hematologic malignancies, this study aims to fill critical gaps in the measurement of health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in early childhood. This involves the translation and cultural adaptation of the Arabic 5-level version of the EuroQol Toddler and Infant Populations instrument (EQ-TIPS-5L) for Egypt and conduct a comprehensive psychometric validation of EQ-TIPS-5L and three Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL) scales in children aged 0–4 years. In addition, caregiver spillover will be assessed using EQ-HWB-9 EMV2.1 and WHO-5.Methods: Translation and cultural adaptation will follow EuroQol Research Foundation guidelines, including forward–backward translation, consensus meetings, cognitive debriefing with 8 caregivers, then face validity testing in a 24 caregivers using QQ-10 questionnaire.  A longitudinal psychometric validation study will be conducted in line with COSMIN standards. The sample will include 200 caregivers of children aged 0–4 years: 100 with malignancies (like acute lymphoblastic leukemia) and 100 healthy controls. Participants will be stratified  by severity. Instruments administered at baseline and at least two-week follow-up will include EQ-TIPS-5L, PedsQL generic modules, along with global health ratings to assess test-retest reliability. Additionally, the spillover effect of the child’s health on the caregiver will be assessed using EQ-HWB-9 EMV2.1 and World Health Organization Five-Item Well-Being instrument, WHO-5. Psychometric evaluation will cover feasibility, ceiling and floor effects, test-retest reliability, known-groups validity, and explanatory power. Data will be disaggregated by age groups, health status, and other relevant variables. The findings will generate robust psychometric evidence for two key EQ instruments and support their wider application in Egypt and the broader MENA region.
