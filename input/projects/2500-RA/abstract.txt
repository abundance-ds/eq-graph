Background: Health literacy refers to individuals’ capacity to read, evaluate, and use health-related information and services. Low health literacy has been associated with poorer health behaviors, worse health and well-being outcomes, and increased mortality risk. Previous studies have examined associations between health literacy and health-related quality of life measured using the EQ-5D-5L, but much of the evidence focuses on overall scores and is based on modest sample sizes. To date, no studies have explored associations with outcomes measured using the new EQ-HWB-9 instrument, which was developed for populations in which the assessment of well-being in addition to health is relevant. Assessing whether health literacy is associated with reported outcomes may inform interpretation and support measurement validity.Objectives: This study aims to examine the associations between health literacy and outcomes measured by the EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9 in a general population sample.Methods:  The study will use data from a cross-sectional online survey conducted in 2025 in a nationally representative sample of 2,000 Hungarian adults. Respondents completed the EQ-5D-5L, EQ-HWB-9, and the four-item Brief Health Literacy Screening Tool (BRIEF). Associations of BRIEF items and total scores with selected EQ-5D-5L dimensions and EQ-HWB-9 items will be examined using Spearman correlations and partial proportional odds ordinal logistic regression. Multivariable linear regression models will be used to assess whether health literacy level predicts differences in EQ-5D-5L values, EQ VAS, and EQ-HWB-9 scores. All regression models will be adjusted for socio-demographic (e.g., age and education level) and health-related covariates (e.g., presence of chronic condition).
