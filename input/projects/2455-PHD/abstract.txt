Background: Early childhood (0–47 months) is a critical period when illness disrupts everyday functioning, such as movement, play, feeding, interacting with others, and managing emotions, yet these outcomes are seldom measured in routine care in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Existing instruments provide valuable clinical information but often lack preference-based utility and robust LMIC validation. EQ-TIPS, EuroQol’s proxy-reported measure for infants and toddlers offers a life-course link to the EQ family but requires cultural adaptation and empirical testing in Indonesia. Parental spillover from child illness is also undermeasured despite its policy relevance.Aim: To adapt, validate, and implement EQ-TIPS for Indonesian infants and toddlers and to quantify caregiver spillover by linking child HRQoL (EQ-TIPS) with caregiver well-being (EQ-HWB-9), thereby extending EuroQol’s life-course measurement framework to early childhood in an LMIC.Methods: Five linked studies will be conducted. (1) An evidence map and COSMIN-based systematic review will profile instruments for ages 0–47 months and align domains with EQ-TIPS. (2) Cross-cultural adaptation of EQ-TIPS to Bahasa Indonesia will follow EuroQol procedures (forward–backward translation, expert review, cognitive debriefing). (3) Qualitative content-validity interviews with caregivers and health professionals will assess relevance, comprehensibility, and comprehensiveness. (4) Psychometric testing of the Indonesian EQ-TIPS will use a cross-sectional and short-term longitudinal design (baseline and 7–14-day retest) to examine distributional properties, reliability (ICC), construct and known-group validity, and responsiveness. (5) Parental spillover will be quantified via correlations, multivariable regression, known-groups comparisons, test–retest reliability, and responsiveness linking EQ-TIPS with EQ-HWB-9.
