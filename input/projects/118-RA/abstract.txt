An effective vaccine is a key strategy for ending the COVID-19 pandemic, but even when vaccine is found and approved for use, most countries will have insufficient initial stockpiles to cover all those who would ideally be vaccinated. Consequently, choices will need to be made as to which groups are prioritised to receive a vaccine. One important criterion is the maximisation of individual health benefits. Evidence that allows decision makers to employ QALYs alongside other metrics (e.g. lives saved) when making difficult resource allocation decisions, such as prioritization of a COVID-19 vaccine, is crucial. Nested into a wider multi-country project to collate and elicit public preferences for prioritising access to a COVID-19 vaccine in up to 12 countries around the world, this project will focus on a key component of the QALY, which is to have contemporary values for population norms that reflect the impact of COVID-19 (e.g. the current social distancing measures) on aspects of health-related quality of life. We will assess the current and pre-COVID-19 health status of individuals using the EQ-5D and explore how health-related quality of life can be used as a metric for the prioritisation of vaccination for COVID-19. These will be achieved through primary data collection and appropriate statistical methods (before/after methods, pooled-data/ panel regression analysis; choice models) of international community survey data (including a choice experiment to identify public preferences for vaccine prioritisation).
