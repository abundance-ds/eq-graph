This study explored the face validity and acceptability of the EQ-HWB-9 when used by informal caregivers as a proxy measure for community-dwelling people living with dementia (PlwD). Conducted as a collaboration between Monash University and the German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases, and building on the EuroQol-funded project “Reliability of the EQ-HWB-S when health fluctuates,” (1592-RA) the project used qualitative data from 73 interviews with caregivers in Germany. The analysis of the qualitative data was done during a research stay at Monash University (Health Economics Group, School of Public Health and Preventive Medicine) from April to May 2025.Informal caregivers and their relatives with a form of dementia were randomly assigned to complete the EQ-HWB-9 using either a “today” or “7-day” recall period at three time points across 14 days. Afterwards, qualitative interviews with the caregivers were conducted to evaluate the appropriateness and usability of the EQ-HWB-9. Interviews were transcribed and analyzed using a qualitative content analysis approach. Most caregivers found the instrument acceptable due to its clarity and broad health coverage. However, limitations were noted in its ability to capture internal emotional states and in the depth of assessment. The “7-day” recall period was associated with reduced adherence to the recall instruction compared to the “today” version.Findings suggest that the EQ-HWB-9 shows promise for proxy use in PlwD, though challenges remain regarding recall periods, proxy interpretation, and caregiver confidence in response selection. These insights contribute to improving the instrument's future application.
