Kenya’s recent health financing reforms necessitate locally relevant health economic evidence to inform evidence-based decision-making processes in healthcare. This study developed Kenya's first EQ5D5L value set, based on the health preferences of its own population, to support economic evaluation and priority setting in healthcare.The study targeted a sample of 1,100 participants who were aged 18 or over, consented to participate, and were proficient in English or Swahili. A stratified random sampling approach selected 10 counties for a nationally representative sample. Data were gathered through computer-assisted, face-to-face interviews using laptops running EuroQol Portable Valuation Technology (EQ-PVT) software version 2.1.  Each respondent completed 10 cTTO and 12 DCE tasks. A cyclic quality-control process was conducted throughout fieldwork.Nine models were estimated, including hybrid models that account for left censoring at full health and for heteroskedasticity. A hybrid heteroskedastic Tobit model was selected as the final value set on the basis of logical consistency, statistical significance, and goodness-of-fit. The final model produced logically ordered and significant disutility coefficients for all levels. Based on the model reported in a manuscript currently under review, the predicted utility for full health (11111) was anchored at 1.00, while the worst state (55555) yielded a utility of -0.532. Pain/discomfort level 5 had the largest decrement (0.361), followed by mobility (0.347). Of the predicted 3,125 values of the EQ-5D-5L, 8.54% had negative values. Scatterplots confirmed strong agreement between observed and predicted values, supporting model validity.This is Kenya's first nationally representative EQ-5D-5L value set. The set provides a locally grounded input for cost-effectiveness analysis and strengthens the evidence base for health priority-setting in Kenya.
