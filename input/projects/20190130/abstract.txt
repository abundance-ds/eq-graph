The EQ-5D-5L uses a “today” recall period, which may not fully capture health-related quality of life (HRQOL) for conditions with fluctuating symptoms. This study examined the performance of the EQ-5D-5L across different chronic diseases—tuberculosis (TB), type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), HIV/AIDS—and periodic attack illnesses such as asthma and chronic skin disorders (CSD), while assessing how alternative recall periods influence HRQOL reporting.A total of 205 adult patients from hospitals in Bandung, Indonesia, completed up to three data collection sessions involving the EQ-5D-5L with varied recall periods (“today,” “past week,” “past month,” and “last time experiencing the illness”), disease-specific HRQOL questionnaires, and a General State of Health (GSH) question. Analyses included paired statistical tests, effect sizes, Gwet’s AC1, Bland–Altman plots, and correlations between EQ-5D-5L and disease-specific scores.Feasibility was excellent, with no missing responses. For TB, T2DM, and HIV/AIDS, EQ-5D-5L scores using “today” were generally stable across one month, particularly among participants reporting no change in health. Agreement coefficients showed substantial to almost-perfect concordance across sessions, and “past month” recall produced scores comparable to those collected one month prior. These findings suggest that the “today” recall period is adequate for stable chronic conditions.In contrast, patients with asthma and CSD showed significantly worse EQ-5D-5L scores when recalling their most recent illness episode compared with their “today” scores (p < 0.001 for EQ-VAS, utility, and LSS). This indicates that the standard recall period underestimates disease burden in episodic conditions. Correlations between EQ-5D-5L outcomes and disease-specific measures were significant across most groups and recall variations, supporting convergent validity.Patients with chronic conditions generally preferred the “today” recall period, while those with episodic illnesses demonstrated mixed preferences between “today” and “last illness experience.”Overall, the EQ-5D-5L performs reliably in stable chronic diseases but may miss important symptom variability in periodic attack illnesses, underscoring the need to consider disease trajectory when selecting or modifying recall periods.
