Background: The EQ Health and Well-being (EQ-HWB) and its shorter version, EQ-HWB-S, are newly developed tools for assessing health and well-being across health, social care, and public services. They provide a more comprehensive evaluation of quality of life (QOL) by capturing physical, emotional, cognitive, and social dimensions. With rapidly aging populations in China, Indonesia, and Hong Kong, reliable tools are needed to address the growing health and social care demands of older adults. However, further validation is required to determine the feasibility, validity, and responsiveness of these instruments in aged care settings. Objectives: This study aims to assess the feasibility of EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S, evaluate the validity and reliability of EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S, and assess responsiveness to detect meaningful changes in health and well-being over time. Methods: The study will involve dyads of elderly residents of aged care facilities and recipients of home care services, along with their caregivers in China, Hong Kong, and Indonesia, with a total of 450 participants (150 dyads from each country). Participants will be aged 65 and above, representing a range of chronic conditions and cognitive abilities. Data collection will combine quantitative and qualitative methods. It will be collected through self-report, proxy, and interviewer-administered methods. Follow-up data will be gathered from the same cohort, with 600 participants (including proxies) invited to complete a follow-up survey one to two weeks after the initial survey to measure responsiveness. Data analysis will assess (1) Feasibility by comparing refuse to participate rate, drop-out rate and completion rates, and identifying missing data patterns, (2) Validity and reliability, using correlation analysis, Cronbach’s alpha, and intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC), (3) Responsiveness by tracking changes in EQ-HWB scores over time and correlating them with clinical outcomes, and (4) Known-group comparisons by evaluating the instruments’ ability to distinguish between participants with varying health conditions. Qualitative feedback will be gathered from 20 participants from each country through semi-structured interviews to explore the feasibility and perceived relevance of the instruments.
