Background: The development of the EQ-5D-Y-5L (Y-5L) has created a demand for mapping the EQ-5D-Y-3L (Y-3L) values to the Y-5L while the international protocol for valuation of the EQ-5D-Y-5L is developed. One of the challenges identified with mapping is having sufficient data across the severity spectrum. This study aimed to recruit patients, and their caregivers attending specialist health services, with presumed severe health profiles, to contribute paired Y-3L and Y-5L responses for the purposes of mapping. The secondary aim was to assess the psychometric performance of the Y-3L, Y-5L and EQ-TIPS in this cohort of children. Methods: Caregivers of children aged 0-18 years, were recruited from specialist outpatient clinics at Red Cross War Memorial Children’s Hospital in South Africa. The performance of the EQ-TIPS and EQ-5D-Y 3L and 5L versions was determined by comparing feasibility with the absolute reduction in the ceiling effect (111111). Discriminatory power was evaluated with Shannon’s H’ index for absolute informativity and Shannon’s J’ index for relative informativity or spread of responses. The redistribution of dimension responses between the 5L and 3L versions was evaluated for inconsistency. If a 5L response is two or more levels removed from a 3L response, that response pair is classified as inconsistent. Known group severity was calculated for the 5L and 3L LSS scores according to severity groupings by PedsQL total score (≤74.2) and EQ VAS (≤80). Results: EQ-TIPS: Data from 176 children aged 0-4 years were included, with a median age of 29 months and more males (n = 95, 54%). The majority of respondents were mothers (n = 137, 81%). Children were grouped by disease category: epilepsy (n = 15, 9%), neuromuscular disease (n = 13 ,7%), chronic gastrointestinal disease (n = 19, 11%), renal disease (n = 48, 27%) and oncological or haematological diseases (n = 72, 41%). The ceiling effect (111111) decreased minimally by 4% from the 3L to 5L. Absolute informativity (H’) of dimensions improved by 0.115 on the 5L, with retention of the spread of responses for all dimensions. Convergent validity was similar, with strong correlations on paired 3L and 5L dimensions. The 3L and 5L were similarly able to significantly differentiate between known groups based on PedsQL and EQ VAS severity cut-offs. EQ-5D-Y: Proxy report data from 328 children aged 5-18 years were included, with a mean (SD) age of 9.8 (4.3) years and more males (n=173, 53%). The majority of mothers were mothers (n=245, 75%). Children were grouped by disease category: epilepsy (n = 55, 17%), neuromuscular disease (n = 59, 18%), chronic gastrointestinal disease (n = 36, 11%), renal disease (n = 73, 22%) and oncological or haematological diseases (n = 105, 32%). The ceiling effect (111111) decreased by 7% from the 3L to 5L. Absolute informativity (H’) of dimensions improved by 0.05 on the 5L, with the greatest improvement in pain/discomfort, with retention of the spread of responses for all dimensions. The 3L and 5L were similarly able to significantly differentiate between known groups based on PedsQL and EQ VAS severity cut-offs. Conclusion: The five-level versions of the EQ-TIPS and EQ-5D-Y have similar or superior performance to the three-level version.
