Introduction: Retrospective assessment can be influenced by the measurement scale, the measurement schedule, and the presence of a substantial health event. Objectives: To assess the correspondence between directly reported and recalled HRQoL in Diabetic Ketoacidosis patients, based on responses from both patients and their proxies. Methods: A total of 388 DKA patients and their proxies were enrolled from April to July 2025. The Amharic EQ-5D-5L was used to assess HRQoL via postal surveys and follow-up phone calls. We employed paired t-tests, Intraclass Correlation Coefficients (ICCs), and Bland–Altman plots to analyze the agreement between the scores reported directly and those recalled at different time points: T1–T2, T1–T3, and T2–T3 for both groups. The proposal received ethical approval from the Mekelle University Institutional Review Board under approval number MU-IRB 2034/2023. Results: For patients, directly reported mean EQ-5D utility at T1 was 0.525, while recalled utility values were 0.479 at T2 (p 0.002) and 0.464 at T3 (p 0.001). Directly reported utility at T2 was 0.821 vs. 0.836 when recalled at T3 (p 0.061). For proxies, directly reported utility at T1 was 0.513 vs. 0.469 (p 0.009) and 0.449 (p 0.001) when recalled at T2 and T3, respectively; T2 utility was 0.801 vs. 0.816 recalled at T3 (p 0.035). Patients' mean EQ-VAS at T1 was 61.5, dropping to 58.5 and 55.8 when recalled at T2 and T3 (both p 0.001); T2 EQ-VAS was 74.7 vs. 75.9 when recalled at T3 (p 0.001). Proxies reported 64.8 at T1, with recall values of 58.0 and 56.18 (both p 0.001); T2 EQ-VAS was 73.57 vs. 74.58 at T3 (p 0.001). Discrepancies between directly reported and recalled values increased over time. Despite significant differences in t-tests, ICCs indicated good agreement (ICC > 0.75) for both EQ-5D utility and EQ-VAS across patients and proxies, as further supported by Bland-Altman plots. Agreement was weakest among patients with mild DKA and frequent DKA, whereas strongest among those with tertiary education. The anxiety/depression and pain/discomfort dimensions showed the lowest agreement for both EQ-5D utility and EQ-VAS across patients and their proxies. Conclusion: Despite significant mean differences, high ICCs suggest good relative agreement between directly reported and recalled EQ-5D utility and EQ-VAS scores across patients and proxies. Thus, recalled scores may not replace direct reports for individual use but are suitable for retrospective group-level analyses when prospective data are lacking.
