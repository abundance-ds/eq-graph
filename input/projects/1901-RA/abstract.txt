The psoriasis bolt-on – a disease-specific EQ-5D-5L measure which includes two additional dimensions of skin irritation and self-confidence – is a leading candidate to become an approved instrument and the first EuroQol endorsed bolt-on. We propose this project to support the advancement of the psoriasis bolt-on by addressing some crucial research gaps including the instrument’s responsiveness, potential conceptual overlap with core EQ-5D dimensions, performance across a range of languages and information important for non-preference based scoring.  Leveraging secondary data from clinical trials of psoriasis patients, we aim to:  examine the psychometric properties of the psoriasis bolt-on, with a focus on responsiveness, and comparison to the core EQ-5D-5L; investigate the structure of the psoriasis bolt-on items a) in relationship to other generic and skin-specific health measures (to assess item performance and construct validity), as well as b) in relationship to the EQ-5D-5L (to examine potential conceptual overlap with EQ-5D core dimensions and assessing the psoriasis bolt-on measurement model); explore non-preference based scoring based on findings of aim 2; and examine potential differences in responsiveness, structure and item functioning across language and country subgroups.  The results on psychometric properties and structure of the psoriasis bolt-on will provide support for the instrument advancing to approved status as well as provide the groundwork for future non-preference based scoring research. This project will also provide some of the first evidence of non-preference scoring of the psoriasis bolt-on, giving it a foundation for broader applications outside of health technology assessment and cost utility analyses.
