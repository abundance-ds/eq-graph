This study conducted a psychometric validation of the EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S instruments in an Irish general population sample, comparing them with the EQ-5D-5L and ICECAP-A. The analysis, based on data from 1,220 respondents, revealed that both EQ-HWB versions demonstrated good psychometric properties, including lower ceiling effects than EQ-5D-5L and ICECAP-A. The EQ-HWB showed strong correlations with EQ-5D-5L and moderate correlations with ICECAP-A, suggesting it captures both health-related quality of life and broader wellbeing aspects. Factor analysis identified a unique five-factor structure spanning health and wellbeing domains. While EQ-5D-5L showed higher sensitivity to health problems, EQ-HWB captured a wider range of wellbeing concepts. The study also provides insights into the potential effects of ongoing modifications to the EQ-HWB, particularly the rewording of positively framed items. These findings contribute to the growing body of evidence on the EQ-HWB's performance and inform its use in various healthcare and policy contexts.
