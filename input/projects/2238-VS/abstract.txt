This work addresses the increasing demand for locally generated health preference data in the context of the country’s transition from donor‑dependent health financing to domestically funded systems. This transition necessitates more efficient domestic resource allocation and demands evidence‑informed priority setting, including the application of health technology assessment (HTA).A cross-sectional, in-person survey of about 1260 adults aged 18 years and older was undertaken utilising a multistage stratified sampling method to account for variations among provinces, encompassing urban-rural and socioeconomic disparities. The study adhered to the standard EuroQol Valuation Technology (EQ-VT) protocol, that includes both Composite Time Trade-Off (cTTO) tasks (10 per participant) and Discrete Choice Experiment (DCE) tasks (12 per participant).Actual data collection commenced on the 15th September 2025 after extensive enumerator training, pilot testing, and pre-testing, utilising computer-based EQ-PVT software under organised supervision. A stringent quality control framework was implemented, encompassing daily monitoring via the Quality Control (QC) tool, evaluation of protocol compliance (including accurate elucidation of worse-than-dead tasks, minimum time thresholds, and logical coherence of responses), and systematic retraining as well as exclusion of subpar interviewers. Data quality assessments were performed at critical milestones during the data collection process.The study was conducted in conjunction with the EQ-VT Support Team and national stakeholders, including the Ministry of Health and the Zambia Immunisation Advisory Board, under the supervision of the Principal Investigator. All data were securely administered, anonymised, and prepared for submission to the EuroQol SAVED repository in accordance with national data protection rules.The analysis utilised hybrid modelling techniques that integrated cTTO and DCE data through Stata software, with model selection grounded in logical consistency, statistical fit, and robustness assessments. The principal deliverables comprise a nationally representative EQ-5D-5L value set, draft manuscript and stakeholder workshops.The resultant value set will serve as an essential instrument for cost-effectiveness analysis, aiding health technology assessment and policy decisions in domains such as vaccine prioritisation, while also acting as a standard for other low- and middle-income nations.
