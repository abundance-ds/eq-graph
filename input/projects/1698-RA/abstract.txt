Objective: In the valuation of EQ-5D-Y-3L health states with composite time trade-off method (cTTO), the joint impact of adult socio-demographic characteristics (parental status and sociocultural variables) and the perspective used (depicting a child as more or less distant from the adult) remains unclear. The current study aimed to fill in this gap. Methods: We recruited a sample of 108 Polish adults. First, their parental status, collectivism, conservatism, religiosity, and pro-euthanasia attitude were measured with a self-administered online survey. Then, the respondents were interviewed and completed cTTO tasks for 5 EQ-5D-Y-3L states using a self-perspective and for a hypothetical child in three perspectives: unknown, known but not own, and own. For each perspective, we measured the perceived psychological distance from the child. We used linear regression models to study the impact of perspective and adults' characteristics (1) on the psychological distance, (2) on the cTTO-elicited utility, and (3) the impact of perspective, adults' characteristics, and psychological distance on the cTTO-elicited utility. Results: Changing the perspective (from own to unknown) increased psychological distance as perceived by adults. This increase was larger in parents. Psychological distance also increased with adult collectivism. Adult conservatism was positively related to utility, especially in farther child perspectives and for a larger psychological distance. Conclusions: How the child is depicted in valuation studies should be carefully chosen, especially in samples consisting of parents. Adults’ conservatism and collectivism should be controlled in those studies.
