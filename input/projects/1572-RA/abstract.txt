Objectives Concerns have been raised about the validity and responsiveness of the EQ-5D in the context of multiple sclerosis (MS), however empirical evidence is limited. This research aims to assess the acceptability, validity and responsiveness of the EQ-5D-3L compared to an MS-specific utility measure, the Multiple Sclerosis Impact Scale Eight Dimensions (MSIS-8D), in people with MS. Methods Analysis was undertaken using data from the UK MS Register (UKMSR), a prospective, longitudinal, cohort study with over 10,000 participants. We evaluated and compared the EQ-5D-3L and the MSIS-8D in terms of acceptability, distributional properties, informativity, convergent and discriminative validity, and responsiveness. The responsiveness analysis investigated changes in EQ-5D-3L and MSIS-8D values before and after (i) commencement of disease-modifying treatment and (ii) onset of new symptoms; and (iii) between time-points with/without a contemporary relapse. Results Of those who provided any data on the UKMSR core survey instruments in spring 2023, 3.14% did not complete the EQ-5D-3L and 8.57% did not complete the MSIS-8D. The ceiling effect for the EQ-5D-3L was 11.13%, compared to 4.62% for the MSIS-8D; floor effects were negligible for both measures. In the informativity analyses, health state density indices (MSIS-8D = 0.577; EQ-5D-3L = 0.215), Shannon Indices (MSIS-8D = 6.978; EQ-5D-3L = 3.393) and Shannon Evenness Indices (MSIS-8D = 0.926; EQ-5D-3L = 0.719) were higher for the MSIS-8D than for the EQ-5D. The EQ-5D-3L and MSIS-8D both correlated strongly (rho > 0.5) with measures of disability, fatigue, walking impairment and depression, and moderately (rho > 0.4) with anxiety. Both measures discriminated significantly (p  0.2. Symptom onset resulted in statistically significant reductions in utility values for the EQ-5D-3L (mean change -0.023 p = 0.0370, SRM -0.099, SES 0.073) and MSIS-8D (mean change -0.022; p = 0.0036, SRM -0.140, SES 0.119). However, both failed to meet the threshold for a meaningful change. Neither measure appeared responsive to DMT initiation (p > 0.05). Conclusion Both measures exhibited good convergent and discriminative validity. While the EQ-5D-3L demonstrated greater acceptability, the MSIS-8D exhibited greater informativity. Although the MSIS-8D appears to offer greater responsiveness compared to the EQ-5D-3L, the responsiveness of both measures in this analysis was limited, underlining existing concerns about the ability of utility measures to fully capture treatment effects in MS. Further research is required to determine the psychometric properties of the EQ-5D-5L in the context of MS.
