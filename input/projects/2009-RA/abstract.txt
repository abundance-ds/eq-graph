ObjectiveIn some health conditions, the EQ-5D-5L core dimensions (CDs) are supplemented with bolt-ons (BOs) to increase sensitivity. For BOs to be usable in cost-utility analysis (CUA), BO value sets must be developed, and their application should ensure consistency with CD value sets and avoid double-counting. This study aimed to (1) define a consistency criterion, (2) propose a method to achieve it, and (3) assess its empirical properties.MethodsWe define consistency through the distribution of disutility values in measurement data. The definition separates the disutility corresponding to added sensitivity from that reflecting the BO-CD overlap. Consistency can be achieved by contracting the disutilities in BO value sets. We analyse 9 BOs using data from the general population (GP, 14 countries) and patient populations (PP, 6 disease areas). CD disutilities were based on the Polish EQ-5D-5L value set, and the BO disutilities were based on published studies.ResultsObservation counts ranged from 10,395 (hearing) to 42,206 (self-confidence) in GP, and from 312 (cognition) to 2,486 (breathing, sleep, tiredness) in PP. Level 5 disutilities ranged from 0.037 (sleep) to 0.385 (cognition). Spearman correlations between CDs and BOs ranged from 0.14 (mobility--self-confidence) to 0.55 (anxiety/depression--self-confidence or social relationships) in GP, and from -0.02 (self-care--cognition) to 0.58 (usual activities--tiredness) in PP. Achieving consistency required contraction factors between 0.864 (cognition) and 0.981 (sleep) in GP, and 0.829 (breathing) to 0.989 (sleep) in PP. Stronger contraction was needed for BOs with larger disutilities (significant) and more strongly correlated with CDs (non-significant).ConclusionConsistency between EQ-5D-5L and BO value sets is attainable through modest disutility reductions (2--17%), eliminating double counting while preserving increased measurement sensitivity.
