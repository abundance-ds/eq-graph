The EuroQol group has commissioned the elicitation of a new UK value set for the EQ-5D-5L. The resulting utility scores were expected to differ from those generated by the 5L-to-3L crosswalk currently recommended for use by NICE. The degree of discrepancy depends on the distribution of health states amongst populations of interest, and was a priori unknown.In this study we estimated the effect of the tariff change on 1) average utility by population sub-group, and 2) quality-adjusted life expectancy (QALE) for the UK population. For (1) we calculated the difference in utility scores between the two value sets for each participant in the EQ-DAPHNIE survey and then identified the demographic and medical characteristics that correlated with this difference. For (2) we combined data from the Health Survey for England and Office for National Statistics life tables to calculate QALE at birth and at different ages for males and females.EQ-DAPHNIE survey participants reported 472 distinct EQ-5D-5L profiles. The new value set yielded higher utility scores for less severe health states and lower scores for severe states compared to the crosswalk (mean utility difference: 0.073; 95%CI: 0.071 to 0.075). Age, sex, and educational attainment independently predicted score discrepancies, with larger differences observed for female and older respondents. Univariate analysis showed larger disutilities associated with eight long-standing illnesses under the crosswalk than the 5L tariff. The new value set generated significantly greater QALE than the 5L-to-3L crosswalk. The difference in (undiscounted) QALE at birth was approximately 5.2 QALYs for women and 4.1 QALYs for males. The absolute difference reduces with age because there are fewer predicted remaining years of life in which the higher utility scores under the 5L value set can accumulate. Conversely, the relative difference in remaining QALE increases with age.
