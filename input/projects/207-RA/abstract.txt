Objectives: In health valuation, choice analyses typically include ancillary parameters, such as scale factors and discount rates, when estimating value sets on a quality-adjusted life year (QALY) scale. These parameters potentially differ among respondents. In this study, we investigate how allowing heterogeneity in scale and rate affect the estimation of US EQ-5D-5L values. Methods: Using the first wave of the 2016 US EQ-5D-5L valuation study, we estimated a conditional logit (CL) and three mixed logit models: random scale, random rate, and both. We hypothesized that scale and rate are correlated and that allowing heterogeneity in these parameters decreases the number of insignificant incremental effects. We confirmed the exploratory findings by re-estimating these models using paired comparison responses from a second wave. Results: In all analyses, scale and rate exhibited significant heterogeneity and were highly correlated. As hypothesized, allowing heterogeneity in rate and scale improved the face validity of the EQ-5D-5L value set by reducing the number of insignificant incremental effects. Nevertheless, the 20 incremental effects estimated by the CL and bivariate mixed logit are highly correlated in both waves. Conclusions: In health preference research, scale heterogeneity confounds preference heterogeneity and vice versa, implying that analysts should allow both simultaneously if at all. Allowing heterogeneity in scale and rate adds three parameters to the estimation (two variances and a correlation) and greatly improves the face validity of the EQ-5D-5L values. This intuitive pattern may be confirmed in future EQ-5D-5L valuation studies as well as influence experimental design and choice analysis in health valuation more generally.
