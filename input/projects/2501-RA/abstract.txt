Background: The current EQ-5D-5L Bolt-on Toolbox includes "best available" items across eight dimensions. While these have been identified by the Descriptive Systems Working Group (DSWG) as promising candidates for official inclusion, there is currently insufficient preference evidence to support their development, approval, and valuation.Objective: To generate actionable preference evidence for the eight candidate bolt-ons using a multi-arm, latent-scale discrete choice experiment (DCE) survey. Specifically, we aim to:Measure the strength of preference for each candidate bolt-on (main effects).Assess differences in main effects across the eight candidates (differential effects).Examine how adding a bolt-on changes the importance of the five core dimensions (relative effects).This project is a collaboration between the DSWG and the Valuation Working Group (VWG) designed to provide timely evidence that will inform bolt-on development, Toolbox approval, and the design of future valuation studies.Ideally, a candidate bolt-on should broaden HRQoL descriptions into areas where the core dimensions may be insufficient. This means it should possess strong measurement properties and have an impact on values (Aim 1), while minimizing distortion of the relative effects of the 5 core dimensions (Aim 3). Candidates that exert larger effects on choices are more relevant to economic evaluations (Aim 2), strengthening the case for their development and approval. Conversely, distortions (Aim 3) imply a conceptual overlap, redundancy, or double-counting between the bolt-on and core dimensions. Consequently, adopting such bolt-ons may compromise the structural integrity and independent valuation of the core dimensions, complicating comparisons with the original instrument.Methods: This formative study will recruit a nationally representative sample of 5,000 US adults (Dynata) for an online DCE survey (LimeSurvey). Data quality will be ensured via geographic validation, automation detection, and attentiveness checks. The DCE survey consists of nine arms: a baseline arm with the five core attributes of the EQ-5D-5L (N=1,000) and eight candidate arms where one "best available" bolt-on is appended (N=500 per arm). Each candidate arm utilizes a common D-efficient design (24 paired comparisons, 10 blocks, 50 respondents per block). In the primary analyses, conditional logit models will be estimated on a latent scale (log-odds) to characterize three effects:Main effects: Differences in latent-scale disutilities (log-odds) between successive dimension levels (Aim 1).Differential effects: Differences in bolt-on main effects across candidate arms (Aim 2).Relative effects: Differences in the ratios of core-dimension main effects between the baseline and candidate arms (Aim 3).Furthermore, we will conduct sensitivity analyses using Bayesian estimation of mixed logit models to ensure the robustness of our frequentist findings. We will also estimate scale-factor models that decompose the main effects of each arm into dimension-specific scale factors and cross-attribute level effects (CALEs). As a secondary analysis, we will evaluate whether appending a bolt-on affects respondent burden (e.g., completion time) or data quality (e.g., left-right bias, task sequence effects) relative to the baseline arm.Beyond these aims, we offer an optional supplement to estimate attribute interactions (bolt-on vs. core, core vs. core). While this supplement doubles data collection costs, it does not increase personnel costs (see budget), thereby leveraging economies of scale to provide more granular preference evidence.
