This study investigated whether EQ-5D-5L health state valuations differ when framed within a palliative care needs context through a large-scale discrete choice experiment (DCE) conducted across Australia (n=2,082), Ireland (n=1,280), and the UK (n=2,009). The research examined how factors like limited life expectancy and available support levels influence how people value different health states. This work contributes important empirical evidence to the ongoing debate about the appropriateness of using standard value sets in palliative care decision-making and highlights the need for more nuanced approaches to health state valuation in end-of-life contexts.
