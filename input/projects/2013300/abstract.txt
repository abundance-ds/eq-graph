Aims: This study examined whether alternative editing and framing of valuation tasks influenced health values by comparing composite time trade-off (cTTO), DCE-duration, and DCE-death methods. We tested whether presenting death as immediate versus delayed, and framing outcomes as gains versus losses, affected value estimates and cross-method comparability.Methods: Phase 1: Online DCE study in the Netherlands (~300 respondents per arm) comparing nine DCE configurations across five framing variants using a Bayesian D-efficient design. Phase 2: Randomized study with 876 participants across three Dutch cities, where trained interviewers administered five TTO framing variants in crossover design (803 interviews retained after quality control).Results: Gain/loss framing had limited influence on valuations in both DCE and TTO methods. However, explicit mention of "immediate death" produced large framing effects, with substantial differences between DCE-duration and DCE-death, and between cTTO and lead-time TTO. Notably, the DCE-duration values in this study were more similar to TTO values than values derived from earlier research. When we explored potential reasons, two new insights were produced. Firstly, joint optimization of the DCE design reduced health state selection bias present in standard efficient design algorithms, which typically favor relatively good health states. Secondly, this led us to re-examine DCE modeling assumptions, finding that constant proportionality (equal value per additional year) does not hold for DCE-duration. When imposed, this assumption distorts duration trade-offs, suppresses values, and introduces extrapolation instabilities. Conclusions: Framing effects don't explain cTTO vs. DCE-duration differences; inappropriate handling of time preferences is more likely responsible. Correct specification of temporal assumptions is essential for valid DCE-duration values and cross-method comparability.
