Grant 1927-RA assessed the content validity of proposed modifications to the EQ-HWB-9 through qualitative cognitive debriefing interviews in the United States (n=34) and Germany (n=25), and quantitative data collection in the Netherlands -(n=3,783 as an add-on to the W-MIC study). The study also implemented a coordinated international effort across ten countries, which included Argentina, Australia, China, Indonesia, Ireland, Hungary, Slovenia, and the UK.Both qualitative arms recruited adults with chronic health conditions, unpaid caregivers, and social care users and followed parallel protocols developed by the UK team, and based on COSMIN content validity methodology. The German arm conducted interviews in two waves: wave 1 (n=18) tested both the modified and experimental versions (EV1.1 and EMV1.2), and wave 2 (n=7 elderly care recipients) tested the Vienna version (EMV2.1). Findings from the US and Germany were broadly consistent in supporting the content validity of the modified instrument, with most items demonstrating strong comprehension and relevance in both countries, though some items (notably anxiety in Germany) showed lower comprehension in one setting than the other.Evidence from the US, and in several cases also from Germany, favoured specific modifications: “difficulty” over “trouble” for the cognition item, “lacked control” over “no control” for the control item, “moderate difficulty” over “some difficulty,” and “a little of the time” over “only occasionally.” Persistent comprehension challenges were identified with the mobility item, where parenthetical examples led some participants to interpret the item as applicable only to users of mobility devices. These findings informed finalization of the EQ-HWB-9 (EMV2.1. the “Vienna version”) and contribute to the evidence base supporting the transition of the EQ-HWB from experimental to approved status.A large quantitative dataset was also collected in the Netherlands and US. The data collection followed the quantitative testing international protocol developed in the UK. Findings are reported elsewhere, as part of a separate grant.
