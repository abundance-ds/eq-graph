This study evaluated the psychometric properties of two specific composite items in the EQ-HWB/EQ-HWB-S compared to their single-domain counterparts from the E-QALY project: (1) "Concentrating/Thinking Clearly" versus the separate "Concentrating" and "Thinking Clearly" items, and (2) "Walking Inside/Outside" versus the separate "Walking Inside" and "Walking Outside" items. Using a combination of classical and modern psychometric analysis of 504 US caregiver-patient dyads, we examined convergent validity, structural validity, item response characteristics, and differential item functioning. Results demonstrated strong correlations (r=0.70-0.78), substantial agreement (κ=0.69-0.79), robust factor loadings (0.877-0.976), and comparable information functions across the latent trait continuum. Both composite items showed acceptable discrimination parameters and minimal differential item functioning across caregiver and patient populations, supporting their validity for measuring cognitive and mobility constructs while offering potential for enhanced efficiency in health utility assessment.
