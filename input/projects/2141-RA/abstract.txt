Background: Informal caregiving can substantially affect health and wellbeing, yet there is limited evidence on the comparative performance of preference-based and wellbeing instruments for capturing caregiver outcomes in economic evaluation. This study compared the EQ-HWB-9 with other commonly used measures to examine domain overlap and psychometric performance among informal caregivers.Methods: A cross-sectional online survey was conducted among adult informal caregivers of individuals with stroke, cancer, or dementia. Participants completed six instruments measuring health and wellbeing: EQ-HWB-9, EQ-5D-5L, ASCOT-Carer, CarerQoL-7D, ICECAP-A, and WiX. Descriptive statistics, floor and ceiling effects, convergent and divergent validity, known-group comparisons, exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses, and test–retest reliability were assessed. Correlations were examined using Spearman coefficients, discriminatory power using Shannon indices, and reliability using intraclass correlation coefficients and weighted Cohen’s kappa.Results: Data from 487 caregivers were analysed. Mean EQ-HWB-9 utility was 0.801 (SD 0.211), comparable to ASCOT-Carer (0.791) and ICECAP-A (0.782). Ceiling effects were lower for EQ-HWB-9 (12%) than EQ-5D-5L (33%). Moderate to strong correlations were observed between EQ-HWB-9 and other instruments (ρ = 0.606–0.720), supporting convergent validity. Factor analysis identified three underlying constructs—psychosocial health, mental health, and physical functioning—with EQ-HWB-9 and WiX sharing mental health components. Known-group analyses showed lower wellbeing among caregivers with higher caregiving hours and those caring for recipients with greater disease severity. Informative response distributions were observed across most EQ-HWB-9 items (Shannon H′ = 1.25–2.11). Test–retest reliability for EQ-HWB-9 was acceptable (ICC = 0.69) with moderate item-level agreement.Conclusions: The EQ-HWB-9 demonstrates acceptable psychometric performance among informal caregivers and captures broader wellbeing domains beyond health status. Its conceptual overlap with other wellbeing measures, particularly mental health constructs, suggests it may provide a useful complementary outcome measure for economic evaluations involving caregiver populations.
