Background: Measuring health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in children is a global priority, yet major evidence gaps remain in low- and middle-income countries, particularly in multi-ethnic Southeast Asia. The EQ-5D-Y is available in a three-level version (Y-3L) and, since September 2024, a formally approved five-level version (Y-5L). Whether the Y-5L offers meaningful advantages over the Y-3L in neurodevelopmental conditions remains unclear. No study has simultaneously examined responsiveness, proxy-child agreement, and cross-ethnic measurement equivalence of both EQ-5D-Y versions in children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD).Methods: We propose a comprehensive psychometric study of EuroQol paediatric instruments in Malaysian children with ASD across the full age range (n≈400). Children aged under 3 years will be assessed using EQ-TIPS-5L (proxy only). Children aged 5–17 years will complete the EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L, by proxy only for ages 5–7 years and by both self-report and proxy for ages 8–17 years. A transition cohort aged 3–5 years will complete both EQ-TIPS-5L and EQ-5D-Y by proxy to examine transition between instruments. The PedsQL 4.0 Generic Core Scales and proxy-reported Quality of Life in Autism Spectrum Disorder scale will serve as comparators. Data will be collected at baseline, 2-week follow-up, and 3-month follow-up.Impact: Psychometric evaluation will include construct validity, reliability, responsiveness, ceiling effects, proxy-child agreement, instrument transition, and exploratory cross-ethnic measurement equivalence. Findings will address EuroQol Younger Populations Working Group priorities and inform guidance on paediatric instrument selection in diverse populations.
