The study had three main aims. First, we translated and upgraded the EQ-5D from the 3L version to the 5L version. Next, we evaluated the measurement equivalency between the paper and digital versions of the EQ-5D 5L Shona version. Finally, we explored the feasibility and acceptability of the digital version, including the barriers and facilitators associated with its implementation in a busy HIV care clinic. Our findings showed the measurement equivalency of digital and paper versions of the EQ-5D 5L in Shona version. Thus, both versions can be used confidently in routine clinical care and research. Additionally, the anxiety/depression item on the EQ-5D 5L demonstrated strong diagnostic accuracy for screening anxiety and depression, outperforming multi-item and condition-specific screeners. Lastly, our pilot study indicates that integrating the digital version of the EQ-5D into HIV care in a low-resource setting is feasible and acceptable. Consequently, a follow-up definitive trial using the EQ-5D 5L is needed to assess the implementation and cost-effectiveness of digital mental health monitoring
