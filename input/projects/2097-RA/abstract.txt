Aim: This study descriptively examined the relationship between the EQ-HWB-9 experimental version (2022) and age, exploring the trajectories in utility, summary scores and dimensions across the life course. Comparisons were made with the EQ-5D-5L and ONS-4 life satisfaction and happiness scores. Methods. Using data from a large UK general population sample (n=12,401), age trajectories of EQ-HWB-9 and EQ-5D-5L utility and summary scores were descriptively compared across continuous age categories and against ONS-4 life satisfaction and happiness item scores. EQ-HWB-9 item distributions and domain rankings were examined across age groups, with domains grouped into physical and psychosocial categories to compare overall patterns. Conceptually overlapping EQ-HWB-9 and EQ-5D-5L domains were assessed to identify similarities and differences in age-related change. Ordinary least squares models tested linear, quadratic and cubic relationships between age and EQ-HWB-9 and EQ-5D-5L scores, adjusting for gender, education, carer status, time and ethnicity, with interaction terms assessing age-gender differences. Gender differences in EQ-HWB-9 trajectories were also compared graphically. Results: The EQ-HWB-9 did not show the typical age-related decline seen with the EQ-5D-5L, rather age had a positive relationship with EQ-HWB-9 utility and summary scores. EQ-HWB-9 summary scores broadly followed the trends observed in the ONS-4 life satisfaction and happiness scores. Physical problems gradually increased with age in both instruments, whereas psychosocial problems generally decreased, resulting in distinct and contrasting life course trajectories across domains. Conclusions: Age-related expectations based on the EQ-5D-5L should not be assumed when interpreting the EQ-HWB-9. Recognising these differences is essential as the EQ-HWB-9 becomes more widely used in applied research and evaluation.
