All self-reported health indicators are susceptible to reporting heterogeneity — the systematic variation in how individuals with the same underlying health use the instrument's response categories. The potential consequences of ignoring response scale heterogeneity extend beyond academic measurement concerns. In the context of EuroQol's EQ-5D instruments, which hold a prominent position in health economics and outcomes research, reporting heterogeneity might bias population health norms and health inequality estimates, undermining both clinical and health technology assessment applications.This Project studies reporting heterogeneity in EQ-5D-5L data in Estonia. The primary aim is to examine whether sociodemographic characteristics systematically shift EQ-5D-5L response thresholds after adjustment for objectively validated health status, and to quantify the resulting bias in unadjusted population health norms. The secondary aim is to investigate the specific role of cognitive functioning in EQ-5D-5L reporting heterogeneity among older adults (65+), with implications for the concurrent validity of EQ-5D-5L and the Cognition bolt-on in this population.The Project uses registry-linked longitudinal survey data from study "Health and quality of life 2025" conducted under EuroQol grant 1960-RA. Reporting heterogeneity will be investigated using the Hierarchical Ordered Probit (HOPIT) model, testing alternative specifications of the latent health equation based on self-reported, registry-validated, and registry-only health indicators.The Project delivers two outputs: a peer-reviewed article on reporting heterogeneity in EQ-5D-5L across sociodemographic groups, and a conference paper — to be developed into a full article — on cognitive functioning as a driver of reporting heterogeneity. Both deliverables directly address PHSWG Priority 3 of the current Call and generate evidence relevant to the DSWG Cognition bolt-on programme.
