Reducing socioeconomic inequalities in health is a common social objective for policymakers. Differences between socioeconomic groups exist in terms of both health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and survival. These can result in large lifetime health inequalities. However, there is a lack of evidence on how inequalities accumulate over the life course and which dimensions of HRQoL contribute most to overall health inequality. This project will analyse how socioeconomic inequalities in HRQoL change with age and how these are determined by the different dimensions of the EQ-5D-3L. Our empirical analysis will yield population-level HRQoL scores for different socioeconomic groups, which can be combined with national life tables to produce estimates of quality-adjusted life expectancy (QALE), a rich summary measure of lifetime health. We will analyse how much of the inequalities in QALEs can be attributed to each EQ-5D dimension. We will use HRQoL data from a long-running, nationally representative survey of the English population, the Health Survey for England, which has intermittently collected EQ-5D-3L data since 2003. We will use all waves that included the EQ-5D-3L to show how inequalities in both HRQoL and QALE have developed over the last 15 years. This project will demonstrate the usefulness of the EQ-5D instrument in measuring and explaining health inequalities over the life course, focussing specifically on the additional insights that can be gained from analysing the detailed EQ-5D health profile data. The results will also provide useful evidence to policymakers seeking to reduce health inequalities in England and internationally.
