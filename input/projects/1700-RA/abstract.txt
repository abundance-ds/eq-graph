Despite the frequent use of cognition bolt-ons with the EQ-5D, no systematic review had previously summarised their development, use and psychometric properties, creating uncertainty for researchers and decision-makers in selecting appropriate items. Our systematic literature review has identified 181 publications with cognition bolt-ons for the EQ-5D-3L or EQ-5D-5L. We identified 52 distinct wordings (three-level: 23, five-level: 29), with the most common dimension titles being cognition (29%) and thinking ability (19%), and examples being concentration (60%), memory (39%) and remembering (31%). The methodological quality of bolt-on development was heterogeneous, often lacking qualitative input in item wording or clear phrasing. Bolt-ons were used in 28 languages across 24 countries, with 64% of the studies originating from the Netherlands. Overall, 16% of the studies used proxy-reporting. Most studies developed (n=38) and used or psychometrically tested EQ-5D+bolt-on(s) (n=120), with fewer valuation and disability weight studies (n=27). These studies covered 71 patient populations, most commonly injuries, dementia, depression, HIV, schistosomiasis and stroke. The most frequently reported psychometric properties were item-level ceiling (n=75) and known-groups validity (n=54). Fewer studies explored convergent or divergent validity (n=8 for each), responsiveness (n=3), patient-proxy agreement (n=2) and test-retest reliability (n=1). None reported on content validity. Five-level bolt-ons generally outperformed three-level bolt-ons in terms of overall psychometric performance. The most-tested bolt-ons were the Janssen 2013 (5L, cognition) and Haagsma 2005 (3L, thinking ability) versions. Future research should prioritise testing the psychometric properties of the most promising bolt-ons using both qualitative and quantitative methods across different populations.
