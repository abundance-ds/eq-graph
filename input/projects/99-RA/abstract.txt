In this study we examined the dimensionality of commonly used measures with the aim to identify a set of potential bolt-ons for the EQ-5D-5L. To do this we used an Australian dataset that collected health, social care and wellbeing measures online from patients and members of the general public. We conducted a content analysis to provide a theoretical framework to support the interpretation of the quantitative results. Quantitative analyses were based on a pool of 79 items from 7 measures. Confirmatory factor analysis was used to assess the dimensionality of health, social care and wellbeing measures, and understand their contribution to quality of life. The relationship between EQ-5D-5L items and the identified factors was used to inform the identification of bolt ons. The dimensionality analysis identified seven factors defined as physical functioning, psychological symptoms, energy/sleep, physical pain, social functioning, needs and satisfaction. This enabled us to present a list of candidate bolt-ons for the EQ-5D-5L.
