IntroductionThe EQ Health and Wellbeing (EQ-HWB) measures represent a new generation of instruments designed to measure both health and broader aspects of wellbeing. While existing studies demonstrate satisfactory psychometric performance of EQ-HWB across general and patient populations, limited evidence exists regarding the comparability of different administration modes, particularly among older adults who often experience challenges with self-completion. In addition, the validity of proxy reporting and interviewer-administered versions remains underexplored. This study aims to evaluate the mode agreement, measurement performance, and user acceptability of self-complete, interviewer-administered, and proxy versions of the EQ-HWB-9 among hospitalized older adults in China.MethodsA mixed-methods study will be conducted among 200 hospitalized adults aged 60 years and above (each accompanied by an informal caregiver) with various conditions in a tertiary specialized hospital in Shanghai. Quota Sampling will be used to recruit two groups of participants based on their ability to complete the questionnaire independently: (1) 100 patients capable of self-completion (allowing full comparison across all three modes) and (2) 100 patients unable to self-complete (enabling comparison between interviewer-administered and proxy versions). Additionally, 20 patient participants and 20 caregiver participants will be invited for post-survey interviews to explore the face validity and acceptability of the instrument.All participants will complete the EQ-HWB-9 following a standardized protocol. Demographic and clinical information, completion time, and missing data will be collected. Quantitative analysis will assess feasibility, ceiling and floor effect, internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha), and agreement between modes using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs), Bland-Altman plots, and Gwet’s agreement coefficient for patient-proxy comparisons. Validity testing will include known-groups comparison. Qualitative data will be analyzed thematically to capture user experiences, perceived clarity, and mode preference.ResultsThe study will provide empirical evidence on the feasibility, reliability, validity, and inter-mode comparability of EQ-HWB administration among hospitalized older adults. Quantitative analyses will determine the level of consistency across self-complete, interviewer-administered, and proxy modes, while qualitative findings will identify challenges related to comprehension, relevance, and administration burden.ConclusionThis study aims to extend the evidence base for the EQ-HWB-9 by addressing critical methodological gaps in mode comparability and proxy assessment. The findings will inform best practices for administering EQ-HWB instruments in clinical settings involving older adults with cognitive or physical limitations, ensuring more inclusive and reliable measurement of health and well-being. It may highlight any mode-specific response variations and provide methodological guidance on the appropriate use of interviewer and proxy administration. By combining quantitative psychometric evaluation with qualitative insights, the study will help refine administration protocols, enhance cultural adaptability, and support the wider implementation of EQ-HWB instruments in ageing populations.
