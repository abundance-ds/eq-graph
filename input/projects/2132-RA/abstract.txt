Objectives: The EQ-5D Bolt-on ToolboxTM is intended to improve the sensitivity of health-related quality-of-life measurement in areas insufficiently captured by the five core dimensions of EQ-5D-5L. However, it remains unclear whether adding information that a person has no problems on a bolt-on dimension changes the perceived value of health states that include problems on the core dimensions. This study examined whether appending a bolt-on at level 1 affects preferences for EQ-5D-5L health states.Methods: An online discrete choice experiment was conducted in a representative sample of the United Kingdom general population recruited via Prolific. Respondents were recruited to one of four arms: core EQ-5D-5L only, or EQ-5D-5L plus one of three bolt-ons (social relationships, vision, or appetite). Respondents chose between living for 10 years in a health state with problems across core dimensions and living for a shorter duration in full health (3, 6, or 9 years). Core states were 22222, 33333, and 44444, with corresponding bolt-on states 22222+1, 33333+1, and 44444+1. Odds ratios comparing choices in each bolt-on arm with the core-dimension arm were pooled using the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel method.Results: Data from 2,391 respondents were analysed. Choice patterns were consistent with expected monotonicity in duration and health-state severity. Adding a level-1 bolt-on did not significantly affect the probability of choosing the impaired health state over full health. Pooled odds ratios were 0.921 for social relationships, 1.038 for vision, and 0.944 for appetite, with no statistically significant effects.Conclusions: Appending information on no problems in a bolt-on dimension did not change the perceived attractiveness of EQ-5D-5L health states with problems in core dimensions. These findings contrast with evidence from studies using the scaling factor approach in bolt-on valuation. Further research is needed to understand how respondents integrate bolt-on information into health-state preferences.
