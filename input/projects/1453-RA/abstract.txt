Objective: Psychometric evaluation of the EQ-5D-5L to date has largely relied on classical test theory or unidimensional item response theory approaches that do not account for its multidimensional structure. This study evaluated the dimensionality, item performance, and stability of the EQ-5D-5L using multidimensional item response theory (MIRT) models reinforced with items from other patient-reported outcome measures.Methods: Confirmatory factor analysis and MIRT were applied across two datasets: the Multi-Instrument Comparison (MIC) study (n=5,487) and the E-QALY psychometric survey (n=903). Each dataset was conceptually mapped to identify relevant non-EQ-5D-5L items to overidentify each EQ-5D-5L domain in a MIRT model. EQ-5D-5L IRT parameter stability was assessed by removing non-EQ-5D-5L items, either one-by-one with replacement, or sequentially without replacement and estimating the change in standardised discrimination parameters and visually inspecting information curves. Results: Both datasets supported three-dimensional EQ-5D-5L MIRT structures (Physical Function including EQ-5D-5L mobility/self-care/usual activities and separate Pain/Discomfort, and Anxiety/Depression dimensions) with good model fit. EQ-5D-5L items demonstrated strong standardised discrimination parameters (MIC: 0.886-0.971; E-QALY: 0.780-0.916). Parameter stability varied by the conceptual breadth of factors. EQ-5D-5L pain/discomfort was stable even with few anchoring items, reflecting narrow conceptual focus. Physical Function and Anxiety/depression exhibited greater sensitivity when item pools diminished, as imbalances occurred in how well the EQ-5D-5L concepts were covered.Conclusions: MIRT models reinforced with non-EQ-5D-5L items provide a feasible approach to inform psychometric evaluation of the EQ-5D-5L. Parameter stability requires sufficient item coverage reflecting EQ-5D-5L content, with attention to both total item number and conceptual breadth within factors.
