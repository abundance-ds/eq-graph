The Interviewer-Administered (IA) version of the EQ-5D-5L was developed to enhance accessibility for respondents unable to self-complete the questionnaire due to illiteracy, low education, illness, or vision-related issues. This study examined the psychometric performance of the newly adapted IA version in Bahasa Indonesia by assessing its agreement with Self-Complete (SC) and Assisted Self-Complete (ASC) modes, along with ceiling/floor effects and test-retest reliability across literate, illiterate, and patient populations. A total of 289 respondents were recruited, comprising literate adults (n = 189), illiterate or low-literacy individuals (n = 50), and patients with either tuberculosis (TB) or type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM) (n = 50). All respondents completed two EQ-5D-5L modes—IA and either SC or ASC—followed by retest administration after two to four weeks. Agreement at the item level was assessed using Gwet’s AC2, while intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) evaluated EQ-VAS and utility scores. Across all groups and dimensions, the IA version exhibited a consistent ceiling effect but no floor effect. The IA version demonstrated high agreement with SC/ASC versions at the dimension level, with Gwet’s AC2 values ranging from 0.86 to 1.00 in most domains and populations. However, notable variations emerged in agreement for the EQ VAS and utility scores. For instance, the ICC for EQ VAS was excellent among TB patients (0.91) but poor among illiterate respondents (0.08). Utility scores similarly varied, with ICCs ranging from 0.12 in the illiterate group to 0.90 among TB patients. Test-retest reliability of the IA version also revealed strong performance at the domain level, with Gwet’s AC2 indicating almost perfect agreement in most cases. However, EQ VAS and utility scores again showed variation, where TB patients displayed the highest ICC values for test-retest reliability (0.77 and 0.87 for EQ VAS and utility, respectively), while illiterate respondents had the lowest (0.29 and 0.07, respectively). These findings suggest that the IA version of EQ-5D-5L is a viable alternative to self-complete modes for populations with limited literacy or physical constraints, particularly in LMIC settings such as Indonesia.
