Caregivers of children and adolescents with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) often face substantial challenges and may experience reduced health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and wellbeing. This study examined the content validity of five EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons and the EQ-HWB long form among caregivers of pediatric individuals with ASD. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with 31 caregivers of children and adolescents aged 8–17 years with ASD in China. The interviews assessed the relevance, comprehensibility, and comprehensiveness of the EQ-5D-5L with bolt-ons and the EQ-HWB. For the EQ-HWB, relevance, comprehensibility, and comprehensiveness were generally rated positively. The most relevant items were “feel anxious?”, “feel frustrated?”, “feel sad or depressed?”, “feel lonely?”, and “have problems with your sleep?”, reflecting key aspects of caregivers’ HRQoL. Sixteen participants considered the measure to capture the main impacts of caregiving on health and wellbeing. However, some physical functioning items were seen as less relevant, certain examples lacked contextual fit, and caregiving-specific social impacts, particularly reduced social participation, were not fully captured. Some conceptual overlap between items was also noted. Response options were generally acceptable, although some participants preferred a longer recall period. For the EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons, the five bolt-ons were generally regarded as relevant, comprehensible, and useful. In terms of perceived relevance and importance, the priority order was sleep, happiness, tiredness, cognition, and social relationships. Compared with the EQ-5D-5L alone, the EQ-5D-5L plus bolt-ons was seen as more comprehensive, particularly through better coverage of sleep, fatigue, mood, and cognitive burden. Nonetheless, social support and financial concerns remained the most frequently identified missing domains, and the response options for the happiness bolt-on were most often seen as needing revision. These findings have two main implications for EuroQol. First, they provide supportive evidence for the relevance and coverage of the EQ-HWB in a caregiver population, helping to strengthen the empirical basis for its finalisation. Second, they suggest that selected EQ-5D-5L bolt-ons may improve the relevance and comprehensiveness of the core measure in caregivers, informing the development of an EQ-5D bolt-on toolbox for use in specific populations and contexts.
