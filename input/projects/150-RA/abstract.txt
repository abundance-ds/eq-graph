The feasibility of collecting routinely quality of life (QoL) data in residential aged care, including the examination of barriers to and facilitators of as well as the psychometric performance of QoL measures, has not been fully explored to date. The aim of this study was to examine the feasibility of measuring routinely QoL in residential aged care. This study comprised of five steps: (i) interviews with residents to explore the face validity of four QoL measures (EQ-5D-5L, EQ-HWB, ASCOT, QOL-ACC) and residents’ views towards QoL data collection; (ii) interviews with proxies (aged care staff and relatives) to examine the appropriateness of the EQ-HWB for proxy reporting residents’ QoL and their perceptions towards QoL data collection; (iii) workshops with aged care staff and relatives to design the process for routine QoL data collection; (iv) pilot of the QoL data collection process; and (v) interviews with aged care staff to reflect and evaluate the data collection process. This study was conducted at two not-for-profit residential aged care facilities in Melbourne, Australia. This study demonstrated that it is feasible to collect routinely QoL data in residential aged care. Further research is needed to address the barriers identified, optimize the efficiency of the process, and maximize the use of data to guide quality improvement strategies. Results further showed that EuroQol instruments (i.e., EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB) hold promise for implementation in aged care sectors, demonstrating good performance and acceptability in this setting.
