Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) is the most common type of arthritis among children and youth. It can cause pain and permanent physical damage to joints, which affects mobility, pain, and daily activities. Current effective treatment methods have improved the health status and health-related quality of life of many JIA patients so that during remission they may not have any severe symptoms and thus their HRQOL during that period may be relatively good. The 3-level self-report version of EQ-5D-Y has been validated in JIA, but the validity and responsiveness of the 5-level version remains under investigation. In Arthritis Society of Canada funded work, our team has studied the validity and responsiveness of the EQ-5D-Y-5L parent-proxy instrument. In this proposal, we aim to build upon this previous work and examine the psychometric properties (validity and responsiveness) of the EQ-5D-Y-5L self-report versions among children with JIA and compare the results to parent-proxy version using both matched and unmatched datasets. Both validity and responsiveness results from the child-report will be compared to parent-proxy analysis results to identify potential differences between the results and if they are correlated to any of the background variables.  The results of the analysis will address a gap in the literature in terms of validity and responsiveness of the EQ-5D-Y-5L self-report version in a population with a range of health states including relatively severe health states, and using a longitudinal dataset with detailed clinical, physician reported and condition specific measures.
