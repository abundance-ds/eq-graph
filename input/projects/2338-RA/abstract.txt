Depression and anxiety are common among individuals with cancer and significantly affect their quality of life and treatment outcomes. The EQ-5D-5L is a widely used instrument to measure health-related quality of life (HRQoL), but the performance of its Anxiety/Depression (A/D) dimension remains uncertain, particularly in oncology populations. This project aims to evaluate the validity and responsiveness of the EQ-5D-5L A/D dimension over time in patients experiencing emotional distress related to cancer.Using secondary data from the REDUCT clinical trial (N = 473), which followed participants at baseline, 4, and 7 months, we will apply longitudinal Item Response Theory (IRT) models to assess convergent validity, and responsiveness. The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) and Patient Health Questionnaire-4 (PHQ-4) will serve as reference measures. We will further examine differential item functioning (DIF) across age and psychiatric history subgroups to test for measurement bias.This study will provide the first longitudinal evidence on the psychometric performance of the EQ-5D-5L A/D dimension in cancer care, supporting more accurate use of this measure in assessing mental health-related quality of life in clinical and economic evaluations.
