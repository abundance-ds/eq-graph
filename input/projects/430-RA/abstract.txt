This project studied the health-related quality of life and multidimensional deprivation in India. Focussing on a recent public health problem of COVID-19 infection, long-COVID symptoms this project evaluates how deprivation in multiple domains is associated with COVID-19 and its post COVID-19 symptoms.  The IndiQol Project seeks to understand how the COVID-19 pandemic altered population wellbeing in India how multidimensional disadvantage changed over time how disruptions in employment, income, education, healthcare access, and social support translated into health-related quality of life (HRQoL) outcomes how socioeconomic and demographic gradients structure inequalities in HRQoL how EQ-5D-5L performs in a culturally and linguistically diverse LMIC context. This project provides unprecedented data on HRQoL, disadvantage, and recovery in India. Its findings lay a strong foundation for future valuation studies, policy engagement, and longitudinal research. The completed work follows the proposed methodological plan and reflects strict adherence to statistical approach to sample design. This project employs a multi-stage, stratified sampling inclusion of six diverse states to ensure heterogeneity. The integration of COVID-19 modules, socioeconomic indicators, and HRQoL measures across harmonised questionnaires and field protocols. The survey was implemented in Hindi and English. The study intentionally spans six states that differ in: economic development (from high-income Haryana to lower-income Odisha), urbanisation, health system capacity, COVID-19 exposure trajectories, literacy and schooling level, sanitation and water systems. This provides the broadest national HRQoL picture collected in India to date.
