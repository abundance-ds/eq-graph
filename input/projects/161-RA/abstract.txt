Bolt-on items can be appended to the EQ-5D core dimensions in order to improve its performance in applications to certain patient populations. Valuation studies have been conducted in order to obtain utility values for the health states described by the EQ-5D classifier appended with a bolt-on. However, the valuation methods used in these valuation studies require large sample sizes and are associated with a high cognitive burden to respondents. A new valuation method, currently referred to as Single Profile Preference Elicitation (SPP), is emerging as a plausible alternative for the valuation of extended descriptive systems. Feedback from testing of SPP in small-scale experiments suggests high user acceptance, ease of administration, and a comparatively low cognitive burden compared to regular DCE. Additionally, preliminary simulation studies indicate that SPP should provide more information per task than regular DCE, which should translate into improved precision or lower sample size requirements. Our project's primary aim was to examine the feasibility and acceptability of using SPP to value multidimensional measures when adding dimensions to existing measures. To achieve this goal, we first conducted a qualitative study (n=17) to explore the feasibility acceptability of using the SPP to value bolt-on items. This information helped us to refine the protocol used in the valuation stage. The second aim of this project was to apply the SPP method to value bolt-on dimensions while retaining the scaling of the reference EQ-5D-5L value set. In the subsequent valuation stage, a sample of 1008 members of the general population completed an online survey, administering regular DCE and SPP for EQ-5D + a bolt-on item, for head-to-head comparison. The results obtained in this stage will lead to the estimation of the preference weights for the bolt-on item and the examination of the precision of the estimates yielded by the SPP compared to regular DCE.
