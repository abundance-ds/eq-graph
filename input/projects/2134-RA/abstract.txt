Respondent burden is an important consideration for patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs), and has motivated the development of shorter versions of PROMs. Despite its importance, respondent burden is ill-defined, and cannot solely be defined as length alone. Existing research on respondent burden, though limited and heterogeneous, indicates the that measuring content and difficulty can also contribute to an understanding of burden. The newly developed EQ-HWB has a shorter version, the EQ-HWB-S, but the perceived burden of both instruments is not clear. This study will use a mixed-methods approach to define, measure and evaluate burden for the EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S for men with prostate cancer. Health and well-being is especially important for this population as survival continues to improve but treatments can have long-lasting impacts. This study will provide evidence on the EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S in a prostate cancer registry population, and on the perceived burden of the EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S. This information can guide decisions on the choice of the EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S in the context of burden.
