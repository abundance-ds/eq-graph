Stroke remains a leading cause of long-term disability globally, frequently resulting in cognitive impairments that significantly impact health-related quality of life (HRQoL). Generic instruments like the EQ-5D-5L, while widely used, lack a specific cognition dimension, potentially underrepresenting the HRQoL burden in post-stroke patients. To address this, cognition bolt-on items have been proposed to enhance the EQ-5D-5L’s sensitivity. This study aims to evaluate the psychometric properties of a recently developed set of cognition bolt-on items (1 global and 5 subdimension items by Mulhern-Sampson) in Chinese post-stroke patients, aligning with the EuroQol Group’s Bolt-on Toolbox initiative. A mixed-method approach will be employed, involving in-depth interviews (n=20) for content validity (qualitative phase) and a cross-sectional study (n=300) with test-retest reliability follow-up (quantitative phase). In the qualitative phase we will assess relevance, comprehensiveness and comprehensibility. In the quantitative phase we will use both classical test theory and item response theory approaches. We will assess the ceiling/floor effects, informativity, convergent validity, explanatory power, known group validity, test-retest reliability and differential item functioning of these bolt-on items. Findings will provide empirical evidence on the bolt-on items’ performance, supporting their refinement and potential inclusion in the EQ-5D Bolt-on Toolbox. For the EuroQol Group, this research will advance the finalization of a validated, culturally relevant cognition bolt-on suite, fulfilling strategic objectives related to the Bolt-on Toolbox.
