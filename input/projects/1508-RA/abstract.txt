This project investigated the feasibility of using the Online elicitation of Personal Utility Functions (OPUF) approach to create value sets for the EQ-HWB-S. We constructed multiple value sets for the EQ-HWB-S in two countries (UK, Germany) and two patient groups (Germany) using the OPUF method, even in smaller sample sizes than traditionally required. We also conducted a test-retest study to assess the reliability of the OPUF method. While the results showed relative stability of model coefficients on an aggregate level, reliability on the individual participant level was low. Finally, we performed qualitative interviews to understand participants' experiences with the OPUF tasks. The interviews revealed confusion around some task instructions, and identified multiple areas for improvement. Overall, our project demonstrated that the OPUF method is a promising approach for estimating value sets for instruments with many dimensions, such as the EQ-HWB-S. However, the method requires further development to improve the reliability and validity of the derived preference weights.
