This study aims to examine the consistency between a new discrete choice experiment (DCE) method including split-triplet choice tasks and conventional composite time trade-off (cTTO) methods in the Japanese context. While previous research in Trinidad and Tobago demonstrated similarity between these methods, their cross-cultural validity, particularly in Asian populations where different preference patterns have been observed, remains uncertain. The study will employ four valuation methods for EQ-5D-5L health states: cTTO with a sample size of 1000 and simple DCE with duration, normal triplet DCE, and split-triplet DCEwith 1500 respondents (for DCE). Data collection will involve face-to-face computer-assisted personal interviews for cTTO and online surveys for DCE methods. Analysis will utilize linear mixed or Tobit models for cTTO data and mixed logit models for DCE data. Non-linear time preference is also considered for DCE data. The research team's connection with Japan's health technology assessment agency (C2H) positions this study to directly inform policy decisions. Confirming consistency between these methods in a non-Western context would provide crucial evidence for their broad applicability and potentially influence methodological guidelines in Japan and other Asian countries.
