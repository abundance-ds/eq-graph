Background: Value sets are essential instruments for conducting robust QALY-based health technology assessments (HTA) and cost-effectiveness analyses in any country. The EQ-5D-Y-5L instrument, designed to capture health-related quality of life in children and adolescents, extends health state measurement by incorporating five severity levels across five dimensions, however, the absence of country-specific value sets constrains its application in pediatric health evaluations. Given India’s sizable adolescent population, there is a pressing need to develop an India-specific EQ-5D-Y-5L value set to support evidence-based resource allocation. Nevertheless, no valuation protocol exists which can provide guidance on which method to use for developing the EQ-5D-Y-5L value set.   Objective: This study assessed different EQ-5D-Y-5L valuation methods, including the extended EQ-5D-Y-3L protocol, and the Discrete Choice Experiments (DCE) with a duration attribute (DCEd), to inform the development of the EQ-5D-Y-5L valuation protocol, which can subsequently be used in developing the EQ-5D-Y-5L value-set for India. Additionally, this study also explored the impact of including the preferences of adolescents in the development of EQ-5D-Y-5L value-set. Methods: There are three key methodological considerations which have guided this study- first, to collect EQ-5D-Y-5L valuation data using an extended version of the EQ-5D-Y-3L protocol (cTTO and DCE) and assess feasibility for developing an India-specific value set.​ Second, collect DCE with duration (DCEd) data to compare results against the extended EQ-5D-Y-3L protocol.​ Third, incorporate adolescent (10–17 years) preferences in latent-scale DCE within the extended EQ-5D-Y-3L protocol to evaluate its impact on utility values across all health states. A cross-sectional EQ-5D-Y-5L valuation study was conducted in Haryana state of India using a 30-cluster sampling design with proportional representation of urban and rural areas. The study targeted adults aged 18 years and above for cTTO, DCE, and DCEd tasks, and adolescents aged 10–17 years for DCE tasks. Following a 5-day EuroQol Valuation Technology (EQ-VT) and DCEd training, face-to-face computer-assisted personal interviews (CAPI) were carried out by eight trained interviewers. Of the 1,533 interviews completed, 1,524 were retained for analysis after excluding nine quality control–flagged cases. Among the interviews finally included in the analysis, 235 were cTTO, 441 were DCE, and 848 were DCEd interviews. The cTTO data was analyzed using a 20-parameter model that accounts for heteroskedasticity and a 20-parameter Tobit model. DCE data was modeled using a mixed logit model, while DCEd data was analyzed using a mixed logit model incorporating exponential discounting. Key results: The sample reflected national rural-urban proportions, with rural participants predominantly (69% in DCE, 72% in cTTO, 67% in DCEd) and balanced sex distribution across tasks. The 26–40-year age group was largest (42.3% of the total respondents), while 10–25-year-olds featured prominently (38%) in latent-scale DCE due to adolescent inclusion; most were married, with mean monthly household expenditure of ₹29,779 (ranging from ₹26,900 to ₹32,000 across cTTO, DCE and DCEd groups). Chronic ailments affected 22.9% (19–26% across the three groups of respondents), and reported substance use (tobacco, alcohol) was present among 20.9% of the study participants.​ In cTTO models, pain/discomfort (PD) was most important dimension (PD5 disutilities: 0.981 heteroskedastic, 1.090 Tobit), followed by mobility (MO; 0.376–0.428), with looking after myself (LAM) least important (0.133–0.186); minor ordering inconsistencies occurred, and worst-state value was –0.967 to –1.354. DCE confirmed PD > MO > LAM ordering (rescaled PD5: 0.806 full sample), with adolescents showing higher PD5 (0.896) and lower LAM5 (0.117) weights, plus lower choice consistency (gamma=0.706). DCEd mixed logit models yielded PD5 weights of 0.788–0.744, MO5 0.409–0.397, LAM5 0.202–0.205, and worst-state values of –0.952 to –0.881, with discount rates varying from –21.1% to 16.3%. Cross-method scatterplots showed high agreement but scale differences, with cTTO valuing mild/moderate states higher and severe states lower than DCE/DCEd. Implications: The findings are a step towards accurate QALY estimation for pediatric interventions, supporting transparent and equitable resource allocation in India's adolescent health programs and interventions. This study demonstrates a degree of methodological homogeneity across different valuation methods (cTTO & DCE versus DCEd) for EQ-5D-Y-5L valuation, with consistent pain/discomfort dominance, strong cross-method agreement, and no significant adult-adolescent preference differences. The findings of this study indicate that there are no statistically significant differences between adult and adolescent preferences in EQ-5D-Y-5L valuation tasks, nor are there meaningful differences in the utility values generated using different valuation approaches (extended EQ-5D-Y-3L protocol with cTTO and DCE vs. DCEd method). These results directly inform the design of the Indian EQ-5D-Y-5L main valuation study, supporting the use of a combined cTTO and latent-scale DCE design as the most suitable approach. Furthermore, the adoption of this design ensures methodological consistency with international EQ-5D-Y valuation studies, most of which rely on adult respondents, while also preserving alignment with the Indian EQ-5D-5L adult valuation study. This harmonization is expected to enhance comparability and facilitate robust application of the resulting value set in future health-economic evaluations. This pilot study in the Indian context as part of the broader EuroQol study programme of work for valuing EQ-5D-Y-5L in India, the Netherlands, Poland and Canada.The evidence thus generated will inform the development of a valuation protocol for the EQ-5D-Y-5L, which would serve as a guidance document to establish a value set for the EQ-5D-Y-5L in different countries.
