The use of EQ-5D value sets in healthcare decision-making underlines the importance of value sets validly representing the population’s health preferences. However, limited evidence exists on which factors might cause value sets to become redundant. The aim of this study was to examine whether major societal challenges, like the COVID-19 pandemic and the war in Europe, might cause health preferences to shift and potentially warrant the re-estimation of EQ-5D value sets. Health preferences of the Danish population were collected through a combination of computer-assisted personal interviews using Zoom following the EQ-VT 2.1 protocol and an online DCE survey. From December 2023 to April 2024, a total of 100 EQ-VT interviews were conducted and 1,003 respondents completed the online DCE survey with sufficient quality. The newly collected data were compared with health preferences collected in the Danish EQ-5D-5L valuation study that was finalised in 2019, immediately before the COVID-19 pandemic. Comparisons showed no significant shifts in health preferences between 2019 and 2024 . These results indicate that national EQ-5D-5L health preferences remain stable over a five-year period with major societal challenges.
