Background and aims: The EQ-HWB-S incorporates multiple response scales (difficulty, frequency, and severity) that require consistent interpretation for accurate scoring, health state description and valuation. Interpretations may also differ due to item translations or cultural aspects, potentially affecting the instrument's validity across diverse populations. This study aims to investigate the cross-cultural interpretation of modified (v1.2) and old version (v1.1.) response options in the EQ Health and Wellbeing Long and Short (EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S) instrument in four countries: UK, USA, China, and Germany.  Methods: A cross-sectional online survey will be conducted with a total of 1,200 participants (300 per country). Participants will complete: 1) response scale quantification tasks where they assign numerical values to each response option (e.g., specifying the number of days associated with "sometimes" on a frequency scale); 2) ranking exercises of response levels 3) slider tasks where they indicate the positions of response options on a continuous scale from 0 to 100 and ordinal ranking of response options; 4) the EQ-HWB/EQ-HWB-S instrument, which includes recent changes to formatting, instructional phrases, and item wording; Data analysis will involve descriptive statistics to summarize participant characteristics and response interpretations. Cross-country comparisons will be made using ANOVA and Kruskal-Wallis tests for quantitative data, and intraclass correlation coefficients.  Significance: As EQ-HWB-S approaches its valuation phase, the findings will confirm whether the response options function as intended or identify where inconsistencies may arise, informing necessary refinements to the instrument. This research is important for enhancing the instrument's cross-cultural validity, ensuring accurate health state descriptions, and improving the comparability of data across diverse populations.
