Background: The current EuroQol call prioritises conceptual and methodological evidence for development of the EQ-5D Bolt-on Toolbox. Within this priority, the DSWG specifically requests conceptual and qualitative proposals addressing how fatigue should be measured and how fatigue items should be described. Fatigue is highly relevant in cardiovascular disease and selected cancer contexts, primarily gynecologic cancer and, where feasible, lung cancer, but it is not explicitly represented in the five core EQ-5D-5L dimensions. This raises a practical instrument-development question for EuroQol: what wording best captures illness-related fatigue in a way that is clear, relevant, and distinct from nearby concepts and existing EQ-5D-5L dimensions [1,4-7]?Objectives: The study will generate patient- and healthcare-professional-informed qualitative evidence that may inform possible refinements to fatigue wording in the EQ-5D Bolt-on Toolbox. It will explore how adult patients with cardiovascular disease and selected cancer populations (primarily gynecologic cancer and, where feasible, lung cancer) understand and describe illness-related fatigue, how they distinguish it from related concepts such as tiredness, weakness, low energy, sleepiness, and vitality, and how they interpret candidate wordings drawn from the 10 translated Toolbox fatigue items and any additional DSWG/Bolt-On Toolbox-recommended alternative phrasings. The study will also consider the broader applicability and limitations of Bahasa Indonesia findings in relation to the English source-language Toolbox and other language versions.Methods: A two-stage qualitative methodological design will be used. Stage 1 will involve open-ended concept elicitation interviews to explore the lived meaning and language of illness-related fatigue. To avoid priming participants' terminology, Stage 1 interviews will begin with spontaneous discussion of symptoms and daily-life impacts before fatigue-related terms, candidate items, or any fatigue-specific scale are introduced. A fatigue scale will not be administered immediately before the Stage 1 concept elicitation interview as an inclusion criterion; if a fatigue measure is used, it will be administered 1-2 weeks earlier for sampling support or after the open-ended elicitation component as a descriptive indicator. In each clinical context, Stage 1 will target 8 patients and 4 healthcare professionals. Stage 2 will involve cognitive debriefing interviews to assess the comprehensibility, relevance, acceptability, and conceptual distinctiveness of candidate wordings drawn from the 10 translated Toolbox fatigue items, with one or two additional alternative phrasings included only if recommended by the DSWG/Bolt-On Toolbox team before Stage 2 commences. Stage 2 will use the same target structure in each clinical context, giving 24 planned interviews per stage and 48 planned interviews overall across cardiovascular and cancer contexts. A small number of patients and healthcare professionals will also be consulted during piloting to support protocol design, topic guide refinement, and interview flow.Expected contribution: The study will provide EuroQol with development-focused evidence on how fatigue is understood and phrased in Bahasa Indonesia and how candidate wording may function across cardiovascular and selected cancer contexts. The study will not claim to determine whether the current UK English Toolbox items are adequate on its own. Instead, it will generate evidence that may inform possible refinements to fatigue wording when considered alongside DSWG guidance, Toolbox team input, and evidence from other languages and patient groups.
