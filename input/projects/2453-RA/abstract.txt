Health-related quality of life (HRQoL) is a cornerstone of person-centred care and outcomes-based healthcare evaluation, providing critical insights into patients’ perceptions of their own health and wellbeing. The EQ-5D-5L is among the most widely used patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) internationally; however, evidence on its measurement performance among hospitalised older adults remains limited. Older adults frequently experience multimorbidity, functional decline, and cognitive or sensory impairments, which may influence both their ability to self-report and the interpretability of responses. Ensuring that the EQ-5D-5L is valid, reliable, and responsive in this population is essential for its use in clinical decision-making, quality improvement, and economic evaluation. This study aims to examine the psychometric performance of the EQ-5D-5L in hospitalised older adults using routinely collected PROMs data. Specifically, it will assess distribution properties (floor and ceiling effects), convergent validity, known-group validity, and responsiveness to change. Data will be drawn from an ongoing, co-designed PROMs collection system developed by the National Centre for Healthy Ageing, Monash University, and Peninsula Health. The system captures EQ-5D-5L data from patients aged 60 years and over at hospital discharge, 3-months and 6-months post-discharge, alongside demographic, clinical, and functional data. Utility scores will be derived using the Australian value set, and analyses will include descriptive, comparative, and longitudinal methods. Findings will generate robust evidence on the suitability of the EQ-5D-5L for use in older inpatient populations and inform best practice for PROMs implementation. This work aligns with EuroQol’s strategic priorities to advance HRQoL measurement across the lifespan.
