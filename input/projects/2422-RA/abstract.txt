This study aims to (1) assess the measurement properties of EQ-5D-5L and a breathing bolt-on in older adults experiencing influenza-like illness (ILI), and (2) examine age-related differences in measurement properties across subgroups (60–64, 65–74, 75–84, 85+). We will conduct a secondary analysis of data from a prospective, observational cohort study in Belgium, the Netherlands, and the United Kingdom over two consecutive ILI seasons. Participants were recruited via 17 general practices and completed baseline assessments of demographics and HRQoL (EQ-5D-5L, breathing bolt-on, EQ VAS). During the ILI season, weekly monitoring captured ARI symptoms, PCR test results, medication use, absenteeism, and HRQoL. Psychometric evaluation will follow established frameworks for bolt-on assessment, comparing EQ-5D-5L alone versus EQ-5D-5L plus breathing bolt-on. To explore potential age-related differences in the performance of the EQ-5D-5L, we will stratify the older adult population into pre-specified age groups and replicate the psychometric assessments within each subgroup. We will also perform differential item functioning (DIF) analysis to examine whether EQ-5D-5L item responses exhibited DIF across age categories and language/country groups.
