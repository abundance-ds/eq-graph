Bayesian efficient designs improve the statistical efficiency of discrete choice experiments (DCEs) by optimizing a chosen design criterion over a prior distribution of anticipated preference parameters; their performance therefore depends critically on how well that prior reflects respondents' actual preferences. When reliable pilot data are not yet available, researchers often do already know the expected sign or ordering of some or all attribute-level coefficients on theoretical grounds. This paper introduces such qualitative information into Bayesian efficient design by truncating the prior to the admissible polyhedron Aβ>0, where A encodes the imposed sign and monotonicity restrictions, and subsequently integrating the design criterion over this constrained space. This approach is evaluated in an EQ-5D health-state valuation study with six designs arms (n = 2,648). Imposing sign and monotonicity constraints on a diffuse standard normal prior reduced the conditional and panel mixed logit D-error by 60% and 50%, respectively, recovering most of the efficiency gain that typically requires informative priors and more than halving sample size requirements. Imposing these constraints on an already accurate informative prior yielded no further improvement. Notably, the observed efficiency gains came at minimal behavioral cost, with dropout, speeding, and fatigue remaining unaffected. Sign and monotonicity constraints therefore provide an easily implemented and potentially performant extension for Bayesian efficient designs when more precise prior parameter information is not yet available.
