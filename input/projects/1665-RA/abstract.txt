Objectives: The EQ Health and Wellbeing Short (EQ-HWB-9) is a generic, preference-weighted instrument with an interim value set for assessing quality of life across health, social care, and informal caregiving. This study evaluated the psychometric properties of the experimental (2022) version and compared its performance with established measures of health and wellbeing.Methods: Data were drawn from a large cross-sectional online survey of the UK general population (n = 11,383). Ceiling/floor effects were assessed at item (>50% extreme responses) and instrument (>15% at min/max score) levels. Construct validity was evaluated through convergent validity using Spearman correlations between EQ-HWB-9 items and conceptually similar items from the Short Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale (SWEMWBS), the ICEpop CAPability measure for Adults (ICECAP-A), the Health Utilities Index Mark 3 (HUI3), and the Office for National Statistics Four Personal Wellbeing Questions (ONS-4). Pearson correlations were used for utility scores. Known-group validity was assessed using effect sizes to examine differences by mental wellbeing, disability, life satisfaction, caregiving status, self-reported health, and age. Agreement with comparator instruments was assessed using Bland–Altman plots and Lin’s concordance correlation coefficient (CCC).Results: Ceiling effects were noted for EQ-HWB-9 mobility and activity items, but not at the instrument level. Strong correlations (rs ≥0.5, P.001) supported convergent validity with SWEMWBS, ICECAP-A, HUI3, and ONS-4. The EQ-HWB-9 effectively distinguished between relevant population subgroups (effect sizes ≥0.8). Agreement was lower with HUI3 utilities compared to others.Conclusions: The EQ-HWB-9 shows strong psychometric performance and is supported for use in UK health and wellbeing assessments.
