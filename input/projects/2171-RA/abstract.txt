The health-related quality of life (HRQoL) of children can significantly impact their parents and caregivers, a phenomenon known as the spillover effect. Generic HRQoL tools often fail to capture this dynamic. To address this, the EQ-HWB-S was developed to measure both individual and caregiver HRQoL. While preliminary validations and spillover assessment exist, broader evidence on its utility in caregiver contexts of young children is scarce.  This study proposes a secondary analysis of pooled data from an ongoing multinational project evaluating HRQoL in children aged 0–4 years and their caregivers across five countries: Argentina, Australia, Canada, Germany and Singapore. The aim of the current proposal is to assess the EQ-HWB-S’s capacity to reflect the spillover effects of children’s health status on caregivers’ HRQoL.  When data will be available in early 2026, we will perform descriptive, regression, and segmentation analyses to examine: (1) overall caregiver HRQoL; (2) correlations between child and caregiver HRQoL; (3) the magnitude and drivers of spillover effects; and (4) caregiver typologies. Analyses will be conducted in R, with adjustments for relevant clinical and sociodemographic variables, and country included as a random effect.  By funding this proposal, the EuroQol Group will obtain novel insights into the spillover body of knowledge, and the EQ-HWB-S’s application across diverse populations and contexts, reinforcing its potential for inclusion in both public health and population studies as well as in economic evaluations for informing health policy and decision-making.
