Introduction: A Nigerian-specific EQ-5D-5L value set is essential to ensure that health utility estimates reflect national preferences, enabling contextually appropriate health technology assessment (HTA) to inform efficient resource allocation decision. The objective of this study is, therefore, to develop an EQ-5D-5L value set for Nigeria.Methods: Adult Nigerians were recruited from 12 states using multi-stage stratified quota sampling based on age, sex, and education. Face-to-face interviews were conducted through Computer-Assisted Personal Interviews using the EQ-PVT software. The interview comprises 2 main parts: composite time-trade-off (cTTO) and discrete choice experiment (DCE). The cTTO data was modelled using generalized least squares (GLS), GLS corrected for heteroskedasticity, Tobit, and Tobit corrected for heteroskedasticity. In contrast, DCE data was analyzed using Conditional Logistic Model (CLM) and Mixed Logistic Model (MLM). Hybrid models combining the cTTO and DCE data were also estimated.Results: A total of 1,200 interviews were conducted. The additive Hybrid Tobit model with intercepts, corrected for heteroscedasticity, and excluded flagged responses was considered the preferred model. The utility values of the best (11111), 2nd best (21111), worst (55555), and 2nd worst (54555) health states are 0.991, 0.963, -0.733, and -0.653, respectively. The most important dimension is Pain/Discomfort followed by Anxiety/Depression, Mobility, Usual Activity, and Selfcare respectively.  Conclusion: The Nigerian EQ-5D-5L value set was developed from a representative population of adult Nigerians using the EQ-PVT. The findings provide essential information to facilitate the HTA research in Nigeria and other West Africa countries to inform policy decision making and to support the progress towards Universal Health Coverage (UHC).  KEY WORDS: HEALTH TECHNOLOGY ASSESSMENT / EQ-5D / VALUATION STUDY / EQ-PVT / NIGERIA/ VALUE SET
