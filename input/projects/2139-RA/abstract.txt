AbstractObjectives: To evaluate the psychometric performance of the EQ-5D-5L and its vision bolt-on in adults with type 2 diabetes in India.Methods: Data from the cross-sectional SMART India diabetes cohort were analysed (n=7,910). Data collected included the EQ-5D-5L, vision bolt-on, EQ VAS, VisQoL, visual acuity, diabetic retinopathy (DR) severity with grading for presence of Diabetic Macular Oedema (DMO), demographic and clinical variables. DR severity was categorized as no DR, non-vision threatening diabetic retinopathy and vision threatening DR. Visual acuity was categorised as visual impairment (visual acuity>0.3 to 1.0 logMAR) and blindness (visual acuity>=1.0 logMAR) based on the US criterion. Additional variables considered included age, sex, diabetes duration, rurality, education level and epidemiological transition level. A comprehensive psychometric analyses was conducted including calculation of ceiling effects, informativity, convergent validity, explanatory power, known-groups validity, as well as a subgroup analysis on convergent validity were assessed.  These were done at the bolt-on and instrument levels using calculated level sum scores.Results: Of the 7,910 participants, 1,213 (15.3%) had visual impairment and 126 (1.6%) were blind. By diabetic retinopathy severity grade, 490 (6.2%) had non-vision threatening diabetic retinopathy (non-VTDR) and 365 had vision threatening DR. The mean age of the study sample was 58.6 years (SD 10.6). Compared with those with normal vision, blind participants more often had diabetes duration ≥10 years (46% vs 29%), vision-threatening DR (13% vs 4%), and ungradable retinal images (50% vs 20%). Problems on the vision bolt-on was reported by 28% (n=1,855), 60% (n=733) and 100% (n=126) of participants with normal vision, visual impairment and blindness. The bolt-on reduced ceiling effects (absolute reduction 7.1% points; relative reduction 13.4%) and improved informativity (Shannon’s H’ 3.55 to 4.46). VA (ρ =0.43) and visQOL mean score (ρ=0.51) showed stronger correlations with the bolt-on than core EQ-5D-5L dimensions (VA: ρ=0.22-0.31; VisQOL: ρ=0.32-0.49). The bolt-on improved model fit for VisQoL (ΔR2=0.103), VA (ΔR2=0.107), any visual impairment (Δ pseudo-R2=0.057), and blindness (Δ pseudo-R2=0.183; all p0.001). Discriminatory ability improved for blindness (Relative efficiency [RE]=1.50), vision-threatening DR (RE=1.42), and DMO (RE 1.41).Conclusions: In this nationally representative cohort of people with diabetes in India, the vision bolt-on demonstrated improved the psychometric performance compared to the EQ-5D-5L alone. These findings support its use in HRQoL measurement and HTA, particularly in LMIC settings in diabetes related visual complications.
