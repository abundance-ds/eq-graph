This PhD project explores recall bias in self-reported health-related quality of life (HRQoL) using the EQ-5D-5L in the general population. Despite the widespread use of retrospective health assessments in health economics and outcomes research, little is known about their accuracy or the factors influencing them. This research addresses how reference points, response styles, and life events affect the validity and comparability of EQ-5D-5L data over time, using the multi-country, longitudinal POPCORN dataset.The project comprises five work packages (WPs):WP1 examines how accurately individuals recall their past HRQoL and identifies socio-demographic, clinical, and psychological predictors of recall bias.WP2 tests whether recall bias is stable across time points, suggesting a trait-like cognitive pattern.WP3 explores whether individuals who evaluate their health relative to age norms exhibit greater recall bias, linking internal standards to retrospective distortion.WP4 investigates whether response heterogeneity —measured using vignette evaluations— explains individual differences in recall bias, providing insight into interpretation of EQ-5D-5L response scales.WP5 studies whether major health events, specifically COVID-19 infection, serve as cognitive anchors that distort retrospective HRQoL assessments.Together, these work packages address core priorities of the EuroQol Foundation, including how response scale heterogeneity, changes in internal standards, and contextual factors affect EQ-5D data quality. By combining longitudinal data, vignette methodology, and detailed individual-level information, this research will generate actionable insights into the interpretation and use of EQ-5D instruments in longitudinal and population health studies. These insights will support better-informed decision-making in clinical research, health monitoring, and policy.
