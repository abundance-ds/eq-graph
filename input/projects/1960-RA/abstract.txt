The project uses EuroQol’s EQ-5D instrument to study the temporal trends and sociodemographic inequalities in self-reported disability patterns that underlie disability-free life expectancy (DFLE). With a focus on the Estonian adult population, where total life expectancy (TLE) has increased substantially since the mid-1990s, the project aims to: a) analyse socio-demographic inequalities and temporal changes of EQ-5D and disability indicator GALI during 2002–2024, b) decompose the activity limitations into specific health profiles and assess their impact to inequalities in DFLE by combining self-reported and registry data, c) develop HRQoL population norms based on EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L for Estonia. The project will extend the currently available data on population-level HRQoL by conducting a repeated, EQ-5D focused study on 2020 survey respondents complemented by individual-level data linkage from health registries. Resulting multilayered longitudinal data opens a unique opportunity for detailed study of individual trajectories and temporal changes in both activity limitations and EQ-5D to decompose activity limitations into specific health profiles. The project will also analyse the role of reporting heterogeneity and have overarching focus on health inequalities. Additionally, the project would result in development of population norms for Estonia, filling the current gap on the lack of EQ-5D specific population norms in the Baltic region. The main deliverables of the project are five academic papers submitted to peer-reviewed journals during the project period of 27 months. The project aims, closely aligned with the priority areas of EuroQol’s Population and health systems working group, are expected to provide significant academic value to EuroQol Consortium and will provide data-driven support for national public health policy in improving population health and addressing health inequalities in Estonia.
