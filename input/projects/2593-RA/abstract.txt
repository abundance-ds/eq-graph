Background: The conventional administration of patient-reported outcome measures relies on predefined, fixed time points. This static approach presents methodological limitations when patients experience frequent and often unpredictable fluctuations in health status due to acute adverse events, treatment modifications, and disease progression. Can AI transform the administration of EQ-5D in routine clinical practice?Aims: This study aims to evaluate the feasibility of AI-driven, event-triggered EQ-5D-5L administration for measuring HRQoL in patients with breast cancer, and to compare HRQoL trajectories derived from event-triggered versus fixed-interval assessments. A secondary objective is to explore the relationship between AI-derived emotional phenotypes and the EQ-5D-5L anxiety/depression response.Methods: The proposed study will be embedded within a large-scale, multi-centre, prospective, controlled, real-world study evaluating AI-driven full-course breast cancer management in China. For the embedded study, 50 patients per group will be enrolled and randomized to the intervention and control groups. In the control group, EQ-5D-5L will be collected at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months. In the intervention group, additional event-triggered assessments are automatically prompted by the AI system upon detection of clinically significant events in addition to the same fixed data collection schedule as in the control group. Feasibility will be evaluated through response rates, data completeness, and assessment adherence. We will investigate whether the patient's emotional state at the precise moment of EQ-5D-5L completion (as detected by the AI during the interaction in which the questionnaire is administered) systematically influences the response to anxiety/depression item, controlling for the underlying characteristics.
