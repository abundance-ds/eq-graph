The research team conducted semi-structured interviews face-to-face and via Zooms with lay persons in seven countries including Argentina, Canada, China, India, Indonesia, Netherlands, and Spain. Trained interviewers used a pre-tested interview guide to elicit important dimensions or concepts as characteristics of poor health and asked interviewees to nominate the five most important health dimensions using a card sorting exercise. The interviews were transcribed, translated into English, and pooled for thematic and frequency analysis. We found that the EQ-5D health dimensions were cross-culturally considered as the most important aspects for describing poor health. The study provides support for the idea that EQ-5D covers aspects of poor health which are broadly relevant to people in a range of geographical and cultural settings. This should give confidence to users who may have concerns about the fact that it was initially developed by researchers in Europe over 30 years ago.
