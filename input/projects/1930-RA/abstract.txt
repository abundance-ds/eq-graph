In some clinical conditions, it may be worthwhile to extend the EQ-5D instruments by adding bolt-ons to the core five domains. In view of a multitude of possible bolt-ons, it is important to develop a reasonably cheap, reliable, and consistent valuation approach. Currently, the most developed idea is the scaling factor approach (SFA). However, SFA can be operationalized in various manners, but there is limited evidence to compare these. We seek to provide evidence in three areas. First, we plan to compare three strategies for linking the bolt-on to existing EQ-5D value set: (a) standalone bolt-on valuation; (b) estimating the SFs by comparing bolt-on preference data with pre-existing core domains importance; (c) estimating the SFs entirely within the new bolt-on valuation study (to account for changes in preferences across studies). Second, we plan to compare the two types of discrete choice experiments (DCE): with and without duration (to reduce the cost, we disregard the time trade-off method in the current proposal). Third, to assess the possibility of applying different SFs for the five core domains. Data will be collected through computer-assisted web interviews in Poland and China, with respondents valuing EQ-5D or EQ-5D+bolt-on health states using DCE with or without duration (hence, four arms). We plan to recruit 3000 respondents in each country (1000 in each of bolt-on arms, 500 in each of EQ-5D arms). We plan to compare the resulting models based on the convergence of estimation, estimation errors, face-validity of the results (e.g., inconsistencies).
