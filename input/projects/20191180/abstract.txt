This project aimed to improve the anchoring of stand-alone Discrete Choice Experiment (DCE) duration value sets onto the QALY scale by incorporating immediate death and Maximum Endurable Time (MET) into the choice tasks and modeling framework. Originally planned as a two-phase study in the UK and the Netherlands, the project was significantly adapted due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Phase 2 was cancelled, and Phase 1 data collection was relocated to Australia for the qualitative component and combined with the RAMPACT project for the quantitative component. We utilized a mixed-methods approach. First, online cognitive interviews with 21 Australian adults evaluated a split-triplet DCE format, revealing that participants actively consider MET and place high value on the final weeks of life, though survey design choices (color, overlap, and terminology for death) heavily influenced their heuristics. Second, quantitative data collection evaluated the inclusion of supplemental pairwise choice tasks. While the study found that incorporating these supplemental tasks successfully improved the statistical identification of the discount rate and produced better-anchored value sets corresponding to cTTO benchmarks, we ultimately found that the intended formal MET model could not be reliably fitted to the collected data.
