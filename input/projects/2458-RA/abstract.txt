The valuation of health-related quality of life (HRQoL) instruments for infants and toddlers remains largely unexplored, limiting the inclusion of very young children in economic evaluations despite this being a critical period for early intervention. The EuroQol Toddler and Infant Populations (EQ-TIPS) instrument was recently developed to measure HRQoL in children aged 0-3 years across six dimensions (movement, play, pain, social interaction, communication, and eating). However, no valuation studies have yet been conducted for EQ-TIPS, primarily due to methodological challenges related to the choice of elicitation method, respondent group, and framing of perspective.This study will assess the feasibility of applying latent discrete choice experiment (DCE) methods to EQ-TIPS-5L valuation through a two-phase mixed-methods design. Phase 1 will involve 20–30 cognitive interviews with parents and general adults to explore comprehension, interpretation, and feasibility of valuation perspectives (own vs. hypothetical child) and age framings within the 0–3-year range. Findings will inform the design of the DCE implemented in Phase 2, where a nationally representative sample of 1,000 Australian adults will complete DCE tasks framed across multiple perspectives and age specifications.Choice data will be analysed under random utility theory using multinomial logit (MNL) and mixed logit (MIXL) models to test for logical consistency, preference heterogeneity, and framing effects. Swait–Louviere tests will be used to assess preference and scale differences across perspectives.The study will generate empirical evidence on the feasibility of latent DCE valuation for EQ-TIPS-5L, identifying which respondent groups and perspectives yield the most logically consistent preferences. Results will inform the development of a standardised EQ-TIPS-5L valuation protocol and provide methodological foundations for incorporating infants and toddlers into economic evaluations.
