**BACKGROUND:** Within the EuroQol, a deep-dive module is a multi-layered measure of one of the EQ-5D dimension. Among the items within a deep-dive module, some may be components of the original items and others may not be listed directly. For example, the self-care item (SC) is a global composite item with two components (washing self and dressing self); yet, an SC deep-dive module may include items beyond those two components (e.g., eating). So far, many studies have examined components of pain/discomfort(PD) and anxiety/depression(AD), but this study is the first to examine the components of SC as well as one of the largest component studies. Also, little is known about their construct validity across age groups, particularly among older adults. **AIMS:** This secondary data analysis examined the relationships between the composite items (namely SC, PD and AD) and their components as a precursor to the development of deep-dive modules. Its results address questions on convergent validity and dimensionality of component items: Aim 1: Are the response levels/patterns of the component items associated with respondent characteristics and EQ-5D-5L responses? Aim 2: Does the global composite item (e.g., self-care) capture the same underlying construct as the two component items (washing self, dressing self)? Furthermore, we assessed each aim in the overall population and by 6 age-specific groups (i.e., differential item functioning). **METHODS:** A 2024 survey of US adults (Dynata) included the EQ-5D-5L and 6 component items of SC, PD and AD. Using these secondary data, we calculated descriptive statistics and conducted onvergent validity analyses examining the association between the component items and other key variables such as EQ-5D-5L responses and respondent characteristics (Aim 1). Furthermore, we assessed whether the global composite items capture the same underlying dimension as their component items (Aim 2). Each analysis was conducted in the full sample (N=6,022) and in 6 age-specific groups (N=802 to 1268) and followed the existing framework for assessing the measurement performance of bolt-ons(Mulhern et al. 2022). The aims of this study complement ongoing work within the DSWG on the development of deep-dive modules, particularly into the psychometrics and limitations of composite items, and a new measure of HRQoL in older adults.
