The EQ-TIPS is a newly developed instrument designed to measure health-related quality of life in infants and toddlers, thereby facilitating economic evaluations. Although it holds significant promise, evidence on its psychometric properties in specific patient populations remains limited. To address this gap, the present study aims to evaluate the psychometric properties of the EQ-TIPS in a clinical sample of infants and toddlers with cerebral palsy, aged 1 to 5 years, in China. A cross-sectional survey design with a 2-week follow-up will be employed. Key assessments will include ceiling and floor effects, convergent validity, explanatory power, known-group validity, and test-retest reliability. The findings will provide the EuroQol Group with valuable insights into the EQ-TIPS's performance in children with cerebral palsy. An additional strength of this study is its evaluation of the instrument in Chinese, a strategically important language. Furthermore, the results will strengthen the evidence base supporting the EQ-TIPS's adoption as an official instrument.
