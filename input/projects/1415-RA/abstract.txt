This project examined the feasibility of collecting measures of health-related quality of life, through the EQ-5D-5L instrument, in a large field experiment in rural Ghana, and explored how self-reported health (as per EQ-5D-5L) relates to preventative health behaviour in those settings. We used data from a randomised controlled trial (RCT) that explored incentives (i.e. a health message video; a health message with a telephone reminder; and a health message with $3.00) for tuberculosis (TB) screening through pop-up clinics in 78 villages. Univariate and multivariable regression analyses showed that neither self-reported health nor cash incentives or telephone nudges), had any significant impact on compliance (i.e. participation in TB screening). This may be due to the fact that by setting up pop-up clinics in each of the villages, we significantly reduced the costs of compliance.
