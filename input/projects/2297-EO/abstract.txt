This project successfully shared new evidence on how EQ-5D-5L value sets differ across regions and methods at ISPOR Europe 2025 in Glasgow, United Kingdom. I presented two posters. The first, “Beyond the Mean: Do Distribution-Based Metrics Offer Greater Insight into Predictors of Societal Health Preferences?”, examined 42 EQ-5D-5L value sets and showed that looking only at the mean hides important distributional patterns. Indicators like the IQR and pits state value performed better in explaining variability, and both EQ-VT version and region consistently influenced value-set characteristics. This reinforces that central tendency alone is not enough when comparing societal preferences. The second, “How Far Have We Come with EQ-5D-5L Value Sets? An Updated Systematic Literature Review of 55 Valuation Studies”, highlighted how rapidly the evidence base has grown. Value sets now span all major world regions, with strong growth in LMICs. The review also found clear methodological shifts towards hybrid valuation, wider use of EQ-VT 2 and above, and more advanced modelling approaches. Through these two presentations, I had the opportunity to speak with health economists, valuation experts, and HTA stakeholders about how these methodological developments can improve the interpretability, comparability, and policy usefulness of EQ-5D-5L value sets globally.
