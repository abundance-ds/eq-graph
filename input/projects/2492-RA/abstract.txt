Background: The EuroQol Health and Wellbeing instrument (EQ-HWB) and EQ-5D Bolt-ons Toolbox represent recent developments within the EuroQol instrument family, designed to capture broader aspects of health and wellbeing. Although EQ-5D instruments have been widely translated, validated, and used in Arabic-speaking populations, the EQ-HWB-9 and generic bolt-on items for sleep, cognition, and fatigue were not yet available in Standard Arabic for Egypt. Given the large Arabic-speaking population and the growing use of EuroQol instruments across the Middle East and North Africa region, producing culturally appropriate Arabic versions is important for future validation, population health measurement, and health outcomes research.Aim: This project aimed to translate and culturally adapt the EQ-HWB-9 EMV2.1, two versions of the EQ-HWB VAS, and seven bolt-on items from the EQ-5D Bolt-ons Toolbox Sleep (items 1 and 2), Fatigue (items 2 and 5), and Cognition (items 1, 3, and 4).Methods: The project followed EuroQol translation guidance for the EQ-HWB and EQ-5D Bolt-ons Toolbox. Two independent forward translations were produced, reconciled into a consensus Arabic version, back-translated into English, and reviewed by the research team to ensure conceptual equivalence and consistency with existing Arabic EuroQol terminology. Cognitive debriefing interviews were conducted with two groups of eight native Arabic-speaking adults: one group tested the EQ-HWB-9 and EQ-HWB VAS versions, and the other tested the seven bolt-on items. Participants represented variation in age, sex, education, socioeconomic background, and health status. Interviews explored clarity, interpretation, preferred wording, and the effect of the VAS preamble and 7-days recall period.Results: The Arabic EQ-HWB-9 iwas generally well understood and culturally appropriate, requiring. Key challenges were identified around the Arabic interpretation of “wellbeing,” the instruction sentence, frequency descriptors, “feel exhausted” item and  “lack of control” item. The EQ-HWB VAS without preamble was understood by respondents, although many performed only one of the two required tasks. Preferences for the VAS with versus without preamble were split, and the 7-day recall period was preferred by most respondents. The seven bolt-on items were also generally clear and acceptable. Cognition items were well understood, although some conceptual overlap was observed between memory and concentration. The tiredness item was more culturally challenging because the Arabic term may also refer broadly to illness, pain, or being unwell, whereas the fatigue 2 item preserved the intended meaning more clearly. Both sleep items were well understood, with equal preference for the version including explanatory wording.Conclusion: This project produced Standard Arabic for Egypt versions of the EQ-HWB-9 EMV2.1, EQ-HWB VAS with and without preamble, and seven bolt-on items. The translated versions provide a foundation for future psychometric validation, EQ-DAPHNIE-related work, and clinical research.
