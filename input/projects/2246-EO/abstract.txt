This project successfully disseminated findings from four distinct studies at two premier international conferences: the International Society for Quality of Life Research (ISOQOL) 32nd Annual Conference and the 11th Annual Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) International Conference, both held in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, USA, in October 2025.At the ISOQOL conference, two oral presentations were delivered. The first, "Exploring Variability in EQ VAS scores: A Systematic Review and Meta-regression of Population Health Studies", was selected as a 2025 New Investigator Oral Presentation Award Finalist. This meta-regression identified that comorbidity prevalence and geographical region (specifically Asia versus Europe) are significant drivers of variability in the EuroQol Visual Analogue Scale (EQ VAS), supporting its utility for cross-regional health comparisons. The second oral presentation, "Measuring What Matters in Primary Care: Health-Related Quality of Life and Its Predictors Among Users of Public Primary Care Clinics in Singapore Using the EQ-5D-5L", analysed data from public polyclinics in Singapore. It highlighted that higher health activation and the absence of comorbidities were linked to better health-related quality of life (HRQoL), whereas Malay ethnicity was associated with lower scores, underscoring significant health disparities.At the PROMIS conference, two poster presentations were delivered. The first, "Differential responsiveness and minimally important differences of patient-reported outcomes: truth or artefact?", revealed that Minimally Important Difference (MID) estimates for the EQ-5D index in spine surgery patients are highly dynamic. The analysis showed perioperative MIDs (0.42) were significantly larger than postoperative MIDs (0.09). This suggests that a response shift—a change in patients' internal standards of measurement—may be at play, urging caution when using global anchors to assess change. The second poster, "Patient-reported outcomes and their associated factors at 1- and 2-year follow-up after lumbar spine surgery", found that patients experienced the largest improvements in pain/discomfort and social functioning one-year post-surgery, with factors like poorer baseline Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROs) and higher education predicting better outcomes.Collectively, this project advanced the scientific understanding of EuroQol instruments by providing key evidence on population-level variability, primary care applications, and critical methodological considerations for interpreting MIDs in surgical populations.
