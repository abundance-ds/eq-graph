Background and objectiveSepsis is one of the leading causes of death worldwide and a major challenge in intensive care medicine. Survivors frequently experience markedly reduced Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQoL), with persistent physical and functional impairments long after discharge. Despite this, HRQoL assessments such as the EQ-5D-5L are rarely conducted during the acute phase of sepsis, and evidence on their psychometric properties in this population remains scarce.The overall aim of the proposal is to test the psychometric performance of the EQ-5D-5L in critically ill patients using a sample of sepsis patients. Specific objectives are to assess feasibility, floor and ceiling effects, construct validity by examining convergent and known-groups validity, and responsiveness. The secondary aims are to (1) compare self- and proxy-reported EQ-5D-5L assessments in sepsis patients, examining effects of administration mode on psychometric properties, and (2) evaluate the longitudinal stability of these properties across three time points.Proposed methodsThe proposed project is a secondary data analysis of existing clinical trial data. Data was collected during a randomized, controlled, interventional, open-label, multi-center trial that included patients with sepsis or septic shock from 03/2022 to 08/2023 in the medical and surgical ICUs of 24 maximum care hospitals.Next to response distribution, feasibility (absolute numbers and percentage of missing values) and floor/ceiling effects (profiles of “11111” and “55555”), construct validity in terms of convergent (Pearson correlation coefficients) and known-groups validity (t-tests, analysis of variance or non-parametric alternatives and effect size) will be assessed. Finally, responsiveness will be determined by effect size and standardized response mean.
