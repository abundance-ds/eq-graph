The integration of patient perspectives into healthcare is essential for improving care quality and effectiveness. Despite global recognition of this need, there remains a challenge in adequately measuring outcomes that matter most to patients. Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are standardized tools designed to capture patients' health status from their own perspective. One prominent PROM, the EQ-5D, is widely used to assess health-related quality of life (HRQoL) across various dimensions such as mobility, self-care, and pain/discomfort. In Alberta, the EQ-5D-5L has been incorporated into healthcare assessment frameworks and surveys to evaluate the effectiveness of health services and ensure they meet patient needs. This project builds upon our previous collaboration with the Workers' Compensation Board (WCB), which focused on evaluating the performance of two key patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) in assessing Return-to-Work (RTW) programs. Our prior work involved a comparative analysis of the EQ-5D and the SF-36, with the goal of providing WCB with insights into the transition from SF-36 to EQ-5D for RTW assessments. The findings indicated that the EQ-5D outperforms the SF-36 in capturing the nuanced health status of workers, demonstrating greater relevance and accuracy. This analysis has been documented in a detailed report shared with Millard Health, presented at the EuroQol Academy meeting in Copenhagen in March 2024, and will be featured in an upcoming manuscript. Globally, the EQ-5D is a leading instrument for measuring health-related quality of life (HRQoL). Endorsed by Alberta Health for self-reported health status assessments, the EQ-5D is integrated into various outcome measurement initiatives across Alberta Health Services, and its use in WCB return-to-work surveys aligns with provincial and national PROMs initiatives led by the Canadian Institute of Health Information (CIHI) and similar OECD efforts. Our proposed project aims to utilize the extensive EQ-5D data collected through WCB surveys to enhance quality improvement initiatives for RTW programs. Specifically, we will: 1) assess the impact of WCB programs on injured workers' HRQoL as measured by the EQ-5D; 2) compare these HRQoL outcomes to Alberta population norms; and 3) evaluate the predictive validity of the EQ-5D for forecasting RTW outcomes. This research is expected to provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of health programs and inform targeted interventions to improve workers' return-to-work experiences, thus contributing significantly to evidence-based quality improvement in health and social care.
