The valuation of EQ-5D health states with the time trade-off method (TTO) requires the respondent to imagine living in hypothetical health states for some number of years. The durations of these imaginary lives span over many years: in lead-time TTO (LT-TTO), the durations of even 20 years are used. In the valuation of health states lasting into such far future, the results may be distorted by time preference: the willingness to trade-off life years for health-related quality of life may change depending on whether nearer or farther future is considered. This project will investigate a way to reduce this time-preference-related distortion by applying narratives to EQ-5D-5L health state descriptions. Narratives (or storytelling) is a widely used tool in public communication, which is demonstrated to increase recipient engagement and reduce the temporal distance from the objects presented in the communication (i.e., those objects are perceived to appear sooner). Specifically, it is proposed that the narrative form of health state descriptions reduces the temporal distance from the occurrence of the valued health states (as compared to the non-narrative form) and, in consequence, reduces the time preference distortion in the measured TTO utilities. That mechanism will be studied experimentally: 150 respondents will each value 5 EQ-5D-5L health states with LT-TTO under one of conditions, i.e., using either narrative or non-narrative descriptions. The elicited utility values will be compared between the conditions, also accounting for the demographic and sociocultural characteristics of the participants (including future-orientation). The results can help improve EQ-5D-5L valuation methodology by suggesting how to reduce time preference distortion.
