This project, funded by the EuroQol Research Foundation, aimed to evaluate the measurement properties of the EQ-5D-Y-5L, compare its performance with the EQ-5D-Y-3L and CHU9D, and describe the current mental health status and its influencing factors among Chinese children and adolescents with mental health issues. The study was conducted as part of the Comprehensive Health and Relevant Factors Measurement among Chinese Young Populations (CHARMING) study, a large-scale repeated cross-sectional survey.A total of 8,292 respondents aged 8–18 years were recruited from ten cities across mainland China. Mental health issues were identified using the Mental Health Scale for Children and Adolescents (MHS-CA), with 931 respondents classified as having mental health issues and 7,361 as general population controls. Measurement properties including ceiling and floor effects, internal consistency (Cronbach's α), Shannon index and evenness, convergent validity with MHS-CA, and known-groups validity were assessed for all three instruments. Multiple linear regression was employed to identify influencing factors of EQ-5D-Y-5L score differences between children with and without mental health issues.Key findings revealed that the EQ-5D-Y-5L reduced ceiling effects compared with the 3L version (34.59% vs. 59.18%), but demonstrated limited overall psychometric performance in children with mental health issues. The CHU9D showed superior information capture (mean H'=0.873) and internal consistency (α=0.782), while the EQ-5D-Y-3L exhibited the strongest known-groups validity. All three instruments showed weak convergent validity with MHS-CA. Multiple regression identified parental depressive symptoms (higher PHQ-9 scores widened the health gap, β=0.23, P0.001) and social support (having more close friends attenuated the gap, β=−1.22, P0.001) as critical determinants of health-related quality of life (HRQoL) disparities. Family environment, physical activity, sleep duration, and parental mental health were consistently identified as key discriminators across all instruments.The findings provide crucial evidence on the comparative effectiveness of generic paediatric HRQoL measures for assessing mental health and highlight the importance of family and social support interventions in improving outcomes for children and adolescents with mental health issues in China.
