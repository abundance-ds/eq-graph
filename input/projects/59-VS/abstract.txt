This study aimed to develop an EQ-5D-5L value set for Uzbekistan to support the measurement and evaluation of health-related quality of life. The project, initially approved in April 2020, faced delays due to the COVID-19 pandemic and the loss of the original principal investigator. In 2023, a new research team was established and trained with the support of EuroQol researchers. Data collection was carried out between May and July 2024 in collaboration with four major medical universities across Uzbekistan. Overall 12 interviewers were requruited and one was dismissed from the study, due to not meeting quality requirements. More than 1,050 interviews were conducted, and 1,007 interviews are presented in the analysis, meeting EuroQol quality protocols. The results reveal that mobility is perceived as the most important health dimension in Uzbekistan, followed by self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression—contrasting with findings from other countries. Age and health experiences were found to be strong predictors of self-reported health on the EQ-VAS scale. The study highlights key policy lessons, including the need for better infrastructure for people with mobility limitations, enhanced support for individuals with reduced self-care ability, and increased investment in inclusive health technologies.
