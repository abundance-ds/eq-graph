The EQ-5D descriptive system could incorporate ‘bolt-on’ descriptors in order to improve measurement sensitivity and relevance in certain contexts. Research has demonstrated that the EQ-5D lacks sensitivity in hearing-related impairment. The aim of this project is to identify one or more items for a hearing bolt-on, using qualitative and quantitative methods. This international research study builds on learnings from the team’s previous and ongoing work developing and testing bolt-on descriptors for cognition, vision, and hearing. This mixed-methods study consists of three phases. In Phase 1a we will conduct a qualitative synthesis, based on a systematic review, to understand the dimensions of quality of life commonly identified by people with experience of hearing problems. Phase 1b will involve a series of group discussions that will be used to reduce, expand, and reformat the candidate domains and terminology identified in 1a. The result of Phase 1 will be the development of candidate bolt-on descriptors, guided by the development criteria used in our ongoing work in cognition and vision. In Phase 2, candidate descriptors will be tested in semi-structured interviews using cognitive debriefing to assess the acceptability and face validity of candidate bolt-ons for hearing. This will allow us to identify bolt-on descriptors to take forward to Phase 3, in which we will conduct an online quantitative survey and use psychometric analyses and item response theory to assess the properties of the candidate bolt-ons using a large sample of patients and the general population from the UK, Australia, and Norway.
