Despite widespread adoption in health technology assessment (HTA), the quality-adjusted life year (QALY) has faced persistent methodological and ethical critiques. Critics argue that QALYs discriminate against the elderly and individuals with disabilities, failing to account for equity or broader societal values. These debates have influenced the policy landscape, particularly in the US. The Affordable Care Act (2010) and the Inflation Reduction Act (2022) have banned the QALY-based decision making across federal programs.  Existing discussions are scattered across health economics, ethics, and policy literature. This systematic literature review aims to identify, appraise, and synthesize the diverse perspectives surrounding QALYs to provide a comprehensive analysis of the existing debate. Because the Group’s long-term financial sustainability is intrinsically linked to the continued recommendations of EQ instruments in the QALY framework for HTA, it is vital that the EuroQol Group actively engages in this debate and shape policy recommendations.
