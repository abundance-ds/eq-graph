Background: When valuing child health (e.g., generating EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L value sets) decisions need to be made on whose preferences and who respondents should think about. Emerging evidence from studies with the public, child valuation experts, and older adolescents themselves, suggests a majority consensual view that older adolescents (16-17-year-olds) should be involved in valuing child and adolescent health states imagining themselves living in impaired health, even when trading off life years as in time trade-off (TTO). However, limited research has assessed the feasibility and content validity of the existing EQ-VT valuation protocol, including TTO, for use in older adolescents. Aim: Using mixed-methods research, the overall aim is to assess the feasibility and content validity of the EQ-VT TTO protocol in the UK to value child health states, using the EQ-5D-Y-3L classification system in older adolescents (aged 16-17 years old). Methods: ‘Think aloud’ TTO interviews will be undertaken with 20 adolescents (50:50 16- and 17-year-olds; 50:50 male/female) valuing health states for themselves, following the EuroQol standardised valuation protocol using the EQ-VT system. Follow-up semi-structured interviews will elicit information on methodological questions relevant to using TTO in adolescents. Data will be analysed using qualitative framework analysis and quantitative analysis of TTO values, designed to assess the feasibility and content validity (comprehensibility, appropriateness, relevance) of using the EQ-VT TTO protocol in 16- and 17-year-olds. Quantitative and qualitative findings will be triangulated to provide further insights. Recommendations will be made for any potential modifications to the EQ-VT TTO protocol for use in older adolescents.
