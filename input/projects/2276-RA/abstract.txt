Emerging research suggests that EQ-5D valuations may exhibit non-additive preferences, where the combined disutility of multiple health problems is smaller than the sum of their individual effects. Recent studies have shown that this pattern can arise not only from true preference structures but also from boundary-induced compression caused by the limited valuation range of bounded tasks such as composite Time Trade-Off (cTTO). This study aims to disentangle these two mechanisms by comparing how interaction effects manifest across cTTO and Discrete Choice Experiment (DCE) data.Using ten existing EQ-5D-5L valuation datasets that each include both cTTO and DCE components collected with the standardized EuroQol Valuation Technology (EQ-VT 2.0), we will estimate four alternative model specifications: (1) dimension-specific interactions (e.g. pain/discomfort × anxiety/depression), (2) count-based severity terms (e.g. N45), (3) data-driven two-way severe-level interactions, and (4) a non-linear power model capturing general curvature in disutility. Model performance will be assessed by RMSE, AIC, BIC, and adjusted R².By comparing interaction patterns between bounded (cTTO) and unbounded (DCE) data, the study will quantify the extent to which observed non-additivity reflects genuine preferences versus boundary effects. Cross-study comparisons will then identify whether consistent interaction structures emerge across countries or if effects are context-specific.
