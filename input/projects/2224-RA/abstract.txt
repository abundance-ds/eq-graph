As global health systems face the challenges of an aging population, improving quality of life at the end of life is increasingly recognized as an essential aspect of care. Patients with advanced illnesses often face challenges that go beyond traditional health metrics, highlighting the importance of broader wellbeing measures to support person-centred care and inform economic evaluations. The EQ-HWB-S is a promising preference-based instrument designed to capture holistic quality-of-life outcomes across health and social care settings. However, its content validity and measurement performance remain unevaluated in populations nearing end-of-life. This study aims to assess the content validity and psychometric properties of EQ-HWB-S in patients with advanced illness through a two-phase, mixed-methods design. In Phase 1, cognitive debriefing interviews with 12 patients will assess the relevance, comprehensibility, and comprehensiveness of the EQ-HWB-S. In Phase 2, we will evaluate construct validity, known-group validity, test-retest reliability, and structural validity in a total of 400 patients with advanced cancer, heart, lung, and kidney diseases. Participants will be recruited from major tertiary hospitals and hospices across Singapore. EQ-HWB-S will also be compared with ASCOT (a social care-related quality of life instrument), ICECAP-SCM (a capability-based well-being measure for people receiving end-of-life care), and the EQ-5D-5L to enable a head-to-head evaluation of their measurement properties in patients with advance illness. Findings will provide robust evidence on the validity and reliability of EQ-HWB-S and inform its broader application in research, clinical, and policy contexts, advancing the measurement of what truly matters to patients nearing the end of life.
