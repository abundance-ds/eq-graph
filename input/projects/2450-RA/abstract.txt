Background: Assessing health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in vulnerable young populations is essential for informing policy and improving healthcare services tailored to their needs. South Korea has been selected as the study site because no validation work has been conducted on EQ-5D-Y in this context so far. The EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L are preference-weighted instruments designed for measuring HRQoL in children and adolescents, yet their applicability in specific vulnerable subgroups remains underexplored. This study aims to validate two HRQoL questionnaires among vulnerable young populations, with a focus on children and adolescents residing in orphanages. We will culturally adapt the EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L, two generic, preference-based instruments designed for measuring the HRQoL of children and adolescents, for use in South Korea and assess their validity and reliability in children and adolescents residing in orphanages. Methods: The project consists of two phases. Phase 1 will focus on the cultural and linguistic adaptation of the EQ-5D-Y (English to Korean) to ensure its relevance and comprehensibility. The study will consult the EuroQol Version Management Committee to verify whether a validated Korean version of both EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L exists. If necessary, a rigorous translation and cross-cultural adaptation process will be undertaken. Cognitive interviews will be conducted to assess content and face validity, ensuring that the translated versions are conceptually equivalent to the original instrument and appropriate for use in the target population.Phase 2 will involve the psychometric evaluation of both EQ-5D-Y versions in vulnerable young populations residing in orphanages. This will include assessing construct validity (convergent and known-groups validity) and test-retest reliability (over 1–2 weeks). A head-to-head comparison between the EQ-5D-Y-3L and EQ-5D-Y-5L will be conducted for both self-reported and proxy-reported versions. Additionally, this study will explore HRQoL outcomes in this population and identify associated predictive factors. Findings from this research will contribute to methodological advancements in HRQoL measurement and provide evidence for selecting the most suitable EQ-5D-Y version for vulnerable young populations.
