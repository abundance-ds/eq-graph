EQ-5D is widely used in economic evaluations and population health assessments. This study used a large population-based cohort with EQ-5D-3L data to describe health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in the general population, examine variations across sociodemographic factors, prevalent health conditions and risk factors, and assess temporal trends and how inequalities developed over time (2002–2021). The cross-sectional analyses included 52,714 respondents aged 23–104 years from the 2021 Stockholm Public Health Cohort (SPHC), larger than most studies on population norms to date. The trend analyses were based on 149,439 observations aged 23–84 from five waves (2002, 2006, 2010, 2014, and 2021).   Calibration weights constructed using registry data of the whole population were applied to reduce non-response bias and improve representativeness. Differences in EQ VAS and EQ-5D-3L index values were examined using multivariable regression models, and modified Poisson regression was used to estimate the likelihood of reporting problems across EQ-5D dimensions. Nonlinear associations between age and HRQoL were modelled using splines, and interactions between survey wave and socioeconomic status were included to evaluate changes in socioeconomic disparities over time.The HRQoL of the general population was generally good but variations across sociodemographic and risk factor groups were observed. HRQoL demonstrated a non-linear relationship with age, with relatively lower scores observed among the youngest adults, increasing through midlife, followed by a marked decline in older age, particularly among those aged 80 years and above. The mean EQ-5D-3L index was lower among females (0.79 [95% CI: 0.79–0.80]) than males (0.83 [95% CI: 0.83–0.84]), and a similar pattern was observed for the mean EQ-VAS scores (75.96 [95% CI: 75.59–76.33] vs. 78.3 [95% CI: 77.62–78.42], respectively). People with lower education, low disposable income as well as those born outside of the European union and UK reported relatively lower HRQoL. Adjusting for age attenuated but did not eliminate these demographic and socioeconomic differences. Persistent associations between behavioral risk factors and HRQoL were also observed. Similarly, people with chronic health conditions, particularly those with long-term illness or multiple health conditions, exhibited markedly lower HRQoL. The preliminary results from analyzing data over two decades indicate that, overall, the mean index values and EQ-VAS scores remained stable throughout the study period. Among females, the EQ-5D index was 0.80 (95% CI 0.80–0.81) in 2002 and 0.80 (95% CI 0.80–0.80) in 2021, and EQ VAS scores were 0.77 (95% CI 0.77–0.78) in 2006 and 0.76 (95% CI 0.76–0.77) in 2021. Among males, the EQ-5D index was 0.85 (95% CI 0.85–0.86) in 2002 and 0.84 (95% CI 0.83–0.84) in 2021, and EQ-VAS scores were 0.79 (95% CI 0.79–0.80) in 2006 and 0.78 (95% CI 0.78–0.79) in 2021. However, distinct patterns across sociodemographic subgroups were observed, indicated by significant interactions with survey years. HRQoL patterns differed by age group, with a relative decline in the younger cohort, relative stability in the middle-aged and older population. Income-related differences also appeared to widen over time, with lower-income groups showing a more marked decline in HRQoL.In conclusion, this study demonstrated substantial variations in HRQoL across sociodemographic groups, behavioral risk factors, and self-reported health conditions, providing insights that can inform healthcare planning and future investments in health and research. The results may also serve as reference values in clinical and economic evaluations, and for comparing outcomes from specific patient subgroups with the general population. Over time, HRQoL seems stable albeit not for all. Notably, HRQoL seemed to decline for younger people and those with low income.
