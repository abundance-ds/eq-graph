Pregnant people represent a specific population group with changes in health states throughout pregnancy, from conception to postpartum. The EQ-5D-5L instrument has been used previously to describe such changes in the health states of pregnant population (by the same group of investigators as well as others) and findings have been published. At the same time, previously published work has been based on cross-sectionally collected data, and no study to date has evaluated monthly changes in HRQoL from pregnancy through postpartum. The concept of intertemporal health measures has been increasingly discussed due to its relevancy and importance in health care decision making. Furthermore, whether instruments like the EQ-5D-5L can be used to detect or predict health issues during pregnancy remains uncertain. Thus, we aim to verify our previously reported findings using a longitudinal assessment of health-related quality of life (HRQoL) with the EQ-5D-5L in a national sample of pregnant people. That way, we are able to assess self-reported HRQoL changes month by month by pregnant persons during their journey from early pregnancy to postpartum (Aim 1). In addition, longitudinal measures permit the assessment of the EQ-5D-5L’s responsiveness to and ability to predict health changes in this particular cohort (Aims 2a & 2b). We expect that results from this longitudinal study will further validate the EQ-5D-5L performances in this unique cohort at a granular level of measuring the monthly changes.
