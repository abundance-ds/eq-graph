**Background** Health economists and other researchers in Nepal are using EQ-5D in their research with proxy value sets from different countries. The Ministry of Health and Population, Health Insurance Board, Social Security Fund and the Department of Drug Administration of Nepal are the key government agencies which have the potential to use cost-utility analysis and health technology assessment in their decision-making. Besides these agencies at the federal level, provincial and municipal governments also have the potential to use cost-utility analysis, particularly in redesigning the package of basic health services that are being provided universally free of user’s fee. Therefore, an EQ-5D-5L valuation study in Nepal can help address the gap and move forward with the adoption of the Health Technology Assessment (HTA) in the country. Additionally, evidence on the direct comparison between value set from Discrete choice Experiments with duration (DCEd) approach and commonly used composite time trade-off (cTTO) will guide researchers to adopt more convenient yet reliable approach for future valuation studies in lower-middle-income country setting. **Objectives** The objective of this study is two-fold: 1) to develop EQ-5D-5L value sets for the adult population in Nepal; and 2) to make a direct comparison between DCEd and cTTO valuation methods in Nepali context. **Methods** We will conduct 1,000 face-to-face interviews following the EuroQol Valuation Technology (EQ-VT) protocol. We will apply a multi-stage cluster sampling technique and the sample will be allocated based on population distribution by age, sex, and geographical areas to represent the Nepal population. The research team consists of multidisciplinary expertise and Nepal team will lead the data collection with trained and qualified interviewers. A system will be established to continuously monitor quality of the collected data using EuroQol group’s tools and expert support from quality control officers. The respondents will complete cTTO, discrete choice experiments (DCE) (without a duration attribute) and split-triplet DCEd tasks during interview. Regression models will be employed for estimating values from the data collected using cTTO and DCE questions. The estimated values using cTTO and DCEd approach will be compared by using scatterplots and Bland-Altman plots. Ethical clearance will be obtained from Brunel University London, UK and Health Research Council, Nepal. **Output** The study will provide EQ-5D value sets for the adult population in Nepal. Additionally, this study will provide evidence on whether the DCEd approach delivers results similar to the commonly used cTTO method for eliciting health state utility values for lower-middle-income country settings. The findings will be published in scientific peer-reviewed journals. We will also conduct workshops, present the findings at conferences, and prepare policy briefs to communicate the findings to the relevant stakeholders in Nepal and internationally.
