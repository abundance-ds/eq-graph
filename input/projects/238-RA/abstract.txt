Background: In 2020-2021, several countries faced another peak of COVID-19 infections, with renewed restrictive policies, continued disruption of daily life, social isolation, economic uncertainties and discontinuation of usual health care. The POPulation health impact of the CORoNavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic (POPCORN) study was set up to investigate the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on health-related quality of life (HRQL) and to study the role of determinants of health. This abstracts pertains to the second wave part of the POPCORN-study, with multiple measurements in the same groups of persons. Methods: We administered a web-based survey repeatedly to participants from Greece, Italy, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, Sweden and the United States. Primary outcome measures were HRQoL (measured by EQ-5D-5L) and mental well-being (measured by WHO-5). Linear regression analyses were performed to estimate the impact of determinants on HRQoL and well-being. In total, 8,097 respondents completed the questionnaire at T1 (April-May 2020) and T2 (May-June 2021). Results: For all countries combined, 33% showed improved HRQoL at T2, whereas 31% deteriorated. In terms of mental well-being, 44% improved and 41% deteriorated. The effect of negative life events on HRQoL and mental well-being was larger than the effect of positive life events. Approximately one in five respondents reported insufficient healthcare access. Respondents with chronic disease, those aged 18–34 and 35–54 years old and respondents from Greece and the UK reported insufficient healthcare access more often compared to their counterparts. Respondents with insufficient healthcare access had both more improved and deteriorated HRQoL compared to respondents with sufficient healthcare, whose HRQoL remained unchanged. We did not find significant interactions between age, gender, socio-economic status (SES) and/or chronic disease status with healthcare access at onset of the COVID-19 pandemic.
