The EQ-5D-Y is a standardized instrument developed by the EuroQol Group to measure health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in children and adolescents. Although designed for self-completion by children aged 8–15 years, its feasibility among younger children remains uncertain. Previous research in Indonesia suggests that even within the recommended age range, some children—particularly those from lower socioeconomic backgrounds—experience comprehension difficulties that may compromise response validity. Understanding whether younger children with chronic illness can meaningfully complete the EQ-5D-Y, and under what conditions interviewer assistance or simplified response structures may improve comprehension, is therefore essential for extending the instrument’s applicability.This study aims to examine the comprehensibility and feasibility of two modes of EQ-5D-Y administration (self-complete and interviewer-assisted) among children aged 6–10 years with chronic illness. Variations in literacy, age, gender, and socioeconomic status will be considered. Specifically, the study seeks to (i) determine whether children aged 6–10 years with chronic illness can understand and complete the EQ-5D-Y-5L, (ii) identify when interviewer assistance is required to support comprehension, and (iii) explore whether the EQ-5D-Y-3L may be more suitable than the 5L for some younger children due to reduced response-level complexity, thereby informing recommendations for administration mode and instrument choice in younger populations.Participants (n = 36) will be purposively selected across combinations of age group (6–7, 7–8, and 8–10 years), gender, and socioeconomic status. A literacy screening will guide assignment to administration mode. Children with adequate literacy will begin with self-completion of the EQ-5D-Y-5L, followed by interviewer assistance if difficulties arise. Children with limited literacy will begin directly with interviewer-assisted administration. If children are unable to complete the EQ-5D-Y-5L even with interviewer assistance, the EQ-5D-Y-3L will be offered to explore whether reduced severity-level complexity improves comprehension. All interviews will conclude with a retrospective, structured debriefing session to examine how children understood the questionnaire. Qualitative thematic analysis will identify patterns of misunderstanding, level inversion, and subgroup differences. Findings will generate evidence-based recommendations regarding minimum age, administration mode, and version suitability, contributing to EuroQol’s methodological work on extending EQ-5D-Y use to younger children with chronic illness.
