In 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared COVID-19 a pandemic, leading to restrictive policies that disrupted daily life. Restrictive policies remained in place until 2024, though their severity varied across time and countries. The POPCORN study (Population Health Impact of COVID-19) was established to examine the pandemic's effects on health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and the role of health determinants. This abstract focuses on the study’s third wave, which includes multiple measurements within the same participant groups. We conducted a repeated web-based survey among participants from Greece, Italy, the Netherlands, the UK, Sweden, and the US. The primary outcomes were HRQoL, measured with EQ-5D-5L, and mental well-being, measured with WHO-5. The POPCORN surveys were administered at three time points (T1: April-May 2020; T2: May-June 2021; T3: April–June 2022). In total, 4,999 respondents completed the T1 and T3 survey. At T3, 240 (5%) participants experienced post-COVID syndrome (PC). We first conducted a cross-sectional analysis, comparing PC participants with three groups: those with only acute COVID-19 (AC), those with at least one chronic condition (CC) but no COVID-19, and healthy participants (PH) with no conditions. In a longitudinal analysis, we assessed changes in HRQoL and mental well-being over time across these four groups. Results showed that PC participants had the poorest HRQoL and mental well-being overall. However, the greatest decline since the start of the pandemic was observed in the AC group, while the impact on PC participants was lower, likely due to pre-existing chronic conditions. Next, we examined how government restriction stringency influenced changes in HRQoL and mental well-being across the total sample over a period of two years. Additionally, we analyzed whether its impact varied between individuals with and without chronic conditions. The results showed that higher government stringency was associated with worsened mental well-being and improved anxiety/depression (EQ-5D-5L) across the total sample. Those with chronic conditions were more likely to experience deterioration compared to healthy individuals. However, the interaction between government stringency and chronic disease status revealed that increased stringency was associated with higher odds of mental well-being deterioration in healthy individuals compared to those with chronic conditions.
