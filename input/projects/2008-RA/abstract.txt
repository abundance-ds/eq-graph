Objectives: Despite growing interest in incorporating carer outcomes into health economic evaluation, evidence on the performance of utility-based measures among informal carers remains limited. This study aimed to assess the performance of the EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9 in capturing outcomes relevant to informal carers.Methods: Data were drawn from the EQ-DAPHNIE survey conducted in English-speaking countries (Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and the United States). The EQ-HWB-9 (UK pilot value set) and EQ-5D-5L (country-specific value sets) were evaluated. Feasibility, distributional properties, convergent validity, and known-group validity were assessed using predefined hypotheses. Subgroup analyses were undertaken by age (≥60 vs <60 years) and financial situation (coping vs financially strained).Results: The sample comprised 6,743 informal carers from Australia (n=1,447), Canada (n=1,265), New Zealand (n=1,435), the United Kingdom (n=1,337), and the United States (n=1,259). A notable ceiling effect (>15%) was observed for the EQ-5D-5L, but not for the EQ-HWB-9. Although most respondents reported no problems on the EQ-5D-5L mobility, self-care, and usual activities dimensions, at least 60% reported problems in the pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression dimensions. For the EQ-HWB-9, impairments were most frequently reported in pain, loneliness, cognition, feeling sad or depressed, anxiety, and control. Both instruments demonstrated moderate correlations with the WHO-5, PROMIS-10, OPQOL-brief, PHQ-2, and GAD-2, and showed good known-group validity across indicators of physical and mental disability, chronic conditions, sleep quality, social support, and the perceived impact of caring on carers’ own health. Findings were consistent across country-specific EQ-5D-5L value sets and remained robust in subgroup analyses.Conclusions: Both the EQ-5D-5L and EQ-HWB-9 appear suitable for assessing outcomes among informal carers. However, evidence on responsiveness remains limited and is needed to more fully evaluate the performance of these measures in this population. Further research is warranted.
