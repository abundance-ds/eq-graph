Objectives This study aimed to evaluate the psychometric properties of the EQ-5D-5L for use in patients with Graves’ disease (GD). Methods A prospective cohort of patients with relapsed GD recruited at three public hospitals in Hong Kong completed the EQ-5D-5L and ThyPRO-39 questionnaires at baseline, 1-month, and 6-month follow-ups. Construct validity was tested by examining the Spearman correlation between EQ-5D-5L and ThyPRO-39 scores at baseline. 1-month test-retest reliability was assessed by Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC), Gwet’s Agreement Coefficient 2 (AC2), and percentage agreement. Responsiveness of EQ-5D-5L index and EQ-VAS scores was assessed using effect size statistics (standardized effect size [SES] and standardized response mean [SRM]). Results Of 125 recruited patients, 101 (80.8%) and 100 (80.0%) patients were followed up at 1- and 6-month, respectively. For construct validity, there was moderate negative correlation was found between EQ-5D-5L index score and ThyPRO-39 Overall QoL-impact score (-0.350), between EQ-VAS score and ThyPRO-39 overall QoL-impact score (-0.451), and between EQ-VAS score and composite score (-0.483), and a strong negative correlation between EQ-5D-5L index score and composite score (-0.567). The Gwet’s AC and percentage agreement were the highest in self-care (0.964 and 0.967), followed by mobility (0.952 and 0.962), usual activities (0.934 and 0.948), pain/discomfort (0.801 and 0.887), and anxiety/depression (0.788 and 0.882). The ICC for the EQ-5D-5L index and the EQ-VAS respectively was 0.707 and 0.700, showing moderate reliability. For patients who reported having ‘worsened’ health at 6-month follow-up, the SES and SRM were -0.66 and -0.42 for EQ-5D-5L index and -1.15 and -1.10 for EQ-VAS, respectively. For patients with ‘improved’ health, SES and SRM were -0.16 and -0.17 for EQ-5D-5L index and 0.19 and 0.20 for EQ-VAS, respectively. Conclusions The EQ-5D-5L demonstrated construct validity, test-retest reliability, and responsiveness to worsened health status among patients with GD. Responsiveness of EQ-5D-5L to health improvement warrants further investigation.
