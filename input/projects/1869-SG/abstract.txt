Background: The relevance of the EQ-5D-Y-5L may be limited for specific population groups due to potential shortcomings such as content validity, sensitivity, and its ability to discriminate between known groups, such as children with moderate/severe obesity or those with normal weight. This study aims to address these challenges by providing essential insights that could enhance the instrument's effectiveness and application. Research Objectives:  Examine how physical activity, sleep, and obesity status impact health-related quality of life (HRQoL), as measured by the EQ-5D-Y-5L, EQ-5D-Y-3L, and Kidscreen-27 instruments, and analyze these effects across different age groups. Assess if changes in the obesity status of children from Fall 2024 to Spring 2025 correspond to changes in EQ-5D-Y-5L scores. Evaluate whether the EQ-5D-Y-5L can effectively differentiate between children with moderate/severe obesity and those of normal weight. Investigate the concurrent validity of the EQ-5D-Y-5L and Kidscreen-27 alongside established measures of physical activity and sleep, using both accelerometry and biometric data.  Study Population: Data will be collected from 243 children in grades 3-5 attending four community schools in the Little Rock School District participating in a green schoolyard program. Data Collection: Measures will include the EQ-5D-Y-5L, EQ-5D-Y-3L, Kidscreen-27, physical activity, and sleep quality assessed through actigraphy. Additional physical activity data will be gathered through direct observations during recess and outside of school hours. Project Context: This study is part of a larger project funded by the National Institute on Minority Health and Health Disparities, NIH (Award Number R01MD018192), now in its second year. Biannual data collection for HRQoL, school climate, and other observations is planned for Fall 2024 and Spring 2025. The study has successfully recruited 243 child-parent dyads, with enrollment of up to 10 per grade per school. This sample size will enable us to successfully achieved stated research objectives, consistent with the most recent COSMIN guidelines. The children's ages range from 8 to 12 years. Methods: State-of-the-art econometric and psychometric methods will be utilized to address the study's objectives. Expected Results: This research program will produce several high-quality publications for leading health economics journals and provide innovative insights into the strengths and weaknesses of the EQ-5D-Y-5L, addressing existing research gaps and aligning with EuroQol's research priorities.
