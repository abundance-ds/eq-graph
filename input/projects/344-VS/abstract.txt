This 18-month study (Project 344-VS), funded from 1 July 2022 to 1 January 2024, aimed to develop Pakistan’s first nationally representative EQ-5D-Y-3L value set. The primary objective was to generate youth-specific utility weights to support equity-focused health technology assessment (HTA) and inform Universal Health Coverage (UHC) decision-making in the country. The valuation study followed a mixed-methods design in accordance with the EuroQol Group’s standardized protocol, incorporating both discrete choice experiments (DCE) and composite time trade-off (cTTO) tasks.The study was implemented in two sequential phases. The first phase involved a national stakeholder consultation held in Islamabad to contextualize the EQ-5D-Y-3L framework, introduce HTA principles, and emphasize the importance of valuation data in guiding resource allocation. Twelve stakeholders from across Pakistan and diverse professional backgrounds participated in a 3.5-hour session. Discussions focused on the need for a national youth value set, appropriate sources of health preferences (adults vs. adolescents), ethical concerns related to valuing life-years, and implementation challenges. Stakeholders acknowledged that while QALYs are not yet widely adopted in Pakistan’s health system, instruments like EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L are increasingly used for research and evidence generation. There was consensus that adolescents aged 11 and above could meaningfully engage in valuation tasks and should be included in data collection. One-third of stakeholders (4 out of 12) recommended using adult preferences initially due to technical and financial constraints. The remaining participants supported involving adolescents, either concurrently or in future phases. Ethical concerns regarding life-year trade-offs for children were not viewed as significant barriers. Parents or caregivers were considered the most suitable proxies when self-reporting by children was not feasible. Key challenges identified included limited awareness of QALYs among policymakers, scarce local utility data, technical capacity gaps, and restricted health budgets.In the second phase, a sample of 1,000 respondents for DCE and 1,000 for cTTO was recruited from the general population (aged 18+) across six major cities Karachi, Lahore, Peshawar, Quetta, Islamabad, and Rawalpindi representing all four provinces and the federal capital. Participants were drawn from both urban and surrounding rural areas and stratified by residence, age, gender, religion (Muslim, Hindu, Christian), and ethnicity (Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashtun, Balochi, Kashmiri, and others) to reflect national demographics. Initial data collection was completed within the grant period. However, to enhance data validity during the analysis phase, supplementary data collection was undertaken at no additional cost with approval from the EuroQol Executive Board. This led to a minor timeline extension, and the overall study was completed by mid-June 2025. Data cleaning and analysis followed immediately. The most frequently reported health problem was anxiety/depression (54.9% in the cTTO sample), while self-care had the fewest reported issues. Pain/discomfort had the greatest influence on preference values; usual activities had the least. Mean EQ VAS scores were slightly higher in the DCE sample (81.02) than the cTTO sample (78.27). All models demonstrated logical consistency. The Pakistani EQ-5D-Y-3L values ranged from –0.34 (worst health state: 33333) to 1.00 (full health: 11111), with 11112 valued at 0.96. All planned deliverables were produced.
