Our previous study (EQproject 127-2020RA) rigorously investigated the internal structure of the EQ-HWB using the psychometric study data (60+ items from which the final EQ-HWB items were drawn). More than half of the items selected for the EQ-HWB instrument have undergone revision, some substantively, and it is unknown whether the found structures would hold for the current version of the instrument. Furthermore, potential issues posed by the three positively worded items (all remaining items are negatively worded) have been noted by users of the experimental EQ-HWB. While we did not previously find positively/negatively worded items to pose a concerning methods effect, it is unknown whether using the 25 items divorced from the 60+ item bank changes item performance.  Given the limitations of the previous study, we propose to:   confirm the structure(s) found for the EQ-HWB and EQ-HWB-S from project 127-2020RA using data on current version of the EQ-HWB, determine the extent of the measurement effect posed by including both positive/negative items in the 25-item EQ-HWB, and  examine stability of structural findings across subgroups with a focus on comparisons across two languages (English and Chinese) and three countries (Ireland, Australia, China).   Results are important for moving the EQ-HWB to approved status, especially providing measurement evidence on the three positively worded items (and whether they need revision). Furthermore, advancing non-preference based scores, especially for the long-version of the EQ-HWB for which preference-based scoring will not be developed, will support application of the instrument across a broad spectrum of settings.
