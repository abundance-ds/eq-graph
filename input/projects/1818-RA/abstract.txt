Background: Over the past few decades, preference-based Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQoL) measures, notably the EQ-5D developed by the EuroQol Group, have gained prominence. Despite numerous studies of measurement properties published in the literature, there is a lack of guidelines for validating preference-based measures and knowledge about the methodological quality of such studies. Aim: This study aims to systematically review and evaluate the methods used in published validity, reliability, and responsiveness studies of EQ-5D-5L across various health conditions. Methods: Adhering to the PRISMA and COSMIN guidelines, our approach involves meticulous identification of research questions followed by a three-pronged search strategy across prominent databases. Selected articles will undergo a rigorous review process, with data extracted systematically and summarized narratively and/or quantitatively. A tool will be developed and used to evaluate the appropriateness and quality of the identified studies Impact: This review provides the first insights into the variations, appropriateness, and quality of methods used to evaluate the measurement properties of EQ-5D-5L. It will contribute to the Descriptive Systems Working Group’s psychometric research programme, aimed at developing guidelines for psychometric validation studies of EuroQol instruments.
