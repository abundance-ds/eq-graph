This systematic review assessed the number, geographical coverage, and methodological characteristics of value sets for six major multi-attribute utility instruments (MAUIs) commonly used in health technology assessment (HTA). Following PRISMA guidelines, we searched five databases from inception to August 2025 for value set development studies across AQoL, EQ-5D-3L, EQ-5D-5L, HUI, QWB, and SF-6D. We identified 145 value sets from 136 studies: 53 (36.6%) for EQ-5D-3L, 56 (38.6%) for EQ-5D-5L, 29 (20.0%) for SF-6D, and ≤3 (≤2.1%) each for remaining MAUIs. Most (94.5%) adopted a societal perspective. EQ-5D-3L and EQ-5D-5L value sets covered 38 and 44 countries respectively, compared with 14 for SF-6D and one each for AQoL, HUI, and QWB. Among societal value sets, 59.7% were published in 2016–2025. Standardised valuation protocols were reported in 78.4% of EQ-5D-3L, 91.7% of EQ-5D-5L, and 46.4% of SF-6D societal studies, but none for AQoL, HUI, or QWB. The findings reveal that MAUIs vary substantially in value set availability, geographic coverage, recency, and methodological standardisation. These are characteristics that warrant consideration alongside psychometric properties when selecting instruments for HTA.
