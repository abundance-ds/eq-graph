The POPCORN wave 4 project investigated the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and mental well-being (MWB) in the general population the EQ-5D-5L and explored which country level and individual factors were associated with lower HRQoL. We collected data using an online survey among the general population of six countries (Greece, Italy, Netherlands, Sweden, United Kingdom (UK) and the United States (US)) in May-July 2023 (POPCORN wave 4). We merged POPCORN wave 4 dataset with wave 1-3 datasets resulting in one longitudinal dataset.  Next, two complementary studies were conducted within the multinational POPCORN cohort across six countries:Study 1: Examined the association between worry related to future pandemics, global geopolitical events and environmental threats and health-related quality of life (HRQoL), and assessed whether resilience and social connectedness moderated these associations. We found that worry about global threats were consistently linked to lower HRQoL, while resilience and social connectedness were associated with better outcomes. However, their buffering effects differed by threat type.Study 2: Identified longitudinal HRQoL trajectories over three years using latent Markov modeling. Six distinct states were observed, ranging from no problems to multiple problems across EQ-5D-5L dimensions. HRQoL remained largely stable, with older age and chronic disease strongly associated with poorer states, and country-level differences favoring healthier profiles in the Netherlands and the US.
