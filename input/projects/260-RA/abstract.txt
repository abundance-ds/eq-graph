Since initial transmission of a novel coronavirus (2019-nCoV, or SARS-CoV-2) in December 2019, there have been over 78 million cases of COVID-19 and more than 1.7 million deaths worldwide. Nearly one-quarter of COVID-19 cases have been diagnosed in the US. In addition to the direct impacts of COVID-19 on morbidity and mortality, mitigation strategies to promote social distancing have disrupted standard healthcare in the US. Pregnant and postpartum healthcare delivery have been particularly impacted by these measures. These disruptions are likely to have adversely impacted maternal health. Although previous research has documented the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on mental health, limited data have documented the physical impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic on maternal health and few studies have documented perceptions of prenatal and postpartum care. Through quantitative and qualitative data collection, this study will collect information on the experiences of pregnant women during the COVID-19 pandemic and how these experiences may influence health-related quality of life (as measured by the EQ-5D-5L instrument). Funding sought by this application would support baseline data collection for a web-based panel survey of 3,600 US pregnant and postpartum women. Results will inform the maternal health impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the healthcare needs of families post-pandemic.
